Cultivation and Processing of Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi)
Source
The dried flower buds of Magnoliaceae plants such as Wangchun Yulan, Yulan, and Wudang Yulan. From January to March, the unopen buds are cut off at the flower stalks; during the day they are sun-dried, and at night stacked to sweat so that the inside and outside have uniform moisture. When dried to about fifty percent, they are piled for 1–2 days and then further sun-dried until completely dry. If it rains, they may be dried by heat.
Properties and Flavor
Taste: pungent; Nature: warm
Processing
Pick out and clean away stem and debris, crush for use; for medicinal use slightly roast; those already opened are inferior, those withered are not good.
Physical Characteristics
(1) Wangchun Yulan
Flower buds are oblong-ovoid, like a brush tip, 1.2–2.5 cm long, 0.8–1.5 cm in diameter; the base often has a short woody stalk about 5 mm long, with whitish dot-like lenticels on the stalk. Bracts 2–3 layers, two per layer, with small scale buds between two layers of bracts; outer surface of bracts densely covered with gray-white or gray-green long pubescence, inner surface brownish without hairs. Perianth segments 9 in 3 whorls, brownish; outer whorl perianth segments strap-shaped, about one quarter the length of the inner two whorls, resembling sepals; stamens numerous, arranged spirally on the lower part of the receptacle, filaments flattened, anthers linear; pistils numerous, spirally arranged on the upper part of the receptacle. Light in weight, brittle in texture. Fragrant aroma, taste pungent and cool with a slight bitterness.
(2) Yulan
Flower buds 1.5–3 cm long, 1–1.5 cm in diameter, with relatively stout basal stalks and light brown lenticels. Bracts outer surface densely covered with gray-white or gray-green pubescence. Perianth segments 9, without marked difference between inner and outer whorls.
(3) Wudang Yulan
Flower buds 3–4 cm long, 1–2 cm in diameter, with stout stalks and reddish-brown lenticels. Bracts outer surface densely covered with pale yellow or pale yellow-green pubescence; in some specimens the outer layer bract pubescence has fallen off, appearing dark brown. Perianth segments 10–15, without marked difference between inner and outer whorls.
Among the three medicinal materials, those with large, unopened, yellow-green buds without stalks are best.
Effects of Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi)
Functions
The dried flower buds of Magnoliaceae plants such as Wangchun Yulan, Yulan, and Wudang Yulan.
Treats headache, nasal congestion and discharges, nasal obstruction, and toothache.
Channels Entered
Lung channel; Stomach channel
Indications
Expels wind and opens the orifices. Treats headache, rhinitis or sinusitis, nasal obstruction, and toothache.
① Ben Jing (Classic Materia Medica): Acts on the five viscera and the body’s cold and heat, wind-induced head pain, facial numbness.
② Bei Lu (Alternate Records): Warms the center and releases the muscles, unblocks the nine orifices, clears nasal obstruction and causes flowing nasal discharge, treats facial swelling radiating to the teeth causing toothache, dizziness and a sensation as if on a carriage or boat. Promotes hair growth, expels white parasites.
③ Yao Xing Lun (Treatise on Medicinal Properties): Can treat facial oiliness. Used for facial luster, promotes brightness.
④ Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao (Daily Flower’s Materia Medica): Unblocks channels and vessels, brightens the eyes. Treats headache, aversion to cold, bodily stiffness, and itching.
⑤ Dian Nan Ben Cao (Southwest Materia Medica): Treats cerebral leakage and nasal sinus issues, expels wind; can be roasted on new tile into powder. Treats facial cold pain, gastric qi pain; take with warm wine.
⑥ Ben Cao Gang Mu (Compendium of Materia Medica): For rhinitis, nasal catarrh, nasal blockage, nasal sores, and post-measles nasal sores, grind into fine powder, add a little musk, dip scallion white into it and insert several times.
⑦ Yu Qiu Yao Jie (Yu Qiu’s Pharmacological Explanation): Drains the lung and descends rebellious qi, facilitates qi and disperses accumulations.
⑧ Jiangxi Zhong Yao (Jiangxi Province Medicinal Materials): Externally can promote uterine contraction and has oxytocic effects.
Dosage and Administration
Internal: Decoction, 3–10 g, preferably decocted in a bag; or used in pills and powders.
External: Appropriate amount, powdered and snuffed into the nostrils; or use its distilled aromatic water and drop into the nostrils.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated for those with yin deficiency and vigorous fire.
① Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu (Collected Annotations on Materia Medica): Chuanxiong (Chuan Xiong) is its envoy. Combines badly with five types of stone resin. Averse to Acorus (Chang Pu), Puhuang (Pollen of Typha), Coptis (Huang Lian), gypsum (Shi Gao), and Huang Huan (Huang Huan).
② Ben Cao Jing Kui (Collected Defects of Materia Medica): Persons with qi deficiency should avoid it; those whose headache is due to blood deficiency with blazing fire should not take it; toothache due to stomach fire is contraindicated.
③ Ben Cao Hui Yan (Collected Sayings on Materia Medica): People with qi deficiency, who only occasionally catch wind-cold causing orifices to be blocked, should not use it.
Formulas Using Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi)
① For rhinitis: Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) half liang (about 9 g), Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 2 qian 5 fen (about 7.5 g), Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) one liang (about 15 g), Mint (Bo He) leaf half qian (about 1.25 g). Dry all in the sun and grind into a fine powder. Each dose two qian (about 5 g), take with scallion and clear tea after meals. (Ji Sheng Fang: Cang Er Powder)
② For rhinitis and sinusitis:
(1) Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) 3 qian (about 7.5 g), three chicken eggs. Cook together; eat the eggs and drink the decoction. (Selected Simple and Effective Prescriptions)
(2) Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) four parts, Goose-Does-Not-Eat-Grass (literally; E bu shi cao) one part. Soak in water 4–8 hours then distill to obtain aromatic water; use as nasal drops. (Guangdong Selected Herbal Prescriptions)
③ For nasal discharge where a mass grows inside the nostril: Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) (dehaired) four liang, Mulberry Root-bark (Sang Bai Pi) (honey-fried) four liang, Gardenia Fruit (Zhi Zi) one liang, Immature Bitter Orange (Zhi Shi), Platycodon Root (Jie Geng), Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) each two liang. Grind all into fine powder. Each dose two qian, take with mild radish soup. (Yao Yi Da Quan)
④ For severe nasal blockage preventing breathing: Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) and Chuanxiong (Chuan Xiong) each one liang, Asarum (Xi Xin) (with sprouts removed) seven qian and a half, Akebia or Mu Tong (Mu Tong) half liang. Grind into fine powder. Use a small amount wrapped in cotton and insert into the nostrils, replace when wet. Cures in five to seven days. (Zheng Zhi Zhun Sheng: Chuanxiong Powder)
⑤ For inability to smell fragrances: Soap Pod (Zao Jiao, also called Pig-Tooth Soap), Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi), Acorus Rhizome (Shi Chang Pu) in equal parts. Grind into powder. Wrap in cotton and insert into nostrils. (Mei’s Newly Compiled Effective Prescriptions)
⑥ For nasal swelling or sores in the nose (often due to alcoholic toxin): Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) one liang, Sichuan Coptis (Chuan Huang Lian) five qian, Forsythia Fruit (Lian Qiao) two liang. Lightly fry all, grind into powder. Take three qian after each meal with plain water. (Miao’s Selected Prescriptions)
⑦ For toothache, swelling, or ulcerated gums: Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) one liang, Cnidium Fruit (She Chuang Zi) two liang, Qing Salt (Qing Yan) five qian. Grind together into powder and apply. (Ben Cao Hui Yan)
⑧ For head and facial swelling and itching as if insects crawling (wind-phlegm type): Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) one liang, Processed Aconite (White Fu Zi), Pinellia Rhizome (Ban Xia), Trichosanthes Root (Tian Hua Fen), Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi), Stiff Silkworm (Jiang Can), Scrophularia Root (Xuan Shen), Red Peony Root (Chi Shao) each five qian, Mint (Bo He) eight qian. Divide into ten doses and take. (Gu Jin Yi Zhun)
⑨ For dizziness, faintness and nausea (cold-phlegm type): Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) one liang, prepared Pinellia (Zhi Ban Xia), Bile-processed Arisaema (Dan Nan Xing), Gastrodia Rhizome (Tian Ma), Dried Ginger (Gan Jiang), Szechuan Lovage Root (Chuan Xiong) each eight qian. Grind into powder, make into pills with water. Take three qian nightly with plain water. (Ben Cao Hui Yan)
⑩ For rhinitis, nasal catarrh, nasal blockage, nasal sores and post-measles nasal sores: Grind Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) into fine powder, add a little Musk (She Xiang), dip scallion white and apply several times; very effective. (Compendium of Materia Medica)
⑾ For slight redness of the nasal tip and sores inside the nose: Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) powdered, add a little Bing Pian (borneol) and Musk (She Xiang), wrap in cotton and insert. (Danxi Xin Fa)
⑿ For inability to smell fragrances: Soap Pod (Zao Jiao), Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi), Acorus Rhizome (Shi Chang Pu) in equal parts. Grind into powder and wrap in cotton to insert into the nostrils. (Mei’s Newly Compiled Effective Prescriptions)
⒀ Mint, Cocklebur, Magnolia, Dahurian Angelica Tea:
Mint (Bo He) 6 g, Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 12 g, Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) 6 g, Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) 6 g, Scallion white (Cong Bai) 3 stalks, Tea leaves 2 g. Put all the above into a pot, decoct, discard dregs and take the liquid. Use as a tea substitute. Efficacy: disperses wind and unblocks the orifices. Indications: treats rhinitis, sinusitis and related disorders.
