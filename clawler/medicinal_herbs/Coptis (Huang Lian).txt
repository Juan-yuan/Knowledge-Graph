Cultivation and Processing of Coptis (Huang Lian)
Source
This item is the dried rhizome of Ranunculaceae plants Coptis chinensis Franch. (Coptis, Huang Lian), Coptis deltoidea C.Y. Cheng et Hsiao (Coptis deltoidea, Ya Lian) or Coptis teeta Wall. (Coptis teeta, Yun Lian). The above three species are commonly called Wei Lian, Ya Lian, and Yun Lian respectively. Harvest in autumn, remove fine roots and sediment, dry, and knock off remaining fine roots.
Nature and Taste
Bitter; Cold
Processing
1. Coptis (Huang Lian): Pick out impurities, wash off mud and sand, moisten thoroughly, slice, dry in the shade. Stir-fry Coptis: fry the Coptis slices over low heat until the surface is a deep yellow, remove and cool.
2. Ginger-processed Coptis (Jiang Huang Lian): squeeze fresh ginger (fresh Ginger, Sheng Jiang) for juice, add an appropriate amount of boiling water, spray evenly into the Coptis slices, after absorption stir-fry over low heat until the surface is a deep yellow, remove and cool. (Per 100 jin of Coptis slices use 12 jin 8 liang of fresh ginger)
3. Evodia-processed Coptis (Yu Huang Lian): first take Evodia (Wu Zhu Yu), decoct thoroughly with appropriate amount of water, remove dregs, then mix the Coptis slices into the decoction until the liquid is absorbed, lightly stir-fry over low heat until slightly dry, remove and air dry. (Per 100 jin of Coptis slices use 6 jin 4 liang of Evodia)
4. Wine-processed Coptis (Jiu Huang Lian): mix the Coptis slices with yellow rice wine (Huang Jiu), let steam slightly, stir-fry until the surface is deep yellow, remove and cool. (Per 100 jin of Coptis slices use 12 jin 8 liang of yellow wine)
Appearance
Wei Lian (Wei Lian): often clustered into tufts, frequently curved, shaped like a chicken claw, single-branch rhizome length 3–6 cm, diameter 0.3–0.8 cm. Surface gray-yellow or yellow-brown, rough, with irregular nodular prominences, fine roots and remnants of fine roots; some internodes have smooth surface like a stem, commonly called "over-bridge." Upper part often retains brown scales, the apex often has remnants of stem or petiole. Hard texture, fracture surface uneven, cork (outer part) orange-red or dark brown, wood (xylem) fresh yellow or orange-yellow, radially arranged, pith sometimes hollow. Slight odor, extremely bitter taste.
Ya Lian (Ya Lian): mostly single-branch, slightly cylindrical, slightly curved, length 4–8 cm, diameter 0.5–1 cm. The "over-bridge" is relatively long. A few residual stems at the apex.
Yun Lian (Yun Lian): curved into a hook shape, mostly single-branch, relatively slender.
Effects of Coptis (Huang Lian)
Actions
This herb is the dried rhizome of Ranunculaceae plants Coptis, Coptis deltoidea, or Coptis teeta.
Main indications: epidemic febrile toxic heat, typhoid, exuberant heat causing vexation of the heart, epigastric distention with nausea and vomiting, bacillary dysentery, hot dysentery with abdominal pain, pulmonary abscess, vomiting, nosebleed, bleeding from below, diabetes (wasting and thirst), pediatric malnutrition (gan ji), roundworm disease, pertussis (whooping cough), throat swelling and pain, inflamed eyes from heat, oral ulcers, carbuncles and sores with toxic swelling, eczema, scalds from boiling liquids and fire.
Channels Entered
Heart channel; Liver channel; Stomach channel; Large Intestine channel
Indications
Drains fire, dries dampness, detoxifies, and kills parasites. Treats epidemic febrile toxicity, typhoid, exuberant heart vexation, epigastric distention with nausea and vomiting, bacillary dysentery, hot dysentery with abdominal pain, pulmonary abscess, vomiting, epistaxis, hematemesis and bloody stools, wasting and thirst (diabetes), pediatric malnutrition (gan ji), roundworm infection, pertussis, throat swelling and pain, inflamed eyes due to fire, oral ulcers, carbuncles and sores with toxic swelling, eczema, scalds.
1. Ben Jing: treats hot qi causing eye pain and corner tearing, brightens the eyes, differentiates intestinal pain and diarrhea, treats women’s vulvar swelling and pain.
2. Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu: relieves Croton (cathartic) toxicity.
3. Bie Lu: treats cold and heat in the five viscera, chronic diarrhea with pus and blood, stops wasting and thirst, treats severe bone-affecting edema, regulates stomach and strengthens the intestines, benefits the gallbladder, treats oral ulcers.
4. Yao Xing Lun: kills pediatric malnutrition parasites, applies to red painful eyes, calms the liver and clears heat toxin.
5. Ben Cao Shi Yi: treats emaciation and shortness of breath.
6. Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao: treats labor-related consumptions and injuries, benefits qi, stops cardiac and abdominal pain, calms fright and irritability, moistens the heart and lungs, promotes flesh growth, stops bleeding; also treats scabies and night sweats, seasonal febrile diseases; steamed in pig stomach into pills for pediatric malnutrition.
7. Ren Zhai Zhi Zhi Fang: can remove bad blood from the orifices of the heart.
8. Zhen Zhu Nang: drains heart fire, for epigastric fullness. Wine-fried or wine-soaked, for conditions above the neck.
9. Wang Haogu: treats primary heart disorders with reversal and excess, and heart accumulation affecting the beam (archaic).
10. Ben Cao Yan Yi Bu Yi: frying with ginger juice, the pungent dispersing property effectively removes heat.
11. Gang Mu: relieves vexation and congestion from overdose of medicines and light powder toxicity.
12. Ben Cao Xin Bian: stops vomiting and eructation of acid, relieves thirst, treats inflamed eyes from heat, calms the heart, stops nocturnal emissions, calms mania, relieves epigastric distention.
13. Ben Cao Bei Yao: treats carbuncles, sores, wine toxicity, fetal toxicity; expels pediatric malnutrition and kills roundworms.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 1.5–3 g; powdered, 0.3–0.6 g each time; or used in pills and powders.
External use: appropriate amount, ground to powder and applied as a poultice; or decoct for washing; or make into an ointment; or soak in juice for use.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in stomach deficiency with nausea and vomiting, spleen deficiency with diarrhea, and early morning kidney leakage; all should be used cautiously.
Formulas with Coptis (Huang Lian)
1. Treating recurrent vexation and agitation of the heart, confusion, palpitations, upper heat, chaotic chest qi, epigastric fullness and oppression, food regurgitated after ingestion: Cinnabar (Zhu Sha) four qian, Coptis (Huang Lian) five qian, raw Licorice (Sheng Gan Cao) two and a half qian. Grind to fine powder, soak in soup to steam into cakes, make into pills the size of millet grain. Each dose ten pills, take after meals by swallowing with saliva little by little. (Ren Zhai Zhi Zhi Fang: Huang Lian An Shen Wan)
2. Treating Shao Yin disorder after two to three days with vexation in the chest and inability to lie down: Coptis (Huang Lian) four liang, Scutellaria (Huang Qin) two liang, White Peony (Bai Shao Yao) two liang, two egg yolks, Donkey-hide gelatin (E Jiao) three liang (some say three ting). Combine five ingredients with six sheng of water, first boil three items and take two sheng, remove dregs, dissolve the gelatin, cool slightly, add egg yolks and stir to integrate. Warm dose seven tai, three times daily. (Shang Han Lun: Huang Lian E Jiao Tang)
3. Treating Heart and Kidney disharmony with palpitations and insomnia: Raw Chuanlian (Sheng Chuan Lian) five qian, Cinnamomum verum inner bark (Rou Gui Xin) five fen. Grind fine, make into honey pills. Take on empty stomach with mild saline soup. (Si Ke Jian Xiao Fang: Jiao Tai Wan)
4. Treating real heat in the Heart channel: Coptis (Huang Lian) seven qian, water one and a half cups, decoct to one cup, take warm away from meals. Reduce for children. (Ju Fang: Xie Xin Tang)
5. Treating epigastric fullness that is soft to pressure, pulse floating above the Guan: Rhubarb (Da Huang) two liang, Coptis (Huang Lian) one liang. Use old two (two-aged) Chen (unfermented?) with two sheng of boiling hemp soup, soak, press and remove dregs. Divide and take warm again. (Shang Han Lun: Da Huang Huang Lian Xie Xin Tang)
6. Treating minor thoracic obstruction located beneath the heart, painful on pressure, pulse floating and slippery: Coptis (Huang Lian) one liang, Pinellia (Ban Xia) half sheng (washed), Trichosanthes seed (Gua Lou) one large piece. Boil these three with six sheng of water; first boil Gua Lou and take three sheng, remove dregs, add remaining drugs and decoct to two sheng, remove dregs. Divide warm into three doses. (Shang Han Lun: Xiao Xian Xiong Tang)
7. Treating great heat with vomiting, moaning, delirium, inability to lie down: Coptis (Huang Lian) three liang, Scutellaria (Huang Qin) two liang, Phellodendron (Huang Bai) two liang, Gardenia (Zhi Zi) fourteen fruits (cracked). Cut these four into slices, add six sheng of water, decoct to two sheng, divide into two doses. Avoid pork and cold water. (Wai Tai Mi Yao Fang: Huang Lian Jie Du Tang)
8. Treating Shang Han with heat in the chest and pathogenic qi in the stomach with abdominal pain and retching: Coptis (Huang Lian) three liang, Licorice (Zhi Gan Cao) three liang (honey-fried), Dried Ginger (Gan Jiang) three liang, Cinnamon twig (Gui Zhi) three liang (peeled), Ginseng (Ren Shen) two liang, Pinellia (Ban Xia) half sheng (washed), Jujube (Da Zao) twelve pieces (cracked). Combine seven herbs with one dou of water, decoct to six sheng, remove dregs. Warm, take three times by day and two times by night. (Shang Han Lun: Huang Lian Tang)
9. Treating vomiting of sour water with wiry and slow pulse: Ginseng (Ren Shen), White Atractylodes (Bai Zhu), Dried Ginger (Gan Jiang), Honey-fried Licorice (Zhi Gan Cao), Coptis (Huang Lian), decoct and drink. (Zheng Yin Mai Zhi: Lian Li Tang)
10. Treating Liver fire: Coptis (Huang Lian) six liang, Evodia (Wu Zhu Yu) one liang or half liang. Grind into powder, make into water pills or steamed cakes. Take fifty pills with warm plain soup. (Dan Xi Xin Fa: Zuo Jin Wan also called Hui Ling Wan)
11. Treating dysentery, spleen diarrhea, and visceral toxic bleeding: Ya Zhou Coptis (Ya Zhou Huang Lian) half jin, remove hairs, cut, stuff into the intestine of a fat pig, tie firmly, place in an earthenware pot, cook with water and wine until soft, take out Coptis, bake, grind to powder, mix and make pills the size of a wutong seed. Each dose one hundred pills, take with rice soup. (Ren Zhai Zhi Zhi Fang)
12. Treating diarrhea: Xuan Coptis (Xuan Huang Lian) and Qingmuxiang (Green Auklandia, Qing Mu Xiang) pounded and sifted, made into honey pills the size of wutong seeds. Take twenty to thirty pills on an empty stomach, twice daily. For those cold in body, roast garlic until cooked and make into pills. Also effective for infants. (Bing Bu Shou Ji Fang: Xiang Lian Wan)
13. Treating severe cold acute dysentery with intestinal sliding and red-white stools like fish brains, day and night irregular, unbearable abdominal pain: Coptis (Huang Lian) six liang, Dried Ginger (Gan Jiang) two liang, Angelica (Dang Gui) three liang, Donkey-hide gelatin (E Jiao) three liang. Grind the four into powder, mix with eight he of strong vinegar to dissolve the gelatin and form into hand-rolled pills adding about a soybean-size amount, dry them. Adults take thirty pills, children one hundred-day-old take three pills, one-year-old take five pills, adjust as appropriate, three times daily. (Qian Jin Fang: Zhu Che Wan)
14. Treating visceral toxicity: Eagle-claw Coptis powder (Ying Zhao Huang Lian mo), use one whole bulb of single-clove garlic, roast until fragrant and soft, grind and combine in a mortar, form pills the size of wutong seeds. Each dose thirty to forty pills, take with aged rice water. (Ben Shi Fang Shi Yi: Suan Lian Wan)
15. Treating spleen affected by dampness with persistent diarrhea, poor digestion of grains, umbilical and abdominal stabbing pain, pediatric malnutrition diarrhea: Coptis (Huang Lian, removed fine roots), Evodia (Wu Zhu Yu, remove stems and fry), White Peony (Bai Shao Yao) each five liang; grind to fine powder, make into paste with flour and form pills the size of wutong seeds. Each dose twenty pills, take with concentrated decocted rice water on empty stomach three times daily. (Ju Fang: Wu Ji Wan)
16. Treating insufficient heart qi with hematemesis and epistaxis, also treats cholera: Rhubarb (Da Huang) two liang, Coptis (Huang Lian) one liang, Scutellaria (Huang Qin) one liang. Decoction in three sheng of water to one sheng, take in a single gulp. (Jin Kui Yao Lue: Xie Xin Tang)
17. Treating diabetes with excessive drinking and sweet urine like fatty bran flakes, frequent urination day and night: one winter gourd (Dong Gua) and Coptis (Huang Lian) ten liang. Cut off winter gourd top and remove seeds, put Coptis powder into the gourd, bake over fire until Coptis is cooked, press cloth to extract the juice. One dose is a large cup, take twice daily, only consume two to three slices of gourd as tolerance allows. (Jin Xiao Fang)
18. Treating women weakened by heat and emaciation: Coptis (Huang Lian) three liang (remove fine roots), Ginseng (Ren Shen) one liang (remove fibrous rootlets), red Poria (Chi Fu Ling) one liang, Astragalus (Huang Qi) one liang (shave), Aucklandia (Mu Xiang) half liang, Turtle shell (Bie Jia) one liang and a half (coated with vinegar, stir-fried until yellow, remove skirt), Bupleurum (Chai Hu) one liang (remove young shoots), Lycii cortex (Di Gu Pi) half liang, Peach kernel (Tao Ren) one liang and a half (soak in decoction to remove skin and tip, remove double kernels, fry with bran until slightly yellow). Grind into powder and fill into a fine young pig stomach, sew closed, steam until soft, grind in a sandpot until paste-like, make pills the size of wutong seeds. Take thirty pills before meals with congee. (Sheng Hui Fang: Huang Lian Pig Stomach Pill)
19. Treating pediatric stomach heat with regurgitation of milk: Coptis (Huang Lian) two qian, processed Pinellia (Xiao Ban Xia) two qian. Grind into fine powder, divide into 100 equal parts, take three times daily, one part each time. (Selected New Medical Methods of Liaoning: Pediatric formula)
20. Treating red painful eyes and removing heat: Coptis (Huang Lian) half liang, Jujube (Da Zao) one piece (sliced). Boil these two in five he of water to one he, remove dregs, strain and use the liquid like sesame-seed sized drops to instill into eyes ten times day and night twice. (Seng Shen Ji Fang: Huang Lian Decoction)
21. Treating carbuncles and swollen toxic sores, whether already ulcerated or not: Coptis (Huang Lian) and Areca (Bing Lang) equal parts, grind to powder, mix with egg white and apply. (Jian Yi Lun Lun)
22. Treating impetigo and acute eczema: Coptis (Huang Lian), Rosin (Song Xiang), Cuttlebone (Hai Piao Xiao) each three qian. Grind to fine powder, add two qian of yellow wax, melt into an appropriate amount of cooked sesame oil, make into an ointment. Apply to affected area three times daily. Before application warm compress the area with a hot towel to soften and remove scabs. (Inner Mongolia Selected New Medical Methods)
23. Treating oral ulcers and mouth sores: decoct Coptis (Huang Lian) in wine and swish occasionally. (Zhou Hou Fang)
24. Treating pediatric oral erosion (kou gan): Coptis (Huang Lian) and Aloe (Lu Hui) equal parts, grind to powder. Take five fen with honey soup. For horse-tooth erosion, add toad ash (Chan Hui) equal parts, halve Indigo naturalis (Qing Dai), a little Musk (She Xiang). (Simple Single Formulas)
25. Treating damage from strong wine or heavy flavors causing lip and tooth pain or gum ulceration or head, face, neck pain: stir-fried Coptis (Huang Lian) one qian five fen, Rehmannia (Sheng Di Huang), Moutan bark (Mu Dan Pi), Angelica (Dang Gui) each one qian, Kudzu (Sheng Ma) two qian. Decoction with water; if there is real heat constipation, add Rhubarb (Da Huang). (Women’s Good Prescriptions: Qing Wei San)
26. Treating pregnancy-related vexation and dry mouth preventing sleep: powdered Coptis (Huang Lian) one qian per dose, take with congee; or steamed Coptis pills with wine are also wonderful. (Women’s Good Prescriptions)
27. Treating scalds from boiling liquids: grind Chuanlian (Chuan Lian) into powder and mix with tea oil to apply. (Chinese Medicine Journal (2):170, 1922)
28. Treating Croton (cathartic) poisoning causing persistent diarrhea: powdered Dried Ginger (Gan Jiang) and Coptis (Huang Lian), take a small measure (approximate one cun bi). (Bu Que Zhou Hou Fang)
