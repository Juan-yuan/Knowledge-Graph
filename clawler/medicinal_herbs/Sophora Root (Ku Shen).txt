Cultivation and Processing of Kushen (Ku Shen)
Source
The root of the leguminous plant Kushen (Ku Shen). Harvested in spring and autumn, with autumn harvests preferred. After digging up the roots, remove the root crown and fibrous roots, wash clean of soil and sand, and sun-dry. Fresh roots are cut into slices and sun-dried, called Kushen slices.
[Original morphology]
Kushen, also called: Shui Huai (Benjing), Di Huai, Tu Huai, Jiao Huai, Bai Jing, Hu Ma, Cen Jing, Lu Bai, Ling Lang (Bielu), Wild Pagoda-tree (Gangmu), Mountain Pagoda-tree Seed, White Calyx.
A subshrub, 50–120 cm high. Roots cylindrical, bark yellow. Stems and branches herbaceous, green, with irregular longitudinal grooves, young parts covered with yellowish fine hairs. Leaves odd-pinnate compound, alternate; stipules linear at the base; leaf blade 20–25 cm long, rachis with fine hairs; leaflets 5–21, with short stalks, ovate-elliptic to oblong-lanceolate, apex rounded or obtuse, base rounded or broadly cuneate, margin entire. Raceme terminal, 10–20 cm long, slightly pubescent; bracts linear; flowers pale yellow-white; calyx campanulate, slightly oblique, apex 5-lobed; corolla papilionaceous, standard petal slightly longer than the other petals, apex nearly rounded; stamens 10, filaments free, fused only at the base; pistil single, ovary superior, ovary stalked and hairy, style slender, stigma rounded. Pod linear, apex with a long beak, not dehiscent at maturity. Seeds usually 3–7, constricted between seeds, black, nearly globular. Flowering May–July. Fruiting July–September.
[Habitat and distribution]
Grows on sunny slopes, grasslands, plains, roadsides, sandy soils and red soils. Distributed throughout China. Produced nationwide, with larger yields in Shanxi, Hubei, Henan and Hebei.
Properties and flavor
Taste bitter; nature cold
Processing
Pick and remove impurities, remove remaining stems, wash off dirt, soak in water until fully moistened, remove, cut into slices and sun-dry; first soak overnight in concentrated sticky-rice leach (thick rice washing water) until scummy foul odor floats and is removed, repeatedly wash, then steam; take out between the Si and Shen time periods (approximately mid-morning to mid-afternoon), sun-dry and finely rasp for use.
Appearance
The dried root is cylindrical, 10–30 cm long, 1–2.4 cm in diameter. Surface with obvious longitudinal wrinkles; lenticels prominent and slightly recurved, extending transversely. Cork layer very thin, brown-yellow or gray-brown, often cracked and rolled outward, easy to peel revealing a yellow smooth bark portion. Texture hard, not easily broken, fracture fibrous. Transverse section yellow-white, cambium obvious. Aroma pungent, taste extremely bitter.
Kushen slices are obliquely cut thin slices, shapes and sizes vary, obliquely circular or long-elliptic, 2–5 cm long, 1–1.5 cm wide, about 2–5 mm thick. Texture hard, cut surface pale yellow-white, with concentric annual rings, wood part showing radiating patterns. Best when neat, yellow-white in color, and bitter in taste.
Effects of Kushen (Ku Shen)
Actions
The root of the leguminous plant Kushen (Ku Shen).
Main indications: heat-toxic dysentery with bloody stools, intestinal-wind rectal bleeding, jaundice, abnormal red or white vaginal discharge, pediatric pneumonia, pediatric malnutrition and parasitic accumulation, acute tonsillitis, anal fistula, rectal prolapse, skin itching, scabies and malignant sores, damp itchy vulvar sores, scrofulous swellings, burns. Externally used for trichomonas vaginitis.
Meridians entered
Liver meridian;
Kidney meridian;
Large Intestine meridian;
Stomach meridian
Indications
Clears heat, dries dampness, kills parasites. Treats heat-toxic dysentery with blood, intestinal-wind rectal bleeding, jaundice, abnormal red or white vaginal discharge, pediatric pneumonia, pediatric malnutrition/parasitosis, acute tonsillitis, anal fistula, rectal prolapse, skin itching, scabies and malignant sores, damp itchy vulvar sores, scrofula, burns. Externally treats trichomonas vaginitis.
① Benjing (Classic Materia Medica): Aids abdominal qi masses, zhengjia accumulations, jaundice, dribbling of urine, promotes urination to remove water, resolves abscesses and swellings, tonifies the middle, brightens the eyes and stops tearing.
② Bielu (Supplementary Records): Nourishes liver and gallbladder qi, calms the five viscera, steadies the will and benefits essence, opens the nine orifices, eliminates latent intestinal heat and fecal strangury, relieves thirst, awakens the drunk, yellow urine in small amounts, treats hidden malignant sores of the lower body, pacifies stomach qi, and increases appetite.
③ Tao Hongjing: Give to people with bad diseases to drink in wine infusion; it also dispels scabies when taken, being capable of killing parasites.
④ Yaoxing Lun (Treatise on Drug Properties): Treats heat-toxic wind, vexation and dryness of the skin producing sores, redness and scaly eruptions, causes hair loss; used to remove great heat drowsiness, treats cold pain in the belly, alleviates abdominal pain from internal evil, relieves body oppression, treats accumulations in the heart and abdomen.
⑤ Tang Bencao (Tang Materia Medica): Treats leg soreness, cures noxious parasites.
⑥ Rihua Yuben Cao (Daily Flowered Materia Medica): Kills childhood parasites. Fry with smoke into powder, take with rice to treat intestinal-wind rectal bleeding and hot dysentery.
⑦ Zhenzhu Nang (Pearl Bag): Removes dampness.
⑧ Diannan Bencao (South Yunnan Materia Medica): Cools blood, relieves heat toxin, scabies and purulent sores. Treats skin itching, blood-wind tinea and sores, stubborn white flakes, intestinal-wind rectal bleeding and hemorrhoidal bleeding. Disperses wind, reduces swelling and toxin, phlegm-toxin.
⑨ Bencao Congxin (New Compilation of Materia Medica): Dries dampness, overcomes heat, treats nocturnal emissions and spermatorrhea.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoct as a decoction, 3–10 g; or used in pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, decocted water for fumigation and washing; or powdered and applied topically; or infused and applied as a lotion.
Precautions and contraindications
1. Avoid in those with spleen and stomach cold deficiency.
2. From Yixue Rumeng (Introduction to Medicine): Use with caution in those with weak stomach.
3. Bencao Jingshu (Commentary on Materia Medica): Long-term use may damage kidney qi; do not use in patients with liver and kidney deficiency without significant heat.
Kushen formulas and prescriptions
① For persistent bloody dysentery: Kushen, stir-fried until charred, powdered, made into water pills the size of wuzi (schisandra) seeds. Take fifteen pills each time with rice water. (Rencun Tang Clinical Formulas)
② For bleeding from anal fistula, intestinal-wind rectal bleeding, alcohol-induced bloody stools: Kushen (sliced, soaked in wine, repeatedly steamed and sun-dried nine times, then fried to yellow powder, cleaned) one jin, Rehmannia (raw Rehmannia Root, Sheng Di Huang) four liang (soaked in wine overnight, steamed until cooked and pounded). Add honey to make pills. Each dose two qian, take with hot plain soup or wine, twice daily. (Waike Dacheng: Kushen Di Huang Wan)
③ For damp jaundice after meals with head dizziness and anxiety and yellowing due to overeating and turbid stomach qi: Kushen three liang, Gentian (Long Dan) one he (powder). Make pills the size of niu dan (ox gall) balls. Take five pills in fresh ginger juice, three times daily. (Buque Zhouhou Fang)
④ For red or white vaginal discharge: Kushen two liang, Oyster shell (Mu Li) one liang five qian. Powder, put into a male pig stomach with three bowls of water, simmer until soft, mash and make pills the size of wuzi; take one hundred pills with warm wine. (Jide Tang Clinical Formulas)
⑤ For lower-body ulcerative leakage: decoct Kushen and wash daily. (Renzhai Zhi Zhi Fang)
⑥ For rectal prolapse of the large intestine: Kushen, Galla Chinensis (Wu Bei Zi), Chen Bi Tu (old wall earth) in equal parts. Decoction for washing, apply powdered Equisetum (Mizai) to the surface. (Yifang Yaoyao Summary)
⑦ For heat accumulation in heart and lungs, kidney wind-toxin affecting the skin causing scabies, severe itching with yellow exudate, and severe wind lesions of hands and feet with brow hair loss and various wind diseases: Kushen thirty-two liang, Schizonepeta (stem removed, Jing Jie) sixteen liang. Grind to fine powder, mix with water into pills the size of wutong seed. Take thirty pills each time with good tea, or with Jing Jie decoction, after meals. (Jufang: Kushen Pills)
⑧ For leaking pusful carbuncles, purulent sores, widespread scaly scabies, urticaria and scabies, severe itching and numbness, wind pain in hands and feet, ruptured skin, extreme scrotal itching, and chronic vulvar itching and damp itch: Kushen one jin (powder), goose feathers (fried in sesame oil to retain fragrance) six liang. Mix with yellow rice paste into pills. Use cinnabar (Zhu Sha) as outer coating. Take with tea twice daily. Or make topical powders, washes, or pastes as needed. (Wang Qiuquan Family Secret: Divine Treasure Pill)
⑨ For scrofulous swellings (laryngeal scrofula): Kushen four liang, pounded to powder, mix with raw Achyranthes Root (Niu Xi) and make pills the size of wutong seed. Take ten pills after meals with warm water, three times daily. (Suishen Beiji Fang)
⑩ For mouse-bite sores and various malignant ulcers: Kushen two jin, dew-harvested bee hive (Lu Feng Fang) two liang, qu (fermentation starter) two jin. Soak with three dou of water for two nights, remove dregs, ferment with glutinous millet two sheng to brew and drink three times daily. A variant adds hedgehog skin for improved effect. (Buque Zhouhou Fang)
⑾ For scalding or hot burns: Kushen in any amount, ground to fine powder, mixed with fragrant oil and applied topically. (Weisheng Baojian: Lv Bai Powder)
⑿ For early-stage auricular carbuncle (also called lunar-eclipse sore) appearing on the helix or behind the ear as a small yellow rice-grain-like nodule with red surrounding, a white pustular tip, intensely itchy, breaking with pus exuding and rapidly spreading to form a large draining area, highly contagious among children: Kushen, Huang Bai, Atractylodes (Cang Zhu), Sepia/Cuttlefish Bone (Hai Piao Xiao) in proportions five qian, five qian, three qian, three qian respectively. Grind extremely fine and mix evenly. Use warm boiled water to make a paste and apply to the affected area, change twice daily. (Zhejiang Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine (6):27, 1958)
⒀ For bleeding from interdental spaces: Kushen one liang, dried alum (Ku Fan) one qian. Powder and apply to the gums three times daily. (Puji Fang)
⒁ For toxic-heat swollen and painful feet with tendency to slough: soak Kushen in wine. (Yao Sengtan Collected Empirical Formulas)
⒂ For dysentery: old Kushen seven liang, powdered Licorice (Gan Cao) seven liang. Grind to powder. Use one qian of fresh ginger and a pinch of old tea to decoct, use the decoction to take the medicine; adults take one qian, infants three to five fen. (Zhongmiao Xian Fang)
⒃ For sudden severe headache like bursting, not from cold or wind-stroke: Kushen, Cinnamon bark (Rou Gui), Pinellia (Ban Xia) in equal parts. Pound, sift, mix with bitter wine and apply topically to relieve pain. (Zhouhou Fang)
⒄ For urinary difficulty: Kushen, Talc (Hua Shi), Zi Bei shell (Beichi, purple shell) in equal parts. Grind and sift into powder. Each dose take one teaspoon with water, or better cooked sunflower root juice. (Waitai)
⒆ For facial darkening, dryness and wind-prickling: Kushen one jin, Red Peony (Chi Shao), Winter Melon Seeds (Dong Gua Zi) each four liang, Scrophularia Root (Xuan Shen) two liang. Grind to powder; each dose one qian, wash the face with the powder. (Puji Fang)
⒇ For otitis media: Kushen 1.5 g, Borneol (Bing Pian) 0.3 g, sesame oil (or vegetable oil) 9 g. Heat the sesame oil to boiling, add Kushen and fry until charred and black, remove, cool slightly and add finely powdered borneol, cool and use. Before use clean the ear with cotton to remove pus, then instill the medicated oil into the ear 2–3 times daily. (Nan Yao: Zhongcao Yixue)
(21) For pediatric mouth sores: Kushen, Orpiment (Huang Dan), Galla Chinensis (Wu Bei Zi), Indigo naturalis (Qing Dai) in equal parts; grind to powder and apply topically. (Surgical Rules)
(22) For drowsiness and lethargy: Kushen three liang, Atractylodes macrocephala (Bai Zhu) two liang, Rhubarb (Da Huang) one liang. Grind to powder, make honey pills the size of wutong seed, take thirty pills after each meal. (Yixin Fang)
(23) For nocturnal emission with reduced appetite: Kushen three liang, Atractylodes (Bai Zhu) five liang, Oyster shell powder (Mu Li Fen) four liang. Grind to powder, use a male pig stomach washed clean, simmer in an earthenware pot until soft, mortar-paste with the medicines, when dry add juice and make beansize pills. Take forty pills with rice soup three times daily. (Baoshou Tang Formulas)
