Cultivation and Processing of Cassia Twig (Guizhi)
Source
The young twigs of Cinnamomum cassia of the Lauraceae family; young twigs are cut between July and August, trimmed into small sections approximately 15 or 30–100 centimeters long, and sun-dried.
Taste and Properties
Pungent; sweet; warm in nature
Characteristics
Twigs are long and cylindrical, often branched, 30–70 cm long, with thick end diameters of 0.3–1 cm; surface brown or reddish-brown, with fine wrinkles and small tubercle-like leaf scars, branch scars and bud scars; lenticels are punctate or ovoid-punctate. Processed pieces are hard and brittle, easily broken; cross-section bark is reddish-brown, with a visible pale yellow ring of stone cells; wood is yellow-white to light yellow-brown; pith is slightly quadrangular. Possesses a characteristic aroma, tastes sweet with a slight pungency, the bark tasting stronger. The best material has young, thin, uniform twigs, reddish-brown color and a strong fragrance.
Effects of Cassia Twig (Guizhi)
Efficacy
The young twigs of Cinnamomum cassia of the Lauraceae family.
Main Indications
Wind-cold exterior syndromes; soreness and pain of shoulders, back and joints; chest impediment with phlegm-fluid; amenorrhea and abdominal masses.
Channels
Bladder channel; Heart channel; Lung channel
Indications
Induces sweating and relieves the muscles, warms the meridians and unblocks the channels. Treats wind-cold exterior syndrome, soreness and pain of shoulders, back and joints, chest impediment with phlegm-fluid, amenorrhea and abdominal masses.
1. Cheng Wuji: expels "bentun" (rushing abdominal pain), harmonizes the muscle exterior, disperses accumulated blood in the lower burner, and benefits lung qi.
2. From Yixue Qiyuan (Medical Origins), "Secret Therapeutic Methods": dispels wind-cold headache, opens the interstices, releases the exterior, and removes superficial wind-damp (the last two characters supplemented from Ben Cao Fa Hui).
3. Ben Cao Jing Shu: expels pathogenic factors from a replete exterior; mainly benefits liver and lung qi, headaches, and wind-bi with stiffness and pain of bones and joints.
4. Yao Pin Hua Yi: acts especially on the upper regions of shoulders and arms, able to lead medicine to painful areas to remove phlegm-congealed blood stagnation between the joints.
5. Ben Cao Bei Yao: warms the meridians and unblocks the vessels, induces sweating and relieves the muscles.
6. Ben Cao Zai Xin: warms the middle and moves blood, strengthens the spleen and dries dampness in the stomach, reduces swelling and promotes diuresis. Treats cold-induced numbness and soreness of hands and feet with tendon twitching and pain, as well as disorders from external cold.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction 1.5–6 g; in large doses can be used up to 15–30 g; or incorporated into pills or powders.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated in febrile diseases with high fever, yin deficiency with exuberant fire, and reckless movement of blood due to blood-heat.
Formulas Containing Cassia Twig (Guizhi)
1. For Taiyang windstroke with floating yang and weak yin—when yang floats the heat is spontaneously emitted, when yin is weak sweat issues spontaneously; chills with aversion to cold, gradually worse with wind, slight feverishness, nasal flaring and retching: Cassia Twig (Guizhi) 3 liang (peeled), White Peony (Baishao) 3 liang, Licorice (Gancao) 2 liang (honey-fried), Fresh Ginger (Shengjiang) 3 liang (sliced), Jujube (Dazao) 12 pieces (split). Finely cut the above five ingredients, use 7 sheng of water, simmer over low heat to obtain 3 sheng, remove dregs, take when warm, one sheng per dose; after taking, wait a short while and sip warm thin congee about one sheng to assist the medicine; cover warmly for about one hour; when the whole body is slightly moist as if sweating it is even better. (Shanghan Lun — Guizhi Tang)
2. For cold-induced disease on the eighth or ninth day with wind and dampness intermingled, body soreness and vexation, inability to turn over, no vomiting or thirst, floating, vacuous and choppy pulse: Cassia Twig (Guizhi) 4 liang (peeled), Prepared Aconite (Fuzi) 3 pieces (processed, peeled, cracked), Fresh Ginger (Shengjiang) 3 liang (sliced), Jujube (Dazao) 12 pieces (split), Licorice (Gancao) 2 liang (honey-fried). Use the above five ingredients with 6 sheng of water, boil down to 2 sheng, remove dregs, divide warm into three doses. (Shanghan Lun — Guizhi Fuzi Tang)
3. For pain of various joints, emaciation, swollen feet as if slipping off, dizziness and shortness of breath, nausea and an urge to vomit: Cassia Twig (Guizhi) 4 liang, White Peony (Baishao) 3 liang, Licorice (Gancao) 2 liang, Ephedra (Mahuang) 2 liang, Fresh Ginger (Shengjiang) 5 liang, Atractylodes (Baizhu) 5 liang, Anemarrhena (Zhimu) 4 liang, Saposhnikovia (Fangfeng) 4 liang, Prepared Aconite (Fuzi) 1 piece (processed). Use the above nine ingredients with 7 sheng of water, boil down to 2 sheng, take warm seven he (approximate measure), three times daily. (Jinkui Yaolue — Guizhi Baishao Zhimu Tang)
4. For chest oppression, various reversals, and heart-suspending pain: Cassia Twig (Guizhi) and Fresh Ginger (Shengjiang) each 3 liang, Immature Bitter Orange (Zhishi) 5 pieces. Use the above three ingredients with 6 sheng water, boil down to 3 sheng, divide warm into three doses. (Jinkui Yaolue — Guizhi Shengjiang Zhishi Tang)
5. For a person who after sweating for a cold feels palpitations below the navel and an urge to "run the piglet" (rushing abdominal pain): Poria (Fuling) half jin, Cassia Twig (Guizhi) 4 liang (peeled), Licorice (Gancao) 2 liang (honey-fried), Jujube (Dazao) 15 pieces (split). Use the above four ingredients with one dou of sweet water, first boil the poria until reduced by 2 sheng; add the other herbs and boil down to 3 sheng, remove dregs, take warm one sheng, three times daily. (Shanghan Lun — Fuling Guizhi Gancao Dazao Tang)

For blood-bi with both yin and yang deficient, slight pulses above the wrist and tightness at the middle of the forearm, external signs of body numbness like wind-bi: Astragalus (Huangqi) 3 liang, White Peony (Baishao) 3 liang, Cassia Twig (Guizhi) 3 liang, Fresh Ginger (Shengjiang) 6 liang, Jujube (Dazao) 12 pieces. Use the above five ingredients with 6 sheng water, boil down to 2 sheng, take warm seven he, three times daily. (Huangqi Guizhi Five-Substance Decoction)

For seminal emission with lower abdominal tension and cold at the glans, dizziness, falling away, very vacuous, faint pulses with slight tightness and movement of empty pulses indicating loss of essence and blood: Cassia Twig (Guizhi), White Peony (Baishao), Fresh Ginger (Shengjiang) each 3 liang, Licorice (Gancao) 2 liang, Jujube (Dazao) 12 pieces, Dragon Bone (Longgu) 3 liang, Oyster Shell (Muli) 3 liang. Use the above seven ingredients with 7 sheng water, boil down to 3 sheng, divide warm into three doses. (Guizhi Longgu Muli Tang)
8. For consumptive deficiency with interior palpitations and bleeding, abdominal pain, nocturnal emission, soreness and pain of the limbs, feverishness of hands and feet, dry throat and mouth: Cassia Twig (Guizhi) 3 liang (peeled), Licorice (Gancao) 2 liang (honey-fried), Jujube (Dazao) 12 pieces, White Peony (Baishao) 6 liang, Fresh Ginger (Shengjiang) 3 liang, maltose syrup one sheng. 9. For women with long-standing abdominal masses where menstruation stopped less than three months but continuous bleeding persists and fetal movement is felt above the navel—this is due to fixed masses damaging the pregnancy; for those who at six months of pregnancy feel movement above the navel, in the first three months bleeding during menses indicates a fetal condition. If bleeding does not stop, it is because the mass has not been resolved; the mass should be reduced: Cassia Twig (Guizhi), Poria (Fuling), Moutan Bark (Mudanpi) (pith removed), Peach Kernel (Taoren) (skin and tip removed and cooked), White Peony (Baishao) all in equal parts. Powder the five ingredients, bind with refined honey into pills the size of rabbit droppings. Take one pill before meals each day; if ineffective, increase to three pills. (Guizhi Fuling Wan; from Jinkui Yaolue)
10. For frostbite: Cassia Twig (Guizhi) 60 g, add 1000 ml water. Boil over high heat, after boiling continue for 10 minutes then remove; pour into a basin and wait until lukewarm (as tolerated by the patient and not scalding the skin), retain the residue for reuse with the next decoction. Immerse the affected limb in the decoction, wash while lightly massaging the lesion. Each immersion about 10–15 minutes, once in the morning and once in the evening. (Xin Zhong Yi supplement 1980, (3):16)
Dietary Therapy
Blood-Pressure-Elevating Tea (Shengya Cha)
Cinnamon Bark (Rougui), Cassia Twig (Guizhi), Honey-fried Licorice (Zhi Gancao) each 10 g. First grate the above three herbs into a coarse powder and set aside. Each time put an appropriate amount of the powdered herbs into a teapot, pour freshly boiled water and steep about 10 minutes, then drink as tea. Replace regular tea and drink frequently, continue for one week.
Effects: Strengthens heart yang, raises blood pressure. Indications: dizziness, mental fatigue, limb weakness, and low blood pressure below 90/60 mmHg due to heart yang deficiency.
