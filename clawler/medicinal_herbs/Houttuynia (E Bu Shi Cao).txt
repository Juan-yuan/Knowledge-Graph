Cultivation and Processing of Small Centipeda Herb (E bu shi cao)
Source
The whole herb of the Asteraceae plant Centipeda (Shi hu sui).
Taste and Properties
Taste: pungent; Nature: warm
Appearance
The whole herb is twisted together into clusters. Fibrous roots are thin, pale yellow; stems thin, much branched, brittle and easily broken, fracture surface yellowish-white. Leaves small, nearly sessile; leaf blades often wrinkled or fragmented, intact leaves when spread are spatulate, surface gray-green or brownish, margins with 3–5 teeth. Capitula (flower heads) yellow or yellow-brown. Odor slight, prolonged smelling causes an irritating sensation; taste bitter; slightly pungent.
Those with gray-green color and a strong pungent odor are best.
Effects of Small Centipeda Herb (E bu shi cao)
Functions
The whole herb of the Asteraceae plant Centipeda (Shi hu sui).
Treats common cold, cold-induced asthma, throat obstruction, whooping cough, sha-syndrome abdominal pain, amoebic dysentery, malaria, malnutrition-related diarrhea, rhinitis, nasal polyps, eye films causing gritty itching, scabies and tinea, traumatic injuries from falls and blows.
Meridians entered
Enters
Lung meridian
; Liver meridian
Main indications
Disperses wind and dispels cold, overcomes dampness and removes eye films, unblocks nasal obstruction. Treats common cold, cold-induced asthma, throat obstruction, whooping cough, sha-syndrome abdominal pain, amoebic dysentery, malaria, malnutrition-related diarrhea, rhinitis, nasal polyps, eye films causing gritty itching, scabies and tinea, traumatic injuries from falls and blows.
① "Supplement to Materia Medica" (Ben Cao Shi Yi): removes eye films; when pressed and stuffed into the nose, the film falls off by itself.
② "Si Sheng Materia Medica" (Four-Tone Materia Medica): unblocks nasal qi, benefits the nine orifices, expels wind-phlegm.
③ "Compendium of Materia Medica" (Ben Cao Gang Mu): detoxifies, clears the eyes, disperses eye redness and swelling and cloudy films, treats deafness, headache and head soreness, treats phlegm-malaria and harsh breathing, nasal obstruction—when stuffed into the nose the obstruction drops by itself; also disperses sores and swellings.
④ "Essentials of Raw Herbal Natures" (Sheng Cao Yao Xing Bei Yao): treats bruises and fractured bones, relieves pain and reduces swelling.
⑤ "Essentials of Collected Medical Forest" (Yi Lin Zuan Yao): unblocks stagnation, dispels cold, arrests malaria, stops dysentery. A powdered form pinched into the nose can induce sneezing to dispel cold stagnation.
⑥ "Illustrated Examination of Plant Names and Realities" (Zhi Wu Ming Shi Tu Kao): treats sha-syndrome.
⑦ "Guangxi Medicinal Plant Illustrations" (Guangxi Yao Zhi Tu Zhi): treats gan-accumulation (childhood malnutrition).
⑧ "Guangxi Chinese Medicinal Substances" (Guangxi Zhong Yao Wu): treats throat toxin.
⑨ "Guangdong Chinese Materia Medica II" (Guangdong Zhong Yao II): treats whooping cough.
⑩ "Guangzhou Military Common Chinese Herbal Manual" (Guangzhou Bu Dui Chang Yong Zhong Cao Yao Shou Ce): treats rheumatic low back and leg pain.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoct as a decoction, 5–9 g; or mash to extract juice.
External use: appropriate amount, crush and apply as a poultice; or crush and stuff into the nose; or grind to powder and pinch into the nose.
Prescriptions using Small Centipeda Herb (E bu shi cao)
① For wind-cold headache, nasal congestion, eye film: Small Centipeda Herb (fresh or dried both acceptable) rubbed and sniffed to induce sneezing; use twice daily. ("Guiyang Folk Herbal Medicine")
② For cold-phlegm harsh wheezing: juice of Wild-garden-coriander (Ye yuan sui) mixed with wine and taken orally. ("Binhu Ji Simple Formulas")
③ For brain-leak (nasal cerebral leakage): fresh Centipeda (Shi hu sui) crushed and stuffed into the nostrils. ("Zhejiang Folk Herbal Medicine")
④ For single or double throat-swelling (dan shuang hou e): Small Centipeda Herb one liang, glutinous rice (nuo mi) one liang. Crush the herb, take the juice to soak the glutinous rice and grind into a slurry; have the patient slowly hold and swallow. ("Guangxi Commonly Used Folk Herbs")
⑤ For eye disease with swelling and redness, dim vision and light sensitivity, obscure scratching pain, wind itch, nasal obstruction, headache, head soreness, external eye films clinging to the eyeball, thick sticky discharge: Small Centipeda Herb two qian, Indigo naturalis (Qingdai) one qian, Chuanxiong Rhizome (Chuanxiong) one qian. Grind to a fine powder, first hold water in the mouth, then use about a grain-of-rice amount to be pinched into the nose until tears flow; not restricted to time of day. ("Yuan Ji Qi Wei" — Hou Bi Bi Yun San)
⑥ For pterygium encroaching on the eye: fresh Small Centipeda Herb two liang. Crush, extract juice, boil and clarify; add a fragment of borneol (Bingpian) one fen and mix evenly, instill into the eye. ("Guangxi Commonly Used Folk Herbs")
⑦ For spleen-cold malaria: Centipeda (Shi hu sui) a handful, pound to extract half a bowl of juice, add half a bowl of wine and take together. ("Binhu Ji Simple Formulas")
⑧ For quotidian malaria and tertian malaria: fresh Small Centipeda Herb, rub into a ball and insert into the nose; initially it causes sneezing—endure somewhat—after one night it is effective. ("Modern Practical Chinese Medicine")
⑨ For amoebic dysentery: Centipeda (Shi hu sui) and Black-leek root (Wu jiu gen) each five qian. Decoction taken once daily; if bleeding is profuse add Xianhecao (Xian he cao) five qian. ("Jiangxi Herbal Medicine")
⑩ For gan-accumulation diarrhea: fresh Centipeda (Shi hu sui) three qian. Decoction taken orally. ("Hunan Materia Medica")
(11) For acute abdominal pain: inflorescences of Qiuzi cao crushed and smelled through the nose to induce sneezing. ("Zhejiang Folk Herbal Medicine")
(12) For damp-toxin leg ulcers: Wild-garden-coriander (collected in summer and dried to powder) five qian each, mercury powder five fen, mixed with tung oil into a paper-separated plaster, sew around and apply after washing with tea; yellow fluid discharges. ("Simple Single Prescriptions")
(13) For swellings and toxins: Wild-garden-coriander one handful, pangolin scale (charred to retain properties) seven fen, Angelica tail (Danggui wei) three qian. Crush and add one bowl of wine, squeeze and take the liquid, apply the residue as a poultice. ("Binhu Ji Simple Formulas")
(14) For hemorrhoidal swelling and pain: Centipeda (Shi hu sui) crushed and applied as a poultice. ("Binhu Ji Simple Formulas")
(15) For psoriasis: Small Centipeda Herb crushed and applied topically. ("Guiyang Folk Herbal Medicine")
(16) For snakebite: fresh Centipeda (Shi hu sui) crushed and applied externally to the wound. ("Quanzhou Materia Medica")
(17) For traumatic swelling and pain: appropriate amount of Small Centipeda Herb, crushed, stir-fried until hot, and applied to the affected area. ("Guangxi Commonly Used Folk Herbs")
(18) For corn (clavus): first pare down the thickened skin of the corn, then crush fresh Centipeda (Shi hu sui) and pack onto the lesion; remove after three to five days. ("Zhejiang Commonly Used Folk Herbs")
