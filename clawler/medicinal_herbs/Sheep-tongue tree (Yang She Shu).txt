Sheep-tongue tree (Yang She Shu)

Cultivation and Processing of the Sheep-tongue Tree (Yangshe shu)
Source
The bark of the Sheep-tongue Tree (Yangshe shu), a plant of the Staphyleaceae family. Harvested in summer and autumn; peel off the bark and sun-dry.
Original Morphology
Tree. Buds, young twigs, and inflorescences densely covered with brown short pubescence; branchlets brown.
Leaves often clustered at the ends of branchlets, alternate; petiole 1–3 cm long; leaf blade narrowly elliptic or obovate-lanceolate, 6–15 cm long, 2–4 cm wide, apex acutely sharp or shortly tapering, base cuneate, margin entire, leaf underside usually glaucous, becoming brownish after drying; midrib sunken on the leaf surface, lateral and reticulate veins raised on the leaf surface, 5–12 lateral veins on each side, branching and forming networks near the margin.
Spike-like inflorescences usually branched at the base, 1–1.5 cm long, often umbrelled when in bud; bracts broadly ovate, about 2 mm long, covered with brown short pubescence; calyx about 3 mm long, lobes ovate, covered with brown short pubescence, about equal in length to the calyx tube, calyx tube glabrous; corolla 4–5 mm long, 5 deep lobes nearly reaching the base, lobes elliptic, apex rounded; stamens 30–40, filaments slender, basally slightly connate; disk annular, glabrous; ovary 3-celled.
Drupe narrowly ovoid, 1.5–2 cm long, narrowed near the apex, persistent calyx lobes erect; stone with shallow longitudinal ridges. Flowering April–August, fruiting August–October.
Habitat and Distribution
Ecological environment: grows in forests at 600–1600 m elevation.
Resource distribution: distributed in Zhejiang, Fujian, Taiwan, Guangdong, Guangxi, Yunnan and other areas.
Therapeutic effects of the Sheep-tongue Tree (Yangshe shu)
Efficacy
The bark of the Sheep-tongue Tree (Yangshe shu), a plant of the Staphyleaceae family. Used for colds with headache, dry mouth, and body fever.
Indications
Clears heat and releases the exterior. Mainly used for colds with headache, dry mouth, and body fever.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoction, 9–15 g.
