Cultivation and processing of Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn)
Source
This item is the dried root of plants of the Cucurbitaceae family: Trichosanthes kirilowii Maxim. or Trichosanthes rosthornii Hemsl. It is harvested in autumn and winter, washed clean, the outer bark removed, sliced into sections or split longitudinally into segments, and dried.
Nature and taste
Sweet, bitter and sour, cool.
Processing
Pick out impurities, separate by size, soak in water until about sixty percent penetrated, take out and let moisten until inside and outside are evenly damp, cut into slices, and sun‑dry; or wash clean with water, take out, sun‑dry until very dry, and pound into small pieces.
Appearance
The dried root is irregularly cylindrical, 5–10 centimeters long, 2–5 centimeters in diameter, surface yellowish white to light brown, wrinkled and uneven, with depressed fine root scars. Texture is firm and heavy, farinaceous, not easy to break. Longitudinal section white, with yellowish vascular bundle strips; transverse section white, scattered with light brown vessel groups and streaks. Aroma slight, taste bland with a slight bitterness aftertaste. The best are those pure white in color, sufficiently powdery, fine and tender in texture, and plump; brown in color and high in fiber are inferior. Henan produces large quantity and superior quality, commonly called Anyang Huafen (ānyáng huā fěn).
Effects of Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn)
Efficacy
This item is the dried root of the Cucurbitaceae plants Gualou or Trichosanthes.
Main indications: febrile disease with intense thirst, wasting and thirst (diabetes‑like), jaundice, lung dryness with bleeding cough, swellings and abscesses, hemorrhoids and fistulae.
Meridians entered
Enters the Lung meridian and the Stomach meridian.
Main actions
Generates fluids, quenches thirst, clears heat, moistens dryness, discharges pus, reduces swelling. Treats febrile disease with thirst, wasting and thirst, jaundice, lung dryness with blood in the cough, swellings and abscesses, hemorrhoids and fistulae.
1. Benjing: treats wasting and thirst, body heat, vexation and fullness, great fever, tonifies deficiency and calms the center, reconnects exhausted and broken conditions.
2. Bielu: removes chronic internal heat in the stomach and intestines, treats various jaundices with yellowing of body and face, dry lips, dry mouth, shortness of breath. Regulates menses, stops minor dysentery and urinary difficulty.
3. Rihuazi Materia Medica: opens the small intestine, discharges pus, reduces toxic swellings, generates flesh and heals wounds, disperses contusions and resolves blood stasis. Treats heat madness, mastitis, back eruptions, hemorrhoids, fistulae and skin sores.
4. Diannan Materia Medica: treats swellings and toxic abscesses, and also stops cough with blood.
5. Bencao Mengquan: treats lateral hernia.
6. Bencao Zheng: cools Heart and Lung, relieves heat and thirst. Lowers heat above the diaphragm, resolves phlegm, reduces mastitis and toxic swellings.
7. Yilin Zuanyao: tonifies the Lung, restrains Qi, reduces fire, tranquilizes the Heart, and at the same time drains Liver constraint, relaxes Liver urgency, clears bladder heat, stops hot strangury and frequent scanty urination, removes Yangming damp‑heat.
8. Modern Practical Chinese Materia Medica: used as a dusting powder to treat skin eczema, tinea versicolor, and abrasions.
Usage and dosage
Internal use: decocted, 9–15 grams; or used in pills and powders.
External use: appropriate amount, ground to powder and scattered, or made into a paste and applied.
Contraindications and cautions
Contraindicated for those with spleen and stomach deficiency cold and loose stools.
1. Bencao Jing Jizhu: Goji (gǒu qǐ) is an assistant for it. It dislikes dry ginger. It is averse to Achyranthes (niú xī) and dry lacquer (gān qī). It reverses aconite (wū tóu).
2. Bencao Jishu: do not use in spleen and stomach deficiency with loose stools.
3. Bencao Huiyan: after sweating and loss of fluids causing thirst, do not rashly apply; in yin deficiency with rising fire and inability of fluids to ascend causing thirst, do not apply indiscriminately.
4. Benjing Fengyuan: in cases of phlegm‑fluid where the liquid is thin and clear, avoid use.
5. Depei Bencao: for gastric deficiency with damp phlegm, yang loss causing thirst, and disease located at the exterior, contraindicated.
Formulas using Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn)
1. To treat lily disease thirst:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn), Oyster Shell (mǔ lì), equal parts. Make into a powder, take by mouth one small spoonful. (Yonglei Qianfang)
2. To treat great thirst: dig a large Gualou root deeply, shave the bark thickly down to the white part, cut into one‑inch pieces, soak in water for one day and night, change the water five times in five days, take out, mash and pound to a pulp, grind it, filter through silk bag as in making powder and dry. Take by water one small spoonful, three or four times daily; can also be made into powder with porridge or eaten with milk curds, no limit to amount, take as needed to stop thirst. (Qianjin Fang)
3. To treat wasting and thirst, remove excess heat in stomach and intestines:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) and Fresh Ginger (shēng jiāng) five liang each; fresh Ophiopogon Root juice (mài dōng), Reed Rhizome (lú gēn) sliced two sheng each, White Imperata Root (bái máo gēn) sliced three sheng. Finely slice the five ingredients, use one dou of water, boil down to three sheng, divide into three doses. (Qianjin Fang)
4. To treat severe jaundice emergency:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) one jin, pound and take the juice six he, take at once; follow with additional dose if yellow fluid does not appear in urine. (Jianbian Danfang)
5. To treat sudden yellowing in children with yellow face and body: fresh Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) pound to extract two he of juice, one large spoonful of honey, warm and mix, take in two doses. (Guangli Fang)
6. To treat cough from deficiency heat:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) one liang, Ginseng (rén shēn) three qián. Grind to powder; each dose one qián, take with rice broth. (Binhu Jijan Fang)
7. To treat unruptured abscess:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) and Adzuki Bean (chì xiǎo dòu) equal parts. Grind to powder, mix with vinegar into a paste and apply. (Zhenglei Bencao)
8. To treat gastric and duodenal ulcers: Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) one liang, Fritillaria (bèi mǔ) five qián, ten eggshells. Grind to powder; each dose two qián, take with boiled water. (Liaoning Common Use Chinese Herbal Manual)
9. To treat swellings and abscesses: Gualou root (guā lóu gēn), cook dry with bitter alcohol, pound and sieve. Mix with bitter alcohol and spread on paper and apply. (Shi Liao Bencao)
10. To treat nipple ulcers: Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) two liang, grind to powder, mix with egg white and apply. (Inner Mongolia Selected New Methods of Chinese Herbal Medicine)
11. To treat postpartum blocked milk with swelling, hardness and pain, mild cases lactation obstruction, severe cases mastitis:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) one liang, Frankincense (rǔ xiāng) one qián. Grind to powder, take with warm wine, each dose two qián. (Yonglei Qianfang)
12. To treat wind‑blister sores:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) and Talc (huá shí) equal parts. Grind to powder, mix with water and apply. (Gan Ji Fang)
13. To treat bayberry wind blisters:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) and Sichuan Lovage Root (chuān xiōng) four liang each, Pagoda‑tree Flower (huái huā) one liang. Grind to powder, make into rice paste pills the size of a paulownia seed. Take on empty stomach with mild ginger soup, seven to eighty pills. (Jianbian Danfang)
14. To treat bruising and injuries, severe chest pain unbearable, chronic cough for many years: Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) any amount, each dose two qián, take with gypsum and bean curd brine mixed. (Diannan Bencao)
15. To treat eruptions entering the eye forming a pterygium:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) half liang, Shed Snake Skin (shé tuì) two qián. Grind fine, take a sheep liver, split it open, put in two qián of the medicine powder, bind with hemp and cook in rice washing water until done, feed frequently. If unable to eat the liver, nursing mothers should eat more. (Yan’s Pediatric Prescriptions)
16. To treat febrile disease with vexation and thirst use fresh Gualou root, pound and compress to extract juice, each dose one he, take frequently.
17. To treat deficiency‑consumption vexation and heat, dry mouth and tongue, thirst:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn), Honey‑fried Licorice (gān cǎo, slightly red, scraped) , Bitter Apricot Kernel (kǔ xìng rén, soaked to remove skins and tips, double kernels, bran‑fried until slightly yellow), Smoked Plum Flesh (wū méi ròu, lightly fried), one liang each. Pulverize the above into a fine powder, cook jujube flesh, add a little honey and form pills the size of a pellet. Each dose wrap one pill in cotton and hold in the mouth to swallow saliva, four to five times daily. (Formulas 16–17 from Sheng Hui Fang)
18. To treat Sun‑stroke convulsion disease, body rigid and about to faint, pulse deep and slow:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) two liang, Cinnamon Twig (guì zhī) three liang (peeled), White Peony Root (bái sháo) three liang, Honey‑fried Licorice (gān cǎo) two liang, Fresh Ginger (shēng jiāng) three liang sliced, Jujube (dà zǎo) twelve pieces split. Take these six ingredients with nine sheng of water, boil down to three sheng, divide into three warm doses, induce slight sweating. (Jinkui Yaolue — Gualou Gui Zhi Tang)
19. To treat urinary difficulty with water retention where the person is bitterly thirsty:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) two liang, Poria (fú líng) three liang, Chinese Yam (shān yào) three liang, Prepared Aconite (fù zǐ) one piece (processed), Dianthus (qú mài) one liang. Grind the five ingredients to powder, make honey pills the size of a paulownia seed, take three pills with water, three times daily; if no effect increase to seven or eight pills until small urination occurs and abdominal harmony indicates effect. (Jinkui Yaolue — Gualou Qu Mai Wan)
20. To treat chronic diarrhea with dry mouth and sore throat, constant desire to drink, agitation:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn), White Poria (bái fú líng), Honey‑fried Licorice (gān cǎo) half liang each; Ophiopogon Root (mài dōng, pith removed) two qián and a half. Grind finely, each dose five qián, cook in one and a half cups of water with two broken Jujubes, boil to seven tenths, strain and take anytime. (Zhengzhi Zhunsheng — Gualou Root Decoction)
21. To treat wasting and thirst where one drinks one shi of water per day:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) three liang, Lead Monoxide (qiān dān) two liang, Kudzu Root (gè gēn) three liang, Prepared Aconite (fù zǐ) one liang. Grind to powder, make honey pills the size of a paulownia seed, take ten pills three times daily, as thirst requires. Reduce aconite in spring and summer. (Qianjin Yaofang)
22. To treat kidney deficiency wasting and thirst refractory to other treatments: Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) and large black soybeans (dà hēi dòu) roasted, equal parts. Grind to powder, make paste pills the size of paulownia seeds, each dose one hundred pills swallowed with black soybean broth. (Weisheng Jiabao — Jiu Huo Wan)
23. To treat internal heat with much phlegm and cough: Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) one liang, Bitter Apricot Kernel (kǔ xìng rén) and Mulberry Root‑Bark (sāng bái pí) and Zhejiang Fritillaria (zhè bèi mǔ) three qián each, Platycodon Root (jié gěng) and Licorice (gān cǎo) one qián each. Decoction in water and take. (Bencao Huiyan)
24. To treat chronic pediatric cough with fresh blood in sputum: Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) any amount ground to fine powder, each dose one qián, take with honey water at any time. (Puji Fang — Dusheng San)
25. To treat excess fire in the Liver channel, hypochondrial distention and fullness, wandering body pain:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) five qián, Moutan Bark (mǔ dān pí), White Peony Root (bái sháo), White Mustard Seed (bái jiè zǐ) two qián each. Decoction in water and take. (Bencao Huiyan)
26. To treat all jaundice disorders in men and women regardless of strength or age:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) one liang, Artemisia capillaris (yīn chén) five qián. Decoction in water and drink as tea. (Bencao Huiyan)
27. To treat spermatorrhea in men:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn), Alisma (zé xiè), and Tuguagen (tǔ guā gēn) two liang each. Pound and sift the three, bind into pills with Achyranthes Root (niú xī) to the size of a paulownia seed. Take three pills before meals. (Waitai)
28. To treat great wind disease with internal heat and accumulated toxins:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) three liang, Rhubarb (dà huáng) three qián, Poxiao (pǔ xiāo) two qián, Honey‑fried Licorice (gān cǎo) five qián. Grind to fine powder, make paste pills the size of a mung bean, take twenty to thirty pills with plain soup. (Yitong Daquan — Detoxifying Pills)
29. To treat hidden parasites below and pinworms invading the lower part:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) four liang, Tingli Seed (tíng lì zǐ) four fen. Combine and sift, soak with mugwort juice into cotton wads, insert into the lower orifice and change three times daily. (Yixin Fang — Gualou Powder)
30. To cause abortion: Gualou root (guā lóu gēn) four liang, Cinnamon Bark (ròu guì) five liang, Achyranthes Root (niú xī) three liang, Dianthus (qú mài) one liang. Slice, use seven sheng of water, boil down to two sheng three he, strain. Warm and divide into three doses; after taking walk five li and then take another dose, no contraindication. (Feminine Good Prescriptions)
31. To treat fetal death in utero with signs of cyanotic nails, distention and oppression, bluish tongue, and in severe cases fecal odor from the mouth:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) alone, roast dry and grind to fine powder, each dose two qián, take with running water. Two doses produce effect. (One Hundred and One Selected Prescriptions)
32. To treat lack of breast milk: sliced Gualou root (guā lóu gēn) one sheng, wine four sheng. Boil to three boils, strain, divide into three doses. (Qianjin Yaofang)
33. To treat smallpox heat producing wind with convulsions:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) two qián, White Stiff Silkworm (bái jiāng cán) one qián. Fry slowly over low heat until old yellow, grind to powder. Each dose two to three fen, take with mint soup. (Chishui Xuanzhu — Gualou Powder)
34. To treat sores of pure yang, swollen painful and warm:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) three liang, Turmeric (jiāng huáng), Angelica Dahurica (bái zhǐ), Red Peony Root (chì sháo) one liang each. Grind to powder. Mix with tea to apply to the affected area. (Wai Ke Shuyao — Yiyang Powder)
35. To treat scrofulous nodes regardless of old or new, ruptured or unruptured:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) and Kushen (kǔ shēn) five qián each, Gleditsia thorns (zào jiǎo cì) forty‑nine pieces (dry‑fried until yellow), Smilax (tǔ fú líng) three jin. Decoction as a tea to drink. Avoid beef. (Xian Nian Ji — Simiao Powder)
36. To treat external scrotal or renal sac cystic pain:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) two liang, Licorice (gān cǎo) three qián. Grind and each dose two qián, take with one cup of un‑lime wine, boil down to seven tenths and take warm on an empty stomach. If unable to drink wine, use water decoction with a little wine added and take together. (Huoyu Xinshu — Tianhua Powder)
Note: un‑lime wine means wine without added lime. Ancient people added lime to wine to prevent souring, but it can congeal phlegm, so medicinal wine should be without lime.
37. To treat throat wind and trismus:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn), Mint (bò hé), tender Mugwort Leaf (nèn ài yè) powdered. Each dose two qián, take with tea; for severe cases take with watermelon juice or well water at night.
38. To treat excess fire in the Spleen channel with swollen painful gums and oral ulcers:
Mongolian Snakegourd Root (tiān huā fěn) five qián, White Peony Root (bái sháo) and Mint (bò hé) two qián each, Licorice (gān cǎo) one qián. Decoction in water and take.
