Cultivation and Processing of Nine-Immortal Herb (Jiu Xian Cao)
Source
It is the whole herb or root of Santalaceae family plants Long-leaved Bairuicao (Changye Bairuicao), Exserted-stigma Bairuicao (Luzhu Bairuicao), Long-flowered Bairuicao (Changhua Bairuicao) or Abrupt-bending Bairuicao (Jizhe Bairuicao). The whole herb is harvested in summer and autumn and sun-dried.
Morphology
Perennial herb, about 50 cm tall. Stems clustered, with obvious longitudinal grooves. Leaves sessile, linear, 4–4.5 cm long, 2.5 mm wide, apex gradually pointed, with 3 veins. Racemose inflorescences axillary or terminal; flowers yellowish-white, bell-shaped, 4–5 mm long; pedicels 0.6–2 cm long; with fine streaks; bract 1, linear, 1 cm long; two bracteoles, narrowly lanceolate, about 4.5 mm long, margins rough; perianth 5-lobed, lobes narrowly lanceolate, apex acute, incurved; stamens 5, inserted at the base of the lobes and enclosed; flowers recessed; ovary stalk 0.5 mm long. Nutlets nearly globose or ovoid, yellow-green, 3.5–4 mm long, surface occasionally with forked longitudinal veins (ribs), persistent perianth shorter than the fruit. Flowering June–July, fruiting August–September.
2. This species differs from Long-leaved Bairuicao (Changye Bairuicao) in that: stems prostrate, branches and leaves sparse; leaves linear, 2.5–3 cm long; flowers nearly bell-shaped; style exserted; nutlets with indistinct longitudinal veins. Persistent perianth inwardly curved and wrinkled. Flowering in June, fruiting in August–September.
3. This species differs from the previous two in that: leaves linear, occasionally linear-lanceolate, 7–17 mm long, with 3 veins; bract located at the base of the pedicel, two bracteoles located beneath the flower, opposite; racemes often compound into panicle-like inflorescences; flowers 6–10 mm long. Nutlets globose, about 4.5 mm wide, surface with longitudinal veins; persistent perianth longer than the fruit. Flowering June–July, fruiting August–September.
4. This species differs from the first three in that: stems with obvious longitudinal grooves. Leaves linear, 3–5 m long, apex often blunt, usually with a single vein. Flowers white; pedicels after flowering incline outward and gradually reflex; one leaf-like bract, two bracteoles; fruit stalk up to 1 cm long, reflexing when fruit is ripe. Flowering in July, fruiting in September.
Habitat and Ecology
1. Grows in grass clumps on barren slopes or under sparse woods.
2. Grows in grass clumps on mountain slopes and under pine forests at altitudes of 2,900–3,700 m.
3. Grows on sunny grassy slopes or under sparse woods at altitudes of 2,600–4,100 m.
4. Grows in meadows or gravelly slopes.
Distribution of Resources
1. Distributed in Northeast China and in Inner Mongolia, Hebei, Gansu, Jiangsu, Sichuan, Yunnan, etc.
2. Distributed in Sichuan and Yunnan.
3. Distributed in Sichuan, Mi Yunnan, Tibet, etc.
4. Distributed from Northeast, North China, Northwest to Southwest regions.
Properties and Flavor
Acrid; slightly bitter; cold in nature
Effects of Nine-Immortal Herb (Jiu Xian Cao)
Efficacy
It is the whole herb or root of Santalaceae family plants Long-leaved Bairuicao (Changye Bairuicao), Exserted-stigma Bairuicao (Luzhu Bairuicao), Long-flowered Bairuicao (Changhua Bairuicao) or Abrupt-bending Bairuicao (Jizhe Bairuicao). Mainly used for treating common cold, heatstroke, pediatric pneumonia, and convulsions.
Channels Entered
Lung; Liver; Spleen channels
Indications
Releases the exterior and clears heat, expels wind and stops spasms. Indicated for common cold, heatstroke, pediatric pneumonia, and convulsions.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoction, 6–12 g; or taken with steamed egg.
