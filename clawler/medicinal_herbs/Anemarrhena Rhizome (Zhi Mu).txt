Cultivation and Processing of Common Anemarrhena Rhizome (Zhi Mu)
Source
The rhizome of the plant Anemarrhena asphodeloides of the Liliaceae family. It is dug in spring and autumn, old leaves and fibrous roots are removed, soil shaken off, and sun-dried or oven-dried to produce the raw hairy rhizome. If the fresh rhizome is peeled of its outer skin and then sun-dried, it becomes the fleshy Common Anemarrhena Rhizome.
Taste and Nature
Bitter in taste; cold in nature
Processing
1. Common Anemarrhena Rhizome (Zhi Mu): select and clean impurities, wash with water, lift out, moisten and soften, slice and sun-dry.
2. Salt-processed Common Anemarrhena Rhizome (Salt Zhi Mu): take slices of Zhi Mu, place in a pan and lightly stir-fry over low heat, spray with saline solution, fry until dry and remove to cool. (For every 100 jin of Zhi Mu slices, use 2 jin 8 liang of salt with an appropriate amount of boiled water to dissolve); first finely rasp on a locust-wood plank, dry by roasting, pound in a wooden mortar, avoid contact with iron utensils; select the plump, moist, inner-white pieces, remove hairs and cut; if the purpose is to guide fire upward use wine to soak and then roast dry, if to guide downward use saline water to moisten and roast.
Description
(1) Raw hairy Common Anemarrhena Rhizome
The rhizome is flattened, cylindrical and long-strip shaped, slightly curved, occasionally branched, 3–15 cm long, 0.8–1.5 cm in diameter. One end has pale yellow stem-leaf scars, commonly called the “gold-wrapped head.” The surface is yellow-brown to brown, with a groove on the upper side and closely arranged annular nodes, densely covered on the nodes with yellow-brown remaining leaf bases; the lower side is slightly convex, with longitudinal wrinkles and pitted root scars or fibrous root scars and residual stems. Texture hard and breaks easily. The fracture surface is yellow-white and granular. Aroma slight, taste slightly sweet with slight bitterness, chewed it is somewhat mucilaginous.
(2) Fleshy Common Anemarrhena Rhizome
Most of the outer skin has been removed; the surface is yellow-white, with occasional remaining hair-like leaf stems and pitted root scars. The best pieces are thick strips, hard in texture, and with a white-yellow fracture surface.
Effects of Common Anemarrhena Rhizome
Functions and Indications
The rhizome of the plant Anemarrhena asphodeloides of the Liliaceae family.
Treats vexing heat and excessive thirst, steaming bone disorder and consumptive heat, lung-heat cough, dry and hard stools, and difficult urination.
Channels Entered
Lung channel; Stomach channel; Kidney channel
Main Indications
Nourishes yin and reduces fire, moistens dryness and lubricates the intestines. Treats vexing heat and excessive thirst, steaming bone disorder and consumptive heat, lung-heat cough, dry and hard stools, and difficult urination.
Used for warm-heat diseases where pathogenic heat is exuberant, high fever, vexing thirst, surging-large pulse and other excess heat patterns of the lung and stomach. Zhi Mu clears heat, drains fire and relieves vexation. It acts synergistically with Gypsum Fibrosum (Shi Gao), for example in Bai Hu Tang.
Used for lung-heat coughs or yin-deficiency dry cough with scanty sputum. This herb clears and drains lung heat and nourishes yin to moisten the lungs; it is often combined with Fritillaria Bulb (Bei Mu) to clear the lungs, transform phlegm and stop cough, as in Er Mu San.
Used for yin deficiency with blazing fire, and signs from lung and kidney deficiency such as steaming of the bones, tidal fever, night sweats, and vexation. Zhi Mu nourishes yin and reduces fire. It is often paired with Phellodendron Bark (Huang Bai) and included in yin-nourishing prescriptions, such as Zhi Bai Di Huang Wan.
Used for yin-deficiency diabetes-type thirst with signs of excessive thirst, copious drinking and frequent urination. This herb nourishes yin, moistens dryness, generates fluids and relieves thirst. Combined with Trichosanthes Root (Tian Hua Fen), Schisandra Fruit (Wu Wei Zi), etc., it enhances effects, as in Yu Ye Tang.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decocted, 6–12 g, or used in pills or powders.
To clear heat and drain fire, nourish yin and moisten dryness it is best used raw; to enter the kidney to reduce fire and nourish yin use salt-processed Zhi Mu.
Contraindications and Cautions
Contraindicated in spleen and stomach cold deficiency with loose stools.
1. Bie Lu: Excessive dosage causes diarrhea.
2. Introduction to Medicine: For cold cough in the lung, kidney qi collapse, or cases without heat with a weak inch pulse, avoid use.
3. Ben Cao Jing Shu: Contraindicated in impotence with premature ejaculation or easy loss of erection, diarrhea from spleen weakness, indigestion, stomach deficiency with poor appetite, kidney deficiency with watery diarrhea, etc.
4. Ben Jing Feng Yuan: Avoid when there is an unresolved exterior syndrome, in dysentery with dry thirst. If people with spleen-stomach deficiency and heat mistakenly take it, it causes diarrhea and reduces appetite, thus it is a great taboo in deficiency damage.
