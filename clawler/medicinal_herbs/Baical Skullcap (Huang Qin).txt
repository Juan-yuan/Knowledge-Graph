Cultivation and Processing of Baical Skullcap (Huang Qin)
Source
The root of the Lamiaceae plant Scutellaria baicalensis (Baical Skullcap). Harvest from spring to early summer (autumn also possible). Select plants grown 3–4 years. After digging up the roots remove stems, lateral roots and soil; when half-dry beat off the corky bark, then sun-dry to complete dryness.
Taste and Properties
Bitter, cold.
Preparation
1. Huang Qin (Baical Skullcap): pick out impurities, remove remaining stems, soak in cold water or briefly blanch in boiling water and take out; after fully moistening, slice and sun-dry (avoid excessive exposure causing reddening).
2. Wine-Processed Huang Qin: take Huang Qin slices, spray with yellow rice wine (huang jiu), mix evenly, lightly stir-fry over low heat, remove and cool to dry. (For every 100 jin [approx. 50 kg] of Huang Qin use 10–15 jin [approx. 5–7.5 kg] yellow rice wine.)
3. Stir-Fried Huang Qin: stir-fry Huang Qin slices over low heat until surface slightly charred, remove and cool.
4. Charred Huang Qin (Huang Qin Tan): stir-fry slices over high heat until surface dark brown and edges blackened while retaining medicinal properties, sprinkle with water, remove and sun-dry.
Characteristics
Dried roots are obconical, twisted and not straight, 7–27 centimeters long, diameter about 1–2 centimeters. Surface deep yellow or yellow-brown. Upper bark is coarser with twisted longitudinal wrinkles or irregular net-like lines; lower bark finer with grain or fine wrinkles; both ends have sparse wart-like lateral root scars. Texture is hard and brittle, breaks easily; cross-section deep yellow with a brown-red central pith. Old roots have a central dark brown or brown-black decayed flake in the cross-section, commonly called withered baical skullcap; roots that are hollow and not firm and split-like are commonly called baical skullcap segments. Roots turn yellow-green when wet or in cold water. Odorless, bitter taste. Best quality are long, thick, solid, yellow and cleaned of outer bark. Short, loose, deep-yellow roots that form segments are inferior. Main production areas are Hebei, Inner Mongolia, Shanxi, Shandong and Shaanxi; also produced in Liaoning and Heilongjiang.
Huang Qin produced in Sichuan and Yunnan is the root of the plant Scutellaria yunnanensis (Dian Huang Qin). The medicinal appearance is similar but thinner, diameter generally about 0.5–1.0 centimeter, often branched, cross-section a distinct yellow-green, and of lesser quality.
Effects of Baical Skullcap (Huang Qin)
Actions
The root of the Lamiaceae plant Scutellaria baicalensis (Baical Skullcap).
Main indications are clearing exuberant heat and alleviating thirst, lung-heat cough, damp-heat dysentery, jaundice, hot painful urinary dribbling, vomiting, epistaxis, uterine bleeding and leakage, red swollen painful eyes, restless fetus, and suppurative swellings and sores.
Channels
Enters the Lung, Gallbladder, Spleen, Large Intestine and Small Intestine meridians.
Indications
Drains excess fire, clears damp-heat, stops bleeding, and calms the fetus. Treats high fever with irritability and thirst, lung-heat cough, damp-heat dysentery, jaundice, heat strangury, vomiting, epistaxis, metrorrhagia and leucorrhea, red swollen painful eyes, restless fetus, and carbuncles and sores.
1. Ben Jing: treats febrile jaundice, intestinal dysentery, diarrhea, promotes urination to eliminate water retention, bleeding below and amenorrhea, treats malignant sores, ulceration and fire ulcers.
2. Bie Lu: treats phlegm-heat, stomach heat, lower abdominal colic, aids digestion, benefits the small intestine, female amenorrhea, dribbling and vaginal bleeding, and infant abdominal pain.
3. Tao Hongjing: treats ben tun (a disorder of surging pain) and heat pain below the navel.
4. Yao Xing Lun: can treat heat toxin, steaming bone disorder, alternating chills and fever, impaired digestion, breaks qi stagnation, treats five kinds of strangury, promotes free discharge, relieves joint vexation, relieves feverish thirst, treats heat abdominal masses and pain, and hardness/distention of chest and abdomen.
5. Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao: descends qi, treats epidemic febrile diseases and sores, promotes pus discharge; treats mastitis and back eruptions.
6. Zhen Zhu Nang: reduces excess yang, cools the heart and clears heat, and unblocks cold constraint.
7. Li Gao: treats fever with bitter taste in the mouth.
8. Dian Nan Ben Cao: drains lung fire upward and bladder fire downward; treats male urinary strangury, sudden uterine bleeding, regulates menstruation and clears heat, calms fetal unrest due to heat, clears fetal heat, and removes solid excess fire in the six channels.
9. Ben Cao Gang Mu: treats wind-heat and damp-heat headaches, ben tun heat pain, fiery cough, pulmonary consumption with foul throat, and various hemorrhages.
10. Ben Cao Zheng: the withered type clears upper-jiao fire, dissolves phlegm and promotes qi, calms asthma and cough, stops bleeding, relieves alternating chills and fever, wind-heat and damp-heat, headaches, resolves pestilence, clears the throat, treats pulmonary consumption and lung abscess, mastitis and back eruptions; especially expels superficial heat and so treats measles, scrofula, ulcers and red eye. The fresh/raw type cools lower-jiao heat and can treat dysentery, bladder heat accumulation, painful urinary retention, large intestine obstruction, bloody stools and uterine bleeding.
11. Scientific Folk Herbs: external washing of wounds has antiseptic action.
Dosage and Administration
Internal: decoction, 3–9 g; or used in pills or powders.
External: appropriate amount decocted for washing; or ground to powder and applied topically.
Precautions and Contraindications
Ben Cao Jing Shu: contraindicated in spleen and lung deficiency-heat.
Contraindicated in cases of middle-jiao cold causing diarrhea, cold abdominal pain, lower abdominal pain due to liver and kidney deficiency, abdominal pain from blood deficiency, spleen-deficiency diarrhea, kidney-deficiency loose stools, spleen-deficiency edema, amenorrhea from blood depletion, qi-deficiency with impaired urination, lung invaded by cold causing wheezing and cough, fetal restlessness from blood deficiency, and yin-deficiency leucorrhea.
Formulas using Baical Skullcap (Huang Qin)
1. For infant heart-heat causing terror crying: Huang Qin (remove black core) and Ginseng (Ren Shen) each one fen. Pound and sift into powder. Each dose one small scoop, take with bamboo leaf decoction at any time. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu: Huang Qin Powder)
2. To drain lung fire and reduce heat phlegm above the diaphragm: Huang Qin slices, stir-fry, make into powder, bind into paste or steam cakes the size of a wutong seed. Take fifty pills. (Danxi Xin Fa: Qing Jin Wan)
3. For chronic bronchitis: Huang Qin and Ting Li Zi (Ting Li Zi) equal parts, finely powdered, sugar-coated into tablets, each tablet contains 0.8 g of crude drug; three times daily, five tablets each time. (Inner Mongolia: Selected New Methods of Traditional Chinese Herbal Medicine)
4. For upper respiratory infection and enteritis: chop Huang Qin, soak in four times the water for four hours, strain off residue, then add twice the water and soak twice more, combine filtrates, add 20% alum solution and adjust pH to 3.5 (per 100 kg Huang Qin use 6–8 kg alum), a yellow precipitate forms, stand four hours, discard supernatant, place precipitate in cloth bag, wash with water and filter, dry, pulverize, granulate and tablet. Take two to three tablets per dose. (Liaoning: Selected New Methods of Traditional Chinese Herbal Medicine)
5. For Shaoyang and Taiyang headaches regardless of side: Huang Qin slices soaked in wine, sun-dried and powdered. Each dose one qian (approx. 3 g), take with tea or wine. (Lan Shi Mi Cang: Xiao Qing Kong Gao)
6. For combined Taiyang and Shaoyang disease with downward diarrhea: Huang Qin three liang, White Peony Root (Bai Shao) two liang, Licorice (Gan Cao, roasted) two liang, Jujube/Chinese Date (Da Zao) twelve pieces (cracked); decoct one dou of water to obtain three sheng, remove dregs. Warmly take one sheng twice daily. (Shang Han Lun: Huang Qin Tang)
7. For strangury and also to promote downward bleeding: Huang Qin four liang, finely cut, decoct in five sheng of water to obtain two sheng, divide into three doses. (Qian Jin Yi Fang)
8. For hematemesis or epistaxis, intermittent or continuous, caused by accumulated heart heat: Huang Qin one liang (remove black core), pound into fine powder. Each dose three qian, decoct in one medium cup of water to reduce to 60% volume. Take warm regardless of time. (Sheng Hui Fang: Huang Qin Powder)
9. For uterine bleeding and downward hemorrhage: Huang Qin ground to fine powder. Each dose one qian, moisten with heated scale-weights and take with wine. (Ben Shi Fang)
10. For women after age forty-nine with remaining menses or excessive continuous bleeding: Huang Qin heart-branch twigs two liang (soaked heavily in rice vinegar for seven days, dry-roasted, repeat soaking and roasting seven times). Powder finely, form vinegar pills the size of a wutong seed. Each dose seventy pills with warm wine on an empty stomach, twice daily. (Rui Zhu Tang: Qin Xin Wan)
11. For fetal fever and restlessness: Huang Qin and Atractylodes (Bai Zhu) equal parts, lightly stir-fry, powder, make honey pills the size of a wutong seed; take three qian each morning and evening with plain water. (Danxi Zuan Yao)
12. For hepatic heat producing corneal films, adults and children: Huang Qin one liang, Fresh Fermented Soybean (Dan Dou Chi) three liang, powder. Each dose three qian; wrap in cooked pig liver and eat, take with warm broth twice daily. Avoid alcohol and wheat flour. (Wei Sheng Jia Bao Fang)
13. For orbital pain due to wind-heat and phlegm: Huang Qin (wine-soaked, stir-fried) and Bai Zhi (Angelica dahurica) powdered, mix with clear tea, two qian per dose. (Danxi Xin Fa)
14. For bleeding from moxibustion sores: wine-fried Huang Qin two qian, powder, take with wine. (Guai Zheng Qi Fang)
15. For fire carbuncle: pound Huang Qin to powder, mix with water and apply topically. (Mei Shi Ji Yan Fang)
16. For postpartum intense thirst and unending drinking: Huang Qin (dried baked on new tile) and Ophiopogon (Mai Dong, core removed) each half liang.
17. For hot phlegm that is red, sticky like glue and hard, with vexation, heart pain, dry mouth and lips, inappropriate laughing and a flooding pulse: Tian Nan Xing (Arisaema), Ban Xia (Pinellia) and Huang Qin equal parts. Powder finely, soak in ginger juice, steam into cakes and form pills. Each dose 40–50 pills. (Za Bing Yuan Liu Xi Zhu: Ban Xia Wan)
18. For phlegm-heat cough with abundant qi and panting: Huang Qin three qian, charred Gardenia (Zhi Zi) and Perilla Seed (Zi Su Zi) each 1.5 qian, Poria (Fu Ling) and Bitter Apricot Kernel (Ku Xing Ren) each one qian. Decoction. (Ben Cao Hui Yan)
19. For pulmonary tidal fever and cough: Huang Qin and Salvia Root (Dan Shen) each 9–22 g, Bai Bu 12–18 g. Decoction; tablets or pills are convenient for long-term use. (Shanghai College of Traditional Chinese Medicine: Fang Ji Xue, Qin Bu Dan)
20. For febrile disease with vexation like fire, delirium and attempt to escape: Huang Qin one liang, Kansui (Gan Sui) one liang (baked until yellow), Gentian (Long Dan Cao) one liang (remove basal stalks). Pound into fine powder, each dose one qian mixed with warm water; shortly after have patient drink three liang of water to induce vomiting when the abdomen is full. Very effective for acute heat disorders. (Sheng Hui Fang)
21. For early febrile disease with agitation, headache and pain in the loins and legs: Huang Qin two fen, Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang) one fen, fresh Rehmannia juice three he, white honey half spoon.
