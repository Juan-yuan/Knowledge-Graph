Bugbane Rhizome (Shengma)

Cultivation and Processing of Bugbane Rhizome (Shengma)
Source
Derived from the rhizomes of Ranunculaceae family plants: Shengma (Shengma), Xing'an Shengma (Xing'an Shengma) and Large Three-leaf Shengma (Da San Ye Shengma). Harvested in spring and autumn by digging up; remove aerial stems and soil, sun-dry until the fibrous roots are dry, then singe with fire or strike out the fibrous roots using a bamboo basket, and sun-dry.
Morphology
1) Shengma (Shengma)
Also called Ma Niao Gan (Ma Niao Gan), Huo Tong Gan (Huo Tong Gan). Perennial herb. Rhizome irregularly nodular, with hollow stem scars, many and long fibrous roots. Stem erect, branched, 1–2 m high, sparsely covered with soft hairs. Leaves pinnately compound several times; petioles densely covered with soft hairs; leaflets ovate or lanceolate, 2–4 cm long, 1–2.5 cm wide, margins deeply serrate, green above, grey-green beneath, both surfaces with short soft hairs. Compound-paniculate inflorescences borne in leaf axils or at branch tips, narrow or sometimes expanded into large panicles; flowers bisexual; calyx 5, ovate, imbricate, 3-veined, white, ciliate; nectary leaves (staminodes) 2, apex 2-lobed, white; many stamens, filaments of unequal length, longer than calyx; carpels 2–5, glandular-hairy, many ovules. Siliqua oblong-elliptic, slightly flattened, apex with a small persistent style, slightly curved. Seeds 6–8. Flowering July–August. Fruiting September. Grows under forests and in grassy mountain slopes. Distributed in Yunnan, Guizhou, Sichuan, Hubei, Qinghai, Gansu, Shaanxi, Henan, Shanxi, Hebei, Inner Mongolia, Jiangsu and other areas.
2) Xing'an Shengma (Xing'an Shengma)
Also called Di Long Ya (Di Long Ya), Ku Long Ya Cai (Ku Long Ya Cai), Dahurian Shengma (Da Hu Er Sheng Ma), Ku Cai Yang (Ku Cai Yang), Ku Li Ya (Ku Li Ya), Ku Zhuang Cai (Ku Zhuang Cai). Perennial herb. Rhizome large, with obvious hollow stem scars and many fibrous roots. Stem erect, solitary, over 1 m tall, densely covered with soft hairs. Twice ternately compound leaves; lower stem leaves with long petioles, petioles hairy, central leaflet stalked, lateral leaflets usually sessile; leaflets ovate to orbicular-ovate, 5–11.5 cm long, central leaflet again 3-lobed deeply or shallowly, margins deeply serrate, both surfaces hairy. Paniculiform compound inflorescence; main peduncle very long, lateral pedicels shorter, flower pedicels 2–4 mm long, all densely covered with gray soft hairs; flowers unisexual, dioecious, rarely bisexual; small bract beneath each flower; male flower calyx 5, obovate, 3-veined; nectary leaves 2, apically deeply 2-lobed, each lobe often with one distinct anther-like appendage; stamens numerous, filaments unequal, slightly flattened, anthers ovoid; ovary degenerate; female flowers with 5 carpels, sessile. Siliqua 5. Seeds many. Flowering July–August. Fruiting September. Grows on hillsides and shaded places. Distributed in Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning, Hebei, Hubei, Sichuan, Shanxi, Inner Mongolia and other areas.
3) Large Three-leaf Shengma (Da San Ye Shengma)
Perennial herb. Rhizome large, with rounded hollow stem scars and many fibrous roots. Stem erect, 1–1.5 m high, glabrous or sparsely soft-hairy. Twice ternately compound leaves, lower ones with long petioles; central leaflet petiole longer, lateral petiole shorter, both covered with white soft hairs; leaflets ovate to broadly ovate, 9–11 cm long, 5–9 cm wide, central leaflet again shallowly 3-lobed, margins with coarse serrations, both surfaces hairy; upper stem leaves smaller, petioles very short. Inflorescence compound-paniculate; main peduncle and pedicels covered with gray soft hairs; flowers bisexual; calyx 5, broadly ovate, caducous; nectary leaf oblong-ovate with a nectar gland at the tip, yellowish-white; many stamens, filaments unequal, slightly flattened, anthers ovoid and 2-celled; carpels 3–5, free, glabrous, with short stalks, many ovules, short style, small stigma. Siliqua 3–5, apex with small persistent style, glabrous. Flowering July–August. Fruiting August–September. Grows in mountain fields and beside streams. Distributed in Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning and other areas.
Properties and Flavor
Sweet, acrid, slightly bitter, cold.
Processing
Remove impurities, briefly soak and wash clean, lift out, moisten thoroughly, slice, and sun-dry.
Effects of Bugbane Rhizome (Shengma)
Efficacy
The rhizome of Ranunculaceae family plants Shengma (Shengma), Xing'an Shengma (Xing'an Shengma) and Large Three-leaf Shengma (Da San Ye Shengma).
Main indications: epidemic febrile pestilence, headache with chills and fever, sore throat, oral ulcers, rashes that do not fully erupt; sinking of middle qi, chronic diarrhea and dysentery, rectal prolapse, uterine bleeding and leukorrhea in women, uterine prolapse; carbuncles, swellings and sores with toxic pus.
Channels entered
Enters the Lung meridian, Spleen meridian, Stomach meridian.
Actions and indications
Raises yang, releases the exterior, disperses rashes, detoxifies. Treats epidemic febrile pestilence, headache with chills and fever, sore throat, oral ulcers, rashes that do not fully erupt; sinking of middle qi, chronic diarrhea and dysentery, rectal prolapse, uterine bleeding and leukorrhea in women, uterine prolapse; carbuncles and sores with toxic swelling.
1) Shennong's Materia Medica (Benjing): treats and resolves all toxins, repels warm diseases and obstructive evils (also called miasma or pernicious qi).
2) Bie Lu (Bielu): treats middle-region abdominal pain and nausea, epidemic toxic pestilence, headache with chills and fever, wind-swelling toxins, sore throat, oral ulcers.
3) Yaoxing Lun (Treatise on Medicinal Properties): treats pediatric wind and convulsions, epidemic febrile disorders. Can treat swollen and painful mouth and lips from wind-damp, rotten smelly tooth roots with heat toxin and pus and blood. Clears wind-heat obstruction in the heart and lungs preventing circulation, oral ulcers, vexation and oppression. Treats carbuncles, pea-sized sores; soak a cotton swab in the decoction and apply to the sore.
4) Rihuazi Materia Medica: calms the soul and settles the spirit, treats wandering wind-swelling toxins, latent halitosis and hidden lesions.
5) Tangye Materia Medica: in the “Main Therapeutic Secrets”: treats lung consumption with coughing of pus and blood, can induce superficial sweating.
6) Dian Nan Materia Medica: releases the exterior in pediatric measles and smallpox, resolves sore toxins, treats swollen throat, wheezing, hoarseness. Treats lung heat, relieves toothache. Treats breast thrush and mumps.
7) Compendium of Materia Medica (Bencao Gangmu): disperses rashes, regulates bleeding, treats yang collapse, vertigo and fainting, deficiency pain in chest and flanks, chronic diarrhea with heaviness, turbid leukorrhea, uterine hemorrhage, hematuria, uterine prolapse, impotence and cold feet.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 3–9 grams; or used in pills and powders.
External use: grind to powder for topical application, decoct for gargling or for washing.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated in cases of excess above and deficiency below, yin deficiency with vigorous fire, and in measles after the eruption.
1) Materia Medica Commentary (Bencao Jingshu): in cases of vomiting blood, epistaxis, cough with copious phlegm, yin deficiency with movement of fire, kidney deficiency, qi rebellion with vomiting, palpitations and fright, mania and epilepsy, avoid use.
2) Dipei Materia Medica: in early-stage cold damage of the Taiyang, when measles show superficial signs, with deficiency of the lower source (lower jiao), yin deficiency with fire blazing, these four conditions are contraindications.
