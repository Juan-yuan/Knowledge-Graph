Cultivation and Processing of Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi)
Source
Siberian Cocklebur Fruit is the mature involucre-bearing fruit of the Asteraceae plant Xanthium sibiricum Patr.; it is a toxic plant, the fruit being the most toxic, and use must strictly follow medical instructions.
Properties and Taste
Taste: bitter; sweet; acrid; nature: warm; slight toxicity
Processing
Remove all impurities, remove spines, sift off ash and debris, lightly stir-fry until yellow, remove and cool.
Description
Dried fruits with involucre present are spindle-shaped to elliptic, about 1–1.7 cm long and 4–7 mm in diameter; surface yellow-green, brown-green or dark brown, bearing numerous hooked spines about 2 mm long. One end has two relatively thicker sharp spines, separated or connected. The outer skin (involucre) is tough, internally divided into two chambers, each enclosing one small achene. The achene is slightly spindle-shaped, one side relatively flat; pericarp gray-black, papery, one end with a columnar base bearing a single spine-like projection; seed light gray, seed coat membranous, containing two cotyledons, embryo root located at the apex. Odor faint, taste slightly bitter, oily. Best quality is large, plump grains of yellow-green color.
Effects of Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi)
Efficacy
Siberian Cocklebur Fruit is the mature involucre-bearing fruit of the Xanthium plant in the Asteraceae family.
Indications
Dispels wind, relieves pain, removes dampness, kills parasites. Treats wind-cold headache, nasal obstruction with discharge (rhinitis/sinusitis), toothache, wind-cold-damp painful obstruction, limb contracture pain, scabies, pruritus.

Indications
Disperses wind, relieves pain, expels dampness, kills parasites. Treats wind-cold headache, nasal obstruction, toothache, wind-cold-damp painful obstruction, limb contracture pain, scabies, pruritus.
1. Ben Jing (Classic of Materia Medica): treats wind-head cold pain, wind-damp encircling obstruction, limb contracture pain, rotting flesh and dead muscle.
2. Ben Cao Shi Yi (Supplement to Materia Medica): soak in wine to dispel wind and tonify.
3. Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao (Rihuazi Materia Medica): treats all wind disorders, fills the marrow, warms the lower back and feet. Treats scrofula, scabies and itching.
4. Ben Cao Meng Quan (Materia Medica Primer): stops headache, unblocks the crown, chases wind toxin down to the bone marrow, kills childhood parasites hidden in dampness.
5. Ben Cao Zheng (Correct Materia Medica): treats nasal obstruction.
6. Ben Cao Bei Yao (Essentials of Materia Medica): good at inducing sweating, dispersing wind-damp, ascending to the brain vertex and descending to the knees, externally reaching the skin. Treats headache, dim vision, toothache, nasal obstruction, removes spines.
7. Yu Qiu Yao Jie (Pharmacological Explanations by Yu Qiu): reduces swelling and relieves obstruction, drains wind and eliminates dampness. Treats scabies, wind-itch and rashes.
8. Yao Yao Fen Ji (Essential Medicines Divisions): treats nasal breathing problems.
9. Hui Yue Yi Jing (Medical Mirror): treats various hemorrhoids.
10. Ben Cao Zai Xin (Materia Medica Revived): treats eye pain.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoct as a decoction, 3–10 g; or used in pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, pound and apply; or decoct in water for washing.
Precautions and Contraindications
1. Do not use for headaches or bi pain due to blood deficiency.
2. Tang Ben Cao (Tang Materia Medica): avoid pork, horse meat, and rice-washing water.
3. Ben Cao Cong Xin (Materia Medica Renewed): disperses qi and consumes blood; people who are deficient should not take it.
Prescriptions using Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi)
1. For wind dizziness or severe headache: Siberian Cocklebur Seed (Cang Er Ren) 3 liang, Gastrodia (Tian Ma) 3 qian, White Chrysanthemum Flower (Bai Ju Hua) 3 qian. (Ben Cao Hui Yan)
2. To eliminate wind-damp bi and limb contracture: Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 3 liang. Pound to powder, use 1.5 sheng of water, decoct to obtain 7 he, remove dregs and sip. (Shi Yi Xin Jing)
3. Treat leprosy: Atractylodes Rhizome (Cang Zhu) 1 jin, Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 3 liang. Make each into powder, bind with cooked rice into pills about the size of a wuzi (Chinese parasol seed). Take three times daily, each dose 2 qian. Abstain from sexual activity for three months. (Dong Tian Ao Zhi)
4. Treat scabies, disperse wind and detoxify: stir-fry Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) with clam meat and eat. (Sheng Cao Yao Xing Bei Yao)
5. Treat women’s wind-itch rashes and persistent pruritus: equal parts Siberian Cocklebur flower (Cang Er Hua), leaf (Cang Er Ye) and seed (Cang Er Zi), pound finely and sift into powder. Each dose 2 qian mixed with dou-lin wine. (Sheng Hui Fang)
6. For continuous purulent nasal discharge: Magnolia Bud (Xin Yi) 0.5 liang, Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 2.5 qian, Angelica Dahurica Root (Bai Zhi) 1 liang, Mint Leaves (Bo He Ye) 0.5 qian. Dry together in the sun and grind into a fine powder. Each dose 2 qian, take after eating with scallion and clear tea. (Ji Sheng Fang — Cang Er Powder)
7. For eye pain and tinnitus: Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) half fen. Pound and mash, add 2 sheng of water, squeeze and filter to obtain juice, cook with 0.5 liang of polished rice into congee and eat, or make into powder or decoction to take orally. (Sheng Hui Fang — Cang Er Zi Congee)
8. For toothache: (a) Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 5 sheng, water 1 dou, boil down to 5 sheng, hold hot in the mouth; if painful spit out, then hold again. (Qian Jin Yi Fang)
8. For toothache: (b) Cocklebur Seed Kernel (Cang Er Ren), refined yellow and ground 60 g; Lophatherum Leaf (Dan Zhu Ye) (stems removed) 500 g; fresh ginger (Sheng Jiang) 120 g; edible white salt 180 g. In a small iron pot (clean), put in the bamboo leaves, add enough water to cover and boil over charcoal into a concentrated juice, then add ginger juice and boil, filter off dregs. Return the filtrate to the pot, boil and gradually add the white salt, stir and reduce to dryness, extinguish heat and remove the medicinal layer, mix with the ground Cocklebur Seed Kernel and grind into a fine powder, seal in a bottle for storage. When needed, take a small amount and apply to the affected tooth, rub three times daily until effective. (Shanghai Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine 1983,(7):34 — Tooth Salt Powder)
9. For carbuncles and toxic swellings: Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 5 qian, lightly fry to powder and take with yellow wine; also apply egg white to the lesion to draw out the carbuncle root. (Experience Collection — Cang Er Wine)
10. For marked ascites and edema with scanty urination: equal parts Cang Er Zi ash and Ting Li Zi powder. Each dose 2 qian, taken with water, twice daily.
11. For wind throat blockage with deep swelling extending to the cheek and frequent belching, called "horse throat blockage": wrap three to seven pieces of Cang Er Zi in cloth and burn to ash, take with water. (Formulas 10–11 from Qian Jin Yao Fang)
12. For scrotal eczema: Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi), Cnidium Seed (She Chuang Zi), Licorice Root (Gan Cao) each 10 g. Add water and decoct to 1000 ml, externally wash the scrotum several times daily. (Compilation of Chinese Medicine Preparations)
13. For acute folliculitis and acute or chronic eczema: Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 120 g (crushed), Sophora Root (Ku Shen) 60 g, Wild Chrysanthemum Flower (Ye Ju Hua) 60 g. Decoction to 2000 ml and use to wash affected areas. For lichenified pruritic lesions, optionally add alum 30 g and Szechuan Lovage Rhizome (Chuan Xiong) 15 g. (External Materia Medica for Sores and Ulcers)
14. For chronic malaria that does not remit: Cang Er Zi and its root and stem may all be used. Scrape and crush into powder, mix with wine and cooked flour into pills, take when needed. (Zhu’s Collected Tested Prescriptions)
15. Treat hemorrhoids: take Cang Er Zi air-dried on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month, pound into powder, take one small spoonful by mouth three times daily until improvement. (Wai Tai citing 'Effective Prescriptions')
16. Treat lower limb ulcers: Cang Er Zi 60–120 g, fry until yellow and grind into powder, raw pork lard 120–240 g, mix and pound into a paste. Before use, clean the wound with lime water (500 g quicklime added to 8 kg boiling water, steep and stand 1 hour, decant the clear liquid), dry and apply the ointment, bandage externally. Change dressing every 5–7 days in winter, every 3 days in summer. (Jiangsu Traditional Chinese Medicine 1966(1):37)
Dietary Therapy Formula
Mint, Siberian Cocklebur, Magnolia Bud and Angelica Dahurica Tea
Mint (Bo He) 6 g, Siberian Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 12 g, Magnolia Bud (Xin Yi) 6 g, Angelica Dahurica Root (Bai Zhi) 6 g, scallion white (Cong Bai) 3 stalks, tea leaves 2 g. Put all the above ingredients into a pot, decoct with water, strain and collect the liquid. Use as a tea replacement.
Efficacy: disperses wind and opens the orifices. Indications: rhinitis, sinusitis and related conditions.
