Cultivation and processing of Asarum (Xi Xin)
Source
The whole herb with roots of Aristolochiaceae plants Liao Asarum (Liao Xi Xin), Asarum (Xi Xin) and Hansung Asarum (Han Cheng Xi Xin).
When transplanted it is harvested after 3–5 years of field growth; when direct-seeded in the field it is harvested after 5–6 years. In mid-September dig up the entire root system, remove the soil, tie into bunches of 1–2 kg each, place in a cool shaded place to air-dry, then pack into storage.
Property and flavor
Acrid; warm; slightly toxic
Processing
Remove impurities, spray with water to moisten, promptly cut into sections, and air-dry.
Lei Gong Pao Zhi Lun: When using Xi Xin, pick off paired leaves one by one, for they harm people when taken. Must remove head soil; soak in gourd water overnight, in the morning strain it out, sun-dry and use.
Appearance
(1) Liao Asarum (Liao Xi Xin)
Usually curled into a mass. Rhizome irregularly cylindrical, 1–10 cm long, 2–4 mm in diameter; surface gray-brown, rough, with nodes, internodes 2–3 mm long. Roots fine, densely produced on the nodes, 10–20 cm long, about 1 mm in diameter; surface gray-yellow; brittle, breaks easily, fracture yellow-white. Basal leaf petiole long, smooth, intact; when leaves are spread flat they are ovate-cordate or reniform-cordate, apex acute or obtuse, base deeply cordate, 4–9 cm long, 5–13 cm wide; upper surface deep green with hairs along the veins, underside densely hairy. Flowers occasionally seen, purple-brown, hemispherical; perianth lobes reflexed from the base and appressed to the perianth tube. Fragrant aroma, pungent taste, slightly numbs the tongue.
(2) Asarum (Xi Xin)
Similar to Liao Asarum but the rhizome is more slender, 5–15 cm long, 1–3 mm in diameter, internodes 0.2–1 cm long; leaf blades thinner, cordate. Odor weaker.
(3) Hansung Asarum (Han Cheng Xi Xin)
Very similar to Asarum, but usually with denser hairs on the underside of the leaf and hairs on the petiole. In cultivated varieties the hair density varies greatly, making it hard to distinguish from Asarum.
Therapeutic effects of Asarum (Xi Xin)
Functions
The whole herb with roots of Aristolochiaceae plants Liao Asarum (Liao Xi Xin), Asarum (Xi Xin) and Hansung Asarum (Han Cheng Xi Xin).
Mainly treats wind-cold headache, nasal obstruction/sinusitis, toothache, phlegm-fluid cough with counterflow, and wind-damp painful obstruction.
Meridians
Lung meridian; Kidney meridian; Heart meridian; Liver meridian; Gallbladder meridian; Spleen meridian.
Indications
Dispel wind, disperse cold, promote urination/resolve fluid retention, open orifices. Treats wind-cold headache, nasal congestion/sinusitis, toothache, phlegm-fluid cough with counterflow, and wind-damp painful obstruction.
(1) Disperse wind-cold: used for wind-cold common cold or headaches due to wind-cold; can be paired with Ephedra (Ma Huang) or Ledebouriella root (Gao Ben).
(2) Warm the channels and relieve pain: used for muscle and joint pain due to cold invading the collaterals.
(3) Warm and transform cold phlegm: used for chronic bronchitis with abundant thin phlegm.
(4) Open the lung orifices: used for rhinitis and sinusitis.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decocted, 1.5–9 g; ground to powder, 1–3 g.
External use: appropriate amount, powdered and blown into the nose, packed into the ear, applied to the navel; or decocted water used for gargling.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated in qi deficiency with spontaneous sweating, headache due to blood deficiency, cough due to yin deficiency, etc.
① Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu: Zeng Qing and zaogen are adjuvants. Contraindicated with wolf’s bane, Cornus officinalis, Astragalus membranaceus. Avoid talc and xiaoshi. Opposes Aconitum.
② Yao Xing Lun: avoid raw lettuce.
③ Ben Cao Jing Shu: In general, for diseases of internal heat and fire rising upwards, excessive heat above and deficiency below, qi deficiency with sweating, headache due to blood deficiency, cough due to yin deficiency, this herb is prohibited.
④ De Pei Ben Cao: contraindicated in wind-heat or yin deficiency.
Formulas containing Asarum (Xi Xin)
① For wind-cold headache, with pain like bursting, pulse faint, wiry and tight:
Asarum (Xi Xin) 1 liang (clean),
Ligusticum chuanxiong (Chuan Xiong) 1 liang,
Prepared aconite (Fu Zi) (processed) ½ liang (clean),
Ephedra (Ma Huang) 1 fen. Cut finely, include roots, add scallion white (Cong Bai), fresh ginger (Sheng Jiang), jujube (Da Zao). Each dose 5 qian, 1.5 bowls of water, decoct to 1 bowl, take in three doses consecutively. (Pu Ji Fang, Xi Xin San)
② For migraine:
Realgar (Xiong Huang) (ground),
Asarum (Xi Xin) (remove shoots and leaves, make into powder) equal parts. Grind the two together evenly. Each dose a character-sized amount; if left side pain, blow into right nostril; if right side pain, blow into left nostril. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu, Zhi Ling San)
③ For common cold with nasal congestion:
Asarum (Xi Xin),
Perilla leaf (Zi Su Ye),
Saposhnikovia root (Fang Feng),
Bitter apricot kernel (Ku Xing Ren),
Platycodon root (Jie Geng),
Mint (Bo He),
Mulberry root bark (Sang Bai Pi). Decoction. (Fang Mai Zheng Zong)
④ For externally contracted cold not resolved, water accumulation under the heart, dry heaves, fever with cough, or thirst, diarrhea, dysphagia, or difficult urination; lower abdominal distention, or shortness of breath:
Ephedra (Ma Huang) (nodes removed),
White peony root (Bai Shao Yao),
Asarum (Xi Xin),
Dried ginger (Gan Jiang),
Licorice (Gan Cao) (honey-fried),
Cinnamon twig (Gui Zhi) (peel removed) each 3 liang,
Schisandra (Wu Wei Zi) half sheng,
Pinellia (Ban Xia) half sheng (washed). Take the eight ingredients with one dou of water, first boil Ephedra, reduce by 2 sheng, skim the foam, add the remaining herbs, boil down to 3 sheng, strain. Warmly take one sheng. (Shang Han Lun, Xiao Qing Long Tang)
⑤ For nasal obstruction: a small amount of powdered Asarum (Xi Xin) blown into the nose. (Pu Ji Fang)
⑥ For chronic toothache not healing:
Asarum (Xi Xin) (remove leaves and shoots),
Long pepper (Bi Ba). Equal parts, coarsely pounded and sifted. Each use half qian, one bowl of water, decoct until several boils, gargle hot and spit cold. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu, Xi Xin Tang)
⑦ For toothache:
Schizonepeta (Jing Jie),
Asarum (Xi Xin),
Beehive (Luofengfang) equal parts. Coarsely powdered, use three qian per dose, one large bowl of water, decoct to 70%, strain and warm gargle and spit cold. (Yu Yao Yuan Fang, Xi Xin San)
⑧ For toothache:
Asarum (Xi Xin) 1 qian,
Phellodendron bark (Huang Bai) 1 qian. Decoction used to rinse the mouth; do not swallow. (Jilin Zhong Cao Yao)
⑨ For phlegm-fluid where qi is driven downwards but causes coughing and chest fullness:
Poria (Fu Ling) 4 liang,
Licorice (Gan Cao),
Dried ginger (Gan Jiang),
Asarum (Xi Xin) each 3 liang,
Schisandra (Wu Wei Zi) half sheng. Take the five ingredients with eight sheng of water, boil down to 3 sheng, strain. Warmly take half a sheng three times daily. (Jin Kui Yao Lue, Ling Gan Wu Wei Jiang Xin Tang)
⑩ For Shao Yin disease, early stage with subsequent fever and a submerged pulse:
Ephedra (Ma Huang) 2 liang (nodes removed),
Asarum (Xi Xin) 2 liang,
Prepared aconite (Fu Zi) one piece (processed, peel removed, broken into eight slices). Take the three herbs with one dou of water, boil Ephedra first, reduce by 2 sheng, skim foam, add the others, boil down to 3 sheng, strain. Warmly take one sheng, three times daily. (Shang Han Lun, Ma Huang Fu Zi Xi Xin Tang)
⑾ For wind entering the abdomen with cutting pain and vexation:
Asarum (Xi Xin),
Evodia fruit (Wu Zhu Yu),
Dried ginger (Gan Jiang) each ½ liang,
Dong quai (Dang Gui),
Saposhnikovia root (Fang Feng) each 1 liang,
Peony root (Shao Yao) 2 liang. Grind into powder; use half liang per dose with 2 liang water, boil to 1 liang, warm and divide into three doses, take in succession. (Yuan He Ji Yong Jing, Xi Xin Tang)
⑿ For children’s mouth ulcers: powdered Asarum (Xi Xin) mixed with vinegar, applied to the navel. (Wei Sheng Jia Bao Fang)
⒀ For halitosis and hidden dental abscess pain: decoct Asarum (Xi Xin) to a strong juice, hold hot and spit cold. (Sheng Hui Fang)
⒁ For deafness: powdered Asarum (Xi Xin) mixed with yellow wax into pills the size of mouse droppings. Wrap one pill in cotton and insert; avoid anger. (Gong Shi Ying Yuan Fang, Cong Er Wan)
⒂ For deficient cold causing hiccup and poor appetite:
Asarum (Xi Xin) (remove leaves) ½ liang,
Clove (Ding Xiang) 2.5 qian. Make into powder. Each dose 1 qian, take with persimmon calyx decoction. (Ben Cao Gang Mu)
⒃ For sudden fainting and loss of consciousness: powdered Asarum (Xi Xin) blown into the nose. (Shi Yi De Xiao Fang)
⒄ For pediatric acute fright and refusal (symptoms: facial pallor, startle pain, inability to speak; or neck rigidity with danger signs; or sudden crying at night): use equal parts Asarum (Xi Xin) and cinnamon heart (Gui Xin), powdered; give a small amount in the child’s mouth.
⒅ For oral sores: equal parts Asarum (Xi Xin) and Coptis (Huang Lian) powdered. First clean the lesion with a cloth, apply the powder; when saliva is expelled it is cured. (Wei Sheng Yi Jian Fang)
⒆ For nasal polyps: periodically blow powdered Asarum (Xi Xin) into the nose.
⒇ Formula for sudden cold-induced cough:
Asarum (Xi Xin) ½ liang (pounded to powder),
Bitter apricot kernel (Ku Xing Ren) ½ liang (soaked in water, remove skin and tips and double kernels, stir-fry with bran until slightly yellow, grind to a paste). Melt ½ liang wax in a pan, then add 1 fen ghee, add Asarum and apricot kernel, roll into pills the size of a date. At any time, wrap one pill in cotton and hold in the mouth to dissolve and swallow the saliva. (Sheng Hui Fang)
