Cultivation and Processing of Feather Cockscomb Seed (Qing Xiang Zi)
Source
The seeds of Qing Xiang, a plant of the Amaranthaceae family. Seeds mature from July to September; cut the above-ground parts or pick the seed spikes and sun-dry, rub out the seeds, then sieve or winnow to remove husks and other impurities.
Flavor and Properties
Bitter; cold in nature.
Appearance
Dried seeds are flattened and round, slightly thicker in the center than at the edge, 1–1.5 mm in diameter and about 0.5 mm thick. Surface smooth, black, and glossy, with a slight concave umbilicus on the side. Seed coat thin and brittle, easily broken; interior white with a slight odor. Commercial samples often have a residual yellowish-white husk covering the seed top; the husk is cap-like with a slender filamentous style about 4–5 mm long. Prefer those that are black, shiny, and plump. Produced throughout the country.
Effects of Feather Cockscomb Seed (Qing Xiang Zi)
Functions
The seeds of Qing Xiang, a plant of the Amaranthaceae family.
Main indications
Red swollen painful eyes, ocular obstructions, hypertension, epistaxis, skin wind-heat itching, scabies.
Channels entered
Liver channel
Actions
Expels wind-heat and clears liver fire. Treats red swollen painful eyes, ocular obstructions, hypertension, epistaxis, skin wind-heat itching, scabies.
1. Ben Jing: Cures bluish discoloration of the lips and mouth.
2. Yao Xing Lun: Treats hepatic heat-toxin attacking the eyes, red obstructions, diminished vision, and ocular swellings. Effective for malignant sores and scabies itching; treats parasitic ulcers of the lower regions.
3. Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao: Treats pathogenic qi of the five zang organs, benefits the brain and marrow, brightens the ears and eyes, calms the liver, strengthens sinews and bones, and dispels wind, cold, and damp bi pain.
4. Diannan Ben Cao: Brightens the eyes. Treats sticky tears that are difficult to open and white opacities covering the eyes.
5. Ben Cao Yuan Shi: Eliminates heart-channel fire pathogens.
6. Hui Yue Yi Jing: Expels wind-heat.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoct as a decoction, 3–15 g.
External use: appropriate amount, grind to powder for topical application; mash to extract juice for nasal instillation.
Precautions and Contraindications
Bencao Beiyao: Contraindicated in cases of markedly dilated pupils.

Clinical Applications
1. Qing Xiang Decoction (from Clinical Applications of Chinese Medicine)
For chronic uveitis: Feather Cockscomb Seed (Qing Xiang Zi) 15 g, sodium sulfate (Yuan Ming Fen) 4.5 g (taken as a rinse), sour jujube seed (Suan Zao Ren) 12 g, Mimeng flower (Mi Meng Hua) and Cassia seed (Jue Ming Zi) each 9 g, Poria (Fu Ling) 12 g, White Hyacinth Bean (Bai Bian Dou) 15 g. Decoction. In this formula Feather Cockscomb Seed brightens the eyes and reduces film obstructions and serves as the chief herb.
2. Qing Xiang Zi Pills (from Zhengzhi Zhunsheng – Classified Formulas)
For liver-heart toxic heat and dark obstructions entering the black of the eye; also for various internal and external eye diseases: Feather Cockscomb Seed (Qing Xiang Zi), Lansi (Lan Shi), Immature bitter orange (Zhi Shi, stir-fried), stir-fried Rhubarb (Da Huang, fried), Chrysanthemum flower (Ju Hua), honey-fried Licorice (Zhi Gan Cao) each 60 g; Cassia seed (Jue Ming Zi), Coptis (Huang Lian), Herba Lycopsidis (Chong Wei Zi), Asarum (Xi Xin), Ephedra (Ma Huang), Plantain seed (Che Qian Zi) each 45 g; dried carp gallbladder and dried chicken gallbladder each 1 piece; antelope horn (Ling Yang Jiao) 90 g. Grind to a fine powder and form pills with refined honey to the size of a wu tong seed. Take 20 pills per dose after meals with tea or water, three times daily. In this formula Feather Cockscomb Seed clears liver heat and brightens the eyes as the chief herb.
3. Eight-Seed Pill (from Yi Bu Quan Lu – Ophthalmology)
For wind-toxin eye diseases and membranous obstructions covering the eye, regardless of duration, and both internal and external eye obstructions: Feather Cockscomb Seed (Qing Xiang Zi), Plantain seed (Che Qian Zi), Schisandra (Wu Wei Zi), Goji berry (Gou Qi Zi), Kochia seed (Di Fu Zi), Herba Lycopsidis (Chong Wei Zi), Cassia seed (Jue Ming Zi), Lepidium seed (Ting Li Zi, fried), Ophiopogon root (Mai Dong, heart removed), Asarum (Xi Xin, tops removed), Cinnamon bark (Rou Gui, coarse bark removed), Raw Rehmannia root (Sheng Di Huang), red Poria (Chi Fu Ling), Alisma (Ze Xie, clay removed), Siler root (Fang Feng, forks removed), Scutellaria root (Huang Qin, black core removed) each 30 g. Grind into a fine powder and form pills with honey to the size of a wu tong seed; take 20–30 pills per dose with clear tea or warm rice water, three times daily. Feather Cockscomb Seed is the chief herb in this formula for brightening the eyes and reducing film obstructions.
4. Cassia–Shepherd’s-purse–Feather Cockscomb Tea
Cassia seed (Jue Ming Zi), Shepherd’s-purse seed (Ji Cai Zi), Feather Cockscomb Seed (Qing Xiang Zi) each 6 g. Grind the ingredients together, put the powder into a gauze bag, place in a cup and pour boiling water over, cover and steep for 5–10 minutes. Drink as a tea frequently; one dose per day.
Actions: Clears heat, dispels wind, brightens the eyes, and lowers blood pressure. Indicated for headaches and dizziness in various stages of hypertension.
