Cultivation and Processing of Chinese Mahonia Leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè)
Source
Leaves of Berberidaceae plants: broad-leaved Mahonia, narrow-leaved Mahonia or South China Mahonia; collected in autumn.
Taste and Properties
Yin Pian Xin Can: bitter, cold.
Characteristics
① Broad-leaved Mahonia leaf: obovate-elliptic, apex gradually acute with a sharp spine, base cordate or nearly truncate and oblique, margins with 2–8 spinous teeth on each side, tooth apices slightly bent toward the underside; upper surface purple-green and glossy, lower surface yellow-green. Texture hard and brittle. Odor faint, taste mild. Produced in Zhejiang, Anhui, Jiangxi and other regions.
② Narrow-leaved Mahonia leaf: narrowly lanceolate, margins with 6–13 spinous teeth on each side; otherwise same as above. Produced in Zhejiang, Anhui, Jiangxi and other regions.
③ South China Mahonia leaf: medicinal character same as broad-leaved Mahonia leaf. Used in Fujian and Yunnan.
According to the Illustrated Examination of Plant Names and Realities, the Shida Gonglao refers to the Berberidaceae plants mentioned above. However, at present medicinal material is only found in a few areas such as Zhejiang and Jiangxi; folk use also occurs in Fujian, Guangdong and Guangxi. The Mahonia leaf used in Tibet is the leaf of Tibetan Mahonia. As for the Mahonia leaves widely used in the market, they are the leaves of the evergreen species Gougu. See the entry for Gougu leaf.
Effects of Chinese Mahonia Leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè)
Actions
Leaves of Berberidaceae plants: broad-leaved Mahonia, narrow-leaved Mahonia or South China Mahonia.
Main indications: pulmonary tuberculosis with hemoptysis, steaming bone tidal fever, dizziness and tinnitus, low back soreness and weak legs, irritability, red eyes.
Channels
Ben Cao Zai Xin: enters the Lung meridian.
Indications
Clears heat and tonifies deficiency, stops cough and transforms phlegm, dries dampness and detoxifies. Treats pulmonary tuberculosis with hemoptysis, steaming bone tidal fever, dizziness and tinnitus, low back soreness and weak legs, irritability, red eyes.
① Ben Cao Zai Xin: treats consumptive cough due to deficiency.
② Illustrated Examination of Plant Names and Realities: treats hematemesis (vomiting blood).
③ Yin Pian Xin Can: treats pulmonary consumption, stops cough and transforms phlegm, reduces deficiency-heat, kills parasites.
④ Modern Practical Chinese Materia Medica: a cooling nourishing tonic. Efficacy similar to Ligustrum fruit (Nǚzhēn Zǐ); suitable for tidal fever, steaming bone, low back soreness, weak knees, dizziness, tinnitus and similar syndromes.
⑤ Lu Chuan Ben Cao: drains fire and reduces fever. Treats febrile disease with fever, vexation, diarrhea, red eyes.
⑥ Common Tibetan Medicinal Herbs: treats damp-heat dysentery, red swollen painful eyes, carbuncles and sores.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 6–9 g.
External use: appropriate amount; grind to powder and apply as a paste.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in patients with spleen and stomach deficiency-cold.
Formulas Using Chinese Mahonia Leaf
For wind-fire toothache: Chinese Mahonia leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè) 3 qian. Decoction, take immediately. One dose daily; if pain is severe, take two doses. (Jiangxi Materia Medica)
Clinical Applications
① Treatment of Pulmonary Tuberculosis
Qinggu San was used to treat 34 cases of pulmonary tuberculosis of the tidal-fever type that were unresponsive to anti-tuberculosis drugs.
Method: Chinese Mahonia leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè) 20 g, Guobo leaf (Guǒ Bǔ Yè) 20 g,
Bletilla rhizome (Bái Jí) 30 g, Yuanshen (Xuán Shēn, Scrophularia root) 10 g, Digu Pi (Dì Gǔ Pí) 10 g, Qinjiao (Qín Jiāo, Gentiana macrophylla) 10 g,
Licorice (Gān Cǎo) 6 g; one dose daily, decocted and taken in three divided doses.
Modifications: for severe hemoptysis add raw Rehmannia (Shēng Dì Huáng) 30 g, soaked in boiling water and taken as a drink; for thirst add Ophiopogon (Mài Dōng) 20 g; for severe coughing with dyspnea add reed root (Lú Gēn) 20 g. One dose daily; one course lasts three months. Before treatment chest X-rays were taken; of the 34 cases all were in the infiltrative stage of pulmonary tuberculosis, three had thin-walled cavities, five were sputum positive for tubercle bacilli, and erythrocyte sedimentation rate was generally elevated.
Results: markedly effective in 24 cases (symptoms disappeared, pulmonary lesions absorbed, cavities closed, ESR returned to normal); improved in 9 cases (symptoms improved, pulmonary lesions partially absorbed, cavities reduced, ESR decreased); ineffective in 1 case (no symptom reduction, pulmonary lesions aggravated and transferred to other therapies).
② Cancer treatment — Formula for Liver Cancer
Chinese Mahonia leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè) 30 g,
Black nightshade (Lóng Kuí) 20 g, Baiying (Bái Yīng) 20 g, eucalyptus leaf (Ān Shù Yè) 20 g, verbena (Mǎ Biān Cǎo) 20 g, Hedyotis diffusa / Oldenlandia (Bái Huā Shé Shé Cǎo) 20 g,
Scutellaria barbata (Bàn Zhī Lián) 10 g, licorice (Gān Cǎo) 10 g. Modifications: for severe hepatic pain add Qiye Lian (Qī Yè Lián) 20 g; for true deficiency add Baishen (Bái Shēn) 5 g; grind and mix into juice. One dose daily, decocted and taken in three divided doses. This formula is suitable for early-stage liver cancer.
Lung cancer formula: Chinese Mahonia leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè) 20 g, Scutellaria barbata (Bàn Zhī Lián) 20 g, common ivy (Cháng Chūn Téng) 20 g, Ji Shiteng (Jī Shǐ Téng) 20 g, Prunella (Xià Kū Cǎo) 20 g,
Houttuynia (Yú Xīng Cǎo) 30 g, Liaogewang (Liǎo Gē Wáng) 10 g,
pomegranate peel (Shí Liú Pí) 15 g, Notoginseng (Tián Qī / Sān Qī) 6 g (ground and mixed); one dose daily, decocted and taken in three divided doses. Modifications: for hemoptysis add charred Puhuang (Pú Huáng Tàn) 10 g and Imperata root (Bái Máo Gēn) 30 g; for dyspnea add mulberry bark (Sāng Bái Pí) and Tingli Zi (Tíng Lì Zǐ) 10 g each. If this formula is taken continuously for three months it can gradually reduce lesions and resolve clinical symptoms.
③ Treatment of Respiratory Tract Inflammation — Bronchitis Formula
Chinese Mahonia leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè) 20 g, knotweed (Hǔ Zhàng) 20 g, Feijingcao (Fèi Jīng Cǎo) 20 g, short earth tea (Ǎi Dì Chá) 20 g; loquat leaf (Pípá Yè) de-haired 15 g, Teng Bai Wei (Téng Bái Wēi) 15 g,
licorice (Gān Cǎo) 6 g; one dose daily, decocted and taken in three divided doses.
Modifications: for abundant white phlegm add Pinellia (Bàn Xià) 8 g; for abundant yellow phlegm add Jinxingcao (Jīn Xīng Cǎo) 20 g. This formula is suitable for acute bronchitis.
Pneumonia formula:
Chinese Mahonia leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè) 12 g; Tu Fuling (Tǔ Fú Líng) 20 g, Ǎi Dì Chá 20 g, Jīn Xīng Cǎo 20 g, E Bu Shi Cao 20 g, Fèng Wěi Cǎo 20 g; licorice (Gān Cǎo) 10 g; one dose daily, decocted and taken in three divided doses.
Modifications: for cough with rusty sputum add Zhimu leaf (Zhì Mù Yè) and Imperata root (Bái Máo Gēn) 20 g each; for high fever not abating add raw gypsum (Shēng Shí Gāo) 20 g; for dyspnea add fine tea leaves 20 g and mulberry bark (Sāng Pí) 15 g. This formula, combined with Western medicine, shows remarkable efficacy.
④ Treatment of Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma
Some have used Chinese Mahonia leaf to treat nasopharyngeal carcinoma. Method: Chinese Mahonia leaf (Shí Dà Gōng Láo Yè) 30 g; Prunella (Xià Kū Cǎo) and fresh pomegranate peel (Xiān Shí Liú Pí) 20 g each; licorice (Gān Cǎo) 5 g; one dose daily.
