Cultivation and Processing of Fineleaf Schizonepeta (Jing Jie)
Source
The whole herb of Fineleaf Schizonepeta (Jing Jie) of the Lamiaceae family; in autumn when the flower spikes are green, the above-ground parts are cut and sun-dried. Some harvest the flower spikes separately before cutting the stems and branches and dry them separately; the former is called Fineleaf Schizonepeta Spikes (Jing Jie Sui) and the latter is called Fineleaf Schizonepeta (Jing Jie).
Properties and Flavors
Acrid, warm.
Processing
Fineleaf Schizonepeta (Jing Jie): pick out impurities, briefly soak in water, remove, cut into sections, and sun-dry.
Fried Fineleaf Schizonepeta: place the cut sections of Jing Jie in a pan, lightly stir-fry over low heat, remove and cool (the method for frying Jing Jie spikes is the same).
Charred Fineleaf Schizonepeta: place the cut sections in a pan, stir-fry over high heat until charred black, preserve its properties, sprinkle a little water, remove and sun-dry (the method for charred Jing Jie spikes is the same).
Description
Dried whole herb; stems quadrangular with longitudinal grooves on four sides, mostly branched in the upper part, length 45–90 cm, diameter 3–5 mm; surface light purplish-red, covered with short soft hairs. Texture light and brittle, easily broken; fracture fibrous, yellowish-white, with a central white loose pith. Leaves opposite, leaflets divided, lobes narrow and elongated, yellowish, wrinkled and curled, often incompletely broken; brittle and easily detached. Inflorescences terminal, spike-like verticillasters, green and cylindrical, 7–10 cm long; corollas mostly fallen leaving only the green calyx tube, inside are four brown-black small nutlets. Aroma fragrant, taste slightly astringent and acrid-cool. Best quality is light purple color, thin stems, many and dense spikes.
Effects of Fineleaf Schizonepeta (Jing Jie)
Functions and Indications
It is the whole herb of Fineleaf Schizonepeta (Jing Jie) in the Lamiaceae family.
Mainly treats common cold with fever, headache, sore and swollen throat, wind-stroke with lockjaw, vomiting blood, epistaxis, blood in the stool; metrorrhagia, postpartum blood syncope; abscesses, scabies, scrofula. Jing Jie spikes (Jing Jie Sui) have the same effects but a stronger dispersing action.
Meridians entered
Enters the Lung meridian and the Liver meridian.
Indications
Releases the exterior and dispels wind, regulates blood; when charred it can stop bleeding. Treats common cold with fever, headache, sore and swollen throat, wind-stroke with lockjaw, vomiting blood, epistaxis, blood in the stool; metrorrhagia, postpartum blood syncope; abscesses, scabies, scrofula. Jing Jie spikes (Jing Jie Sui) have the same effects but a stronger dispersing power.
① Ben Jing (Classic Materia Medica): treats cold and heat, mouse-fistula, scrofula producing ulcers, breaks up masses and qi accumulations, moves stagnant blood, removes damp-bi.
② Yao Xing Lun (Treatise on Drug Properties): treats malignant wind and harmful wind, facial deviation, stubborn body bi pain, heart deficiency with forgetfulness, increases strength and vigor. It expels pernicious pathogenic qi and toxins, relieves labor fatigue, treats insect swellings; take a handful, cut, with five sheng of water boil down to two sheng, cool and take in two doses, mainly unblocking and benefiting the blood vessels, transmitting qi to the five zang organs that are deficient, able to induce sweating and dispel cold wind; also pound to powder and mix with vinegar to seal poisonous swellings.
③ Meng Shen: for postpartum wind-stroke with body rigidity, grind to powder and take with wine.
④ Shi Liao Ben Cao (Dietary Materia Medica): aids the spleen and stomach.
⑤ Shi Xing Ben Cao (Dietary Nature Materia): treats blood-exhaustion and constrained wind-qi fullness, back and spine pain, spontaneous sweating, treats male beriberi, tendon and bone vexing pain and yin-yang toxins, typhoid headache, vertigo, and cramping of the hands and feet.
⑥ Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao (Rihuazi Materia Medica): benefits the five zang, reduces food stagnation and descends qi, sobers intoxication. Cooked as a vegetable in warm dishes or brewed as tea, treats head wind with sweating; boiled in soybean sauce decoction to treat sudden severe cold disease.
⑦ Ben Cao Tu Jing (Illustrated Materia Medica): treats head wind, deficiency-induced fatigue, scabies, and gynecological blood-wind.
⑧ Dian Nan Ben Cao (Yunnan Materia Medica): treats traumatic injuries and is applied to poisonous ulcers. Treats vomiting blood. Jing Jie spikes (Jing Jie Sui) clear wind from the head and eyes, stop headache, brighten the eyes, relieve heat and pain of the lung, liver and throat, reduce swelling, eliminate toxins, and disperse sores and abscesses. Treats blood in the stool, stops excessive uterine bleeding, eliminates wind-heat, and unblocks lung qi and nasal orifices.
⑨ Ben Cao Gang Mu (Compendium of Materia Medica): disperses wind-heat, clears the head and eyes, benefits the throat, reduces sores and swellings. Treats stiff neck, black specks in the eyes, eruptive sores, prolapsed yin, vomiting blood, epistaxis, lower bleeding, bloody dysentery, metrorrhagia, and hemorrhoidal discharge.
Dosage and Administration
Internal: decoction, 3–10 g; or used in pills or powders.
External: appropriate amount, decoction for fumigation and washing; pound and apply; or grind to powder and mix.
Cautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated in exterior deficiency with spontaneous sweating, and in headache due to yin deficiency.
① Yao Xing Lun (Treatise on Drug Properties): long-term use of Jing Jie may cause excessive thirst and illness.
② Wei Hang Ji Tan (Weihang Jitan): those who take Jing Jie wind medicines should avoid eating fish.
③ Ben Cao Gang Mu (Compendium of Materia Medica): incompatible with donkey meat and scaleless fish.
④ Ben Cao Jing Qiu (Materia Medica Classic Qiu): contraindicated in those with painful exterior deficiency who sweat; do not use for blood-deficient cold-heat not caused by wind-damp or wind-cold; in yin deficiency with blazing fire and red face leading to headache, be careful not to mistakenly administer.
