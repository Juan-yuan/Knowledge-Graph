Cultivation and Processing of Great Burdock Achene (Niubangzi)
Source
The fruit of the Asteraceae plant Arctium (burdock). When the fruits mature in August to September, collect in batches. Sun-dry, thresh out the fruits, remove impurities, then sun-dry until completely dry. Can be used raw or stir-fried until yellow.
Distribution
Mainly produced in Hebei; also in Jilin, Liaoning, Zhejiang, Heilongjiang and other places. In addition, Sichuan, Henan, Hubei, Shaanxi and other areas also produce it. Output is larger in the northeast, and Zhejiang products are of better quality.
Properties and Taste
Pungent and bitter, cold.
Processing
Great Burdock Achene (Niubangzi): pick out impurities and sieve off soil particles.
Stir-fried Great Burdock Achene (Chao Niubangzi): take clean Great Burdock Achenes, put them in a pan, stir-fry over low heat until slightly puffed, the outside becomes slightly yellow and emits a light fragrance, then remove and cool.
Description
The achene is oblong-ovoid, about 6 millimeters long, with a central diameter of about 3 millimeters. The pericarp is grayish-brown, with several slightly raised longitudinal ribs, one more distinct in the middle; the whole surface has sparse spots and resembles a dense network pattern. One end is slightly narrow and slightly curved, with a light-colored small dot at the apex; the other end is bluntly rounded, slightly wider, with a small pit. The longitudinal surface is somewhat prominent, the margin is smooth, rounded and thick. The pericarp is relatively hard; when broken the kernel splits into two cotyledons, gray-white and oily. Odorless, slightly bitter. The best specimens are large, full, with a grayish-brown pericarp.
Effects of Great Burdock Achene (Niubangzi)
Functions
The fruit of the Asteraceae plant Arctium (burdock).
Main indications: wind-heat cough, swollen and painful throat, rashes that fail to surface, itchy urticaria, carbuncles and sores with toxin.
Channels Entered
Enters
the Lung meridian
,

Main Actions
Disperse wind-heat, open the lungs and expel rashes, reduce swelling and detoxify. Used for wind-heat cough, swollen and painful throat, rashes that fail to surface, itchy urticaria, and carbuncles and sores with toxin.
① Bielu (Bielu): brightens the eyes, tonifies the middle, dispels wind injury.
② Yaoxing Lun (On Drug Properties): dispels various wind, benefits the waist and legs, and also disperses various nodules and the vexing heat-toxin of sinews and bones.
③ Shiliao Benca o (Dietary Materia Medica): roast to powder, brew like tea three bi, promotes urination.
④ Bencao Shiyi (Supplement to Materia Medica): treats wind-toxin swellings and various atrophies.
⑤ Yixue Qiyuan (Origins of Medicine): reduces and benefits the throat and diaphragm. Zhuzhi Miyao (Secret Essentials of Indications): moistens the lungs and disperses qi.
⑥ Li Gao: treats wind-damp pruritic rashes, wind-heat of the throat, disperses the toxins of swellings and ulcers, and benefits stagnant qi in the waist and knees.
⑦ Bencao Gangmu (Compendium of Materia Medica): eliminates toxins of rashes.
Usage and Dosage
Internal: decoct, 5–10 g; or included in powdered formulas.
External: appropriate amount, decocted for gargling.
Precautions and Contraindications
Bencao Jingshu (Commentary on the Materia Medica): do not use recklessly in cases of qi deficiency with a pale complexion and spontaneous bowel movements or diarrhea; for carbuncles that have already suppurated, it is inappropriate to use unless there is constipation.
Formulas Containing Great Burdock Achene (Niubangzi)
① To disperse wind and relieve excessive phlegm and saliva obstructing the throat and diaphragm: Great Burdock Achene (slightly fried) (Niubangzi (Chao)), Schizonepeta spikes (Jingjie Sui) each one liang, Licorice (honey-fried) (Gancao (Zhi)) half liang. Grind to powder; after eating lie down at night; take two qian with a little decoction, action will be gradual. (Bencao Yanyi)
② Treating throat obstruction: Great Burdock Achene six fen, Ma-lian Seed eight fen. Pound the two into a powder; on an empty stomach take one warm-water measure (fangcun bi) gradually increasing to one and a half, twice daily. (Guangji Fang)
③ For wind-heat obstructing the throat with generalized edema: Great Burdock Achene one he (half raw half cooked), pound into powder, mix with hot wine and take one qian. (Jingyan Fang)
④ For wind-heat attacking the upper burner with hanging carbuncles and swelling pain: Eshi (stir-fried) and Licorice (raw) each one liang. Make into powder; each dose two qian, one cup of water, decoct to six fen, hold in the mouth for a while then swallow. (Puji Fang - Qi Guan San)
⑤ For rashes that fail to surface: finely ground Great Burdock Achene five qian, decocted Tamarix (Chengliu) and take to induce surfacing. (Bencao Huiyan)
⑥ For wind-heat skin lesions with widespread pruritic rashes: Great Burdock Achene and Duckweed (Fuping) in equal parts. Use Mint (Bohe) decoction to take two qian, twice daily. (Yangsheng Biyong Fang)
⑦ For wind-swelling with itchy toxic rashes: Great Burdock Achene, Scrophularia (Xuanshen), Stiff Silkworm (Jiangcan), and Mint (Bohe) each five qian. Make to powder; each dose three qian, take with plain decoction. (Fangmai Zhengzong)
⑧ For phlegm-induced fainting and headache: Inula Flower one liang, Great Burdock Achene one liang (lightly fried). Pound to a fine powder, mix without regard to timing, take one qian with waxed-flour tea. (Shenghui Fang)
⑨ For headache radiating to the eyes with blurred vision: Great Burdock Achene, Xanthium Fruit (Cang'erzi), Sweet Chrysanthemum (Ganju hua) each three qian. Decoction and take. (Fangmai Zhengzong)
⑩ For wind-heat turning into painful fixed joints attacking the fingers causing red swelling and numbness, possibly advancing to shoulders, back and knees, triggered by summer heat or constipation: Great Burdock Achene three liang (stir-fried between paper), Fresh fermented soybeans (Xin Douchi) (stir-fried), Notopterygium (Qianghuo) (remove reed) each one liang, dry Raw Rehmannia two liang and a half, Astragalus (Huangqi) one liang and a half (honey-fried). Grind to fine powder; take two qian with decoction on an empty stomach before meals, three times daily. (Ben Shi Fang - Niubangzi San)
⑾ For wind-heat of the head and face, or phlegm-toxin of the neck and throat, wind-heat dental carbuncle: Great Burdock Achene, Mint (Bohe), Schizonepeta (Jingjie), Gardenia (Zhizi), Moutan Cortex (Mudanpi), Dendrobium (Shihu), Scrophularia (Xuanshen), Prunella (Xiakucao); decoct in water and take. (Yangke Xinde Ji - Niubang Jieji Tang)
⑿ For wind-related toothache: stir-fried Great Burdock Achene, decoct and gargle then spit out. (Yannian Fang)
⒀ For breast distension from wind: Mouse-sticky Seed with musk and wine swallowed. (Xiuzhen Fang)
⒁ For perianal carbuncle: Mouse-sticky Seed three qian stir-fried, finely powdered, mixed with one spoon of honey and one spoon of purified niter, take with warm wine on an empty stomach. (Xiuzhen Fang)
⒂ For heat stagnation in the upper burner with sudden swelling and painful obstructed throat: Eshi (lightly fried until fragrant) one liang, Licorice (honey-fried) one fen, Schizonepeta (stems removed) half liang. Pound the three into a fine powder. Each dose two qian, five-tenths of a cup of water, decoct to a boil and take warm. Taking a few sips of boiling decoction is also acceptable. (Shengji Zonglu - Eshi San)
⒃ For eruptive rashes with high fever, constipation with hard stools, or mouth and tongue ulcers and swollen painful throat: Mouse-sticky Seed four liang (stir-fried until fragrant), Licorice (honey-fried), Cimicifuga (Shengma), Belamcanda root (Shegan) each one liang. Make into a coarse powder. Each dose three qian, one large cup of water, decoct to six fen, strain and take warm. (Pediatric Smallpox Formulas - Shegan Shuzhanzi Tang)
⒄ For toxic rashes and mumps with swelling and pain: Great Burdock Achene, Bupleurum (Chaihu), Forsythia (Lianqiao), Sichuan Fritillaria (Chuan Beimu), Schizonepeta (Jingjie) each two qian. Decoction and take. (Bencao Huiyan)
⒅ For epidemic pestilence with large-headed swellings: Dali Seed (Dali Zi) and Saposhnikovia (Fangfeng) in equal parts. Grind to powder. Each dose five qian, one large cup of yellow wine, decoct together and take warm on an empty stomach; induce sweating under covers. (Songfeng on Epidemics)
