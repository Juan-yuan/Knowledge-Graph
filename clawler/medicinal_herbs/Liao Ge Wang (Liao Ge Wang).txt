Cultivation and Preparation of Liaogewang (liao ge wang)
Source
Stems and leaves of Nanling Raohua (nan ling rao hua) in the Thymelaeaceae family. Stems and leaves can be harvested year-round; wash, cut into sections, sun-dry or use fresh.
[Original morphology]
Nanling Raohua (nan ling rao hua) is a semi-evergreen small shrub, up to 1 m tall. The whole plant is smooth and hairless. Stems erect, much branched, young branches reddish-brown. Root bark and stem bark are rich in cottony fibers and not easily broken. Leaves opposite, nearly sessile; leaf blades obovate to oblong-elliptic, 2–5 cm long, 0.8–1.5 cm wide, apex obtuse or shortly acuminate, margin entire, base cuneate, many lateral veins very fine, brown when dry. Flowers yellow-green, several flowers clustered at branch tips, arranged in compound umbel-like inflorescences or nearly sessile capitulum-like heads; flowers bisexual, without bracts, perianth tubular, apex 4-cleft, glabrous; stamens 8, in two whorls attached to the inner perianth tube, filaments short, anthers oblong; ovary obovoid or oblong-elliptic, with a rounded stigma. Drupes ovoid or ellipsoid, bright red when mature. Flowering and fruiting in summer and autumn.
[Habitat and distribution]
Ecological environment: grows in hillside shrub thickets, by roadsides and village margins.
Resource distribution: distributed in Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Taiwan, Hunan, Guangdong, Guangxi, Guizhou, Yunnan and other areas.
Taste and properties
Taste bitter; pungent; nature cold; toxic
Processing
Steamed leaves can be pounded to a pulp for external application or squeezed for juice and applied externally. Roots can be steamed until cooked, sliced and sun-dried. Steam leaves, wash, air-dry in the shade, cut into sections and set aside.
Characteristics
Stems cylindrical, branched, of varying lengths, 8–25 mm in diameter; surface of thick stems light brown to dark brown-black, with irregular coarse longitudinal wrinkles, lenticels prominent, often two connected transversely, sometimes several connected to form a ring; surface of thin stems dark brown-red, with fine longitudinal wrinkles and opposite leaf petiole scars, sometimes with raised small branch remnants. Hard in texture; fracture surface shows numerous cottony fibers in the cortex. Leaves irregularly curled, when flattened oblong-elliptic, margin entire, pale yellow-green to light green, veins slightly raised beneath; petiole short, about 2 mm long. Brittle, easily broken. Odor faint, taste slightly bitter.
Therapeutic effects of Liaogewang (liao ge wang)
Functions
Stems and leaves of Nanling Raohua (nan ling rao hua) in the Thymelaeaceae family. Indicated for suppurative swellings and sores; scrofula; rheumatic pain; traumatic injuries from falls and blows; snake and insect bites.
Meridians
Heart; Lung; Small Intestine channels
Indications
Clears heat and detoxifies, transforms phlegm and disperses nodules, reduces swelling and relieves pain. Mainly used for suppurative swellings and sores, scrofula, rheumatic pain, traumatic injuries, snake and insect bites.
Usage and dosage
Internal: decoction (should be decocted for a long time, more than 4 hours), 6–9 g;
External: appropriate amount, pounded and applied, powdered and mixed for topical application, or decocted for washing.
Precautions and contraindications
Avoid in individuals with weak constitutions and in pregnant women.
Formulas using Liaogewang (liao ge wang)
① For phlegm-fire sores (nodular ulcers in the axilla or groin or cramping pain of the limbs): Liaogewang leaves 5 qian, add a little table salt, pound together and apply to the affected area. (Lingnan Materia Medica)
② For "hexi feng" (crane-knee wind): Nanling Raohua (nan ling rao hua), Jiegucao (jie gu cao). Decoction with water, take with wine. (Hunan Materia Medica)
③ For sores and toxic swellings; traumatic injuries from falls and blows; snake and insect bites; infant scalp sores: fresh Liaogewang stems and leaves, pounded and applied externally or squeezed for juice and applied externally. (Guangzhou Military Handbook of Common Chinese Herbs)
④ For sores and breast abscesses: an appropriate amount of Liaogewang leaves, pounded and applied to the lesion. (Guangxi Materia Medica)
⑤ For unnamed swellings and toxins: Dinggewang leaves (ding ge wang), pounded, add a small amount of rice wine, apply to the affected area. (Jiangxi Herbal Handbook)
