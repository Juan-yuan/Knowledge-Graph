Cultivation and processing of coriander (Húsuī)
Source
The whole plant with roots of the Apiaceae family, coriander (Húsuī). Harvested in spring, washed and sun-dried.
Morphology
An annual herb, entire plant glabrous. Taproot slender, usually spindle-shaped, with many lateral roots. Stem erect, hollow, 20–60 centimeters tall, with fine ribs. Basal leaves with long petioles, pinnately divided 1–2 times, segments broadly ovate or fan-shaped, cuneate at base; cauline leaves alternate, petioles shorter, pinnately divided 2–3 times to the base, ultimate segments narrowly linear. Compound umbel terminal, or opposite leaves; 3–6 umbel rays, about 2–6 centimeters long, usually no involucre; small involucels commonly three, linear-conical; flowers small, white or pale red; calyx with five teeth at apex; five petals, obovate, peripheral flowers in the small umbels irregular with large ray petals; stamens five, alternating with petals, anthers oblong-ovoid, dorsifixed, filaments slightly curved at tip; pistil single, inferior ovary, style slender, apex bifurcate, stigma capitate. Fruits nearly globose, 3–5 millimeters in diameter, with ten wavy primary ribs and twelve vertical secondary ribs. Flowering April to July. Fruiting July to September.
Properties
Pungent, warm.
Description
Dried whole herb: leaves mostly curled and fallen, grass-yellow; stem withered, about 1 millimeter thick; rootlets coiled, with a strong characteristic aroma. Best when the color is slightly green and the aroma is strong. Widely produced in most regions of the country.
Effects of coriander (Húsuī)
Functions
The whole herb with roots of the Apiaceae family, coriander (Húsuī).
Main indications: delayed eruption of measles, food stagnation.
Channels entered
Enters the Lung channel and the Spleen channel.
Treats
Promotes sweating and eruption of rashes, reduces food stagnation and descends qi. Treats delayed eruption of measles and food accumulation.
1. Cui Yuxi, "Dietary Classic": regulates digestion and descends qi.
2. "Dietary Materia Medica": benefits the five zang, supplements tendons and vessels, promotes digestion and appetite, treats intestinal wind, used on hot cakes wrapped in food.
3. "Jiayou Materia Medica": reduces food stagnation, treats the five zang, supplements deficiencies, benefits the intestines, clears small abdominal qi, relieves heat from the limbs, stops headache, treats unresolved measles and non-erupting pox like pea-sized sores — when made into wine and sprayed it will immediately erupt them, opens the orifices.
4. "Everyday Materia Medica": reduces food and transforms qi, clears stagnation in the large and small intestines. Treats headache and toothache, relieves fish and meat poisoning.
5. "Medical Forest Essentials": raises and disperses yin qi, wards off pathogenic qi, induces sweating, assists eruption of rashes.
6. Chen Nianzu: transforms phlegm.
Usage and dosage
Internal: decoction, 9–15 g dried, 15–30 g fresh; or crush to extract juice.
External: appropriate amount, decoction for washing; or mash and apply; or express juice for application.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated when measles rash has fully erupted, or when, though not erupted, heat-toxin is stagnant and not due to external wind-cold.
1. "Qianjin Food Therapy": do not eat long-term, causes forgetfulness. Hua Tuo said: those with body odor from coriander, halitosis, or hidden dental problems, eating it aggravates these; those with internal pathogenic qi should not eat it; it can provoke chronic illnesses; especially contraindicated for recent traumatic wounds.
2. "Dietary Materia Medica": prolonged consumption in cold people leads to weak legs, should not be eaten with Xiehao (Xiéhāo); causes foul-smelling sweat that is hard to cure. Do not eat long-term; as a smoked vegetable it harms mental vigor. Roots can induce chronic disease.
3. "Compendium of Materia Medica": those taking tonic medicines or medicines containing Atractylodes (Báizhú) or Moutan (Mǔdān) should not eat this.
4. "Annotations to the Materia Medica Classic": people with qi deficiency should not eat. If measles or pox are not erupting smoothly, and it is not due to exterior wind-cold invasion or foul qi contact, it is not appropriate to use.
5. "Medical Forest Essentials": excessive consumption causes blurred vision and exhausts qi.
Formulas using coriander (Húsuī)
1. To hasten eruption of pediatric measles/pox: coriander (Húsuī) 3 liang. Finely cut, add two large cups of wine, boil, pour over the coriander, cover to prevent vapor escape, when slightly cooled remove dregs, lightly spray from below the nape downward, spray the back, loins, both feet, chest and abdomen thoroughly; do not spray the face. (From "Shenghui Fang": Coriander Wine)
2. Treating persistent childhood erythema: apply coriander juice topically. (From "Bingbu Shouji Fang")
3. Treating multiple snake venom bites: equal parts Hekou pepper (Hékǒujiāo) and coriander seedlings (Húsuī miáo), etc.; crush and apply. (From "Qianjin Essentials")
4. Treating small intestine heat with urinary difficulty: root of dongkui (Dōngkuí gēn) a large handful, coriander (Húsuī) 2 liang, talc (Huáshí) 1 liang (powder). Grind the first two finely, add 2 sheng of water, decoct to 1 sheng, add talc powder, take warm in three doses. Also treats bloody strangury. (From "Shengji Zonglu": Kui Gen Drink)
5. Treating rectal prolapse: coriander (cut) one sheng, burn and use the smoke to fumigate the anus. (From "Zimu Milu")
6. Treating wind-cold cold with headache and ear congestion: perilla leaf (Zǐsū yè) 6 g, fresh ginger (Shēngjiāng) 6 g, coriander (Húsuī) 9 g. Decoction and take orally. (From "Gansu Herbal Manual")
7. Treating excessive heat-toxin and early pea-like sores: coriander (Húsuī) a handful (finely cut), raw Rehmannia root (Shēng Dìhuáng) 3 liang (finely cut). Combine, crush and press to obtain juice, take on an empty stomach in one gulp. (From "Shenghui Fang")
8. Treating hemoptysis: equal parts coriander (Húsuī) and seaweed (Hǎizǎo), wash off sand and grit, add appropriate oil and salt, cook 3–4 hours, eat three times daily, one bowl each time. (From "Hunan Materia Medica")
9. Treating indigestion and abdominal bloating: fresh whole coriander (Húsuī) 30 g. Decoction and take orally.
10. Treating cold-deficient stomach pain: fresh coriander (Húsuī) 15–24 g. Decoction in wine or water and take orally. (Items 9–10 from "Fujian Herbal Medicine")
11. Treating stomach cold distention and pain: coriander (Húsuī) 15 g, black pepper (Hújiāo) 15 g, mugwort leaf (Àiyè) 6 g. Decoction and take orally. (From "Sichuan Materia Medica", 1979)
12. Treating pregnancy-related nausea and vomiting: a handful of fresh coriander (Húsuī), add perilla leaf (Zǐsū yè) and patchouli (Guǎnghuòxiāng) 3 g each, dried tangerine peel (Chénpí) and Amomum fruit (Shārén) 6 g each. Decoct and pour into a large pot, direct the pot mouth to the patient’s nose and have her inhale. (From "Chinese Medical Journal", 1964, (1):24)
13. Treating edema: appropriate amount of coriander (Húsuī) placed inside the abdomen of a crucian carp, cooked in sesame oil and eaten. (From "Jilin Herbal Medicine")
14. Treating anal pruritus: coriander (Húsuī) powdered, mix with cooked egg yolk, mash with sesame oil and insert into the anus, use three consecutive applications. (From "Hunan Materia Medica")
15. Treating internal insect poison: crush half a cup of coriander root juice and take at any time; the insect will immediately descend; taking it with wine is even more effective. (From "Shenghui Fang")
