Cultivation and Processing of Mulberry Leaf
Source
From the leaves of the mulberry (Morus) of the Moraceae family. Leaves are harvested after frost in October to November; remove fine branches and impurities, then sun-dry.
Morphology
A deciduous shrub or small tree, 3–15 m tall. Bark gray-white with linear shallow fissures; root bark yellow-brown or reddish-yellow, strongly fibrous.
Simple leaves alternate; petiole 1–2.5 cm long; leaf blade ovate or broadly ovate, 5–20 cm long, 4–10 cm wide, apex acute or acuminate, base rounded or nearly cordate, margin with coarse or rounded teeth, sometimes irregularly lobed, glabrous and shiny above, with short hairs along veins beneath and hair in the axils; three basal veins arising and interlacing with finer veins into a reticulate pattern, more distinct on the underside; stipules lanceolate, falling early.
Flowers unisexual, dioecious; male and female inflorescences arranged in spike-like catkin inflorescences, axillary; female spikes 1–2 cm long, pubescent, peduncle 5–10 mm; male spikes 1–2.5 cm long, pendulous, slightly pubescent; male flowers with 4 perianth segments and 4 stamens, with a sterile pistil in the center; female flowers with 4 perianth segments fused at the base, stigma 2-lobed.
The achenes are aggregated into many clustered ovoid or oblong multiple fruits, 1–2.5 cm long, green at first and becoming fleshy, dark purple or red when ripe. Seeds small. Flowering April–May, fruiting May–June.
Habitat and Distribution
Ecology: grows on hills, slopes, beside villages, in fields, etc.; mostly cultivated.
Resource distribution: distributed throughout the country.
Taste and Properties
Taste: bitter and sweet; nature: cold.
Processing
Mulberry Leaf (Sang Ye): pick out impurities, rub or crumble, remove stems, and sieve off dirt.
Honey-fried Mulberry Leaf (Mi Sang Ye): take clean mulberry leaves, mix with refined cooked honey and a little boiling water, combine and let sit briefly, then place in a pan and stir-fry over low heat until no longer sticky to the touch; remove and cool. (Per 100 jin of mulberry leaves use 20–25 jin of refined honey)
Characteristics
Leaves often wrinkled and broken. Intact leaves have petioles 1–2.5 cm long; when flattened leaf blades are ovate or broadly ovate, 8–15 cm long, 7–13 cm wide, apex gradually acute, base truncate, rounded or cordate, margin serrate or with blunt teeth, some irregularly divided.
Upper surface yellow-green or pale yellow-brown, some with small wart-like projections; lower surface slightly lighter, veins prominent, fine veins reticulate, veins sparsely pubescent, vein bases with tufted hairs. Brittle texture. Slight aroma, taste mild and slightly bitter and astringent. Best when leaves are large and yellow-green in color.
Therapeutic Effects of Mulberry Leaf
Functions
From the leaves of the mulberry (Morus) of the Moraceae family.
Treats wind-warm febrile conditions with fever, headache, red eyes, thirst, lung-heat cough, wind-bi (wind-damp obstruction), urticaria, and elephantiasis of the lower limbs.
Channels Entered
Enters the Lung channel
Liver channel
Indications
Expels wind and clears heat, cools the blood and brightens the eyes. Treats wind-warm febrile disease with fever, headache, red eyes, thirst, lung-heat cough, wind-bi, urticaria, and lower limb elephantiasis.
1. Shennong Bencao Jing: dispels cold and heat, induces sweating.
2. Tang Bencao: decoct to a concentrated juice; treats beriberi and edema, benefits the large and small intestines.
3. Meng Shen: roast and fry and drink as a tea to stop thirst.
4. Bencao Shiyi: treats cholera with abdominal pain, vomiting and diarrhea; in winter use dried leaves cooked strongly. Finely scrape and boil in a large pot until reduced to the color of brown sugar to remove chronic wind and lingering blood stasis.
5. Rihuazi Bencao: benefits the five viscera, unblocks the joints, descends qi when decocted and taken; expels wind-pain and induces sweating; used for traumatic bleeding; for snake or centipede bites, mash with salt and apply.
6. Bencao Tujing: boil into a decoction and pour over hands and feet to relieve wind-bi.
7. Danxi Xinfa: bake dry into powder, take with rice soup on an empty stomach to stop night sweating.
8. Bencao Mengquan: decoct and wash the eyes to remove wind-tears, reduce edema and foot swelling, descend qi, and benefit the joints.
9. Compendium of Materia Medica: treats consumptive low fever and cough, brightens the eyes, promotes hair growth.
10. Bencao Congxin: nourishes dryness, cools the blood, and stops bleeding.
11. Baicao Jing: treats intestinal wind.
12. Bencao Qiuzhen: clears the lungs and purges the stomach, cools the blood and dries dampness.
13. Bencao Qiuyuan: stops hematemesis and bleeding from traumatic wounds.
14. Shandong Chinese Materia Medica: treats sore throat, swollen painful gums, and facial edema.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 4.5–9 g; or incorporated into pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, decoct for washing or pound and apply as a poultice.

Dietary/Therapeutic Recipes
1. Chrysanthemum-Mulberry-Mint-Bamboo Leaf Tea
Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua) 5 g, Mulberry Leaf (Sang Ye) 5 g, Mint (Bo He) 3 g, Lophatherum/Bamboo Leaf (Dan Zhu Ye) 30 g, Imperata Root (Bai Mao Gen) 30 g. Place all ingredients in a teapot, pour boiling water over and steep for 10 minutes. Drink as a tea substitute as desired.
Function: acrid-cool release of the exterior, clears and benefits the throat. Indications: wind-heat common cold, fever and headache, spontaneous sweating, nasal congestion, sore and swollen throat, cough with thick phlegm.
2. Mulberry Leaf and Fu Xiao Mai Tea
Mulberry Leaf (preferably frost-bitten) 10 g, Floating Wheat (Fu Xiao Mai) 30 g. Rub the mulberry leaves to break them up and place in a large teacup. Boil the Fu Xiao Mai in a pot and strain to obtain the juice; use the Fu Xiao Mai juice to infuse the mulberry leaves, cover and steep for 10 minutes. Drink as a tea frequently.
Function: clears heat and stops sweating. Indications: low-grade fever and night sweats in pulmonary tuberculosis.
3. Mulberry-Chrysanthemum-Cicada Slough Decoction
Mulberry Leaf 10 g, Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua) 10 g, Cicada Slough (Chan Yi) 6 g, white sugar as needed. Select and clean the mulberry leaves and chrysanthemum, remove the head and feet of the cicada slough, place all in a pot with appropriate water, bring to a boil over high heat then simmer for 15 minutes, strain and add sugar to taste. Drink regularly.
Function: disperse wind and clear heat, calm convulsions. Indications: pediatric convulsions with fever, headache, cough, and nasal discharge.
4. Safflower-Mulberry-Chrysanthemum Decoction
Safflower (Hong Hua) 3 g, Mulberry Leaf (Sang Ye) 10 g, Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua) 10 g. Place the mulberry leaf, chrysanthemum and safflower in a cup and pour boiling water over, cover and steep briefly. First use to fumigate the affected eye, then drink when warm. Twice daily for 3 days.
Function: disperse heat, reduce swelling, relieve pain and brighten the eyes. Indications: acute infectious conjunctivitis.
5. Mulberry-Mint-Chrysanthemum-Loquat Leaf Tea
Ingredients: Mulberry Leaf 500 g, Chrysanthemum Flower 250 g, Loquat Leaf (Pi Pa Ye) 250 g, Mint (Bo He) 250 g.
Preparation: coarsely powder the above herbs and place in clean gauze bags, each bag 10–15 g. Take one bag, place in a teacup and pour boiling water over, cover and steep for 10 minutes. Drink as a tea frequently.
Function: dispel wind and clear heat, release the exterior and clear the lungs. Indication: early wind-heat common cold.
