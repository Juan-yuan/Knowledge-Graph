Mahonia (Gong Lao Mu)
Source: https://zhongyibaike.com/wiki/%E5%8A%9F%E5%8A%B3%E6%9C%A8
Names
Earth Yellow-Cypress (Tu Huangbai), Yellow Heaven Bamboo (Huang Tianzhu), Mouse Does Not Climb (Shu bu pa), Mountain Yellow-Cypress (Shan Huangbai), Large-leaved Huanglian (Da ye Huanglian), Ten Big Merits (Shi da Gonglao), Umbrella-handle Huanglian (San ba Huanglian), Large Mouse Yellow (Da lao shu huang), Mouse Yellow (Lao shu huang), Mouse Thorn (Lao shu ci), Thorn Huanglian (Ci Huanglian), Boxwood (Huangyang mu), Goat-horn Lily (Yangjiao lian), Earth Huangqin (Tu Huangqin), Goat-horn Huanglian (Yangjiao Huanglian), Eight-corner Goat (Ba jiao yang), Earth Huanglian (Tu Huanglian).
Morphology
1. Broad-leaved Shi Da Gonglao: evergreen shrub, 1–4 m high. Stem surface earth-yellow or brown, rough, cross-section yellow. Leaves alternate, thickly leathery, petiolate, base expanded and clasping the stem; odd-pinnate leaves, 25–40 cm long, 7–15 leaflets, lateral leaflets sessile, broadly ovate, unequal in size, 4–12 cm long, 2.5–4.5 cm wide, terminal leaflet larger and petiolated, apex gradually acuminate, base broadly cuneate or nearly rounded, margins revolute, with 2–8 large spine-like serrations per side, upper surface dark green and glossy, lower surface yellow-green. Racemose inflorescences at stem apex, erect, 5–10 cm long, 6–9 fascicled clusters, 1 small bract; sepals 9, arranged in three rows; flowers yellow-brown, petals 6, oblong, apex with two shallow lobes, base with two dense glands; stamens 6; pistil 1. Berries ovoid, about 5 mm in diameter, bluish-black when mature, covered with bloom. Flowering August–October, fruiting October–December.
2. Narrow-leaved Shi Da Gonglao: evergreen shrub, 1–2 m high. Stem erect, bark gray, much branched. Leaves alternate; odd-pinnate; petiole base swollen; leaves coriaceous, 5–13 leaflets, narrowly oblanceolate to lanceolate, 6–12 cm long, 0.7–1.5 cm wide, apex long acuminate with a sharp spine, base cuneate, margins with 6–13 spine-like serrations per side, upper surface fresh dark green and glossy, veins inconspicuous, lower surface yellow-green; veins 3-forking from the base. Racemose inflorescences arise from the axils of terminal branch scales, 3–6 cm long, pedicel base with an involucre, bracts ovate-triangular; sepals 9, petal-like; petals 6, yellow, oblong, entire; stamens 6, filaments thread-like, anthers short-cleft; ovary ovoid, without a style, stigma capitate. Berries ovoid, mature fruit ovoid, bluish-black when ripe, covered with bloom. Flowering July–August, fruiting August–October.
Ecological environment
1. Grows in sunny hillside shrubs, also cultivated.
2. Grows in hillside shrubs and roadsides. Also cultivated in gardens.
Resource distribution
1. Distributed in Shaanxi, Anhui, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Henan, Hubei, Hunan, Sichuan and other areas.
2. Distributed in Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Hubei, Hunan, Guangdong and other areas.

Cultivation and processing of Mahonia (Gonglao mu)
Source
This product is the dried stems of Berberidaceae plants Broad-leaved Shi Da Gonglao Mahonia bealei (Fort.) Carr. or Narrow-leaved Shi Da Gonglao Mahonia fortunei (Lindl.) Fedde.
Processing
Remove impurities from the crude drug, grade, wash clean, moisten thoroughly, slice thinly, dry, and sieve off dust.
Appearance
This product consists of nearly circular slices, 1–2.5 cm in diameter. Surface gray-yellow to brown, rough, soft, slightly elastic, outer bark easily peels off. Cross-section shows narrow bark, brown, wide wood, bright yellow, several concentric rings and closely arranged radiating striations visible, pith darker in color. Texture hard. Slight odor, bitter taste.
Properties and meridian tropism
Bitter, cold. Enters the liver, stomach, and large intestine meridians.
Functions and indications
Clears heat and dries dampness, drains fire and relieves toxicity. Used for damp-heat diarrhea and dysentery, jaundice, red swollen painful eyes, stomach-fire toothache, sores and boils, abscesses, dysentery; jaundice-type hepatitis.
Effects of Mahonia (Gonglao mu)
Efficacy
This product is the dried stems of the Berberidaceae plants Broad-leaved Shi Da Gonglao or Narrow-leaved Shi Da Gonglao. Used for damp-heat diarrhea and dysentery, jaundice, red swollen painful eyes, stomach-fire toothache, sores and boils, abscesses, dysentery; jaundice-type hepatitis.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoct as a decoction, 5–10 g.
External use: appropriate amount, decoct for washing; or grind to powder and apply as a paste.
Precautions and contraindications
Those with a cold-deficient constitution should avoid use.
