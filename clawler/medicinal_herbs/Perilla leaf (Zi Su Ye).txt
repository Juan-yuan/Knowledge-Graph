Cultivation and Preparation of Perilla Leaf (Zi Su Ye)
Source
This product is the dried leaves (or with young shoots) of the Lamiaceae plant Perilla frutescens (L.) Britt. Harvest when branches and leaves are luxuriant in summer, remove impurities, and sun-dry.
Property and Taste
Pungent, warm.
Processing
Remove impurities and old stems; or spray with clean water, chop, and dry.
Character
The leaves of this product are often wrinkled, rolled, or broken; intact leaves when flattened are ovate, 4–11 cm long and 2.5–9 cm wide. Apex long-acuminate or acute, base rounded or broadly cuneate, margin with rounded serrations. Both surfaces purple or upper surface green and lower surface purple, sparsely covered with gray-white hairs; lower surface has numerous depressed glandular scales. Petiole 2–7 cm long, purple or purplish green. Texture brittle. If with young shoots, branch diameter 2–5 mm, purplish green, with pith in the center of the cross section. Fragrant aroma, taste slightly pungent.
Effects of Perilla Leaf (Zi Su Ye)
Efficacy
This product is the dried leaves (or with young shoots) of Perilla of the Lamiaceae family.
Main Indications: wind-cold common cold, aversion to cold with fever, cough, wheeze, chest and abdominal distention and fullness, restless fetal movement. Also able to relieve fish and crab poisoning.
Channels Entered
Enters the Lung Channel (Fei Jing), and Spleen Channel (Pi Jing).
Indications
Used for wind-cold common cold with fever and aversion to cold, headache and nasal congestion, accompanied by cough or chest oppression. This product can disperse exterior cold and open and promote Lung qi; it can be used together with Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang). If cough is present, it is often paired with Apricot Kernel (Xing Ren) and Peucedanum (Qian Hu), as in Apricot-Perilla Powder (Xing Su San); if there is qi stagnation with chest oppression, it is often combined with Nutgrass (Xiang Fu) and Aged Tangerine Peel (Chen Pi), as in Su San.
Used for Spleen and Stomach qi stagnation with chest fullness and vomiting. This product moves qi, widens the middle, harmonizes the stomach and stops vomiting. For cold-dominant cases, it is often used with Agastache (Huo Xiang); for heat-dominant cases, it may be used with Coptis (Huang Lian). For qi stagnation with phlegm accumulation, it is often combined with Pinellia (Ban Xia) and Magnolia Bark (Hou Po).
Used for morning sickness in pregnancy with chest and abdominal fullness; often combined with Aged Tangerine Peel (Chen Pi) and Amomum (Sha Ren) to strengthen antiemetic and fetal-stabilizing effects.
Used for abdominal pain, vomiting and diarrhea caused by eating fish or crab; used alone or with Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang) and Angelica Dahurica (Bai Zhi) decocted and taken.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decocted, 5–10 g.
External use: appropriate amount, pounded and applied, ground into powder and mixed in, or decocted for washing.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated in warm diseases and those with deficient qi.
1. "Ben Cao Jing Shu": If the disease belongs to yin deficiency and manifests as fever from deficiency or aversion to cold and headache, use with caution or do not administer, because the disease requires contraction and tonification. Also not suitable when fire rising causes vomiting.
2. "Ben Cao Tong Xuan": Prolonged use can deplete true qi.
