Cultivation and Processing of Reed Rhizome (Lu Gen)
Source
The rhizome of the grass-family plant common reed (Phragmites australis, Lu Wei). Harvested in spring, summer, and autumn; wash off soil, cut away remaining stems, buds and rootlets on the nodes, peel off the membranous leaves, and sun-dry. Or bury in moist sand for fresh use.
Nature and Taste
Sweet; cold
Appearance and Properties
① Fresh Reed Rhizome
Also called: Living-water Reed Rhizome. Cylindrical or flattened cylindrical, of varying lengths, about 1.5 centimeters in diameter. Surface yellowish-white and glossy, tip tapered like a bamboo shoot, green or yellow-green. Nodes present along the whole length, internodes 10–17 millimeters long, with remaining rootlets and bud scars on the nodes. Light and tough, not easily broken. Transverse section yellow-white, hollow, peripheral wall about 1.5 millimeters thick, showing small pores arranged in a ring; outer bark loose and peelable. Odorless, sweet taste.
② Dried Reed Rhizome
Flattened long-cylindrical. Surface glossy, yellowish-white, nodes harder and appearing reddish-yellow, longitudinal wrinkles between nodes. Light and pliant, not easily broken, odorless, slightly sweet. Best quality are thick, yellowish-white, glossy, without rootlets, and tender. Produced nationwide.
Actions of Reed Rhizome (Lu Gen)
Efficacy
The rhizome of the grass-family plant common reed.
Treats febrile disease with vexation and thirst, stomach-heat vomiting, dysphagia and obstruction in the epigastrium, rebellious stomach qi, pulmonary consumption, and lung abscess. Also relieves puffer fish (fugu) poison.
Meridians Entered
Lung meridian; Stomach meridian; Bladder meridian
Main Indications
Clears heat, generates fluids, relieves vexation, and stops vomiting. Treats febrile disease with vexation and thirst, stomach-heat vomiting, dysphagia and obstruction in the epigastrium, rebellious stomach qi, pulmonary consumption, and lung abscess. Also relieves puffer fish (fugu) poison.
① Bie Lu (Bielu): treats wasting-thirst and hostile heat, stops small difficult urination.
② Yao Xing Lun (Yaoxing Lun): can relieve great heat, open the stomach. Treats intractable belching and hiccup.
③ Tang Ben Cao (Tang Ben Cao): cures incessant vomiting with inability to eat, stomach heat, and lingering illness after typhoid.
④ Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao (Ri Huazi Ben Cao): treats alternating chills and fever with vexation and oppression, heart heat in pregnancy, and also thirst in dysentery.
⑤ Ri Yong Ben Cao (Riyong Ben Cao): relieves puffer fish poison.
⑥ Ben Cao Meng Quan (Ben Cao Mengquan): relieves alcohol poisoning and fish or crab poisoning.
⑦ Ben Cao Yuan Shi (Ben Cao Yuan Shi): treats dry retching and cholera.
⑧ Yu Qiu Yao Jie (Yu Qiu Yao Jie): clears and descends Lung and Stomach, disperses constraint and vexation, generates fluids and stops thirst, relieves vomiting and inability to eat, treats dysphagia, belching, and agitation.
⑨ Yi Lin Zuan Yao (Yi Lin Zuan Yao): can leach dampness and promote urination, treats lung abscess.
⑩ Tian Bao Ben Cao (Tianbao Ben Cao): clears the heart and benefits the kidney, removes ocular haze, dizziness, tinnitus, sore ulcerations, confusing dreams and inversion, and seminal emission.
⑾ Zhongguo Yao Zhi (Zhongguo Yao Zhi): treats constipation.
⑿ Nanjing Folk Herbs (Nanjing Minjian Yao Cao): treats sore throat.
⒀ Shandong Chinese Materia Medica (Shandong Zhong Yao): treats edema.
⒁ Sichuan Materia Medica (Sichuan Zhong Yao Zhi): treats maculopapular rashes, dry tongue, and lack of fluids.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoct, 15–30 g for dried, 60–120 g for fresh; or pound fresh and extract juice.
External use: appropriate amount, decoct and wash.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in those with spleen and stomach cold deficiency.
Prescriptions Using Reed Rhizome (Lu Gen)
① For Taiyin warm disease with extreme thirst and sticky white phlegm that will not flow: pear juice, water chestnut juice, fresh reed rhizome juice, Ophiopogon tuber juice (Mai Dong), lotus root juice (or use sugarcane juice), adjust amounts according to the situation, mix evenly and take cool; those who strongly dislike cold may take in thick soup warmed before use. (Wen Bing Tiao Bian: Five-juice Drink)
② For the five types of dysphagia with chest obstruction, qi stagnation, vexation, retching and inability to eat: Reed Rhizome 5 liang (about 125 g). Shred and boil with three large bowls of water down to two bowls, remove dregs, no strict timing, take warm. (Jin Kui Yu Han Fang)
③ For persistent vomiting and reversal: Reed Rhizome 3 jin (about 1.5 kg). Cut and boil to a strong decoction, drink frequently. (Zhou Hou Fang)
④ For post–cold-disease vomiting, rebellious stomach and dry retching with inability to eat: raw Reed Rhizome (cut) and bamboo shavings (Zhu Ru) each one sheng (a traditional volume), polished rice three he, fresh ginger three liang. Take the four ingredients, boil with five sheng of water down to two and a half sheng, drink as desired. (Qian Jin Fang: Lu Gen Drink)
⑤ For bone-steaming tidal fever and pulmonary consumption with vexation and inability to eat: Reed Rhizome (cut to weight), Ophiopogon tuber (Mai Dong) (heart removed), Di Gu Pi (root-bark), each ten liang, fresh ginger ten liang (including skin, sliced), dried tangerine peel (Chen Pi) and Poria (Fu Ling) each five liang. Cut all six herbs, boil with two dou of water down to eight sheng, squeeze out dregs, divide into five warm doses to be taken about eight or nine li apart, three doses by day and two by night, then induce sweating; avoid vinegar-flavored foods. (Xuan Gan Chuan Shi Fang)
⑥ Cholera with vexation: Reed Rhizome three qian, Ophiopogon tuber one qian. Decoction and take. (Qian Jin Fang)
⑦ For fish poisoning with facial swelling, delirium, or deathly condition after eating seabass: drink plenty of Reed Rhizome juice; also treats crab poisoning. (Qian Jin Fang)
⑧ For bleeding gums: decocted Reed Rhizome water as tea. (Hunan Yao Wu Zhi)

⑩ For pertussis with hemoptysis: Reed Rhizome 30 g, Selaginella (Juan Bai) 6 g, Mu Hudie 6 g, beef-skin jelly 7.5 g. Decoction and take. (Hunan Yao Wu Zhi)

Dietary Therapy Recipes
1. Fresh Reed Rhizome Congee
Fresh Reed Rhizome 100 g, green peel (Qing Pi) 5 g, polished rice 100 g, two slices fresh ginger. Wash fresh reed rhizome, cut into thin segments, soak with green peel in cold water for 30 minutes, bring to a vigorous boil over high heat, then simmer over low heat for 20 minutes. Remove herb residue, add washed polished rice and cook into congee. Five minutes before removing from heat, add fresh ginger. Divide into two warm doses per day.
Efficacy: drains heat and harmonizes the stomach, nourishes yin and relieves pain; suitable for peptic ulcer patients of the Liver-Stomach accumulated-heat pattern, presenting with epigastric burning pain, irritability, easy anger, acid regurgitation and bitter taste.
2. Chrysanthemum and Reed Rhizome Tea
Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua) 10 g, Reed Rhizome 60 g. Wash reed rhizome, chop, put together with chrysanthemum into a pot, decoct and strain to obtain the liquid. Drink as a tea.
Efficacy: clears lung heat, promotes urination, and vents rashes. Indicated for pediatric measles, chickenpox and similar conditions.
