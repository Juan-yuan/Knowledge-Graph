Cultivation and Processing of Curly Dock (Niu'er Dahuang)
Source
From the root of the Polygonaceae plant Curly Dock (Rumex crispus). The roots are harvested in April–May, washed clean, sun-dried or used fresh.
Morphology
Perennial herb, 50–100 cm tall. Roots are thick, yellow, with a sour taste. Stems erect, usually unbranched, with shallow grooves. Leaves alternate; stipule sheaths membranous, tubular, often rupturing; leaf blades lanceolate or narrowly oblong-lanceolate, 12–18 cm long, 2–4.5 cm wide, apex short-acuminate, base gradually narrowed, margins with wavy folds, glabrous on both surfaces. Many flowers cluster in leaf axils or form short racemes, composing a narrow, elongated panicle; perianth segments 6 in 2 whorls, persistent; stamens 6; styles 3, brush-like. Achenes trigonous with sharp ridges, about 2 mm long, brown and shiny. Flowering and fruiting period June–August.
Habitat and Distribution
Ecological environment: grows in wet places by ditches, riverbanks and beside marshy meadows.
Distribution: occurs in Northeast China, North China and in Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Fujian, Taiwan, Guangxi, Guizhou and other regions.
Taste and Nature
Bitter; cold in nature
Characteristics
Roots are irregularly conical and cylindrical, 10–20 cm long, up to 2.5 cm thick, solitary or with several branches at the middle. The root crown bears a dried stem base surrounded by many brown, flattened dried leaf bases. Surface brown to dark brown with irregular longitudinal wrinkles and many nearly round rootlet scars. Hard texture; fracture yellow-brown and fibrous.
Slight odor, bitter taste.
Pharmacological Actions
1. Antitussive, expectorant and antiasthmatic effects
Decoctions of the root and decoctions after deproteinization, administered orally to mice, showed significant antitussive effects (ammonia aerosol cough model), although mice exhibited abdominal distension, diarrhea and piloerection. Emodin and chrysophanol isolated from the root both showed marked antitussive effects, with emodin stronger than chrysophanol; total anthraquinones had mild antitussive effect, while rhein showed no antitussive effect. Decoctions and deproteinized decoctions had expectorant effects (phenol red method in mice), while total anthraquinones showed no obvious effect. Total anthraquinones given orally to guinea pigs produced a marked antiasthmatic effect (histamine aerosol method), but emodin, rhein and chrysophanol were ineffective. A mixture of chrysophanol with the residue remaining after extraction of the above components produced an antiasthmatic effect in guinea pigs.
2. Antibacterial effects
Rhein, emodin and chrysophanol isolated from the root exhibited varying degrees of inhibition in vitro against Group A streptococcus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae and Moraxella catarrhalis. Whole-plant extracts inhibited Staphylococcus aureus and Escherichia coli. A root tincture showed significant inhibitory activity against Microsporum canis on Sabouraud agar, with a minimum effective concentration of 1.56–3.12%.
3. Antitumor effects
Six days after inoculation of mice thigh muscle with Sarcoma-37, a single subcutaneous injection of an alcoholic extract of Rumex crispus root produced observable tumor damage at 6–48 hours after dosing; the acidic extract may be more potent.
4. Other effects
The roots and rhizomes contain emodin and other anthraquinone derivatives, hence a laxative effect; they also contain a fairly high tannin content (3.62–6.42%), hence an astringent action; roots and rhizomes also contain vitamin B1 (up to 10.26 µg/g), and can be used as a stomachic and tonic. They also contain an irritant substance named rumicin (molecular formula C14H10O4), which acts as a rubefacient and dispersant and can kill skin parasites; however it can cause dermatitis and gastrointestinal disturbances in animals (sheep, horses).
Therapeutic Effects of Curly Dock (Niu'er Dahuang)
Actions
From the root of the Polygonaceae plant Curly Dock (Rumex crispus). Primarily treats acute and chronic hepatitis, enteritis, dysentery, chronic bronchitis, hematemesis, epistaxis, hematochezia, metrorrhagia, heat-bound constipation, carbuncles and toxic swellings, scabies, and alopecia.
Channels Entered
Heart; Liver; Large Intestine
Indications
Clears heat and detoxifies, cools the blood and stops bleeding, purges and kills parasites. Indicated for acute and chronic hepatitis, enteritis, dysentery, chronic bronchitis, hematemesis, epistaxis, hematochezia, metrorrhagia, heat-bound constipation, carbuncles and toxic swellings, scabies, and alopecia.
Dosage and Administration
Internal: decoction, 10–15 g.
External: appropriate amount, pounded and applied; or ground to powder and applied topically.
Precautions and Contraindications
Sichuan Materia Medica: contraindicated in diarrhea due to spleen deficiency.
Prescriptions Using Curly Dock (Niu'er Dahuang)
1) For scabies: 4 liang Curly Dock (Niu'er Dahuang), 2 liang Laojunxu; stew with pork femur and take. (Chongchuang Materia Medica)
2) For hematemesis: fresh Curly Dock (Niu'er Dahuang), stew with shakou meat and take. (Chongqing Materia Medica)
