Cultivation and Processing of Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao)
Source
It is the capitulum inflorescence with flower stalk of the plant Gu jing cao in the Eriocaulaceae family.
Harvested in August to September; pull out the flower stalks, remove mud and impurities, and sun‑dry.
Properties and Flavor
Pungent and sweet, cold.
Appearance
A capitate inflorescence with the flower stalk. The inflorescence is flattened and round, 4–5 millimeters in diameter; at the base there are scaly pale‑yellow involucre bracts, tightly arranged in a disk; 30–40 small flowers, gray‑white, very densely arranged, surface covered with a white fine powder; when rubbed by hand, many black granules and gray‑green small seeds can be seen. The inflorescence is attached to a slender flower stalk about 15–18 centimeters long, yellow‑green, glossy; texture soft, not easily broken. Odorless, mild taste, becomes adhesive after prolonged chewing. Best quality is when the capitula are pearl‑sized and compact, gray‑white, with short, yellow‑green stalks, and free of roots, leaves and impurities.
Effects of Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao)
Functions
It is the capitulum inflorescence with flower stalk of the plant Gu jing cao in the Eriocaulaceae family.
Main indications: ocular opacities, night blindness, headache, toothache, throat obstruction, and epistaxis.
Channels Entered
Enters the Liver and Stomach channels.
Actions
Expels wind and disperses heat, brightens the eyes and reduces ocular opacities. Treats ocular opacities, night blindness, headache, toothache, throat obstruction and epistaxis.
① Kaibao Materia Medica: Mainly treats throat obstruction, tooth wind pain, and various ulcers and scabies.
② Compendium of Materia Medica: Treats headache, eye blindness and ocular films, post‑smallpox films, and stops bleeding.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decocted as a decoction, 9–12 grams; or charred into pills or powders.
External use: burn to preserve the ash, grind to a powder and sprinkle.
Precautions and Contraindications
① Ben Cao Shu: Avoid with iron.
② Depei Ben Cao: Contraindicated for eye disorders due to blood deficiency.
Formulas Using Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao)
① For wind‑heat ocular film or unclear vision at night: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 1–2 liang, duck liver 1–2 pieces (if no duck liver use white tofu). Add appropriate boiled water and stew for one hour; take after meals, once daily. (Fujian Folk Herbal Remedies)
② For ocular films: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) and Saposhnikovia root (Fang Feng) in equal parts. Grind to powder and take with rice water. (Compendium of Materia Medica)
③ For infant ocular films after smallpox: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 1 liang, raw clam shell powder 0.5 fen, black soybean skin 2 qian; add White Peony (Bai Shao) 3 qian (stir‑fry briefly with wine). Grind to fine powder. Take one leaf of pig liver, slice thin with a bamboo knife, fill with the powdered medicine, tie with grass string, place in a ceramic vessel with water and cook slowly until done; give the child to eat at any time, including the cooking juices, once or twice a month until cured. (Shengsheng Zhongmiao Fang)
④ For children with night blindness suddenly not seeing at night: one whole he‑goat liver, do not wash with water, split open with a bamboo knife, insert a pinch of Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao), cook in an earthenware pot until done, give to eat daily. Avoid iron utensils. If the child refuses to eat, roast and crush into pills the size of mung beans, take thirty pills each dose with tea. (Weisheng Jiabao Fang)
⑤ For unilateral or bilateral headache: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 1 liang, grind to powder, mix with wheat flour to make a paste on xuan paper as a plaster, apply to the painful area; replace when dry. (Yao Sengtan Ji Yan Fang)
⑥ For cerebrovascular headache: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) powder, verdigris (Tong Lu) powder each 1 qian, Mirabilite (Mang Xiao) 0.5 qian (powder). Grind and mix; place a pinch in the nostril and blow into the nasal cavity, or for unilateral headache blow into the nostril on the affected side. (Shengji Zonglu — Pipewort Flower Powder)
⑦ For dental wind sores, exposed gums: Burn Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) to ash 1 fen, Alum (Bai Fan) ash 1 fen, Toad venom (Chan Su) one piece (roasted), a little Musk (She Xiang). Grind together to a powder; take a small amount and apply to the affected area. (Sheng Hui Fang — Pipewort Flower Powder)
⑧ For epistaxis that will not stop all day with vexation: Pound Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) into a powder, mix two qian with hot wheat soup and take internally. (Sheng Hui Fang)
⑨ For infantile liver heat with hot palms and soles: whole Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) plant 2–3 liang, pig liver 2 liang. Add boiled water and stew for one hour; take once or twice daily. (Fujian Folk Herbal Remedies)
⑩ For wind‑toxin red eyes, whether old or new: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) remove roots 1 liang, wash clean with well‑spring water, grind to 0.5 liang; roasted fermented soybeans (douchi) dried 0.5 liang; well moss roasted and dried 0.5 liang. Pound these four ingredients into a fine powder. Each dose two qian, take on an empty stomach, mix with well water and take. (Shengji Zonglu)
⑾ For red swollen painful eyes: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 15 g, Shepherd’s‑purse (Jie Cai) 15 g, Wedelia/Small‑leaf ground tea (Ai Di Cha) 15 g. Decoct in water and take. (Hunan Materia Medica)
⑿ For infantile ocular gary with redness and itching: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 1 liang, Atractylodes (Cang Zhu) 0.1 liang peeled and scraped and lightly roasted, Cicada slough ash 0.1 liang, talc powder 0.1 liang. Grind to a fine powder; each dose one qian. (Puji Fang)
⒀ For post‑smallpox eye films or itchy tearing: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 1 liang, raw clam shell powder 2 liang, grind to powder. Take one leaf of boar liver, slice thin with a bamboo knife, pack in the powder, tie with ramie thread, place in a ceramic vessel with water, cook slowly until done, and give the child to eat. (Weisheng Yijian Fang)
⒁ For scaly eruptions with ocular films: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) and pork trotter (stir‑fried) and Mung bean skin and Cicada slough in equal parts. Grind to powder; each dose three qian, take after meals with rice‑soaked water. (Zheng Zhi Zhunsheng)
⒂ For infantile night blindness where the child cannot see at night: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 0.5 liang, Licorice (Gan Cao) 0.5 liang, lightly roasted to slight redness and scraped; Dried Ginger (Gan Jiang) 0.1 liang, scraped. Pound to powder, make with one liang of flour into a baked cake with the medicine placed in the center, warm slowly until cooked, give with good tea. Take one dose each morning; vision may return after three days. (Puji Fang)
⒃ For brain pain and brow pain: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 2 qian, Earthworm (Di Long) 3 qian, Frankincense (Ru Xiang) 1 qian. Grind to powder; take half a qian each time, burn in a smoking tube and let the smoke enter the nostrils left and right. (Ben Cao Shu)
⒄ For common cold with fever, headache, and pharyngitis: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 9–12 g, decoct in water and take. (Shaanxi‑Gansu‑Ningxia‑Qinghai Selected Herbs)
⒅ For severe head wind with eyes closed and extreme pain: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao), Earthworm (Di Long), Realgar (Xiong Huang) in equal parts. Grind to powder, wrap in plum‑red paper rolled into a tube, burn and inhale the smoke through the nose; the eyes will open, then take other medicines. (Danxi Zhaixuan)
⒆ For infantile heatstroke with vomiting and diarrhea: whole Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) plant 30–60 g, fish‑brain stone (Yu Nao Shi) 9–15 g. Decoct in water and take twice daily for several consecutive days until cured. (Quanzhou Materia Medica)
⒇ For malnutrition and accumulation: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) 15 g, Sigesbeckia (Wei Ling Cai) 15 g. Decoct in water and take. (Hunan Materia Medica)
(21) For various sequelae, persistent leukorrhea and difficult urinary dribbling: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) and pig bone marrow each 1 liang. Decoction in wine and take, adjusting to taste. (Wen Tang Ji Yan Fang)
(22) For urinary stones: Pipewort Flower (Gu jing cao) and Imperata cylindrica (Zhuo Rong Cao) each 30 g. Roast and take with water. (Guizhou Herbal Remedies)
