Cultivation and processing of Bai Xian Pi
Source
This product is the dried root-bark of the Rutaceae plant Bai Xian (Dictamnus dasycarpus Turcz.). The roots are dug in the spring and autumn seasons; remove soil and coarse bark, strip the root-bark, and dry.
Properties and taste
Bitter; Salty; Cold
Processing
Pick out impurities, remove coarse bark, wash clean, moisten slightly, slice, sun-dry or stir-fry with wine.
Appearance
The Bai Xian root-bark appears as rolled tubes, length 5–15 cm, diameter 1–2 cm, thickness 2–5 mm. The outer surface is gray-white or pale gray-yellow, with fine longitudinal wrinkles and fine root scars, often with raised granular small dots; the inner surface is off-white, with fine longitudinal lines. The texture is brittle; when broken dust flies, the fracture surface is uneven, slightly lamellate; when the outer layer is removed and held to the light, tiny sparkling spots are visible. It has a mutton-like odor and a slightly bitter taste. Preferred are large strips, thick-fleshed, coated gray-white, with separated fracture surfaces.
Actions of Bai Xian Pi
Functions
This product is the dried root-bark of Bai Xian, a Rutaceae plant.
Main indications are wind-heat sores and toxins, scabies, pruritic dermatitis, rheumatic painful obstruction, and jaundice.

Indications
Expels wind, dries dampness, clears heat, detoxifies. Treats wind-heat sores and toxins, scabies, pruritic dermatitis, rheumatic painful obstruction, and jaundice.
① Benjing: Treats wind in the head, jaundice, reversed coughing, urinary dribbling, swelling and pain in the female genital region, damp-bi causing muscle death with inability to flex or extend, to start or stop, or to walk.
② Bie Lu: Treats restless limbs, intermittent excessive abdominal heat, drinking water, desire to walk, loud crying, childhood convulsions, and postpartum residual pain.
③ Yao Xing Lun: Treats all hot-toxin winds, malignant wind, wind sores, scabies with red ulceration, brittle eyebrow and hair loss, spasmodic skin and muscle disorders, high fever with aversion to cold; chiefly resolves heat jaundice, alcohol jaundice, acute jaundice, grain-related jaundice, and labor-related jaundice.
④ Bingbu Shou Ji Fang: Treats lung cough.
⑤ Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao: Unblocks joints, benefits the nine orifices and the blood vessels, and treats all wind-bi with weakness and lassitude of tendons and bones; unblocks small-intestine water qi, seasonal epidemic diseases, headaches and eye pain. The root-bark is good; the flowers have the same functions.
⑥ Ben Cao Yuan Shi: Treats all scabs, malignant wind, scabies, yangmei, and various sores with heat toxins.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoct 6–15 g; or make into pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, decoct for wash or grind to powder and apply.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated in cases of deficiency-cold.
① Bencao Jing Jizhu: Incompatible with mantis egg-case (Piao Xiao), Platycodon root (Jie Geng), Poria (Fu Ling), and Smilax rhizome (Bi Xie).
② Bencao Jing Shu: Those with cold deficiency in the lower part of the body should not use, even if damp symptoms are present.
Formulas of Bai Xian Pi
① For wind-heat stored in the lungs, toxic qi attacking the skin causing pruritus, chest and diaphragm obstruction, occasional vexation: Bai Xian Pi (Bai Xian Pi), Siler root (Fang Feng) (remove lateral branches), Ginseng (Ren Shen), Anemarrhena rhizome (Zhi Mu) (roasted), Adenophora root (Nan Sha Shen) each one liang, Baical skullcap root (Huang Qin) (remove black core) three fen. Pound the above six into a powder. Each dose two qian, water one cup, decoct to six tenths, take warm, lie down after eating. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu Bai Xian Pi powder)
② For epileptic jaundice: Bai Xian Pi, Artemisia capillaris (Yin Chen), Artemisia (Hao) in equal parts. Boil with two zhong of water, take twice daily. (Shen Shi Zun Sheng Shu Bai Xian Pi decoction)
③ For plague lesions with cores and suppurating blood: Bai Xian Pi, boil and take one sheng. (Bu Que Zhou Hou Fang)
④ For postpartum stroke when the weak patient cannot take other medicines: Bai Xian Pi 3 liang. With 3 sheng of water, boil down to 1 sheng, divide and take. Those who tolerate alcohol may boil equal parts wine and water. (Xiao Pin Fang Yi Wu Bai Xian Tang)
⑤ For skin eczema and pruritus: Bai Xian Pi and Sophora root (Ku Shen) each 90 g, made into water pills. Each dose 6 g, twice daily, taken with warm water. Bai Xian Pi may also be used alone—appropriate amount decocted for external wash once or twice daily. (Qingdao Zhong Cao Yao Shou Ce)
⑥ For palmar eczema ('goose palm wind'): chew Bai Xian Pi in the mouth until mushy and rub on the hands. (Wan Shi Mi Chuan Wai Ke Xin Fa)
⑦ For acute hepatitis: Bai Xian Pi 9 g, Artemisia capillaris (Yin Chen) 15 g, Gardenia fruit (Zhi Zi) 9 g, Rhubarb (Da Huang) 9 g. Decoction. (Nei Meng Gu Zhong Cao Yao)
⑧ For postpartum wind-deficiency: Bai Xian Pi and Angelica pubescens root (Du Huo) each 3 liang. Coarsely powdered, wine and water each two cups, decoct to two cups, divide into three doses. Alternatively, Bai Xian Pi alone is also effective. (Wei Sheng Yi Jian Fang)
⑨ For traumatic bleeding: grind Bai Xian Pi into a fine powder and apply externally. (Ning Xia Zhong Cao Yao Shou Ce)
Remarks
In the Xinjiang region, the root-bark of the related species narrow-leaved Bai Xian (Xia Ye Bai Xian) is also used as Bai Xian in medicine.
