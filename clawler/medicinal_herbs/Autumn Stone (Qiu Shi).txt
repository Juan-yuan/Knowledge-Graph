Habitat and Distribution
Mainly produced in East China.
Nature and Taste
Salty, cold.
Processing
There are two types: Pale Qiushi (Dàn Qiūshí) and Saline Qiushi (Xián Qiūshí).
Pale Qiushi (Dàn Qiūshí): take washed and sun-dried renzhongbai (Rénzhōngbái), grind into powder, add Bletilla (Bái Jí) and starch water as auxiliary materials, mix, then press with a mold into small square blocks and sun-dry.
Saline Qiushi (Xián Qiūshí): take salt (Shíyán) and clean spring water, boil and filter, heat the filtrate to evaporate and dry into a powdery frost called Qiushi frost. Then place the Qiushi frost in a lidded porcelain bowl, calcine over a stove for two hours, cool and it will congeal into a solid block.
1. Ye Mengde (Ye Mengde), Shuiyun Lu (Shuǐ Yún Lù): Those who refine Qiushi in the world rely only on fire refinement; this medicine must undergo both yin and yang refinements to be supreme. Yang refinement method: use more than ten shi of human urine, each placed in wooden barrels; for each shi add one bowl of soap-pod (Zàojiá) juice, stir rapidly with a bamboo pole hundreds or thousands of times, wait for clarification, discard the clear liquid and keep the sediment, and make another barrel and stir until clear as before; take one or two dou of the concentrated juice, filter clean, put into a pot and simmer until dry, scrape off and pound finely, then dissolve with clear broth and boil, pour over a paper-lined winnowing tray and boil again, repeat several times until the color is white as snow, then use a sand box to consolidate and calcine with fire into substance, pour out; if the medicine is not yet formed, calcine one or two more times until the color is like translucent jade, grind finely, put into the sand box to compress, apply top heat and nurture for seven days and nights, take out and spread on earth to remove fire-toxins and make powder. Yin refinement method: use four or five shi of human urine in a large jar, add half new water, stir many times, let settle, discard the clear liquid and keep the sediment, add new water and stir and clarify again until there is no foul smell and the settled material is like greasy powder, then sun-dry, scrape off and grind again, mix with a boy's breast milk into a paste, sun-dry fiercely, repeat this nine times to make the powder.
2. Bencao Mengquan (Běncǎo Méngquán): In autumn collect children's urine; for each barrel of urine add seven qian of gypsum powder, stir twice with mulberry branches, after about half ke the essence gradually sinks to the bottom and the clear liquid with white floats on top; wait for clarification and pour off the liquid, then add fresh urine and mix as before, add powder and stir, pour off leaving the sediment—do not deviate. After all urine has been processed and the clear liquid poured off, then add one barrel of autumn-dew water and stir with a mulberry branch, let stand and pour off again; repeat several times to wash away dregs and impurities and remove the salty foul taste. When finished, seal heavily with paper and wait until dry and firmly congealed, then take out whole. The light, clear essence will float and congeal on the surface and be white in quality. The original gypsum powder and other heavy turbid dregs will sink and gather at the bottom, being blackish and dull; keep the surface portion and scrape off and discard the bottom. If instead one seals in a jar and gently heats for half a ke, although the color becomes very white the nature becomes warm and it is ultimately inferior to sun-dried material.
3. Benjing Fengyuan (Běnjīng Fèngyuán): Yin collection method for Qiushi: form dozens of lead balls the size of a lead pellet, each made of two pieces pressed together with many drilled holes, place them into the urine barrels to soak, each day pour off the overnight urine and replace with fresh urine to continue soaking; at the autumn harvest collect them and store in a lead container. Yang refining of Qiushi: take hundreds of pairs of straw sandals, the older the better, wash and sun-bleach in running water for seven days to remove yellowing, then soak them in urine barrels, sun-expose by day and soak by night for about a month, dry in the sun, burn to ash in strong sun and stir frequently to burn thoroughly, pour boiling water to leach and clarify for several days, dry the pot, add rainwater to dissolve and cook, filter through layers of bamboo sieves and paper to clarify and repeat for over half a month, then boil dry in a silver can until the color is white as frost; collect into lead containers. Another yang refinement method: use a boy's urine, boil in a pot until dry; the pot should first be heated red-hot and greased with fragrant oil and washed so the residue will not stick and damage the pot, initially like oil residue, place into a yang-cheng can or small iron cauldron, calcine to red, dissolve with hot water and place into a spouted pot, cover the spout with straw and slowly pour out, underlay with a bamboo sieve lined with paper to filter clear, then dry over gentle fire and collect into lead containers, after which it will not dissolve.
Characteristics
A powdery aggregate. Appears as small square blocks or flattened round shapes, some often stamped with red characters indicating Pale Qiushi, diameter 1.5–2.2 cm. White or gray-white, surface flat but not smooth; dull, opaque. Hard and brittle, easily crushed, fracture powdery and uneven. Slight odor, mild taste. Insoluble in water. The best specimens are intact blocks, dry and without salty or foul odor. Pale Qiushi (as described in Benjing Fengyuan) is often gray-white or light-red small square blocks with a not-very-smooth, dull surface; hard and brittle; mild taste. The best are intact, dry and without salty or foul odor.
Effects of Qiushi (Qiūshí)
Functions
Is a processed product of renzhongbai (Rénzhōngbái) and salt (Shíyán).
Main indications: bone-steaming consumptive fever, cough, coughing blood, swollen and painful throat, dysphagia with regurgitation, seminal emission, leukorrhea, turbid urine with dripping, and female red or white leukorrhea.
Channels
Enters the Lung meridian and Kidney meridian.
Indications
Nourishes yin and reduces fire. Treats bone-steaming consumptive fever, cough, coughing blood, swollen and painful throat, dysphagia with regurgitation, seminal emission, leukorrhea, turbid dripping urination, and female red or white leukorrhea.
1. Bencao Mengquan (Běncǎo Méngquán): nourishes kidney water, nourishes the dantian (dān tián), calms and harmonizes the five viscera, moistens the triple burner, reduces cough with thick reverse phlegm, dispels the pathogenic heat of bone-steaming, softens accumulations and hard masses, brightens the eyes and clarifies the heart.
2. Yixue Rumun (Yīxué Rùmén, Medical Introduction): treats emaciation with chronic cough, dimmed vision and dizziness, abdominal distension and fullness with panting, soreness of the loins and knees, seminal emission and leukorrhea.
3. Gangmu (Gāngmù, Compendium): treats deficiency consumptive cold disorders, frequent and involuntary urination.
4. Bencao Beiyao (Běncǎo Bèiyào): nourishes yin and reduces fire.
5. Benjing Fengyuan (Běnjīng Fèngyuán): can nourish yin and reduce fire without harming the stomach, tonify the original fire of the lower source, disperse blood stasis, assist yin essence, suppress pathogenic fire and return true yang, stop deficiency-heat cough with blood, and treat bone-steaming consumptive disease.
6. Modern Practical Chinese Medicine: for internal use mostly employ Pale Qiushi (Dàn Qiūshí) for chronic oral and laryngeal inflammations; Saline Qiushi (Xián Qiūshí) is more often used externally for oral and pharyngeal ulcers.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: in pills or powders, 4.5–9 g.
External use: grind to powder and sprinkle.
Cautions
Contraindicated in those with spleen and stomach deficiency-cold.
Qiushi (Qiūshí) Formulas
1. For male and female deficiency and emaciation: Qiushi (Qiūshí) 1 liang, dried Shanyao (Shānyào) 1 liang. Grind to powder; separately mix Shanyao with wine into a paste, form pills the size of a wutong seed and coat with dried Shanyao. Take twenty pills with warm wine or rice gruel as desired. (Hong’s Collected Tested Formulas)
2. To tonify kidney water and treat deficiency: True Qiushi (Zhēn Qiūshí) 10 liang, white Poria (Bái Fúlíng) 4 liang, lotus seed (Liánzǐ) 4 liang, Shanyao (Shānyào) 4 liang, fennel seed (Xiǎo Huí Xiāng) 2 liang. Make into wine pills and take with plain rice decoction on an empty stomach. For women add raw Rehmannia (Shēng Dìhuáng) 2 liang, prepared Rehmannia (Shú Dìhuáng) 4 liang, Chuanxiong (Chuānxiōng) 3 liang, and use jujube (Dàzǎo) flesh to form the pills. (He’s Jisheng Lun, Qiushi Dan)
3. For excessive thinking or sexual indulgence damaging heart qi, causing seminal emission and frequent urination: Qiushi (Qiūshí) and white Poria (Bái Fúlíng) each 4 liang, lotus seed (Liánzǐ) and gorgon seed (Qiànshí) each 2 liang. Powder, steam with jujube flesh and form pills the size of a wutong seed. Take thirty pills with saline broth on an empty stomach. (Yonglei Qianfang, Qiushi Four-Essence Pills)
4. For leukorrhea and seminal emission: Qiushi (Qiūshí) 1 liang, white Poria (Bái Fúlíng) 5 qian, Cuscuta (Tǔsīzǐ, fried) 5 qian. Powder, use one cup of boiling decoction and one cup of well water to cook to a paste and form pills the size of a wutong seed. Take one hundred pills with saline broth. (Zheng Family Heirloom Formula, Qiushi Jiaogan Dan)
5. For turbid qi separating clear, essence scattering and forming dripping turbid urine with yellow, white or dark red like fat or honey oil: Qiushi (Qiūshí), deer antler glue (Lùjiǎojiāo, fried), mantis egg case (Sāng Piāoxiāo, roasted) each half liang, white Poria (Bái Fúlíng) 1 liang. Powder, make into paste and form pills the size of a wutong seed. Take fifty pills with ginseng (Rénshēn) soup. (Renzhai Zhizhi Fang, Qiushi Pills)
6. For red or white leukorrhea: true Qiushi (Zhēn Qiūshí) ground to powder, steam and mash jujube (Dàzǎo) flesh and form pills the size of a wutong seed. Take sixty pills with vinegar decoction on an empty stomach. (Zhaiyuan Fang)
7. For dysphagia with regurgitation: Qiushi (Qiūshí), each dose one qian, take with plain broth. (Yifang Zhaiyao)
