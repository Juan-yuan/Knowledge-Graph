Cultivation and Processing of Creeping Gentian (Qing Yu Dan Cao)
Source
The whole herb of the Gentianaceae plant Creeping Gentian (Man Long Dan). Harvested after autumn.
Original Morphology
Perennial, prostrate herb. Stems slender. Leaves opposite, papery, ovate‑lanceolate, 4–7 cm long, 1.2–2.3 cm wide, apex gradually acuminate, base rounded, margins entire, dark green; three main veins prominent. Flowers axillary; calyx tubular, 5‑lobed, lobes triangular‑lanceolate; corolla campanulate, 5‑lobed, pale purple, with secondary lobes between the main lobes. Berry subglobose, about 1.5 cm long, red‑purple, with a long stalk extending beyond the persistent calyx. Flowering in autumn.
Properties and Taste
Pungent; bitter; cool
Effects of Creeping Gentian (Qing Yu Dan Cao)
Functions
The whole herb of the Gentianaceae plant Creeping Gentian (Man Long Dan). Mainly used for wind‑heat cough, jaundice, rheumatic painful obstruction, and roundworm infections.
Channels Entered
Lung; Liver; Spleen channels
Main Indications
Disperses wind and clears heat, strengthens the spleen and drains dampness, kills parasites. Indicated for wind‑heat cough; jaundice; rheumatic painful obstruction; and roundworm infections.
Usage and Dosage
Internal: decoction, 15–30 g; or infused in alcohol; or cooked into congee.
External: appropriate amount, decocted in water for fumigation and washing.
Formulas Using Creeping Gentian (Qing Yu Dan Cao)
1. For jaundice: Creeping Gentian root (Qing Yu Dan Gen) three liang, decoct and drink.
2. For wind‑heat cough: fresh Creeping Gentian (Qing Yu Dan Cao) one to two liang, stewed with pork and eaten.
3. For rheumatism: Creeping Gentian root (Qing Yu Dan Gen) five liang, soaked in wine and taken; alternatively the vine may be decocted for fumigation and washing.
4. To expel roundworms: Creeping Gentian (Qing Yu Dan Cao) five qian,
Solomon's Seal (Yu Zhu)
three qian,
Rice (Da Mi)
one handful. Cook into a thin porridge and consume in two divided doses.
