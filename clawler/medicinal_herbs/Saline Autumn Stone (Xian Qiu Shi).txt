Saline Autumn Stone (Xian Qiu Shi)

Cultivation and Processing of Xianqiushi (Xian Qiu Shi)
Source
An artificial calcination product of edible salt.
Habitat and Distribution
Mainly produced in Tongcheng, Anhui. Sold in Fujian, Shanghai, Hankou, Guangzhou, Tianjin and other places.
Nature and Taste
Salty; cold; non-toxic.
Processing
Take edible salt, add clean spring water and decoct, filter; heat the filtrate to evaporate, dry into a powdery frost; then place the powdery frost in a lidded porcelain bowl and calcine over a furnace for 2 hours; after cooling it solidifies into a block-like solid.
Description
Consists of aggregates of fine grains. Intact pieces are basin-shaped or steamed-bun-shaped; the upper end is flat, the middle slightly concave, the lower end semicircular, diameter 6–7 cm. Surface smooth, white or pale yellow-white, lustrous; opaque. Relatively heavy, hard and brittle, easily crushed. The fracture surface shows irregular crystals with a glassy luster. Hygroscopic. Slight odor, salty taste. Readily soluble in water. The best specimens are whole blocks that are white, lustrous, and salty.
Effects of Xianqiushi (Xian Qiu Shi)
Efficacy
An artificial calcination product of edible salt. Mainly treats steaming-bone disorder and consumptive fever, deficiency cough, seminal emissions, red or white leukorrhea, summer-heat irritability, mouth ulcers, and throat swelling and pain.
Channels
Heart; Kidney; Lung meridians.
Main Indications
Nourishes yin and astringes essence, clears the heart and reduces fire. Mainly for steaming-bone disorder and consumptive fever, deficiency cough, seminal emissions, red and white leukorrhea, summer-heat irritability, mouth ulcers, and throat swelling and pain.
Dosage and Administration
Internal: grind to a powder, 1.5–4.5 g each time, twice daily; or included in pills or powders.
External: appropriate amount, grind to a powder and insufflate into the throat.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in patients with edema. After edema subsides, this product can replace edible salt. Do not exceed 2 g per day.
1. Baoqing Materia Medica (Zhezhong): In cases of edema, thirst and cough, taking additional salt will instead greatly aggravate them.
2. Nan Yao "Chinese Herbal Medicine": Serves as a substitute for edible salt in patients with edema, but should not be used in excess.
