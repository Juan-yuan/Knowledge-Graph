Cultivation and Preparation of Herba Pterocephali (Yi Shou Cao)
Source
This herb is a commonly used Tibetan medicinal material; it is the root or whole herb of the Dipsacaceae plant Pterocephalus with spatulate leaves. It is harvested in late summer to early autumn, impurities are removed, and it is air-dried.
Morphology
Pterocephalus with spatulate leaves is a perennial acaulescent herb, 30–50 cm tall. The whole plant is covered with white soft hairs. Roots thick, woody, nearly conical. Leaves all basal, forming a rosette; leaf blades oblanceolate, 5–18 cm long and 1–2.5 cm wide, apex obtuse or acute, base gradually narrowed into a winged petiole, margins entire or once pinnately deeply divided, lobes 3–5 pairs, obliquely ovate or lanceolate, 1–2 cm long, terminal lobe larger and lanceolate. Midrib conspicuous and white on the underside, lateral veins not obvious; upper surface green, sparsely covered with white coarse appressed hairs; underside gray-green, densely covered with coarse stiff hairs, denser on both sides of the midrib; margins with long marginal hairs. Flowering stems arise from the leaf rosette, 10–35 cm tall, leafless. Capitulum solitary at the stem apex, up to 3 cm in diameter; involucre in 2–3 series, oblong-ovate and pubescent, margins with long marginal hairs; bracts linear-spatulate, up to 12 mm long with a slender claw at the base; calyx completely divided into about 20 soft featherlike awns; corolla white to pale purple, tubular-funnelform, 12–15 mm long, apex shallowly 5-lobed with the uppermost lobe larger and the lowest pair smallest; stamens 4, slightly exserted from the corolla tube, anthers black-purple; ovary inferior, enclosed in a cup-shaped long-hairy involucre. Achenes 3–5 mm long, obovate, light brown, with 8 longitudinal ribs and sparse appressed hairs, with about 20 brown persistent calyx spines approximately 10 mm long, covered with white feathery hairs. Flowering and fruiting from July to October.
Habitat and Distribution
Ecological environment: grows on grasslands, roadsides and in rock crevices at altitudes of 1800–4800 m.
Resource distribution: found in Qinghai, Sichuan, Yunnan and Tibet.
Nature and Taste
Bitter and cold; slightly toxic.
Processing
Remove impurities, wash, cut into sections, and dry.
Characteristics
The roots are subrounded, 5–20 cm long and 0.8–2.5 cm in diameter; surface brownish or dark brown with twisted longitudinal wrinkles and yellow-white dotlike root hair scars, periderm easily peeled; apex often with several helix-twisted rhizome clusters, some upper parts densely covered with brown leaf petiole remnants. Light in weight, brittle, breaks easily, fracture uneven, wood white. Basal leaves gray-green and mostly fragmented; intact leaves lanceolate to elongate-elliptic, margins entire, base often shallowly to moderately pinnately divided, both surfaces covered with coarse hairs. Flowering stems hairy, capitulum nearly spherical, 0.8–2.5 cm in diameter; flowers white to pale yellow, calyx featherlike and numerous. Odor slight, taste bitter.
Actions of Herba Pterocephali (Yi Shou Cao)
Functions
It is the root or whole herb of the Dipsacaceae plant Pterocephalus with spatulate leaves. It is mainly used for externally contracted fever, vexation in febrile disease, diarrhea and dysentery, and damp-heat Bi syndrome.
Indications
Clears heat and detoxifies, expels wind and eliminates dampness, and relieves pain. Indicated for externally contracted fever, vexation in febrile disease, diarrhea and dysentery, and damp-heat Bi syndrome.
Usage and Dosage
Oral administration: decoction, 3–9 g.
