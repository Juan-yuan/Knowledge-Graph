Cultivation and Processing of Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi)
Source
It is the fruit of plants of the Verbenaceae family, single-leaf Man Jing (Dan Ye Man Jing) or Man Jing (Man Jing).
Harvest when the fruits mature in autumn.
Sun-dry, remove impurities, store in a dry place, prevent dampness and mildew.
Properties and Flavors
Taste: pungent; bitter; nature: slightly cold
Processing
1. Stir-fried Man Jing Zi (Chao Man Jing Zi): sift clean of ash and debris, remove any remaining calyx lobes, place in a pan and stir-fry over strong fire until scorched yellow, lightly spray with water, cool.
2. Lei Gong Pao Zhi Lun (Lei Gong’s Treatise on Processing): when preparing Man Jing Zi, remove the pedicel and the lower white membrane, soak in wine for one day, then steam from the Si hour to the Wei hour, take out and sun-dry for use.
Description
Dried fruits spherical, diameter 4–6 millimeters. Surface gray-black or dark brown, covered with gray-white bloom, with four longitudinal grooves; under magnification densely dotted with pale yellow small spots. Base has a membranous persistent calyx and small fruit stalk; the persistent calyx envelops one-third to two-thirds of the fruit, edges five-toothed, often deeply split into two lobes, gray-white, densely covered with fine soft hairs. Light in weight, tough in texture, not easily broken; transverse section shows pericarp gray-yellow with brownish oil spots; internally divided into four chambers, each chamber containing one seed; seed kernel white and oily. Smell peculiar and aromatic, taste slightly pungent. Best quality: large, full, aromatic, without impurities.
Actions and Effects of Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi)
Functions
It is the fruit of plants of the Verbenaceae family, single-leaf Man Jing (Dan Ye Man Jing) or Man Jing (Man Jing).
Main indications: wind-heat common cold, general and unilateral headache, toothache, red eyes, internal eye pain, dim vision with excessive tearing, damp-bi syndrome with contraction and cramping.
Channels Entered
Liver channel; Stomach channel; Bladder channel
Indications
Disperses wind-heat and clears and benefits the head and eyes. Treats wind-heat common cold, general and unilateral headache, toothache, red eyes, internal eye pain, dim vision with excessive tearing, damp-bi with contraction and cramping.
① Ben Jing (Classic of Materia Medica): treats cold and heat between the muscles and bones, damp-bi with contraction and cramping, brightens the eyes, strengthens the teeth, opens the nine orifices, expels white parasites.
② Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu (Collected Annotations to the Materia Medica): treats baldness and falling hair.
③ Bie Lu (Alternate Records): expels long parasites, treats wind headache, tinnitus, tearing of the eyes; tonifies qi, makes one’s complexion glossy and lustrous.
④ Yao Xing Lun (On the Nature of Medicinals): treats pathogenic wind, can promote growth of beard and hair.
⑤ Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao (Ri Hua Zi’s Materia Medica): benefits the joints, treats red eyes and convulsive disorders.
⑥ Zhen Zhu Nang (Pearl Pouch): cools channel blood, stops headache, treats internal eye pain.
⑦ Wang Haogu: dispels Liver wind.
⑧ Yi Lin Zuan Yao (Essentials of Medical Forest): disperses heat, expels wind, and also dries dampness.
⑨ Guangxi Chinese Materia Medica Records: treats stomach pain.
⑩ Guangzhou Military Commonly Used Chinese Herbs Manual: disperses wind and clears heat, pacifies the Liver and relieves pain. Treats colds with fever, eye pain and dizziness, wind-damp musculoskeletal pain, indigestion, enteritis with diarrhea, traumatic swelling and pain.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoct, 6–10 g; or soak in wine; or include in pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, decoction for external wash.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in cases of headache and dizziness due to blood deficiency with internal heat and in those with stomach deficiency.
① Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu (Collected Annotations to the Materia Medica): incompatible with Aconite (Wu Tou) and Gypsum (Shi Gao).
② Yi Xue Qi Yuan (Origins of Medicine): persons with weak stomach should not take it, for fear of producing phlegm.
③ Ben Cao Jing Shu (Commentary on the Materia Medica): headache and eye pain not due to wind but due to blood deficiency with internal heat are contraindicated.
④ Ben Cao Hui Yan (Collected Sayings on Materia Medica): if weakness, numbness and cramping are not due to wind-damp but due to yang deficiency, blood depletion and tendons failing, do not use; cold hernia or beriberi not due to external yin-damp but due to liver and spleen frailty, also do not use.
Formulas Using Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi)

② For wind-cold invading the eyes with swelling, pain, tearing, dryness and blurred vision: Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi) 3 qian; Schizonepeta (Jing Jie) and Tribulus (Bai Ji Li) each 2 qian; Bupleurum (Chai Hu) and Saposhnikovia (Fang Feng) each 1 qian; Licorice (Gan Cao) 5 fen. Decoction with water. (Ben Cao Hui Yan)
③ For eye disorders due to overwork and irregular diet: Astragalus (Huang Qi) and Ginseng (Ren Shen) each 1 liang; honey-fried Licorice (Zhi Gan Cao) 8 qian; Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi) 2.5 qian; Phellodendron (Huang Bai) 3 qian (stir-fried with wine four times); White Peony (Bai Shao Yao) 3 qian. Chew and masticate the above, take 3–5 qian per dose, decoct in water. (Lan Shi Mi Cang, Man Jing Zi Decoction)
④ For cold and headache due to common cold: Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi), Perilla leaf (Zi Su Ye), Mint (Bo He), Angelica dahurica (Bai Zhi), Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua) each 9 g. Decoction with water. (National Compendium of Chinese Herbs)
⑤ For external wind-heat headache and eye pain, and neurogenic headache: Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi) 9 g; Mulberry leaf (Sang Ye), Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua), Mint (Bo He), Angelica dahurica (Bai Zhi), Schizonepeta seed (Jing Jie Zi) each 9 g. Decoction with water. (Hunan Materia Medica Records)
⑥ For exuberant lung-heat with copious phlegm, wheeze and urgent panting: Man Jing Shi (white skin removed) (Man Jing Shi), Rhubarb (Da Huang, scraped), Clematis (Wei Ling Xian, cleaned of soil), Gastrodia (Tian Ma) each 1 liang. ⑦ For unilateral headache: Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi) 10 g, Sweet chrysanthemum (Gan Ju Hua) 8 g, Ligusticum (Chuan Xiong) 4 g, Asarum (Xi Xin) 3 g, Licorice (Gan Cao) 4 g, Angelica dahurica (Bai Zhi) 3 g. Boil 500 ml water down to 200 ml, take three times daily. (Modern Practical Chinese Herbs)
⑧ For hypertension with dizziness: Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi) 9 g, Wild chrysanthemum (Ye Ju Hua), Uncaria (Gou Teng), Cassia seed (Jue Ming Zi) each 12 g. Decoction with water. (Hunan Materia Medica Records)
⑨ For wind-toxin affecting the eyes with redness, swelling, itching and pain: Coptis (Huang Lian) and Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi) each half liang, Schisandra (Wu Wei Zi) 2 qian. Grind finely, divide into three portions, boil fresh water, strain clear juice and use to wash by hand; effective. (Yin Hai Jing Wei, Wind-clearing Eye Wash)
⑩ For corneal opacity: Single-leaf Man Jing fruit (Dan Ye Man Jing) 15 g, Abalone shell (Shi Jue Ming) 9 g, Horsetail (Mu Zei) 6 g. Decoction with water. (Fujian Materia Medica Records)
⑾ For deficiency-related dim vision: take Man Jing Zi (Man Jing Zi) one sheng, boil in nine sheng of water until juice is exhausted, take out and sun-dry; repeat twice, then grind into powder. Each dose two qian mixed with warm water, two to three times daily. (Pu Ji Fang)
⑿ For otitis media: Single-leaf Man Jing (Dan Ye Man Jing) and Mahonia leaves (Shi Da Gong Lao Ye) each 15 g, Xanthium (Cang Er Zi) 9 g. Decoction with water. (Fujian Materia Medica Records)
⒀ For deafness: take Man Jing Zi slightly fried one sheng, soak in wine two sheng, in cold season seven days, in hot season three days, then decant and discard dregs. Drink as desired; even long-standing deafness will be cured. (Pu Ji Fang, Man Jing Wine)
⒁ For early-stage breast abscess: Man Jing Zi 1 liang 2 qian, fry and grind to powder. Boil wine and water each one bowl to one bowl, take half bowl and apply the residue to the affected area. (Ben Cao Hui Yan)
⒂ For postpartum poor milk flow with retention and hot swelling: Man Jing Shi (calcined to preserve nature) and Gleditsia spine (Zao Jiao Ci, calcined to preserve nature) each 1 liang. ⒄ For sudden urinary retention during pregnancy: Man Jing Zi 2 liang. Pound into a fine powder, take one qian mixed in decocted scallion white soup, take at any time. (Pu Ji Fang)
