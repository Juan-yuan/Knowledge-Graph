Cultivation and processing of Phellodendron bark
Source
This item is the dried bark of plants of the Rutaceae family: Phellodendron species or the Huangpi tree. The former is commonly called Sichuan Phellodendron, the latter commonly called Guan Huang Bai. After peeling the bark, remove the rough outer bark and dry in the sun.
[Macroscopic morphology]
1. Phellodendron
Also called: Huangboluo, Huangbai li. A deciduous tree, 10–25 meters high; the outer layer of the bark is gray with a rather thick cork layer, the surface has longitudinal fissures, the inner bark is bright yellow. Twigs are usually gray-brown or light brown, rarely red-orange. Leaves opposite, imparipinnate, 5–13 leaflets; leaflet petioles short; leaflets oblong-lanceolate, oblong-ovate-lanceolate or nearly ovate, 5–11 cm long, 2–3.8 cm wide, apex gradually acuminate, base usually unequal broadly cuneate or nearly rounded, margins with fine rounded teeth or nearly entire, often ciliate; upper surface dark green, young leaves with soft hairs along the veins, older leaves glabrous. Lower surface pale, young leaves with soft hairs along the veins, older leaves only with long white hairs at the base of the midrib. Inflorescences paniculate, axis and branches hairy when young; flowers unisexual, dioecious, small; calyx 5, ovate; petals 5, oblong, yellow-green; male flowers with 5 stamens protruding beyond the petals, filaments hairy at the base; in female flowers the abortive stamens are scale-like, pistil 1, ovary superior, very short style, capitate stigma, 5-lobed. Drupes globose, about 8–10 mm in diameter, purple-black when ripe, usually 5-seeded. Flowering May–June. Fruiting September–October. Grows in mixed broadleaf forests on mountains or near mountain valleys and torrents. Distributed in Northeast China and North China.
2. Huangpi tree
Also called: Huipi bai, Hua Huang Bai. A deciduous tree, 10–12 meters high. Outer bark gray-brown, very thin, without a thickened cork layer, inner layer yellow; twigs usually dark red-brown or purple-brown, smooth and glabrous. Leaves opposite; imparipinnate, 7–15 leaflets with short petioles; leaflets oblong-lanceolate to oblong-ovate, 9–14 cm long, 3–5 cm wide, apex gradually acuminate, base broadly cuneate or nearly rounded, usually unequal on both sides, upper surface dark green with only the midrib hairy, lower surface light green with long soft hairs. Inflorescences paniculate, axis and branches densely short-hairy; flowers unisexual, dioecious; calyx 5, ovate; petals 6, oblong; male flowers with 6 stamens greatly exceeding the petals, filaments very long, base with long white soft hairs; abortive stamens in female flowers small, pistil 1, ovary superior, 5 chambers, style short; stigma 5-lobed. Drupes globose, 1–1.2 cm in diameter, densely clustered, purple-black when ripe, usually 5-seeded. Flowering May–June. Fruit ripens in October. Grows in mixed broadleaf forests on mountain slopes and ravines. Distributed in Sichuan, Hubei, Guizhou, Yunnan, Jiangxi, Zhejiang and other regions.
Properties and flavor
Bitter, cold.
Character
1. Eastern Phellodendron
Also called: Guan Bai, Guan Huang Bai. The dried bark of the plant Phellodendron. Appears as slightly curved plate-like pieces, edges irregular, varying length and width, thickness about 2–4 mm. Cork layer may be retained or removed; cork relatively thick, surface grayish-white; where cork is removed, surface brown-yellow, flat or wrinkled with lenticels; inner surface gray-yellow. Texture relatively loose, brittle, fracture fibrous, pale yellow slightly greenish. Smell faint, taste bitter. Powder becomes sticky when mixed with water and stains the water yellow. Best quality when pieces are large and thick, fresh yellow, without cork. Main production areas: Liaoning, Jilin, Hebei. Also produced in Heilongjiang and Inner Mongolia. Liaoning produces the most.
2. Sichuan Phellodendron (from Depei Bencao)
Dried bark of the Huangpi tree and its variants. Slightly curved plate-like pieces, edges irregular, varying length and width, thickness about 3–5 mm, cork mostly removed. Outer surface deep yellow, relatively flat, with longitudinal ridges and brown lenticels; inner surface grayish-yellow or yellow. Texture hard and light, brittle, fracture fibrous, splits in flakes, fresh yellow. Smell faint, taste bitter, chewed it is mucilaginous and can stain water yellow. Best when pieces are large, thick, fresh yellow, without cork. Main production areas: Sichuan, Guizhou, Hubei, Yunnan. Also in Hunan, Gansu, Guangxi. Sichuan and Guizhou produce the most.
Effects of Phellodendron bark
Efficacy
This item is the dried bark of Phellodendron species of the Rutaceae family or the Huangpi tree. Main indications: dysenteric disorders from heat, diarrhea, excessive thirst (Xiaoke), jaundice, flaccid paralysis, nocturnal emission, turbid painful urination, hemorrhoids, bloody stools, leucorrhea, steaming bone disorders with night fever, red painful eyes, mouth and tongue sores, carbuncles and toxic swellings.
Channels entered
Enters the Kidney meridian and the Bladder meridian.
Main indications
Clears heat. Dries dampness, drains fire, detoxifies. Treats dysenteric disorders from heat, diarrhea, excessive thirst (Xiaoke), jaundice, flaccid paralysis, nocturnal emission, turbid painful urination, hemorrhoids, bloody stools, leucorrhea, steaming bone disorders with night fever, red painful eyes, mouth and tongue sores, carbuncles and toxic swellings.
1. Benjing: treats accumulative heat in the five zang and intestines and stomach, jaundice, intestinal hemorrhoids; stops dysenteric diarrhea, female abnormal uterine bleeding of red or white, sores of the genitals.
2. Bie Lu: cures fright qi between the skin, heat and redness of the flesh, red painful eyes, mouth sores.
3. Yaoxing Lun: treats male impotence. Treats rectal bleeding like pieces of duck or chicken liver; and sores on the male organ, apply powdered herb.
4. Bencao Shiyi: treats heat sores and eruptions, worm sores, dysentery, bleeding, kills insects; decocted for internal use treats excessive thirst.
5. Rihuazi Bencao: calms the heart and relieves fatigue, treats steaming bone disorders, cleanses the liver, brightens the eyes, excessive tearing, dry mouth, heart heat, kills pinworms, treats roundworm heart pain, scabies, when honey-fried treats nasal discharge, intestinal wind, hematochezia, acute swollen pain.
6. Zhenzhu Nang: treats kidney water disorders. Bladder deficiency, various atrophies and collapse, weakness of waist and knees.
7. Yixue Qiyuan: “Secrets of Main Treatments” says it drains the bladder dragon-fire, facilitates discharge of bound urine, reduces lower-jiao edema, dysentery with early bloody stools, umbilical pain, tonifies kidney water deficiency.
8. Yongyao Xinfa: treats unbearable sore carbuncles.
9. Lan Shi Mi Cang: drains pathogenic qi of the Chong vessel. Treats summer qi surging up causing inability to breathe and noisy panting preventing lying down.
10. Materia Medica: used topically for pediatric head sores.
11. Modern Practical Chinese Materia Medica: for sprains and contusions, grind to powder and mix into a paste for topical application.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoction, 3–9 g; or used in pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, grind to powder and apply as a plaster, or decoct for wash.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated in spleen-deficiency diarrhea and in persons with weak stomach and poor appetite.
1. Bencao Jing Jizhu: averse to dry lacquer (gan qi).
2. Bencao Jieshu: contraindicated in people with both yin and yang deficiency, diseases accompanied by spleen-stomach weakness, poor food intake and poor digestion, or concurrent diarrhea; those who dislike cold foods and like hot foods; kidney-deficient early-morning diarrhea; upper heat and lower cold with urinary incontinence; cold pain in the lower abdomen, cold uterus; blood deficiency infertility, yang deficiency heat, stagnant blood, postpartum blood-deficiency fever, metal wound fever; heat after carbuncle ulceration, food-injury fever, yin deficiency urine difficulty, small water not flowing after smallpox, blood deficiency insomnia, blood-deficiency irritability, spleen-yin insufficiency causing diarrhea, etc.; in these patterns salty methods are contraindicated.
Formulas using Phellodendron bark
1. Treating pediatric dysenteric bloody stools: Phellodendron bark (Huangbo) half liang (about 7.5 g), Red Peony Root (Chi Shao) four qian (about 10 g). Grind to fine powder, form pills with rice and swallow the size of sesame seeds. Each dose one to twenty pills before meals with rice water; for larger children increase pills. (Yan’s Pediatric Formulas)
2. Treating dysentery: Phellodendron bark 300 g, Fanbai herb (Fan Bai Cao) 450 g, Qin Pi (Qin Pi) 300 g. Boil Fan Bai Cao and Qin Pi fully and 200 g of Phellodendron together twice, combine decoctions, reduce over low heat to a paste, grind remaining 100 g Phellodendron to fine powder and mix into the paste, stir, low-temperature dry, grind to fine powder. Each dose 1–2 g, three times daily. (Selected New Therapies of Chinese Herbs, Liaoning)
3. Treating cold-heat diarrhea during pregnancy and postpartum: Phellodendron bark one jin (500 g), Coptis (Huang Lian) one sheng (unit), Gardenia (Zhi Zi) twenty fruits. Cut finely, soak in five liters of water overnight, boil three times, drink one sheng (approximate measure) continuously day and night until used up. For vomiting add Aged Tangerine Peel (Chen Pi) a handful and Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang) two liang. (Qianjin Yifang)
4. Treating pediatric summer diarrhea: Phellodendron bark, roast to powder, form pills with rice broth the size of millet grains; take one to twenty pills with rice broth. (Shiquan Bojiu Fang)
5. Treating excessive thirst with frequent urination but good appetite: Phellodendron bark one jin, water one sheng, boil until three to five times boiled, drink when thirsty, drink freely for several days. (Duxing Fang)
6. Treating cold-invasion with yellowing and fever: fifteen ripe Gardenia (split), Licorice (Gan Cao) one liang (prepared), Phellodendron bark two liang. Boil these three in four sheng water to one and a half sheng, remove dregs, take warm. (Shanghan Lun: Zhi Zi Bai Pi Tang)
7. Treating muscle and bone pain due to damp-heat: Phellodendron (stir-fried), Atractylodes (soaked in rice washing water then fried). Grind both into powder, take with boiling water mixed with ginger juice. Both have strong yang-promoting qi; when external excess is present add a little wine. (Danxi Xinf a: Er Miao San)
8. Treating severe heat with night diarrhea, startlement and confusion, epigastric distention and dry tongue: Phellodendron (coarse bark removed) one liang. Grind to fine powder with silk floss, add Borneol (Bing Pian) one qian, mix evenly, bind with processed honey into pills the size of a wutong seed. Take one to nineteen pills with a concentrated decoction of Ophiopogon (Mai Dong). (Shengji Zonglu: Qingxin Wan)
9. Treating leucorrhea, nocturnal emission, seminal emission and sliding discharge: Phellodendron one jin (roast on new tile until red), genuine clam shell powder (Zhen Ha Fen) one jin. Grind to fine powder, add drops of water to form pills the size of a wutong seed. Take one hundred pills on an empty stomach with wine. (Su Wen Bingji Baoming Ji: Zhenzhufan Wan)
10. Treating spontaneous sweating from the lower yin, dizziness and sore waist and knees: Phellodendron three qian, Atractylodes four qian, Sichuan pepper thirty grains; add 2000 ml water, decoct to 600 ml. Each dose 100 ml, three times daily, finish in two days. (Zhongji Yikan (3):177, 1966)
11. Lowering yin fire and tonifying kidney water: Phellodendron (stir-fried to brown) and Anemarrhena (soaked in wine then stir-fried) each four liang, Prepared Rehmannia root (steamed with wine) and Tortoise shell (roasted with honey) each six liang. Grind to powder, mix with pork spinal marrow and honey into pills. Take seventy pills on an empty stomach with salted white soup. (Danxi Xinf a: Da Bu Wan)
12. Reducing heart fire and benefiting kidney water: Phellodendron three liang, Amomum (Sha Ren) one and a half liang, Licorice one liang. Grind to fine powder, make pills the size of a stick. Take five pills, use Cistanche (Rou Cong Rong) half liang chopped, soak in a large cup of wine overnight, next morning decoct on empty stomach to three or four boils, remove dregs, take with clear wine. (Qi Xiao Liang Fang: Feng Sui Dan)
13. Treating epidemic conjunctivitis with red eyes: Phellodendron, remove coarse bark, grind to powder, wrap in damp paper, coat with yellow clay and bake until dry. Use a pellet sized as a bean, wrap in gauze, soak in a cup of water, steam over rice and use the hot steam to wash and fumigate. One pellet can be used three to five times. (Yanke Longmu Lun: Wu Xing Tang)
14. Treating infant palpebral conjunctivitis: soak Phellodendron in breast milk and apply drops. (Xiaopin Fang)
15. Treating mouth and tongue sores: chew crushed Phellodendron and hold in the mouth. (Qianjin Fang)
16. Treating pediatric swollen tongue: soak Phellodendron in bamboo sap and finely apply on the tongue. (Qianjin Fang)
17. Treating foul oral ulcers: Phellodendron five qian, verdigris three qian. Mix to powder and apply to remove discharge and promote healing. (Xiaopin Fang)
18. Treating lip sores itching and pain: Phellodendron powder mixed with root juice of Epilobium and applied. (Shengji Zonglu)
19. Treating lung congestion and nasal sores and swelling: Phellodendron and Areca (Bing Lang) equal parts, crush to powder, mix with lard and apply. (Shenghui Fang)
20. Treating mastitis, various abscesses on the back and engorged breasts: crush Phellodendron to powder, sieve, mix with egg white, apply thickly; when dry reapply. (Buque Zhouhou Fang)
21. Treating carbuncles and toxic swellings: Phellodendron bark (stir-fried), processed Chuan Wu Tou (prepared) equal parts. Grind to powder and apply, leave on the head, frequently moisten with rice-washing water. (Bin Hu Ji Jian Fang)

[Dietary therapy]
1. Salvia and Phellodendron wine
Salvia (Dan Shen) 30 g, Phellodendron bark (Huang Bai) 10 g, white wine 0.5 kg. Soak Salvia in white wine; after 7 days take 20–30 ml daily, 2–3 times a day.
Effect: Clears heat, cools the blood, promotes blood circulation.
2. Phellodendron and mung bean soup
Phellodendron bark (Huang Bai) 10 g, Mung beans (Lu Dou) 250 g, white sugar a little. Decoction of Phellodendron strained, add mung beans and cook until soft, add white sugar, cool and drink as desired.
Effect: Clears and drains damp-heat, drains fire and detoxifies.
