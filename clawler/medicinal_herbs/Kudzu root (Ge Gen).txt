Cultivation and Processing of Kudzu Root (Ge Gen)
Source
This item is the dried root of the Fabaceae plants wild kudzu Pueraria lobata (Willd.) Ohwi or Thomson's kudzu vine Pueraria thomsonii Benth.
Morphology
1. Wild Kudzu (Ye Ge)
A perennial deciduous climbing vine up to 10 m long. The whole plant is covered with yellowish-brown coarse hairs. The tuberous root is cylindrical, fleshy, the outer bark grayish-yellow, inside mealy and very fibrous. The stem base is stout and the upper part is much branched. Leaves are pinnately trifoliate; the terminal leaflet stalk is relatively long; leaf blades rhomboid-ovate, 5.5–19 cm long, 4.5–18 cm wide, apex gradually acute, base rounded, sometimes shallowly lobed, lateral leaflets smaller, obliquely ovate, unequal on both sides, abaxial surface pale with a farinose coating, both surfaces covered with short appressed soft white hairs; stipules are peltate, ovate-lanceolate, small stipules needle-shaped. Racemes are axillary or terminal, corolla bluish-purple or purple; bracts narrow linear, early deciduous, bracteoles ovate or lanceolate; calyx campanulate, 0.8–1 cm long, 5 calyx teeth lanceolate, two upper teeth connate, the lower tooth longer; standard petal nearly round or ovate with a slightly emarginate apex and two short auricles at the base, wings narrowly elliptic and shorter than the standard, often with an auricle at the base on one side, the keel petal slightly longer than the wings; stamens 10, diadelphous; ovary linear, style curved. Legume linear, 6–9 cm long, 7–10 mm wide, densely covered with yellowish-brown long stiff hairs. Seeds ovate, reddish-brown, glossy. Flowering April to August, fruiting August to October.
2. Thomson's Kudzu Vine (Gan Ge Teng)
A vine. Roots enlarged. Stems and branches covered with yellowish-brown short hairs or mixed with long stiff hairs. Leaves trifoliate with long petioles; stipules lanceolate-elliptic with hairs; leaflets rhomboid-ovate to broadly ovate, sometimes 3-lobed, 9–21 cm long, 8–18 cm wide, apex shortly acuminate, base cordate. Racemes axillary; bracteoles ovate; calyx campanulate, 1.2–1.5 cm long, 5 lanceolate calyx teeth longer than the calyx tube, covered with yellow long stiff hairs; corolla purple, 1.3–1.8 cm long. Pods oblong, flattened, 10–12 cm long, 1–1.2 cm wide, densely covered with yellowish-brown long stiff hairs. Seeds reniform or round. Flowering June to September, fruiting August to October.
Taste and Properties
Sweet; Pungent; Neutral
Physical Characteristics
(1) Intact wild kudzu roots are mostly cylindrical. Commercially they are often cut into slanted, longitudinal, or transverse slices of uneven sizes. Surface brown with longitudinal wrinkles, transverse lenticels and irregular rootlet scars visible. Texture firm; fracture rough; yellowish-white in cross-section; 1–3 concentric ring layers faintly visible. Strongly fibrous, slightly farinaceous. Slight aroma, mildly sweet taste.
(2) Fen Ge root is cylindrical, spindle-shaped or semi-cylindrical, some are thick longitudinal or oblique slices of varying sizes. Peel removed, surface yellow-white or pale yellow; unpeeled surface gray-brown. Hard and heavy in texture, less fibrous, in some specimens cottony, very farinaceous. Superior material has large tubers, firm texture, white color, abundant starchiness, and few fibers.
Effects of Kudzu Root (Ge Gen)
Functions
This item is the dried root of the Fabaceae plants wild kudzu or Thomson's kudzu vine.
Main indications: Shanghan and febrile headache with neck stiffness, vexation and fever with excessive thirst, diarrhea, dysentery, rashes that do not surface, hypertension, angina pectoris, deafness.
Channels
Spleen channel; Stomach channel; Lung channel; Bladder channel
Main Uses
Raises yang and releases the exterior, brings out rashes and stops diarrhea, relieves vexation and clears heat. Treats cold damage and febrile diseases with headache and nape stiffness, vexation with fever and thirst, diarrhea, dysentery, rashes that fail to surface, hypertension, angina pectoris, deafness.
1. Ben Jing (The Classic): Treats wasting-and-thirst (Xiao Ke), excessive body heat, vomiting, various obstructions, generates yin qi, and detoxifies poisons.
2. Bie Lu (Alternate Records): Cures cold damage wind-stroke headache, releases the muscles and the exterior, promotes sweating, opens the interstices. Treats traumatic injuries and relieves pain, treats flank wind pain. Fresh root juice treats wasting-and-thirst and severe febrile disease.
3. Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu (Collected Annotations on Materia Medica): Kills the poisons of wild kudzu, croton, and many poisons.
4. Tao Hongjing: Pounded fresh and the juice drunk to resolve epidemic fever. Kudzu root powdered treats traumatic bleeding and also treats malaria and sores.
5. Yao Xing Lun (Treatise on Medicinal Properties): Treats rebellious qi rising to the head, vomiting, opens the stomach to descend food, antidotes alcohol poisoning, stops vexation and thirst. Boiled powder treats traumatic bleeding and seasonal febrile heat.
6. Tang Ben Cao (Tang Materia Medica): Powder taken orally treats dog bites; drinking the juice is beneficial.
7. Ben Cao Shi Yi (Supplement to Materia Medica): Fresh breaks blood, resolves sores, can induce miscarriage, antidotes alcohol poisoning, treats body heat and jaundice, and painful, scanty urination.
8. Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao (Ri Hua Zi Materia Medica): Treats chest and diaphragm heat, vexation and mania, stops bloody dysentery, unblocks the small intestine, discharges pus and stops bleeding, applied to snake and insect bites.
9. Kai Bao Ben Cao (Kaibao Materia Medica): For children with heat and abdominal fullness, soak and mash kudzu root and give the juice to drink.
10. Yi Xue Qi Yuan (Origins of Medical Learning): Eliminates spleen and stomach deficiency heat and thirst.
11. Zhang Yuan Su: Releases the exterior and dispels evil, promotes eruption of difficult-to-emerge childhood rashes.
12. Compendium of Materia Medica (Ben Cao Gang Mu): Disperses constrained fire.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 10–15 g; or mash and take the juice.
External use: appropriate amount, pound and apply as poultice.
Precautions and Contraindications
1. Zhang Yuan Su: Do not take in large doses for fear of injuring the stomach qi.
2. Ben Cao Zheng (Correct Materia Medica): Its nature is cool and prone to cause nausea; use with caution in those with cold stomach.
3. Ben Cao Cong Xin (New Compilation of Materia Medica): Particularly contraindicated in summer when there is profuse spontaneous sweating due to exterior deficiency.
Prescriptions Using Kudzu Root (Formulas)
1. For Taiyang disease with stiff neck and back, little or no sweat, aversion to wind: Kudzu root 4 liang, Ephedra (Ma Huang) 3 liang (nodes removed), Cinnamon Twig (Gui Zhi) 2 liang (bark removed), Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang) 3 liang (sliced), Licorice (Gan Cao) 2 liang (honey-fried), Peony (Bai Shao) 2 liang, Jujube (Da Zao) 12 pieces (quartered). Combine these seven herbs in one dou of water; first boil ephedra and kudzu, reduce by two sheng, remove white scum, add the other herbs, boil to obtain three sheng, strain, warm and take one sheng; this should produce a slight sweat. (From Shang Han Lun: Ge Gen Tang)
2. For Taiyang disease with Gui Zhi pattern where the physician wrongly purged the patient causing persistent diarrhea and a rapid pulse (exterior not yet released), wheezing and sweating: Kudzu root half a jin, Licorice (Gan Cao) 2 liang (honey-fried), Baikal Skullcap (Huang Qin) 3 liang, Coptis (Huang Lian) 3 liang. Use these four herbs in eight sheng of water; first boil kudzu, reduce by two sheng, add the other herbs, boil to obtain two sheng, strain, divide and take warm. (From Shang Han Lun: Ge Gen Huang Qin Huang Lian Tang)
3. For cold-damage epidemic heat with strong fever, wind-heat, headache, limb pain, rashes erupting or not: Cimicifuga (Sheng Ma), Kudzu root (fine grated), Peony (Bai Shao), Licorice (Gan Cao) (grated, honey-fried) in equal parts. Coarsely powder; each dose four qian, water one bowl and a half, decoct to one bowl, take warm as needed. (From Yan's Pediatric Formulas: Sheng Ma Ge Gen Tang)
4. For early-stage measles eruption incomplete with high fever and small papules not yet surfacing: Kudzu root, Cimicifuga (Sheng Ma), Platycodon Root (Jie Geng), Peucedanum root (Qian Hu), Saposhnikovia root (Fang Feng) each one qian, Licorice (Gan Cao) five fen. Decoction to take. (From Quan You Xin Jian)
5. For heat-toxin causing lower bleeding or bleeding triggered by eating hot foods: Fresh kudzu root 2 jin, mash and take one sheng of juice, mix with one sheng of lotus root juice and take together. (From Mei Shi Ji Yan Fang)
6. For incessant vomiting of blood from a heated heart: Fresh kudzu root juice half a da sheng, swallow at once. (From Guang Li Fang)
7. For daily nosebleed not stopping with vexation: Fresh kudzu root, mash and take the juice; each dose one small cup. (From Sheng Hui Fang)
8. For pregnancy heat disease with chest vexation: Kudzu root juice 2 sheng, divided into three doses. (From Shang Han Lei Yao)
9. For sudden persistent retching: Mash kudzu root, wring out the juice, take one sheng less a small amount. (From Bu Que Zhou Hou Fang)
10. For alcohol intoxication not awakening: Kudzu root juice one dou and two sheng, drink and recover consciousness. (From Qian Jin Fang)
11. For food poisoning from vegetables causing madness, vexation, vomiting and diarrhea to near death: Boil kudzu root and give the decoction to drink. (From Bu Que Zhou Hou Fang)
12. For improper dosing of medicine causing bitter vexation in the heart: Drink fresh kudzu root juice generously. If fresh is unavailable, kudzu root powder five he with water may be taken, or it can be decocted. (From Bu Que Zhou Hou Fang)
13. For acute intestinal obstruction: Kudzu root and Gleditsia sinensis (Zao Jiao / Zhu Ya Zao) each 1 jin, add 4000 ml water, boil for 40 minutes, strain; keep the decoction warm on a hot plate at a tolerable temperature. Separately fold ten layers of gauze about one Chinese foot square into four pads, soak in the decoction and wring slightly to remove excess liquid, alternately place on the abdomen as a sustained hot compress, each application one hour, two to three times daily. (Journal of Henan Medical College 9(4):203, 1965)
14. For traumatic injuries with wind-stroke and severe spasms nearing death: Pound one jin fresh kudzu root, cut finely, boil in one dou of water to obtain five sheng, strain and take one sheng warmed; for many, pound to a fine powder, mix three pinchfuls into warm wine. If the mouth is clenched and cannot open, take copious bamboo sap and plenty of fresh kudzu root until spontaneous recovery; diet also beneficial. (From Zhou Hou Fang)
15. For initial cold-damage with headache and internal heat with flooding pulse: Use Kudzu root 4 liang, add two sheng water and one sheng fermented soybean (dou chi), boil together to half a sheng and take. Adding fresh ginger juice is better.
16. For vexation, restlessness, and thirst: Use kudzu powder 4 liang mixed into water in which millet has been soaked overnight, cook and take with rice gruel.
17. For early-stage boils: Burn kudzu vines to ash, mix with water and apply as a poultice.
Dietary Therapy
Kudzu Starch Gruel (Ge Fen Geng)
Kudzu starch 250 g, Schizonepeta spikes (Jing Jie Sui) 30 g, Light fermented soybeans (Dan Dou Chi) 150 g. Make the kudzu starch into noodles; boil Schizonepeta spikes and Dan Dou Chi in water until 6–7 boils, remove dregs and take the liquid, cook the kudzu noodles in the liquid until done. Eat once in the morning and once in the evening.
Efficacy: Nourishes the liver and kidneys, extinguishes wind and opens the orifices. Indications: Suitable for wind-stroke due to liver and kidney yin deficiency, slurred speech, clouded consciousness, hemiplegia, cerebral arteriosclerosis in middle-aged and elderly. Also useful for stroke prevention.
