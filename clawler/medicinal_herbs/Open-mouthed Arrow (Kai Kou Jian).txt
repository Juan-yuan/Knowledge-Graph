Bamboo-root Seven (Zhu gen qi)
Cultivation and Processing of Arisaema (Kai kou jian)
Source
The rhizomes of Arisaema (Kai kou jian) and Sword-leaf Arisaema (Jian ye kai kou jian) of the Liliaceae family. Can be harvested year-round; remove leaves and fibrous roots, wash clean, use fresh or slice and sun-dry.
Morphology
1. Arisaema (Kai kou jian) is a perennial herb. Rhizome elongate-cylindrical, diameter 1–1.5 cm, many-noded, green to yellow. Leaves basal, 4–8; leaf blades obovate-lanceolate, linear-lanceolate or strap-shaped, 15–65 cm long, 1.5–9.5 cm wide, apex gradually acuminate, base gradually narrowed; two cataphylls. Spike-like inflorescence lateral, erect, densely many-flowered, 2.5–9 cm long; bracts ovate-lanceolate to subulate, with several sterile bracts clustered at the inflorescence apex; perianth short campanulate, 5–7 mm long; segments 6, ovate, 3–5 mm long, 2–4 mm wide, yellow or yellow-green, fleshy; stamens 6, filaments dilated at the base, sometimes fused with each other, free above, incurved, anthers ovoid; ovary globose, 3-loculed, style indistinct, stigma blunt triangular, apex 3-cleft. Berry globose, 8–10 mm in diameter, purple-red when ripe, with 1–3 seeds. Flowering April–June, fruiting September–November.
2. Sword-leaf Arisaema (Jian ye kai kou jian) is a perennial herb. Rhizome cylindrical, brown or green, stems up to 10 cm long, many-noded. Leaves numerous, distinctly two-ranked, strap-shaped, 35–50 cm long, 5–12 mm wide, apex long-acuminate, base enlarged and clasping the stem, margins slightly revolute when dry. Spike-like inflorescence lateral, densely many-flowered, 4–5.5 cm long; bracts subulate or triangular-subulate, longer than the flowers, with several sterile bracts clustered at the inflorescence apex; perianth simple campanulate, 5–5.5 mm long; segments 6, ovate, spreading, 2–2.5 mm long, 1.5–2 mm wide, fleshy, brown or green, margins white-membranous and eroded-looking; stamens 6, filaments thick, dilated and wrinkled at the base, adnate to the perianth tube, free above, shorter than the anthers, anthers ovoid; ovary ovoid, 3-loculed, style indistinct, stigma blunt triangular, apex 3-cleft. Berries 5–8 mm in diameter, red-black, with 1–3 seeds. Flowering in June, fruiting in October.
Ecological Habitat
1. Arisaema (Kai kou jian) grows in shaded moist places under forests, by streams, or beside roads.
2. Sword-leaf Arisaema (Jian ye kai kou jian) grows under forests at altitudes of 1100–3200 m.
Distribution
1. Arisaema (Kai kou jian) is distributed in central and southern China and in Shaanxi, Anhui, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Taiwan, Sichuan, Yunnan, etc.
2. Sword-leaf Arisaema (Jian ye kai kou jian) is distributed in Yunnan.
Properties and Taste
Bitter; pungent; cold; toxic
Characteristics
The rhizome of Arisaema (Kai kou jian) is flattened-cylindrical, slightly twisted, 10–15 cm long, about 1 cm in diameter. Nodes distinct, slightly swollen, with buds and membranous scale-like leaves at the nodes; internodes short. Surface yellow-brown to yellow-green, wrinkled. Transverse section pale yellow-white, fine granular. Odor absent, taste bitter and astringent.
Effects of Arisaema (Kai kou jian)
Efficacy
The rhizomes of Arisaema (Kai kou jian) and Sword-leaf Arisaema (Jian ye kai kou jian) of the Liliaceae family. Used for diphtheria, throat swelling and pain, rheumatic painful obstruction, traumatic injuries from falls and blows, stomach pain, carbuncles and ulcers, poisonous snakebites, and rabid dog bites.
Main Indications
Clears heat and detoxifies, dispels wind and eliminates dampness, disperses stasis and relieves pain. Indicated chiefly for diphtheria, throat swelling and pain, rheumatic pain, traumatic injuries from falls and blows, stomach pain, carbuncles and ulcers, poisonous snakebites, and rabid dog bites.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 1.5–3 g; powdered, 0.6–0.9 g.
External use: appropriate amount, pounded and applied.
Precautions
Contraindicated in pregnancy.
Notes
This substance is toxic; there have been reports of poisoning when up to 3 qian were used, therefore the dosage must not be excessive. In poisoning, symptoms such as headache, dizziness, nausea and vomiting may be observed; stop the medicine immediately and provide timely rescue.
