Cultivation and Processing of Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi)
Source
This item is the dried root of the Apiaceae plant Dahurian Angelica (Angelica dahurica (Fisch. ex Hoffm.) Benth. et Hook.f.) or Hangzhou Dahurian Angelica (Angelica dahurica (Fisch. ex Hoffm.) Benth. et Hook.f. var. formosana (Boiss.) Shan et Yuan). It is harvested in summer and autumn when the leaves turn yellow, the fibrous roots and soil are removed, and it is sun-dried or dried at low temperature.
Properties and taste
Pungent, warm.
Appearance
The root is long-conical, 10–25 cm long, 1.5–2.5 cm in diameter. Surface gray-brown or yellow-brown; root head bluntly four-angled or nearly round, with longitudinal wrinkles, lateral root scars and lenticel-like transverse protrusions, some arranged in four longitudinal rows. Apex has a depressed stem scar. Texture firm; fracture surface white or gray-white, mealy; vascular cambium ring brown, nearly square or nearly round; cortex scattered with numerous brown oil-dots. Odor aromatic; taste pungent and slightly bitter.
Effects of Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi)
Functions/Efficacy
This item is the dried root of the Apiaceae plant Dahurian Angelica or Hangzhou Dahurian Angelica.
Mainly treats headache, pain of the supraorbital ridge, toothache, nasal obstruction/sinusitis, cold-damp abdominal pain, intestinal wind and hemorrhoids/anal fistula, red or white vaginal discharge, carbuncles and sores, dry itchy skin, scabies and tinea.

Indications
Expels wind, dries dampness, reduces swelling, and relieves pain. Treats headache, supraorbital ridge pain, toothache, nasal obstruction/sinusitis, cold-damp abdominal pain, intestinal wind and hemorrhoids/anal fistula, red or white vaginal discharge, carbuncles and sores, dry itchy skin, scabies and tinea.
① 'Ben Jing': Treats women's abnormal red and white vaginal discharge, blood obstruction causing swelling of the genitals, alternating cold and heat, wind of the head invading the eyes causing tears, promotes flesh growth and moisturizes the skin.
② 'Bie Lu': Cures prolonged thirst due to wind-evil (or possibly prolonged diarrhea), vomiting, fullness of the two flanks, wind pain with dizziness of the head, and itchy eyes.
③ 'Yao Xing Lun': Treats stabbing pain in the heart and abdomen and abdominal blood stasis pain, dispels wind-evil, is indicated for women's metrorrhagia and retching, clears the eyes and stops tearing, treats women's prolonged spotting and low back and abdominal pain; able to corrode pus.
④ 'Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao': Treats red eyes and ocular pterygium, secures threatened abortion and prevents slippage, breaks old blood and replenishes new blood, treats mastitis, back swellings, scrofula, intestinal wind, hemorrhoids and fistula, promotes pus discharge, treats ulcers, scabies and tinea, relieves pain and generates flesh, removes facial pockmarks and scars.
⑤ 'Dian Nan Ben Cao': Expels wandering wind of the skin, stops stomach cold and abdominal pain from cold, and relieves whole-body cold-damp pain.
⑥ 'Compendium of Materia Medica (Bencao Gangmu)': Treats nasal obstruction, epistaxis, toothache, supraorbital pain, large-intestine wind constipation, bloody urine, women's blood-wind dizziness and irregular menses, reversal of the stomach with vomiting; antidote for arsenic poisoning, snakebite, and knife or arrow wounds.
Dosage and Administration
Internal: decoct, 3–10 g; or used in pills or powders.
External: appropriate amount, grind to powder and sprinkle or mix into a poultice.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated for those with yin deficiency and blood-heat.
① 'Bencao Jing Jizhu': Angelica sinensis (Dang Gui) acts as its assistant; incompatible with Xuan Fu Hua (Inula Flower).
② 'Bencao Jing Shu': Contraindicated when vomiting is due to excess fire. Do not use for red or white vaginal discharge caused by yin deficiency with blazing fire and blood heat. When an abscess has ulcerated, gradually reduce and stop.
Formulas using Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi)
① To treat headache and eye pain: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) 4 qian, raw Aconite (Chuan Wu) 1 qian. Grind into powder; each dose about one small pellet ('zi'), taken with tea. For some with eye pain, first hold water in the mouth, then insufflate this into the nose for faster effect. (From Zhu's Collected Tested Prescriptions: Baizhi Powder)
② To treat various wind-induced vertigo, women before and after childbirth suddenly attacked by wind-evil, heaviness of head and eyes and blood-wind headache, sudden cold-warm changes, clouded spirit, and cold-damage wind-dizziness: Fragrant Bai Zhi (Xiang Bai Zhi) (soak and wash with boiling water four to five times) made into powder, bind with refined honey into pills the size of a bead. Take one pill per dose, often chewed with Schizonepeta (Jing Jie) and waxed tea. (From Bai Yi Xuan Fang: Du Liang Wan)
③ To treat hemicranial headache: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi), Asarum (Xi Xin), Gypsum (Shi Gao), Frankincense (Ru Xiang), Myrrh (Mo Yao) (oil removed). These ingredients in equal parts, made into a fine powder, insufflate into the nose; if left-sided pain, insufflate into the right nostril; if right-sided pain, insufflate into the left. (From Zhong Fu Tang Selected Good Prescriptions: Baizhi-Xixin Nasal Powder)
④ To treat supraorbital rim pain due to wind-heat with phlegm: Baical Skullcap (Huang Qin) (fried with wine) and Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi). Make into powder; take 2 qian with clear tea. (From Danxi Xinfa)
⑤ To treat nasal obstruction/sinusitis: Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi), Saposhnikovia (Fang Feng), Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) each 8 fen, Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 1 qian 2 fen, Szechuan Lovage Root (Chuan Xiong) 5 fen, Northern Asarum (Xi Xin) 7 fen, Licorice (Gan Cao) 3 fen. Decoction in water, take four doses in succession. Avoid beef. (From Yang Yi Da Quan)
⑥ To treat intestinal wind: Fragrant Bai Zhi (Xiang Bai Zhi) finely powdered, mixed with rice gruel and taken. (From Bai Yi Xuan Fang)
⑦ To treat constipation due to intestinal wind: Fragrant Bai Zhi (Xiang Bai Zhi) fried and powdered, 2 qian per dose, take with rice soup with a little honey, take two consecutive doses. (From Shi Bian Liang Fang)
⑧ To treat hemorrhoidal swelling and pain: first smoke with Gleditsia Fruit (Zhu Ya Zao), then mix Dahurian Angelica Root powder with goose bile and apply. (From Yi Fang Zhai Yao)
⑨ To treat leukorrhea, intestinal putrid pus, persistent urinary dribbling with severe foul smell, and cold abdominal pain requiring pus drainage: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) 1 liang, Single-leaf Red Mallow Root 2 liang, White Peony Root (Bai Shao) (white variety), Alum (Bai Fan) each 0.5 liang (alum calcined until dry, then separately ground). Make into powder and roll into wax-coated pills the size of a catalpa seed; take on an empty stomach or before meals with rice gruel, ten to fifteen pills until the pus is exhausted, then use other medicines to tonify. (From Ben Cao Yan Yi)
⑩ To treat swelling-from-toxin heat and pain: mix Bai Zhi powder with vinegar and apply as a poultice. (From Wei Sheng Yi Jian Fang)
⑾ To treat carbuncles and red swelling: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) and Rhubarb (Da Huang) in equal parts. Make into powder; take 2 qian with rice gruel. (From Jing Yan Fang)
⑿ To treat knife and arrow wounds: chew Fragrant Bai Zhi (Xiang Bai Zhi) to a pulp and apply. (From Bin Hu Ji Jian Fang)
⒀ To treat headache: Gypsum (Shi Gao) and Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) each 2 qian, Mint Leaves (Bo He) and Mirabilite (Mang Xiao) each 3 qian, Turmeric (Yu Jin) 1 qian. Make into an extremely fine powder, hold water in the mouth, and retain it in the nose. (From Lan Shi Mi Cang: Baizhi Powder)
⒁ To treat unilateral headache: Gleditsia Fruit (Zhu Ya Zao) (remove fibrous membrane), Fragrant Bai Zhi (Xiang Bai Zhi), White Aconite (Bai Fu Zi) in equal parts. Make into powder. Each dose 1 qian, taken with waxed tea after meals. (From Xu Ben Shi Fang)
⒂ To treat unbearable headache, whether unilateral or bilateral, and to treat red eye and toothache: Dried Ginger (Gan Jiang) and Fragrant Bai Zhi each half liang, Artemisia Fruit (Hao Jiao Zi) 1 qian. Make into a fine powder. Use about 0.5 qian daily divided into three doses, insufflate finely into the nose and massage both temples; the pain stops immediately. (From Ji Feng Pu Ji Fang: Tong Ding San)
⒃ To treat severe eye pain: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi), Red Peony Root (Chi Shao), Saposhnikovia (Fang Feng), Asarum (Xi Xin) in equal parts. Make into powder. Each dose 1 qian with one cup of water and 2 qian rock sugar; remove dregs and take warm at any time. (From Xu Ben Shi Fang)
⒄ To treat persistent clear nasal discharge: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) finely powdered, mixed with mashed scallion white into pills the size of a small bean. Take 20 pills per dose with tea. (From Zheng Zhi Zhun Sheng: Baizhi Pills)
⒅ To treat gastric pain from ulcers: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi), White Peony Root (Bai Shao), Bletilla (Bai Ji) each 10–30 g, White Cardamom (Bai Dou Kou) 6–12 g. One dose daily, decoct in water and divide for administration. (From Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine 1983(12):34 — Si Bai Decoction)
⒆ To treat continuous metrorrhagia: Fragrant Bai Zhi (Xiang Bai Zhi) 1.5 liang, Dragon Bone (Long Gu) 1 liang, Schizonepeta Leaves (Jing Jie Ye) 0.5 liang. Grind into a fine powder. Each dose 2 qian, taken with warm wine; may also be taken with rice gruel or soup before meals. (From Yang Family Recipes: Fang Xiang San)
⒇ To treat chronic knee rheumatism ("crane knee wind"): take fresh Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi), decoct in wine to a paste, store in a ceramic jar. Daily take 2 qian of the paste with aged wine; additionally apply 2–3 qian topically to the affected area until it subsides. (From Waike Quansheng Ji)
(21) To treat infantile mastitis and nipple erosion extending to surrounding tissue: Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) 2 qian, Oyster Shell Powder (Mu Li) 5 qian, Borneol (Bing Pian) 2 fen. Make into fine powder and apply to the affected area. (From Wai Ke Zhen Quan: Baizhi Powder)
(22) To treat poisonous snakebite: Fragrant Bai Zhi (Xiang Bai Zhi) powdered. Mix with Ophiopogon decoction (Mai Dong) to drink; in urgent cases freshly drawn water is acceptable. (From Hong's Collected Tested Prescriptions)
(23) To treat metal or iron injuries and tetanus: Coptis (Huang Lian), Areca Nut (Bing Lang), Aucklandia (Mu Xiang), and Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) in equal parts. Make into a fine powder and apply to the wounded area; bleeding will stop. For women's postpartum bleeding, mix 1 qian with a boy's urine; for visceral toxic bleeding, decoct in water and take. (From Ji Feng Pu Ji Fang: Baizhi Powder)
(24) To treat fish-bone lodging: Pinellia (Ban Xia) 5 liang (washed), Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) 5 liang. Pound and sieve these two items. Take one spoonful (approximate measure); it will induce vomiting and expel the bone. Avoid mutton and maltose. (From Wai Tai)
Dietary Therapy Recipes
1. Stewed Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) with Bird's Nest
Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) 9 g, bird's nest 9 g, rock sugar to taste. Double-boil Bai Zhi and bird's nest until very soft, strain off dregs. Add rock sugar to taste and double-boil briefly; take 1–2 times daily. Functions: tonifies the lung and nourishes yin, stops cough and stops bleeding.
2. Mint, Cocklebur and Dahurian Angelica Tea
Mint (Bo He) 6 g, Cocklebur Fruit (Cang Er Zi) 12 g, Magnolia Flower (Xin Yi) 6 g, Dahurian Angelica Root (Bai Zhi) 6 g, Scallion white (Cong Bai) 3 stalks, Tea leaf 2 g. Put all the above herbs into a pot, decoct, strain and use the liquid. Drink as a tea substitute.
Efficacy: disperses wind and opens the orifices. Indications: treats rhinitis, sinusitis and related disorders.
