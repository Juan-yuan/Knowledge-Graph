Cultivation and Processing of Incised Notopterygium (Qianghuo)
Source
The rhizome and root of plants of the Apiaceae family: Notopterygium incisum (Qianghuo) or Notopterygium forbesii (broad-leaved Qianghuo). Cultivated plants are dug 3 to 4 years after planting in the autumn after leaning out the seedlings and before sprouting in early spring by cutting off the aboveground parts, excavating the rhizomes, shaking off mud and sand, chopping off the leaf bases, removing the fine roots, cutting into short sections 10 to 13 cm long, and sun-drying or oven-drying.
Properties and Taste
Pungent and bitter, warm in nature.
Appearance
(1) Qianghuo
The rhizome is cylindrical, slightly curved, 4 to 13 cm long, 0.6 to 2.5 cm in diameter. The top has stem scars. The surface is brownish to dark brown, with yellow where the peel has flaked off. Internodes are shortened and form tightly raised rings, resembling silkworms (commonly called Can Qiang); or the nodes are elongated, bamboo-joint like (commonly called Zhujie Qiang). On the nodes there are many punctate or nodular root scars and broken scale remnants. The body is light, brittle, and breaks easily. The fracture surface is uneven with many fissures; the periderm is yellow-brown to dark brown, oily, with brown oil spots; the wood is yellow-white with distinct rays; the pith is yellow to yellow-brown. Aromatic odor, slightly bitter and pungent taste.
(2) Broad-leaved Qianghuo
Rhizomes and roots cylindrical; the top has remnants of stem and leaf sheaths; the root is conical with longitudinal wrinkles and lenticels; the surface is brownish, with denser annular rings near the rhizome, 8 to 15 cm long, 1 to 3 cm in diameter (commonly called Tiao Qiang). Some rhizomes are large and irregularly nodular with several stem bases at the top and finer roots (commonly called Da Tou Qiang). Texture is loose and brittle, breaks easily. The fracture surface is relatively flat; periderm light brown; wood yellow-white. Odor is milder.
The best quality is from thick rhizomes with transverse nodes like silkworm-segments, brown surface, dense fracture texture, many cinnabar-like oil points, and strong aroma.
Effects of Qianghuo
Efficacy
The rhizome and root of plants of the Apiaceae family: Notopterygium incisum (Qianghuo) or Notopterygium forbesii (broad-leaved Qianghuo).
Used for impotence and spermatorrhea, enuresis and frequent urination, cold pain in the lower back and knees, wheezing due to kidney deficiency, early morning diarrhea; externally for vitiligo and alopecia areata. Releases the exterior and disperses cold, expels wind and overcomes dampness, relieves pain.
Meridians Entered
Enters the Bladder meridian; the Kidney meridian.
Main Indications
Disperses exterior cold, expels wind-dampness, and benefits the joints. Treats wind-cold common cold, headache without sweating, wind-cold-damp bi painful obstruction, stiff neck with contracted sinews, soreness and aching of bones and joints, wind-water edema, boils and sores with toxic swelling. Used for impotence and spermatorrhea, enuresis and frequent urination, cold pain in the lower back and knees, wheezing due to kidney deficiency, early morning diarrhea; externally treats vitiligo and alopecia areata.
1. For externally-contracted wind-cold with aversion to cold, fever, headache, body aches and other symptoms. This herb has a strong dispersing effect on wind-cold and analgesic effect. Often used with Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng), Angelica dahurica root (Baizhi), Asarum (Xixin), etc., such as in Nine-Ingredient Qianghuo Decoction (Jiuwei Qianghuo Tang).
2. For limb and joint pain, shoulder and back soreness due to invasion of wind-cold-dampness, especially suitable for upper-body pain. This herb expels wind and overcomes dampness, disperses cold and relieves pain. Often used with Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng), as in Quanbi Decoction (Juanbi Tang).
① From Yaoxing Lun: Treats pernicious wind, loss of voice and muteness, many itchings and blood scaly eruptions, paralysis of hands and feet, facial deviation, obstinate bi pain all over the body.
② From Rihuazi Bencao: Treats all wind disorders and qi disorders, sinew and bone contracture, emaciation of limbs, vertigo, red painful eyes and submerged-fluid disorders, overexertion injuries, deficiency and cold qi, soreness and pain of bones and joints, and unblocks and benefits the five zang.
③ From Zhenzhu Nang: For Taiyang meridian headache, removes various bone and joint pains, also warms the gallbladder.
④ From Pinhui Jingyao: Main for pain all over the body and in all joints, exterior eight-wind pernicious influences, removes new and old wind-dampness, discharges rotten flesh abscesses and sores.
⑤ From Bencao Beiyao: Drains liver qi, searches liver wind, treats wind-damp complex, Taiyang meridian headache, diseases of the Governing Vessel, stiff spine and collapse, rigid or flexible spasms, windstroke with muteness, vertigo and red painful eyes.
⑥ From Huiyue Yijing: Treats evil constriction and aversion to cold, robust fever without sweat.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 3 to 10 g; or used in pills and powders.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated for bi pain due to blood deficiency.
Formulas Containing Qianghuo
① For common cold with fever and tonsillitis: Qianghuo (Qianghuo) four to five qian, Isatis root (Banlangen), Dandelion (Pugongying) each one liang. Decoction, one dose per day, take in two divided doses. (Affiliated Longhua Hospital, Shanghai College of Traditional Chinese Medicine)
② For releasing and treating cold disease: Qianghuo one and a half liang, Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng) one and a half liang, Atractylodes rhizome (Cangzhu) one and a half liang, Asarum (Xixin) five fen, Ligusticum chuanxiong rhizome (Chuanxiong) one liang, Angelica dahurica root (Baizhi) one liang, Rehmannia root raw (Sheng Dihuang) one liang, Baical skullcap root (Huangqin) one liang, Licorice root (Gancao) one liang. Grind the above into powder, decoct with water and take. If there is urgent sweating, take with hot gruel; if sweating is slow, take warm but not with soup. (From Cishi Nanzhi: Nine-Ingredient Qianghuo Decoction)
③ For relieving acute dental-cold: Qianghuo, Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng), Atractylodes rhizome (Cangzhu), Asarum (Xixin), Ligusticum chuanxiong rhizome (Chuanxiong), Angelica dahurica root (Baizhi), Rehmannia root raw (Sheng Dihuang), Baical skullcap root (Huangqin), Licorice root (Gancao). Decoction; for rapid sweating take with hot gruel, for slow sweating take warm and not with soup. (Zhang Yuansu Nine-Ingredient Qianghuo Decoction)
④ For Taiyang meridian headache: Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng) 2 fen, Qianghuo 3 fen, Adzuki beans (Hongdou, Chixiaodou) two pieces. Grind to powder and insert into the nose. (From Yuji Weiyi)
⑤ For invading cold affecting the head causing toothache, cold extremities and coldness of mouth and nose: Qianghuo one qian, Prepared aconite (Fuzi) and Dried ginger (Ganjiang) each five fen, honey-fried Licorice root (Gancao) eight fen. Decoction. (From Yixue Xinwu: Qianghuo Fuzi Decoction)
⑥ For wind-damp complex causing body pain, pulling pain preventing flexion and extension, or slight swelling and numbness of the body: Qianghuo (remove leaf bases), Prepared aconite (Pao Fuzi, peeled and cored), Atractylodes rhizome (Baizhu), Licorice root (Gancao, honey-fried) in equal parts. Each dose four qian, water one and a half cup, fresh ginger five slices, boil down to seven tenths, strain, take warm at any time. (From Jisheng Fang: Qiang-Fuzi Decoction)
⑦ For shoulder and back pain preventing turning the head, spinal pain and stiff neck, waist like broken, neck as if pulled out: Qianghuo and Angelica pubescens root (Duhuo) each one qian, Ligusticum (Gaoben), Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng), Licorice root (Gancao, honey-fried), Ligusticum chuanxiong rhizome (Chuanxiong) each five fen, Vitex seed (Manjingzi) three fen. Make one dose, two cups water, reduce to one cup, strain, take very warm on an empty stomach before meals. (From Nei Wai Shang Bian: Qianghuo Victory Dampness Decoction)
⑧ For pediatric common cold: Qianghuo one qian, Ginseng (Renshen) one qian, Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng) one qian, Ligusticum chuanxiong rhizome (Chuanxiong) one qian. Grind as one dose, fresh ginger three slices, Mint (Bohe) seven leaves, one cup water, reduce to seven tenths, take at any time. (From Yingtong Baiwen: Qianghuo Decoction for Children)
⑨ For postpartum cold disease: Qianghuo, Cyperus (Xiangfu), Perilla leaf (Zisuye) each one qian five fen, Angelica sinensis root (Danggui) one qian, White peony root (Baishao), Bupleurum root (Chaihu), Aged tangerine peel (Chenpi) each one qian two fen. Add three green onion whites, decoct and take at any time. (From Dantai Yu'an: Qiang-Su Drink)
⑩ For unbearable brow-bone pain: honey-fried Licorice root (use raw in summer), Qianghuo and Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng) each three qian, wine Baical skullcap root (Huangqin) one qian (omit Huangqin in winter; if the pain is aggravated by eating and heat, double this herb). Grind and mix; each dose five qian, two cups water, reduce to one cup, strain, take after meals. (From Lanshi Mancang: Xuanqi Decoction)
⑾ For headache with dizziness and faintness on rising: Ligusticum chuanxiong rhizome (Chuanxiong), Qianghuo, Vitex seed (Manjingzi), Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng), Angelica dahurica root (Baizhi), Asarum (Xixin), Ligusticum (Gaoben), Gypsum (Shigao) in equal parts. Decoction. (From Yixue Qimeng: Chuanxiong-Qianghuo Powder)
⑿ For stagnation of Taiyang qi in the hands causing shoulder and back pain preventing turning the head; stagnation in the foot Taiyang causing spinal pain and stiff neck, waist like broken, neck as if pulled out: Qianghuo and Angelica pubescens root (Duhuo) each one qian, Ligusticum (Gaoben), Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng), Licorice root (Gancao, honey-fried), Ligusticum chuanxiong rhizome (Chuanxiong) each five fen, Vitex seed (Manjingzi) three fen. ⒀ For wind-damp complex with body soreness, neck and arm heaviness, difficulty moving, cold numbness of hands and feet, heavy knees and legs, weak tendons and vessels: Angelica sinensis root (Danggui, soak in wine and remove earth, soak overnight), Qianghuo (remove leaf bases), Curcuma (Jianghuang), White peony root (Baishao), Astragalus (Huangqi, honey-fried), Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng, remove leaf bases) each one liang and a half, Licorice root (Gancao, honey-fried) half liang. Grind and mix; each dose half liang, two cups water, fresh ginger five slices, boil to one cup, strain, warm take at any time. (From Yang Family Collection: Juanbi Decoction)
⒁ For dampness wandering into the four limbs with swollen painful joints: Qianghuo and Cimicifuga (Shengma) each one qian, Angelica pubescens root (Duhuo) seven fen, Atractylodes rhizome (Cangzhu), Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng, remove leaf bases), Clematis root (Weilingxian, remove leaf bases), Atractylodes macrocephala rhizome (Baizhu), Angelica sinensis root (Danggui), Poria (Fuling, peeled), Alisma rhizome (Zexie) each half qian. Grind and make one dose, two cups water, reduce to one cup, strain, warm take one dose before meals and one after meals. (From Weisheng Baojian: Da Qianghuo Decoction)
⒂ For wind-cold-damp lingering in the muscles causing weak, numb hands and feet, or qi-blood disharmony with numb obstruction: Sliced Curcuma (cleaned of dirt), Qianghuo, Atractylodes macrocephala rhizome (Baizhu), Stephania root (Fangji) each one liang, Licorice root (Gancao, lightly honey-fried) half liang. Each dose four qian, water one and a half cup, add fresh ginger ten slices, boil to eight tenths, strain. If disease is in the upper body take after meals; if in the lower body take before meals. (From Ju Fang: Wu Bi Decoction)
⒃ For tinea with swelling and pain of the feet: Qianghuo one and a half qian, Angelica sinensis body (Danggui shen) one qian, Bitter orange (Zhishi) and Rhubarb (Dahuang) each five fen. Grind into pieces the size of sesame beans, make one dose, two and a half large cups water, reduce to one cup, strain, take warm on an empty stomach; induce one liang of diarrhea, pain stops. (From Yixue Faming: Yiqi Exorcise Dampness Decoction)
⒄ For watery edema: Qianghuo and Radish seed (Laifuzi, stir-fried) each one liang. Grind to powder, take with wine. (From Shiyi De Xiao Fang: Chuanhuo Powder)
⒅ For throat obstruction and trismus: Qianghuo three liang (finely grated), Burdock seed (Niubangzi) one liang (pounded to powder). First decoct the Qianghuo in three large cups of water and take one and a half large cups, strain. Add one fen of alum (white vitriol), mix evenly; each dose one small cup, add two qian of burdock powder to the decoction. When administering, use a wooden spatula to pry open the mouth and pour in; success is when the patient vomits. (From Shenghui Fang)
⒆ For postpartum wind-stroke with trismus, obstinate numbness of the limbs, or limbs like bowstrings: Qianghuo and Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng) each three liang, soybeans one sheng (stir-fry until skins crack). Soak the two herbs in wine, then after a night stir in the fried beans hot into the wine, mix, seal for one day, then steep and cook down in a jar for a long time; take eight he (measures), cover and extract the juice quickly; administer by bean dribbling; take Qianghuo and Fangfeng to good effect. (From Jingxiao Chanbao)
⒇ For tetanus after taking Zuo Long Pill or after purgation with continued convulsions: Qianghuo, Angelica pubescens root (Duhuo), Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng), Sanguisorba root (Diyu) each one liang. Grind and make one dose; each dose five qian, water one and a half cup, reduce to one cup, strain, take warm. If fever add Baical skullcap root (Huangqin); if excess saliva add Pinellia (Banxia). (From Baoming Ji: Qianghuo Decoction)

(22) For wind-heat toxin forming scrofula and lymph node swellings: Qianghuo (remove leaf bases) one and a half liang, Stiff silkworm (Jiangcan, stir-fried) one liang. Pound the two into a powder. Each morning on an empty stomach take four qian with honey-wine, repeat at night. (From Shengji Zonglu: Internal-Reducing Qianghuo Powder)
(23) For purulent otorrhea: Typhonium rhizome (Baifuzi, prepared), Qianghuo (remove leaf bases) each one liang. Grind to fine powder. Take one pig or sheep kidney, cut open, put half a qian of the powder into each kidney, do not add salt, wrap in damp paper and roast until cooked, at dawn take warm and swallow, then continue with porridge to suppress. (From Shengji Zonglu: Two-Sage Powder)
(25) Two-Huo Chuanxiong Wine: Qianghuo 15 g, Angelica pubescens root (Duhuo) 15 g, Ligusticum chuanxiong rhizome (Chuanxiong) 20 g, black beans 30 g, hemp seed (Huomaren) 30 g, rice wine 200 ml. Precisely process the above, crush to powder, place in a clean bottle, add wine to soak, seal for 21 days, then open and filter to remove residue and retain the infusion.
Take twice daily, morning and evening, 10 to 25 ml each time.
Efficacy: expels wind, promotes blood circulation, relieves spasms. For early-stage stroke with neck and nape rigidity, shoulder and back soreness, limb stiffness, occasional aversion to wind and fever.
