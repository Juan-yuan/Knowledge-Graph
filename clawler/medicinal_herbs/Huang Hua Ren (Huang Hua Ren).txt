Ba Du San (Ba du san)
Cultivation and processing of Yellow-flowered Sida (Huanghuaren)
Source
The whole herb of the Malvaceae plant Ba Du San (Ba du san). Harvested in autumn, used fresh or sun-dried.
Morphology
Ba Du San is an erect subshrub about 1 m tall. Young branches are covered with stellate long soft hairs. Leaves dimorphic; petiole 5–10 mm long, covered with stellate soft hairs; stipules awl-shaped, shorter than the petiole; lower leaves broadly rhombic to fan-shaped, 2.5–5 cm long, nearly as wide, apex short-acuminate to rounded, base cuneate, margin with two teeth; upper leaves oblong-elliptic to oblong, 2–3 cm long, both ends blunt to rounded, upper surface sparsely covered with stellate hairs or coarse appressed hairs to nearly glabrous, underside densely covered with gray stellate tomentum. Flowers solitary or clustered at branch tips, pedicel about 1 cm long, densely covered with stellate glandular hairs, with nodes above the middle; calyx cup-shaped, about 7 mm long, lobes triangular, sparsely covered with stellate soft hairs; flowers yellow, 1–1.5 cm in diameter, petals obovate, about 8 mm long; stamens about 5 mm long, covered with long stiff hairs. Fruit nearly globose, about 6 mm in diameter, schizocarps of 8–9 mericarps, sparsely covered with stellate soft hairs, with short awns. Seeds dark brown, smooth, 2 mm long, hilum clothed with white soft hairs. Flowering period: June to November.
Habitat and distribution
Ecological environment: grows on barren slopes and in shrubbery, at pine forest edges, along roadsides and stream banks.
Resource distribution: distributed in southwest China and Guangxi and other regions.
Nature and Taste
Bitter; cold
Effects of Yellow-flowered Sida (Huanghuaren)
Functions
The whole herb of the Malvaceae plant Ba Du San (Ba du san). Used for acute mastitis, acute tonsillitis, carbuncles and furuncles, swelling-toxicity, enteritis, bacillary dysentery, amenorrhea in women, and traumatic injuries from falls and blows.
Channels
Lung; Liver meridians
Indications
Clears heat and resolves toxicity, invigorates blood and removes blood stasis. Treats acute mastitis, acute tonsillitis, carbuncles, swelling-toxicity, enteritis, bacillary dysentery, amenorrhea in women, and traumatic injuries.
1. Dian Nan Ben Cao: Treats women's insufficient lactation, mastitis, breast masses, redness and swelling; disperses various sores and swellings with toxin; treats pediatric hematuria and urinary bleeding; relieves skin itching, disperses wind and reduces fever. Stem and leaf powdered finely, mixed with vinegar and applied to carbuncles and ulcerous sores.
2. Yunnan Chinese Herbal Medicine: Invigorates blood and removes stasis, detoxifies, unites bones, and promotes lactation. Treats amenorrhea, carbuncles, gunshot wounds, and fractures.
3. Selected Yunnan Chinese Herbal Medicines: Anti-inflammatory, detoxifying and promotes tissue regeneration. Treats acute mastitis, acute tonsillitis, enteritis, and bacillary dysentery.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoction, 9–15 g.
External use: appropriate amount, crush and apply as a poultice.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated in pregnancy.
Formulas of Yellow-flowered Sida (Huanghuaren)
1. For scrofula and carbuncles: crush fresh leaves of Ba Du San (Ba du san) and apply to the affected area. (Selected Yunnan Chinese Herbal Medicines)
2. For traumatic injuries: crush fresh leaves of Ba Du San (Ba du san), add brown sugar or wine, mix and apply to the affected area. (Selected Yunnan Chinese Herbal Medicines)
3. For insufficient lactation: 3–5 qian of Ba Du San (Ba du san), stewed with pig's trotters and taken. (Yunnan Chinese Herbal Medicine)
