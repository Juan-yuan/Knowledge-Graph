Cultivation and Processing of Butterfly-bush Flower
Source
The dried flowers or flower buds of Mimenghua, a plant of the Loganiaceae family. Clustered flower buds are picked before opening between February and March, stems and impurities removed, then sun-dried.
Properties and Taste
Sweet, cold.
Processing
Pick out impurities and sieve off ash and soil.
Description
An inflorescence of many small clustered buds. Shape and size vary, surface gray-yellow or light brown, densely covered with downy hairs. Single bud short rod-shaped, thicker at the top and thinner at the base, 3–6 mm long, apex rounded and slightly swollen; calyx bell-shaped, four-cleft; corolla tubular, lobes dark purple, hairs very sparse. Whole material soft and brittle, cross-section black in the center. Slight fragrance; sweet with a slight bitter, pungent note. Best quality when buds are densely clustered, gray-yellow, hairy and soft in texture.
Effects of Butterfly-bush Flower
Functions
The dried flowers or flower buds of Mimenghua, a plant of the Loganiaceae family.
Indications: red swollen painful eyes, excessive tearing and photophobia, dim vision and corneal opacities, wind-strung ulcerating eyes.
Channels
Wang Haogu: enters the Liver meridian.
Main Actions
Expels wind, cools the blood, moistens the Liver and clears the eyes. Treats red swollen painful eyes, excessive tearing and photophobia, dim vision and corneal opacities, wind-strung ulcerating eyes.
Clears the Liver and brightens the eyes: this herb is a specialist for ophthalmic disorders. For eye disease due to blood deficiency with Liver heat it is often combined with Goji Berry (Gou Qi Zi) and Cuscuta Seed (Tu Si Zi). For eye disease due to excess heat in the Liver meridian it is often combined with Qing Xiang Seed (Qing Xiang Zi) and Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua).
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoct 3–9 g; or used in pills or powders.
Formulas of Butterfly-bush Flower
1. For wind invasion with eye symptoms—dimmed vision, sticky tears and photophobia, eyelids with wind papules, gritty obstruction difficult to open, itching or pain, gradually forming a veil membrane with unclear vision, chronic unilateral headache drawing the eyes with progressive reduction in visual acuity, dullness and aching, and sudden red swollen pain: Butterfly-bush Flower (cleaned) (Mi Meng Hua), Abalone Shell (Shi Jue Ming) (boiled with salt in running water for one fu period then filtered and ground to powder), Horsetail (Mu Zei), Tribulus (Ji Li) (stir-fried to remove spines), Notopterygium (Qiang Huo) (remove stems), Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua), equal parts. Grind to a fine powder; each dose one qian, taken with preserved tea (la cha) after meals, twice daily. (Bureau Prescription: Mimenghua Powder)
2. For corneal opacity and eye opacities: Butterfly-bush Flower (Mi Meng Hua) and Phellodendron Bark root (Huang Bai) (washed and scraped) one liang each. Pound and sift into a powder, bind with honey into pills about the size of a parasol-tree seed; each dose ten to fifteen pills after meals, taken at bedtime with warm water, or taken with a decoction sweetened with maltose. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu: Mimenghua Pills)
3. For photophobia and blurred vision due to Liver and Gallbladder deficiency: Butterfly-bush Flower (Mi Meng Hua), Notopterygium (Qiang Huo), Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua), Vitex Fruit (Man Jing Zi), Qing Xiang Seed (Qing Xiang Zi), Horsetail (Mu Zei), Abalone Shell (Shi Jue Ming), Tribulus (Ji Li), Goji Berry (Gou Qi Zi), equal parts. Grind to a powder; each dose three qian, taken after meals with clear tea. (Yin Hai Jing Wei: Mimenghua Powder)
4. For aversion to light and photophobia of the eyes: Butterfly-bush Flower 3 qian, Raw Rehmannia Root (Sheng Di Huang) 2 qian, Scutellaria (Huang Qin) 2 qian. Decoction.
5. For ulceration and damp-rot of the eyelid with excessive tearing: Butterfly-bush Flower 3 qian, Atractylodes (Bai Zhu) 2 qian, Solomon's Seal (Yu Zhu) 5 qian. Decoction. (Formulas 4–5 from Ben Cao Hui Yan)
6. For pediatric malnutrition with attacking eyes and impending blindness: Butterfly-bush Flower one liang, Quisqualis Fruit kernel (Shi Jun Zi rou) 3 qian, White Wuyi (Bai Wu Yi) 5 qian, Picrasma (Hu Huang Lian) 2 qian, Aloe (Lu Hui) 1 qian. Grind together and bind with sugar into pills the size of a chickpea; one pill each morning and evening, taken down with warm water.
7. For all chronic eye disorders from prolonged strain with one eye dominant, causing fatigue of the eye, producing a feeling of swelling, pain and blurred vision: Butterfly-bush Flower 5 qian, Sweet Chrysanthemum (Gan Ju Hua) 2 qian, Ophiopogon (Mai Dong) (heart removed) 8 qian, Angelica sinensis (Dang Gui) 1 qian 5 fen, Solomon's Seal (Yu Zhu) 4 qian. Decoction. (Formulas 6–7 from Ben Cao Hui Yan)
8. For Liver deficiency and decreased visual acuity: Butterfly-bush Flower 12 g, Goji Berry (Gou Qi Zi) 12 g, Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua) 12 g, Raw Rehmannia Root (Sheng Di Huang) 12 g, Chu Shi Seed (Chu Shi Zi) 12 g, Quince (Mu Gua) 6 g, Cortex Fraxini (Qin Pi) 6 g. Roll with honey into pills; each dose 9 g, taken three times daily. (Sichuan Materia Medica, 1982)
9. For night blindness: Butterfly-bush Flower 15 g, Qing Xiang Seed (Qing Xiang Zi) 15 g, Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) 12 g. Grind finely, stuff into pig liver and cook until done then dry and bake, add Plantain Seed (Che Qian Zi), Cuttlebone (Hai Piao Xiao) and Yeming Sha (Yeming Sha) 9 g each, mix to a fine powder; take 9 g morning and evening with warm water, for three consecutive doses. (Gansu TCM Effective Prescriptions Collection)
10. For dizziness: steam Butterfly-bush Flower with a small chicken, strain off dregs and take the soup and meat. (Miao Ethnic Medicine Collection)
11. For pertussis (whooping cough): use Yangerduo Duojian steamed with rice soup oil, honey or sugar; alternatively stuff Yangerduo Duojian into peeled, cored pearl pears, add honey and steam. (Kunming Common Folk Herbs)
