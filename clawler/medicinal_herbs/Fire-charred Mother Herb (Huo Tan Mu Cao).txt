Fire-charred Mother Herb (Huo Tan Mu Cao)

Names
Fire-charcoal-hair (Essentials of the Nature of Raw Medicinal Herbs), Black Charcoal Seed (Extended Illustrated Examination of Plant Names and Substances), Medicine Transporter (Classified Nature of Medicinal Herbs), Mountain Buckwheat Grass (Fujian Folk Medicinal Herbs), Yellow Eel Vine, Dizzying Medicine (Sichuan Materia Medica), Black White Rice Grass (Quanzhou Materia Medica), Fire-charcoal Star, Magpie Sugar Plum (Lingnan Materia Medica), Red Earth Benefit, Black Rice Vine, Water Sandthorn Seed, Quail Cormorant Rice, Water-Reducing Sha (Fujian Chinese Medicinals), Red Plum-leaf, White Rice Grass, Large-leaf Sandbank (Guangdong Chinese Medicinals), Mouse Sugarcane (Guangxi Chinese Medicinals).
Cultivation and Processing of Fire-charcoal-mother Herb
Source
The whole herb of a Polygonaceae plant, Fire-charcoal-mother Herb. Harvested in summer and autumn, sun-dried.
[Morphology]
A perennial erect or semi-climbing herbaceous plant, about 1.5 meters long. Stems slightly winged with ridges, smooth or sparsely haired or glandular-hairy, reclining on the ground or clinging to supports, lower part woody, often branched, prostrate individuals root at the nodes, young branches purple-red. Leaves alternate, petiolate, with a winged petiole; leaf blades ovate-elliptic or ovate-triangular, 7–12 centimeters long, entire or with fine rounded teeth, base truncate, rounded or nearly cordate, sometimes with two auriculate lobes; upper-branch leaves cordate, with short petioles or sessile and clasping the stem; upper surface fresh green or with V-shaped dark markings, underside with hairs along the main vein; stipule sheaths membranous, obliquely truncate. Capitate inflorescences, further arranged into panicles or umbel-like clusters, inflorescence axis often with pustular hairs, without an involucre; bracts smooth, usually acuminate; small flowers white, pale red, or purple; perianth 5-parted; stamens 8; ovary superior, style 3-cleft. Achenes ovoid, black, three-angled, enclosed by persistent perianth. Flowering in September (Sichuan).
[Habitat and Distribution]
Grows on sunny grassy slopes, forest edges, and roadside moist soils in hilly areas. Distributed in Taiwan, Fujian, Jiangxi, Guangdong, Guangxi, Yunnan, Sichuan, and Guizhou. Produced in Guangdong, Guangxi, Sichuan, Guizhou, Fujian and other regions.
Taste and Nature
Sour and sweet, cold.
① Illustrated Materia Medica: taste sour, neutral, non-toxic.
② Compendium of Essential Tastes: taste sour and sweet, nature neutral, non-toxic.
③ Compendium (Bencao Gangmu): sour, neutral, toxic.
④ Essentials of the Nature of Raw Medicinal Herbs: taste sour, nature cold.
⑤ Guangxi Chinese Medicinals: taste slightly sour and slightly astringent, nature slightly cold.
Characteristics
Dried stems very long, brown to brown-purple, with longitudinal wrinkles, internodes rather long, nodes swollen; brittle and easily broken, pith loose. Leaf blades wrinkled, dried yellow or yellow-green, purple-black patches on both sides of the midrib, faintly visible; stipule sheaths tawny yellow-brown, often broken and incomplete. Smell faint, taste slightly bitter. Best when dry and free of impurities.
Effects of Fire-charcoal-mother Herb
Functions
The whole herb of a Polygonaceae plant, Fire-charcoal-mother Herb. Used for diarrhea, dysentery, jaundice, wind-heat sore throat, weakness with dizziness, summer fever in children, convulsions, female leukorrhea, abscesses and damp sores, and traumatic injuries.
Indications
Clears heat and drains dampness, cools the blood and resolves toxicity. Treats diarrhea, dysentery, jaundice, wind-heat sore throat, weakness with dizziness, summer convulsions in children, convulsions, female leukorrhea, abscesses and damp sores, and traumatic injuries.
① Illustrated Materia Medica: dispels wind-heat from the skin, leucorrhea, and painful swollen joints and abscesses of the bones and joints.
② Essentials of the Nature of Raw Medicinal Herbs: stir-fried with honey and eaten can stop dysentery. Apply to sores, apply to bruises, paste on ulcerated feet, draw out toxins, dry exudation, and promote closure.
③ Lingnan Collection of Medicinal Herbs: treats childhood febrile convulsions and abdominal distention.
④ Fujian Folk Medicinal Herbs: activates blood, resolves toxicity, stops dysentery, treats leukorrhea.
⑤ Sichuan Materia Medica: treats dizziness and blurred vision, qi deficiency and tinnitus.
⑥ Guangzhou Military Common Chinese Medicinals Handbook: clears and drains damp-heat, reduces stagnation and resolves toxicity. Treats dysentery, enteritis, indigestion, hepatitis, tonsillitis, pharyngitis, boils and abscesses, traumatic sprains, dermatitis, eczema, and itching.
⑦ Nanchuan Common Chinese Medicinals Handbook: tonifies qi and activates blood circulation, dispels wind and clears heat. Treats weakness, wind-heat conditions, dizziness, and pain from blood and qi stagnation.
⑧ Guangdong Chinese Medicinals: cools the blood and resolves toxicity. Treats childhood summer heat.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoct, 9–15 g; fresh material 30–60 g.
External use: appropriate amount, pounded and applied; or decocted water for washing.
Formulas Using Fire-charcoal-mother Herb
① For red and white dysentery: Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao) and
Sea Gold Sand (Hai jin sha) pounded to extract juice, diluted with boiling water, add a little sugar and take. (Lingnan Collection of Medicinal Herbs)
② For dysentery, enteritis, indigestion: Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao), Little Phoenix-tail (Xiao feng wei), Cloth-dregs Leaf (Bu zha ye) each six qian, decoct in water and take. (Guangdong Selected Prescriptions of Chinese Medicinals)
③ For acute and chronic bacillary dysentery: Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao), Wild Rose Mallow (Ye mu dan) each two liang. Decoction in water, one dose daily, divided into three portions. For chronic bacillary dysentery, the same dosages can be used as a retention enema twice daily, seven to ten days per course. (Guangxi New Therapies of Chinese Medicinals Prescription Collection)
④ For damp-heat jaundice: Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao) one liang,
Chicken-bone Herb (Ji gu cao) one liang, decoct in water and take. (Guangxi Chinese Medicinals)
⑤ For female leukorrhea: fresh Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao) two to three liang, white
Cockscomb Flower (Bai ji guan hua) three to five blooms. Add water as appropriate, decoct to half a bowl, take after meals, twice daily. (Fujian Folk Medicinal Herbs)
⑥ For skin wind-heat, leucorrhea, and painful swollen joints and abscesses of bones and joints: Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao) leaves, decocted water for washing. (Lingnan Collection of Medicinal Herbs)
⑦ For abdominal distention: Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao), decocted water for fumigation and washing and pounded application. (Lingnan Collection of Medicinal Herbs)
⑧ For abscesses: fresh Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao) one liang, decocted in water, adjusted with wine and taken; the dregs mixed with honey or glutinous rice and pounded, applied to the affected area. (Fujian Chinese Medicinals)
⑨ For eczema: fresh Fire-charcoal-mother Herb (Huo tan mu cao) one to two liang, decoct and take; also take fresh whole herb, decoct and wash. (Fujian Chinese Medicinals)
