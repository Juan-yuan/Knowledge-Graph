Names

Cultivation and Preparation of Plowhead Grass (Li Tou Cao)
Source
From the whole herb of the Violaceae plant Heartleaf Violet (Xin Ye Jin Cai). Harvest the whole herb during the fruit ripening period in April to May, remove soil, use fresh or sun-dry.
Morphology
Heartleaf Violet is a perennial herb. No aerial stem or stolons. Rhizome short and thick, nodes close, about 4–5 mm thick; many branched roots, relatively stout and elongated. Leaves numerous, basal; petioles usually nearly equal to the leaf blade during flowering and much longer than the blade during fruiting; stipules short, adnate to the lower part of the petiole, about 1 cm long, the free part spreading; leaf blade ovate, broadly ovate or triangular-ovate, rarely reniform, 3–8 cm long and 3–8 cm wide, apex acute or slightly obtuse, base deeply cordate or broadly cordate, margin with many rounded obtuse teeth, both surfaces glabrous or sparsely short-hairy. Flowers pale purple; flowers do not rise much above the leaves; near the middle there are two linear-lanceolate small bracts; calyx lobes broadly lanceolate, apex gradually acuminate, basal appendage about 2 mm long, apex obtuse or truncate; upper and lateral petals obovate, lower petal long obcordate; spur cylindrical, 4–5 mm long and about 2 mm thick. Capsule ellipsoid, about 1 cm long.
Habitat and Distribution
Ecological environment: grows on forest edges, open grassy areas under forests, mountain grass clumps, and beside streams and valleys.
Resource distribution: distributed in the southwest and in Jiangsu, Anhui, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Hunan and other regions.
Taste and Properties
Bitter; slightly pungent; cold in nature
Effects of Plowhead Grass (Li Tou Cao)
Efficacy
The whole herb of the Violaceae plant Heartleaf Violet (Xin Ye Jin Cai). Mainly treats carbuncles and swellings, mammary carbuncles, intestinal abscess with bleeding, purulent osteomyelitis, jaundice, red swollen painful eyes, scrofula, external traumatic bleeding, and snakebite.
Meridians
Liver; Spleen meridians
Indications
Clears heat and detoxifies, resolves stasis and discharges pus, cools the blood and clears the liver. Indicated for carbuncles and swellings, mammary carbuncles, intestinal abscess with bleeding, purulent osteomyelitis, jaundice, red swollen painful eyes, scrofula, external traumatic bleeding, and snakebite.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoction, 9–15 g, fresh 30–60 g; or take pounded juice.
External use: appropriate amount, pounded and applied as a poultice.
Prescriptions of Plowhead Grass (Li Tou Cao)
1. For nodules on the back: roots of Dabei Grass (Da Bei Cao), crush and mix with sugar or red pepper and apply to the affected area; surround the area with a ring of flour dust. (Nanjing Folk Herbal Medicine)
2. For carbuncles, boils, and unnamed swellings: equal amounts of fresh Plowhead Grass (Li Tou Cao) and fresh Wild Chrysanthemum leaves (Ye Ju Hua), mash together and apply to the lesion; or mash the whole fresh Plowhead Grass with a little white sugar and apply, change once daily. Also take one wineglass of the mashed juice internally. (Zhejiang Common Folk Herbs)
3. For abscesses, boils, carbuncles, mastitis, and felons: mash fresh Plowhead Grass and apply; or dry and grind to a powder, mix with egg white and apply, change one to two times daily.
4. For boils: grind Plowhead Grass into powder, make pills with rice paste about the size of a wutong seed, nine pills per dose, swallow with hot water.
5. For postpartum blood stasis with knife-like stabbing pain: one liang of fresh Plowhead Grass (about 50 g), chop finely, mix with two eggs and beat together, stir-fry slightly with oil, then add water and decoct for oral administration.
6. For venomous snakebite: mash fresh Plowhead Grass and apply to the wound, change one to two times daily. (Prescriptions from Jiangxi Folk Herbal Medicine)
7. For external traumatic bleeding: Plowhead Grass (Li Tou Cao), Wood Sorrel (Cujiangcao)
Each in appropriate amounts, mash and apply externally to the wound, cover with gauze and press and bandage; or use Plowhead Grass alone mashed and applied. (Jiangxi "Herbal Manual")
8. For brine poisoning: mash fresh Plowhead Grass to obtain two wineglassfuls of juice, dilute with boiling water and drink. (Zhejiang Common Folk Herbs)
