Cultivation and Preparation of Saposhnikovia Root (Fangfeng)
Source
The root of Saposhnikovia, a plant of the Apiaceae (Umbelliferae) family. It can be harvested in spring or autumn. After digging up the roots, remove stems, leaves and soil, sun-dry until about eighty percent dry, tie into bundles, then continue sun-drying until thoroughly dry.
Properties and Taste
Pungent, sweet; warm.
Processing Methods
1. Saposhnikovia Root (Fangfeng): remove remaining stems, soak in water, take out, fully moisten, slice, and sun-dry.
2. Stir-fried Saposhnikovia Root (Fangfeng): take Fangfeng slices, lightly fry in a pan until deep yellow, remove and cool.
3. Charred preparation: place Fangfeng slices in a pan, stir-fry over medium heat until the exterior is black and the interior is yellow-brown. Sprinkle an appropriate amount of water to extinguish sparks, remove, and air overnight.
4. Honey-fried: take Fangfeng, add honey and fry until the honey is absorbed, then cool. For each 1 kg of Fangfeng slices, use 0.3 kg of honey.
Description
Roots are long-conical or long-cylindrical, tapering toward the lower part, sometimes slightly curved, 15–30 cm long, 0.5–2 cm in diameter.
Surface gray-brown, rough, with longitudinal wrinkles, numerous transverse lenticels and dot-like protruding fine root scars.
The root crown has distinct dense annular rings; some parts have obvious dense rings, and some show brownish hairy leaf bases remaining on the ring scars.
Light in weight, loose texture, easily broken; fracture surface uneven, cortex light brown with fissures and scattered yellow-brown oily spots, wood portion pale yellow.
Distinctive odor, slightly sweet taste.
Best quality is stout roots with fracture surfaces showing cortex light brown and wood pale yellow.
Effects of Saposhnikovia Root (Fangfeng)
Efficacy
The root of Saposhnikovia (Apiaceae).
Main indications: external contraction by wind-cold, headache, dizziness, neck stiffness, wind-cold-damp Bi syndrome, soreness and pain of bones and joints, limb cramps, tetanus.
Meridians Entered
Enters the Bladder channel, Liver channel, and Spleen channel.
Indications
Releases the exterior, dispels wind, overcomes dampness, relieves pain. Treats external wind-cold, headache, dizziness, neck stiffness, wind-cold-damp Bi syndrome, soreness and pain of joints, limb cramps, tetanus.
1. "Benjing": treats severe wind causing vertiginous headache, aversion to wind, wind pathogen, blindness, wind moving throughout the body, painful numbness of bones and joints, vexation and fullness.
2. "Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu": neutralizes the toxicity of Aconite (Fuzi).
3. "Bielu": treats hypochondriac pain, wind in the flank affecting head and face, limb contractures, and internal spasms in the breast and sores.
4. "Rihuazi Materia Medica": treats thirty-six types of wind, all male debility from labor, tonifies the center and benefits the spirit, wind-red eyes, stops tears and flaccidity, unblocks and benefits the five viscera and channels, treats overexertion and injury, emaciation and night sweats, vexation with heaviness; calms the spirit, steadies the will, and harmonizes qi and vessels.
5. "Zhenzhu Nang": for the body, dispels upper wind; for the extremities, dispels lower wind.
6. "Yao Lei Fa Xiang": a general wind remedy. Drains lung excess, disperses stagnant qi in head and eyes, removes wind pathogen from the upper burner.
7. Wang Haozhan: disperses liver qi.
8. "Changsha Yaojie": promotes circulation in channels and collaterals, expels dampness, unblocks joints, relieves pain, relaxes tendons and vessels, relieves cramps and contractures, revitalizes limbs and joints, treats paralysis, restrains spontaneous sweating and night sweats, stops leakage and uterine bleeding.
9. "Ben Cao Qiu Yuan": detoxifies poisons from Aconite (Wutou), Genkwa (Yuanhua), wild mushrooms and other hot toxic medicines.
Usage and Dosage
Internal: decoct, 5–10 g; or used in pills or powders.
External: appropriate amount, decoct for fumigation and washing.
Generally used raw; stir-fried for diarrhea; charred for hemostasis.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated in blood-deficiency with acute spasms, or headache not caused by wind pathogen.
1. "Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu": incompatible with dried ginger (Ganjiang), Veratrum (Lilu), Smilax (Bailian), and Genkwa (Yuanhua).
2. "Tang Materia Medica": wary of Bi Xie (Bixie).
3. "Ben Cao Jing Shu": contraindicated in diseases with blood-deficiency spasms, headache not due to wind-cold, diarrhea not due to cold-damp, constipation of the bowels, pediatric spleen-deficiency convulsions, chronic convulsions and spleen wind, qi rising causing vomiting, fire rising causing cough, yin-deficiency night sweats, yang-deficiency spontaneous sweating, and similar conditions.
4. "Depei Bencao": contraindicated in primordial qi deficiency and diseases not caused by wind-damp.
