Cultivation and Processing of Jasmine Leaf
Source
The leaves of Jasmine (Moli), a plant of the Oleaceae family. Harvested in summer and autumn, washed clean, used fresh or sun-dried.
[Original morphology]
Erect or climbing shrub, up to 3 m tall. Branchlets cylindrical or slightly compressed, sometimes hollow, sparsely covered with soft hairs. Leaves opposite, simple; petiole 2–6 mm long, with short soft hairs and a joint. Leaf blade papery, circular, ovate-elliptic or obovate, 4–12.5 cm long, 2–7.5 cm wide, ends rounded or blunt, base sometimes slightly cordate; except for the vein axils beneath which often bear tufts of hairs, otherwise glabrous. Inflorescences cymose, terminal, usually with three flowers, sometimes solitary or up to five; peduncle 1–4.5 cm long, with short soft hairs; bracts minute, conical; pedicel 0.3–2 cm long; flowers very fragrant; calyx glabrous or sparsely with short soft hairs, lobes linear; corolla white, corolla tube 0.7–1.5 cm long, lobes oblong to nearly rounded. Fruit globose, about 1 cm in diameter, purple-black. Flowering May to August, fruiting July to September.
[Habitat and distribution]
Widely cultivated throughout southern China. Originally native to India.
Properties and flavor
Pungent, slightly bitter, warm in nature.
Appearance
Leaves mostly curled and wrinkled; when flattened they are broadly ovate or elliptic, 4–12 cm long and 2–7 cm wide, ends rather blunt, with yellow clustered hairs in the vein axils beneath; petiole short, 2–6 mm long, slightly pubescent. Slight fragrance, slightly astringent taste.
Effects of Jasmine Leaf
Efficacy
The leaves of Jasmine (Moli) of the Oleaceae family. Used for externally contracted febrile disorders, dysentery with abdominal distention, swollen painful feet (beriberi), and poisonous insect stings or bites.
Channels
Lung; Stomach meridians
Indications
Dispel wind and release the exterior; reduce swelling and relieve pain. Indicated for externally contracted fever, dysentery with abdominal distention, swollen painful feet (beriberi), and poisonous insect stings or bites.
Usage and dosage
Internal: decoct, 6–10 g.
External: appropriate amount, decoct for washing or crush and apply as a poultice.
