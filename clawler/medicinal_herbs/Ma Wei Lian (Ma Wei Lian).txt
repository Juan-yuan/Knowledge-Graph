Cultivation and Processing of Mawei Lian
Source
The rhizomes and roots of plants of the Ranunculaceae family such as Manyleaf Thalictrum (Duoye Tangsongcao) and Baikal Thalictrum (Beijiaer Tangsongcao). Harvested from September to November to the following January to February; after digging, shake off earth and sand, trim off stems and shoots, and sun-dry.
Taste and Properties
Taste bitter; nature cold
Description
The dried rhizome has many bulbils at the upper end, each bulbil about 4 millimeters thick, with traces of stem and shoot scars and often enclosed by thin scale-leaf fragments. The rhizome is elongated, externally brownish; the ventral surface densely produces bundled fibrous roots shaped like a horse tail; the fibrous roots are 13 to 25 centimeters long, 2 to 3 millimeters thick, externally reddish-yellow or golden yellow, glossy, with fine longitudinal striations; the old cork and cortex often shed in ring-like segments, and those not yet shed can be removed by rubbing with the fingers.
Light in weight, brittle and easily broken. The rhizome cross-section has a brownish outer ring and a yellow woody pith inside; the fibrous root cross-section is deep yellow with a thin layer of golden-yellow epidermis externally. Odor slight, taste slightly bitter. The best quality is when the root strands are uniform and golden yellow in color.
Effects of Mawei Lian
Functions and Actions
The rhizomes and roots of Ranunculaceae plants such as Manyleaf Thalictrum (Duoye Tangsongcao) and Baikal Thalictrum (Beijiaer Tangsongcao).
Main Indications
Dysentery, enteritis, infectious hepatitis, common cold, measles, carbuncles and furuncles, conjunctivitis.

Indications
Clears heat and dries dampness, detoxifies. Treats dysentery, enteritis, infectious hepatitis, common cold, measles, carbuncles and furuncles, conjunctivitis.
1. From "Gangmu Taiyi": removes pathogenic heat from the skin, inner membrane, exterior and sinews; used for pediatric colds and pox.
2. From "Commonly Used Tibetan Medicinal Herbs": clears heat and detoxifies, dispels wind and cools the blood, reduces inflammation and stops dysentery. Treats conjunctivitis, infectious hepatitis, carbuncles and furuncles, dysentery. Leaves and flowers treat arthritis.
3. From "Xinjiang Handbook of Chinese Medicinal Herbs": clears heat and dries dampness.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoct as a decoction, 3–15 g, or grind to a powder, or make into an instant powder.
External use: appropriate amount; fresh material pounded and applied; or decoction used to wash; or dry product ground to powder and sprinkled; or made into an ointment and applied.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in those with spleen and stomach deficiency-cold.
