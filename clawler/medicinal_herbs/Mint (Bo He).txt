Cultivation and processing of Mint (bohe)
Source
This product is the dried aerial parts of the Lamiaceae family Mentha genus plant Mint (bohe, Mentha haplocalyx Briq.). In the summer and autumn seasons when stems and leaves are luxuriant or when flowers have opened to the third whorl, choose sunny days, harvest in successive cuts, and dry in the sun or in the shade.
Properties and flavor
Acrid, cool.
Processing
Pick out impurities, remove remaining roots; first shake off the leaves and set them aside, then spray the stems with clean water until thoroughly moistened, cut into sections, sun-dry, and finally mix evenly with the leaves.
Description
The whole dried herb: stems are quadrangular-cylindrical, 15–35 cm long, 2–4 mm in diameter, yellow-brown with purple or green, with nodes; internodes 3–7 cm long; upper parts have opposite branches; surfaces covered with white pubescence, denser on the ridges; texture brittle, easily broken, fracture surface whitish, hollow. Leaves opposite, often curled and wrinkled, mostly broken; upper surface dark green, lower surface light green, with white pubescence; brittle. Branch tips often bear verticillaster inflorescences, yellow-brown, many corollas remaining. Aromatic smell, spicy-cool taste. The best are whole-dried, rootless, leaf-abundant, green in color, and strongly aromatic.
Actions of Mint (bohe)
Functions and indications
This product is the dried aerial parts of the Lamiaceae family Mentha genus plant Mint (bohe).
Used for wind-heat common cold, early-stage warm-epidemic febrile disease, headache, red eyes, sore throat with obstruction, mouth ulcers, urticaria, measles, and chest and hypochondriac distention.
Channels entered
Lung channel, Liver channel.
Main indications
Disperse wind-heat; clear the head and eyes; release rashes. Used for wind-heat common cold, early-stage warm-epidemic febrile disease, headache, red eyes, sore throat with obstruction, mouth ulcers, urticaria, measles, and chest and hypochondriac distention.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoction, 3–6 g, should not be boiled for long, add near the end of decoction; or used in pills and powders.
External use: appropriate amount, decocted water applied first or crushed juice applied as a topical dressing.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated in yin deficiency with blood dryness, exuberant liver yang, or exterior deficiency with profuse sweating.
1. From Yao Xing Lun: Newly ill convalescents should not eat; it causes continuous deficient sweating.
2. From Qianjin·Food Therapy: Promotes emaciation and thirst disease.
3. From Benjing Fengyuan: Excessive or prolonged use causes deficient cold; do not use in yin-deficiency fever, cough, spontaneous sweating.
4. From Bencao Congxin: Its acrid aroma diffuses qi; excessive use harms the lungs and injures the heart; the deficient should avoid it.
Formulas using Mint (bohe)
① Clear the upper burner and transform phlegm, benefit the throat and diaphragm, treat wind-heat: powdered mint refined with honey into pills the size of gorgon seed; take one pill at a time. White sugar may also be used. (Jianbian Danfang)
② Treat red, painful, suppurating eyes: mint soaked overnight in fresh ginger juice, sun-dried and powdered, use one qian each time, soak in boiling water to wash. (Mingmu Jingyan Fang)
③ Treat scrofulous swellings that have formed lumps, are painful, ulcerate, with continuous pus discharge irrespective of distance: one bundle of mint about bowl-sized (shade-dried), ten strips of Gleditsia pods (zaojia; each one chi two cun long and not worm-eaten, remove black skin, coat with vinegar, roast until golden). Crush, soak in one hu of wine for three nights, take out and sun-dry, then soak three more nights; continue until all wine is used up, bake dry, grind into powder, mix with cooked rice to form pills the size of wutongzi. Before meals take twenty pills with Astragalus (huangqi) decoction; halve the dose for children. (Shenghuifang: Mint pills)
④ Treat wind-induced itching: large mint, cicada slough equally powdered, take one qian with warm wine. (Yonglei Qianfang)
⑤ Treat bloody dysentery: decoction of mint leaves taken alone. (Puji Fang)
⑥ Treat continuous nosebleed: drop mint juice into nostril; or boil dry mint in water, wrap with cotton and pack into the nose. (Benshi Fang)
⑦ Treat bee and wasp stings: apply mint plaster. (Meng Shen, Bixiao Fang)
⑧ Treat fire toxin sores like cauterization, internal fire-toxin causing sores on the buttocks with oozing juices: frequently apply decocted mint. (Yishuo)
⑨ Treat earache: drop fresh mint crushed juice into the ear. (Mindong Bencao)
⑩ Treat men and women with wind-cold cough and nasal congestion with heavy voice: wild mint (ye bohe) 2 qian, aged tangerine peel (chenpi) 2 qian, apricot kernel (bitter apricot kernel, kuxingren) 2 qian (skin and tips removed); add bamboo leaves (danzhuye) 15 pieces, decoct in water and take. (Diannan Bencao)
⑾ Treat conjunctivitis: wash mint leaves with cold boiled water and then soak in breast milk for 10–30 minutes. Rinse affected eye with 5% saline warm water, then place mint leaf over the affected eye for 10 minutes and replace as needed, several times daily. (Fujian Yaowuzi)
⑿ Treat cerebrospinal leak, nasal fetid discharge: wild mint in any amount, decoct in water, take with rice wine dropwise. (Diannan Bencao)
⒀ Treat dry or moist scabies arising from damp-heat causing severe persistent itching: mint 1 liang, Stemona (baibu) 1 liang, Kochia seed (difuzi) 1 liang. Decoction used to wash one to two times daily. (Jiren Jiyan Fang)
⒁ Treat hidden skin rash that does not surface with itching: mint leaves 10 g (bohe), Schizonepeta (jingjie) 10 g, Saposhnikovia (fangfeng) 10 g, cicada slough (chantui) 6 g. Decoction taken orally. (Sichuan Zhongyao Zhi 1980)
Dietary therapy recipes
1. Chrysanthemum-Mulberry-Mint-Bamboo Leaf Tea
Chrysanthemum (juhua) 5 g, Mulberry leaf (sangye) 5 g, Mint (bohe) 3 g, Lophatherum leaf (danzhuye) 30 g, Imperata root (baimaogen) 30 g. Put all ingredients in a teapot, pour boiling water and steep for 10 minutes. Use as a tea and drink as desired.
Functions: Acrid and cool to release the exterior, clears and benefits the throat. Indications: wind-heat common cold, fever and headache, spontaneous sweating and nasal congestion, swollen painful throat, cough with viscous sputum.
2. Mint-Xanthium-Magnolia-Angelica Tea
Mint (bohe) 6 g, Xanthium fruit (cangerzi) 12 g, Magnolia flower (xinyi) 6 g, Angelica dahurica (baizhi) 6 g, white part of scallion (congbai) 3 stalks, tea leaves (chaye) 2 g. Put the above medicines into a pot, decoct in water, remove dregs and take the decoction. Drink as tea.
Functions: Disperse wind and open the orifices. Indications: rhinitis, sinusitis and related disorders.
3. Mint with Brown Sugar Drink
Mint (bohe) 15 g, brown sugar (hongtang) 60 g. Decoction and sweeten with sugar, can be used as a tea.
Functions: Dietary therapy for liver cancer — this medicinal diet clears heat, benefits diuresis and reduces jaundice; may be selected for jaundice and ascites.
4. Apricot Kernel and Mint Porridge
Apricot kernel (bitter apricot kernel, kuxingren) 30 g (peel and tips removed), fresh mint (bohe) 10 g, hulled rice (gengmi) 50 g. Boil the apricot kernels in boiling water until 70% cooked, add the hulled rice and continue to cook; when nearly done add the mint and cook until finished.
Functions: Acrid dispersing to release the exterior, warms the lungs and stops wheezing.
5. Mint Porridge
Fresh mint (bohe) 30 g, japonica rice 60 g. Decoction of mint is prepared and strained for the liquid; cook the japonica rice into porridge, when cooked add the mint decoction and simmer briefly. One dose daily, divide into morning and evening portions.
Functions: Unblock the diaphragm and benefit the throat, disperse wind-heat. Indications: early-stage measles, exterior wind-heat.
6. Mulberry Leaf-Mint-Loquat Tea
Mulberry leaf (sangye) 500 g, yellow chrysanthemum (juhua) 250 g, loquat leaf (pipaye) 250 g, mint (bohe) 250 g. Grind the above into a coarse powder, pack into clean gauze bags, each bag 10–15 g. Take one bag per cup, pour boiling water and steep, cover and let sit for 10 minutes. Drink as a tea frequently.
Functions: Disperse wind and clear heat, release the exterior and clear the lungs. Indications: early-stage wind-heat common cold.
