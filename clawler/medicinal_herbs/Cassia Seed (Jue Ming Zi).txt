Cultivation and Preparation of Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi)
Source
They are the mature seeds of the leguminous plants Cassia (Jue Ming) and Small Cassia (Xiao Jue Ming). At late autumn when the fruits mature and the pods turn yellow-brown they are harvested by cutting the whole plant and drying, threshing out the seeds, and removing impurities.
Properties and Taste
Salty and bitter; neutral to cool; non-toxic
Preparation
Fried Cassia Seed (Chao Jue Ming Zi): Take clean Cassia seeds, place in a pan and stir-fry until they emit a slight aroma, remove and let cool.
Appearance
(1) Jue Ming (Cassia seed, Jue Ming): Four-angled short cylindrical, one end bluntly rounded, the other end oblique with a point, length 4–6 mm, width 2–3 mm. Surface brown-green or dark brown, smooth and glossy; there is one raised ridge on both dorsal and ventral surfaces; on each side of the ridge there is an oblique light-brown linear groove running from the hilum toward the micropyle. Texture hard. In transverse section the seed coat is thin; endosperm gray-white, semi-translucent; embryo yellow, two cotyledons overlapping and folded in an S-shape. Whole seeds have a slight odor; broken seeds have a faint beany odor; taste slightly bitter and somewhat sticky.
(2) Small Jue Ming (Xiao Jue Ming): Short cylindrical, length 3–5 mm, width 2–2.5 mm. On each side of the ridges there is a broad light yellow-brown band. The best quality has plump grains and a green-brown color.
Effects of Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi)
Efficacy
They are the mature seeds of the leguminous plants Cassia (Jue Ming) and Small Cassia (Xiao Jue Ming).
Main indications: wind-heat red eyes, green blindness, sparrow eye, hypertension, hepatitis, cirrhotic ascites, habitual constipation.
Meridians
Liver Meridian
;
Gallbladder Meridian
;
Kidney Meridian
Indications
Clears the liver, brightens the eyes, promotes diuresis, and relieves constipation. Treats wind-heat red eyes, green blindness, sparrow eye, hypertension, hepatitis, cirrhotic ascites, habitual constipation.
1. Ben Jing: Treats green blindness, ocular discharge with red and white membranes, red painful eyes, lacrimation; long-term use benefits essence and improves vision.
2. Bie Lu: Treats bluish discoloration of the lips and mouth.
3. Yao Xing Lun: Benefits the five viscera and eliminates liver heat.
4. Rihuazi Materia Medica: Aids liver qi, benefits essence and fluids; as a ground powder applied topically it reduces swelling and toxicity; cauterizing the temples treats headache, and applied to the crown stops nasal bleeding; used as a pillow better than black beans, treats head wind and brightens the eyes.
5. Ben Cao Yan Yi Bu Yi: Resolves snake venom.
6. Sheng Cao Yao Xing Bei Yao: Treats pediatric five "gan" disorders and is used to rub ringworm and scabies.
7. Yi Lin Zuan Yao: Drains pathogenic water.
8. Hunan Materia Medica: Brightens the eyes and promotes diuresis. Treats dizziness, beriberi, edema, pulmonary abscess, and chest obstruction.
9. Guangzhou Troops Handbook of Common Chinese Herbs: Clears the liver and brightens the eyes, promotes diuresis and relieves constipation. Treats hepatitis, cirrhotic ascites, hypertension, pediatric malnutrition (gan accumulation), night blindness, wind-heat eye pain, and habitual constipation.
Dosage and Administration
Oral administration: decoction, 9–15 g.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in cases of diarrhea and low blood pressure. Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu: Yishi (Achillea seed) serves as its guiding herb; incompatible with hemp seed (Da Ma Zi).
Formulas Containing Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi)
1. For loss of sight, with no other ocular disease and no visual perception, as if seeing through silk: Horseshoe Cassia Seed (Ma Ti Jue Ming) two sheng, pounded and sieved; take with congee one fang-cun spoonful. Avoid fish, garlic, pork, and pungent vegetables. (Seng Shen Ji Fang: Jue Ming San)
2. For red swollen painful eyes: stir-fried Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) ground, mixed with tea, apply to both temples; replace when dry. Also treats hot head-wind headache. (Zhai Yuan Fang)
3. For sparrow eye: Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) 2 liang, Kochia seed (Di Fu Zi) 1 liang. Pound and sieve into a powder. After meals, take one qian mixed with clear congee. (Sheng Hui Fang)
4. To nourish the liver and brighten the eyes: Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) one sheng, Vitex seed (Man Jing Zi) one sheng (soak in good wine five sheng, boil away the wine and sun-dry). Pound and sieve into a powder. Each dose take two qian with warm water, after meals and before bed. (Sheng Hui Fang: Jue Ming Zi San)
5. For acute conjunctivitis: Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) 3 qian, Chrysanthemum flower (Ju Hua) 3 qian, Vitex seed (Man Jing Zi) 2 qian, Horsetail (Mu Zei) 2 qian; decoct in water and take. (Hebei Chinese Materia Medica Handbook)
6. For hypertension: (a) Cassia Seed appropriate amount, fry until yellow, grind into coarse powder, add sugar and soak in boiling water; each dose 3 g, three times daily. (b) Cassia Seed 15 g, Prunella (Xia Ku Cao) 9 g, decoct in water and take continuously for one month. (National Compilation of Chinese Herbs)
7. For pediatric malnutrition accumulation: Grass Cassia Seed (Cao Jue Ming) 9 g, grind to powder; one chicken liver, mash, add a little white wine, mix into a cake, steam until cooked and take. (Jiangxi Herbal Medicine)
8. For chronic ringworm not healing: Cassia Seed ground to fine powder, mix with a small amount of calomel (Qing Fen), rub the lesion to cause slight breakage, then apply the medicine. (Bai Yi Xuan Fang)
9. For long-standing blindness: Cassia Seed two sheng ground to powder; take one spoonful after meals with thin congee.
10. For acute conjunctivitis: Grass Cassia Seed (Cao Jue Ming), Chrysanthemum flower (Ju Hua), Cicada molting (Chan Tui), Celosia seed (Qing Xiang Zi) each 15 g; decoct in water and take. (Qingdao Chinese Herbal Medicine Handbook)
11. For red swollen eyes: stir-fry Cassia Seed, grind fine, mix with tea to a paste and apply to the temples; replace when dry; swelling subsides overnight.
12. For hot head-wind headache: same prescription as above.
13. For nosebleed that will not stop: Cassia Seed three qian ground to powder, add water to make a paste, apply to the chest.
14. For spreading ringworm: Cassia Seed one liang, three qian of powder, add a little mercury and calomel (Qing Fen), grind extremely fine until mercury specks cannot be seen; rub the lesion to break the skin then apply the medicine.
15. For early stage back ulcers: Grass Cassia Seed (raw, Cao Jue Ming) one sheng, pound; raw licorice (Gan Cao) one liang by weight, cut up. Add three sheng of water, boil down to one sheng, warm and divide into two doses. (Pu Ji Fang)
16. For acute keratitis: Cassia Seed 15 g, Chrysanthemum flower 9 g, Gu Jing Cao 9 g, Schizonepeta (Jing Jie) 9 g, Coptis (Huang Lian) 6 g, Akebia (Mu Tong) 12 g; decoct in water and take. (Sichuan Materia Medica, 1979)
17. For night blindness: Cassia Seed and Goji berry (Gou Qi Zi) each 9 g, an appropriate amount of pig liver; decoct, eat the liver and take the soup. (Zhejiang Medicinal Plants)
18. For wind-heat migraine: Cassia Seed and Wild Chrysanthemum (Ye Ju Hua) each 9 g, Ligusticum (Chuan Xiong) 6 g, Vitex seed (Man Jing Zi) 6 g, Whole Scorpion (Quan Xie) 6 g; decoct and take. (Zhejiang Medicinal Plants)
19. For chronic constipation and stubborn post-stroke constipation: Cassia Seed one sheng, stir-fry until aromatic, grind to fine powder, moisten with water and form into pills; take three times daily, one qian each time, continue for 3–5 days until bowel movements are regular and formed without diarrhea; thereafter take a small amount daily to maintain regularity and promote appetite and recovery. (Ben Cao Tui Chen)
20. For habitual constipation: Cassia Seed 18 g, Prunus seed (Yu Li Ren) 18 g; brew with boiling water as a tea. (Anhui Chinese Herbs)
21. For stomatitis: Cassia Seed 60 g; concentrate decoction and frequently gargle. (Anhui Chinese Herbs)
22. For fungal vaginitis: Cassia Seed appropriate amount; decoction used as fumigation and washing for the vulva and vagina. (Zhejiang Medicinal Plants)
Dietary Recipes
1. Grass Cassia Seed and Rabbit Liver Soup
Two rabbit livers, Grass Cassia Seed (Cao Jue Ming) 12 g, a little salt. Put the Grass Cassia Seed into a small cloth bag and together with the rabbit livers place into a pot with clear water and simmer. Season with salt. Drink the soup and eat the rabbit liver with the meal.
Effect: Clears the liver and brightens the eyes, tonifies the middle and benefits qi. Indications: red swollen painful eyes, blurred vision, excessive tearing and other eye disorders.
2. Cassia Seed, Sophora Flower (Huai Hua) and Cattail Pollen (Pu Huang) Tea
Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) 10 g, Sophora flower (Huai Hua) 6 g, Cattail pollen (Pu Huang) 6 g. Put the three ingredients into a thermos, pour boiling water over them, cover and steep for 10 minutes. One dose per day, drink frequently as a tea.
Effect: Lowers blood pressure and blood lipids. Indications: coronary heart disease, hypertension, atherosclerosis, hypercholesterolemia, etc.
3. Double Jue Ming Congee (Shi Jue Ming and Jue Ming Zi)
Shi Jue Ming 25 g, Cassia Seed 10 g, White Chrysanthemum (Bai Ju Hua) 15 g, japonica rice 100 g, rock sugar 6 g. Stir-fry the Cassia Seed until aromatic and remove. Put the white chrysanthemum and Shi Jue Ming into a clay pot and decoct to obtain the juice; strain and discard residues; wash the rice and cook with the medicinal decoction into a thin congee, add rock sugar and eat. Take morning and evening; a course is 3–5 days.
Effect: Nourishes the liver and suppresses rising yang, clears the liver and brightens the eyes. Indicated for red swollen painful eyes, photophobia with excessive tearing, head fullness and headache, or dizziness, blurred vision, and dry eyes due to liver and kidney deficiency or hyperactive liver yang.
4. Chrysanthemum and Cassia Congee
White Chrysanthemum (Bai Ju Hua) and Grass Cassia Seed (Cao Jue Ming) each 10 g, rice 100 g, a little rock sugar. Stir-fry the Grass Cassia Seed until aromatic, decoct with chrysanthemum to obtain the juice, add rice and cook into a thin congee; when cooked add rock sugar and simmer briefly. Take once daily.
This congee can clear the liver, brighten the eyes, lower blood pressure, and can also treat red swollen painful eyes, photophobia with tearing, headache and dizziness, and constipation.
5. Cassia Seed, Cistanche (Rou Cong Rong) and Honey Tea
Stir-fried Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) and Cistanche (Rou Cong Rong) each 10 g, honey to taste. Put both into a teacup, pour boiling water, cover and steep for 10 minutes, add honey to taste. Drink as a tea frequently.
Effect: Moistens the intestines and relieves constipation. Indicated for habitual constipation and senile constipation.
6. Cassia Seed, Nan Sha Shen, Goji and Achyranthes Soup
Cassia Seed 9 g, Nan Sha Shen 15 g, Achyranthes (Niu Xi) 9 g, Goji berry (Gou Qi Zi) 15 g, honey to taste. Put the above four herbs into a pot, decoct, strain and add honey. One dose daily, take for several days.
Effect: Nourishes yin and clears heat, nourishes the liver and brightens the eyes. Indicated for yin-deficient heat-type glaucoma and similar conditions.
7. Hawthorn, Cassia Seed and Lotus Leaf Soup
Hawthorn (Shan Zha) and Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) each 15 g, half a lotus leaf (He Ye). Slice hawthorn, shred lotus leaf, and decoct together with Cassia Seed. Use the decoction as a tea.
Effect: Suitable for coronary heart disease, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, obesity, etc. Cassia Seed can inhibit increases in serum cholesterol and the formation of aortic atherosclerotic plaques, and its aqueous extract has antihypertensive effects. Lotus leaf quenches thirst, disperses blood stasis, aids the spleen and stomach, and reduces edema. Combined with hawthorn, it can reduce fat and lower blood pressure, promote weight loss and fitness, and is a good medicinal diet for dizziness and vertigo due to hypertension.
8. Cassia Seed, Shepherd's Purse Seed and Celosia Seed Tea
Cassia Seed, Shepherd's purse seed (Ji Cai Zi), and Celosia seed (Qing Xiang Zi) each 6 g. Grind the ingredients to powder, put into a gauze bag, place in a cup and pour boiling water, cover and steep 5–10 minutes. Drink frequently as a tea, one dose per day.
Effect: Clears heat, dispels wind, brightens the eyes, and lowers blood pressure. Indicated for headaches and dizziness in various stages of hypertension.
9. Cassia Seed Congee
Cassia Seed (fried) 10–15 g, japonica rice 100 g, a little rock sugar. First stir-fry the Cassia Seed until aromatic and remove; after it cools put into a clay pot, decoct in water and strain to obtain the decoction; cook the rice with the medicinal decoction and rock sugar into congee. One dose daily; take 5–7 days as a course.
Effect: Clears the liver, brightens the eyes, relieves constipation, lowers blood pressure and blood lipids. Indicated for red swollen painful eyes, photophobia with tearing, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, habitual constipation, etc.
10. Chrysanthemum, Cassia and Prunella Soup
Chrysanthemum flower 12 g, Grass Cassia Seed 30 g, Prunella (Xia Ku Cao) 5 g. Put the three herbs into a pot, decoct in water and strain. One dose daily, take for 5–7 days as a course.
Effect: Clears liver-fire and lowers blood pressure. Indicated for hypertensive headaches and dizziness.
