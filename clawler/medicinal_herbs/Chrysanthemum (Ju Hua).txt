Cultivation and Processing of Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua)
Source
The capitulum (head inflorescence) of Chrysanthemum, a plant of the Asteraceae family. Harvest when flowers are in full bloom before the first frost; processing methods vary by producing area and material type.
White chrysanthemum (Bai Ju): cut flower-bearing branches, tie into small bunches, hang upside down to dry in the shade. Then pick off the flower heads.
Chu chrysanthemum (Chu Ju): pick off the flower heads. Sulfur-fumigate; when sun-dried to about sixty percent dry, sieve into ball shapes and sun-dry.
Tribute chrysanthemum (Gong Ju): pick off the flower heads and dry by baking.
Hang chrysanthemum (Hang Ju): includes Hang white chrysanthemum and Hang yellow chrysanthemum; Hang white chrysanthemum: pick off the flower heads, steam and then sun-dry; Hang yellow chrysanthemum: dry with charcoal heat.
Properties and Flavor
Taste: sweet; bitter; nature: slightly cold
Appearance and Characteristics
(1) Hairy chrysanthemum (Hao Ju)
Capitula conical-obtuse or cylindrical, sometimes slightly compressed and lobed, diameter 1.5–3 cm. Involucre discoid, with 3–4 layers of involucre bracts, ovate to elliptic, herbaceous, yellow-green or brown-green, externally with soft hairs, membranous margin; receptacle hemispherical, without receptacular bracts or receptacle hairs. Ray florets several rows, female, located at the periphery, colored off-white, erect, stiff, longitudinally folded and contracted, scattered with golden glandular dots; disc florets numerous, bisexual, located centrally and concealed by ray florets, yellow, apex 5-toothed. Achenes undeveloped, without pappus. Light in weight, soft and moist in texture, brittle when dry. Fragrant aroma, taste sweet and slightly bitter.
(2) Chu chrysanthemum (Chu Ju)
Irregular globose or depressed-globose, diameter 1.5–2.5 cm. Ray florets white, irregularly twisted, involute, margins wrinkled, sometimes with light brown glandular dots; disc florets mostly concealed.
(3) Tribute chrysanthemum (Gong Ju)
Depressed-globose or irregular globose, diameter 1.5–2.5 cm. Ray florets white or off-white, ascending obliquely, some reflexed, margins slightly involute and wrinkled, usually without glandular dots; few disc florets exposed.
(4) Hang chrysanthemum (Hang Ju)
Disc-shaped or depressed-globose, diameter 2.5–4 cm, often several connected into clusters. Ray florets generally white or yellow, flat or slightly folded, often adherent to each other and often without glandular dots; disc florets numerous and exposed. Best quality is when flowers are intact without scattered petals, color white (or yellow), strong fragrance, and free of impurities.
Actions of Chrysanthemum
Efficacy
The capitulum of Chrysanthemum, a plant of the Asteraceae family.
Indications
For headache, dizziness, red eyes, vexation and heat in the chest, sores and abscesses, swellings and toxin.
Channels Entered
Enters the Lung meridian; Liver meridian
Main Uses
Dispels wind, clears heat, brightens the eyes, detoxifies. Treats headache, dizziness, red eyes, vexation and heat in the chest, sores, and swellings with toxin.
1. Ben Jing (Classic Materia Medica): Treats various wind-induced dizziness, swelling and pain, eyes feeling as if dislodged with tearing, skin necrosis and muscle death, aversion to wind and damp painful obstruction, benefits blood and qi.
2. Bie Lu (Supplemental Records): Heals wandering low back pain, removes vexation and heat in the chest, calms the stomach and intestines, benefits the five vessels, and regulates the four limbs.
3. Tao Hongjing: White chrysanthemum (Bai Ju): treats wind-induced vertigo.
4. Yao Xing Lun (Treatise on Medicinal Properties): Can treat hot wind causing vertigo and collapse, pain of brain and bones, and disperses various forms of wind affecting the body.
5. Ri Hua Zi Ben Cao (Daily Flower Materia Medica): Benefits the blood vessels, treats wandering wind of the limbs, vexation, oppression in the chest and diaphragm, as well as sores and toxins and headaches; used as a pillow to brighten the eyes.
6. Zhen Zhu Nang (Pearl Pouch): Nourishes the blood of the eyes.
7. Yong Yao Xin Fa (Essentials of Drug Use): Removes ocular film and brightens the eyes.
8. Wang Haogu: Treats liver qi deficiency.
9. Gang Mu Shi Yi (Supplement to the Compendium): Specifically enters the yang aspect. Treats wind-induced dizziness and relieves alcohol toxin and sores and swellings. Yellow tea chrysanthemum: brightens the eyes and dispels wind, pacifies liver qi, treats dizziness and blurred vision, nourishes blood and moistens the complexion, enters the blood level; white tea chrysanthemum: unblocks lung qi, stops rebellious cough, clears heat in the triple burner, treats muscular heat, enters the qi level.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction 10–15 g; or used in pills or powders; or steeped as tea.
External use: appropriate amount, decocted water for washing; or pounded into a poultice.
Precautions and Contraindications
Ben Cao Hui Yan (Materia Medica Notes): In cases of qi deficiency and cold stomach, poor appetite, and diarrhea, use sparingly. Contraindicated in yang deficiency or headache with aversion to cold.
Prescriptions Using Chrysanthemum
1. For wind-heat headache: Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua), Gypsum (Shi Gao), Chuanxiong (Chuan Xiong) each 3 qian. Grind to powder. Each dose 1.5 qian, taken with tea. (Simple Single Formula)
2. For Taiyin wind-warmth with cough, slight fever, and mild thirst: Bitter Apricot Kernel (Ku Xing Ren) 2 qian, Forsythia (Lian Qiao) 1.5 qian, Mint (Bo He) 8 fen, Mulberry Leaf (Sang Ye) 2.5 qian, Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua) 1 qian, Bitter Platycodon Root (Ku Jie Geng) 2 qian, Licorice (Gan Cao) 8 fen, Reed Root (Lu Gen) 2 qian. Two cups of water, decoct down to one cup, take three times daily. (Wen Bing Tiao Bian — Mulberry-Chrysanthemum Drink)
3. For wind-induced vertigo: Sweet chrysanthemum flowers dried rapidly. Make into powder, wrap in rice as offering and steam to make wine for drinking. (Xu Sibo — Chrysanthemum Wine)
4. For hot-toxin wind attacking upwards with red eyes, dizzy head, blurred vision and facial swelling: Baked Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua, baked), Baked Paifengzi (Pai Feng Zi, baked), Processed Licorice (Gan Cao, processed) each 1 liang. Pound the three ingredients and sieve into a powder. At bedtime take 3 qian dissolved in warm water. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu — Chrysanthemum Powder)
5. For various eye obscurations and dimness: Sichuan pepper (Shu Jiao, remove seeds and close the mouth, fry to sweat, 1.5 jin, pound and sieve to a powder) 1 jin, Sweet Chrysanthemum Flower (baked and powdered) 1 jin. Mix the two powders evenly with 15 jin of fat Rehmannia (prepared Di Huang) sliced and pounded, press and collect about 8–9 dou of juice, mix the earlier powdered herbs into the juice until even, sun-dry slightly until not too dry, place in a pan and sun-dry for three to four days until dry but not over-dried, then add 2 jin refined honey and pound thousands of times into pills the size of a catalpa seed. Each dose 30 pills, taken on an empty stomach at noon with warm water. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu — Night Brightening Pills)
6. For liver and kidney deficiency with virtual fire flaring upwards, red painful swollen eyes, long-term blurred vision, tearing in wind, photophobia, dizziness, night sweats, tidal fever, and weakness of the feet: Goji Berry (Gou Qi Zi), Sweet Chrysanthemum Flower (Ju Hua), Prepared Rehmannia Root (Shu Di Huang), Cornus (Shan Zhu Yu), Prepared Chinese Yam (Huai Shan Yao), Poria (Bai Fu Ling), Moutan Bark (Mu Dan Pi), Alisma (Ze Xie). Refined honey made into pills. (Yi Ji — Qi Ju Di Huang Wan)
7. For liver and kidney deficiency and blurred vision: Sweet Chrysanthemum Flower 4 liang, Morinda Root (Ba Ji Tian, remove central core) 1 liang, Cistanche (Rou Cong Rong, wine-soaked, peeled, fried, sliced and baked) 2 liang, Goji Berry 3 liang. Grind to fine powder, make pills with refined honey the size of a catalpa seed. Each dose 30–50 pills, taken with warm wine or salted broth on an empty stomach before meals. (Ju Jing Wan — Chrysanthemum and Jing Pill)
8. For post-illness ocular film: White Chrysanthemum (Bai Ju Hua) and Cicada Molting (Chan Tui) equal parts; make into powder. Each use 2–3 qian with a little honey, decoct in water and take. (Jiu Ji Fang — Emergency Prescriptions)
9. For carbuncles: White Chrysanthemum 4 liang, Licorice (Gan Cao) 4 qian. Decoct in water and take immediately; decoct the residue again and take. (Wai Ke Shi Fa — Chrysanthemum and Licorice Decoction)
10. For knee wind (rheumatic knee pain): Aged Mugwort (Ai Ye), Chrysanthemum. Make into a knee pad and use for a long time. (Fu Shou Jing Fang)
11. For unilateral or bilateral headache: Sweet Chrysanthemum Flower, Gypsum (Shi Gao), Chuanxiong (Chuan Xiong) each 3 qian. Grind to powder. Each dose 3 qian, taken with clear tea. (Wei Sheng Yi Jian Fang)
12. For red swollen painful eyes: Tea chrysanthemum 15 g, Tribulus (Bai Ci Li) 15 g, Horsetail (Mu Zei) 15 g, Cicada Molting (Chan Tui) 6 g. Decoction in water and take. (Sichuan Materia Medica, 1982 edition)
13. For hypertension: White chrysanthemum 15 g, three red dates. Decoction in water and take. (Fujian Materia Medica)
14. For low back pain: Chrysanthemum 2 sheng, Daphne (Yuan Hua) 2 sheng, Azalea (Yang Zhi Zhu) 2 sheng. 17. For common warts: Chrysanthemum 30 g, 100 ml 30-degree baijiu (white liquor) (if baijiu is too strong, dilute with boiled water to 30 degrees). 18. For drinking without getting drunk: On the ninth day of the ninth lunar month take a pinch of chrysanthemum powder immediately before drinking. (Qian Jin Yao Fang)
Dietary Therapy Recipes
1. Egg and Chrysanthemum Soup
One egg, Chrysanthemum 5 g, lotus root juice appropriate amount, a little mature vinegar. Mix beaten egg with chrysanthemum, lotus root juice and vinegar, steam over water until cooked; take once daily.
Efficacy: Hemostatic and promoting blood circulation, reduces swelling and relieves pain. Suitable for advanced esophageal cancer with increased coughing and marked vomiting.
2. Safflower, Mulberry Leaf and Chrysanthemum Soup
Safflower (Hong Hua) 3 g, Mulberry Leaf (Sang Ye) 10 g, Chrysanthemum (Ju Hua) 10 g. Place mulberry leaves, chrysanthemum and safflower in a cup, pour boiling water, cover and steep briefly; first use the vapor to fumigate the affected eye, then drink warm. Twice daily for three consecutive days.
Efficacy: Disperses heat and reduces swelling, relieves pain and brightens the eyes. Indicated for acute infective conjunctivitis and related conditions.
3. Chrysanthemum and Licorice Decoction
White chrysanthemum (or chrysanthemum) 120 g, Licorice (Gan Cao) 12 g. Add water and decoct, divide into 3–4 doses.
Efficacy: Clears heat and detoxifies. Indicated for sores and swollen painful abscesses.
4. Mulberry, Chrysanthemum and Cicada-Shell Drink
Mulberry Leaf 10 g, Chrysanthemum 10 g, Cicada Molting (Chan Tui) 6 g, sugar to taste. Clean mulberry leaves and chrysanthemum, remove head and feet of cicada molting, place together in a pot with appropriate water, bring to a boil over high heat then simmer over low heat for 15 minutes, strain and collect the decoction, add sugar and stir. Drink regularly.
Efficacy: Releases exterior wind-heat and calms convulsions. Indicated for pediatric convulsions with fever, headache, cough and rhinorrhea.
5. Chrysanthemum and Oolong Tea
Hang Chrysanthemum 10 g, Oolong tea 3 g, pour boiling water to steep as tea.
Efficacy: Pacifies the liver, dispels wind-heat, generates fluids and relieves thirst, diuretic, can lower serum cholesterol and help prevent atherosclerosis. Suitable for hypertension due to liver-yang rising and yin deficiency with relative yang excess.
6. Chrysanthemum and Reed-Root Tea
Chrysanthemum 10 g, Reed Root (Lu Gen) 60 g. Wash and chop reed root, decoct together with chrysanthemum, strain and drink as tea.
Efficacy: Clears lung heat, promotes urination and brings out rashes. Indicated for pediatric measles and chickenpox.
7. Chrysanthemum Congee
Chrysanthemum (ground to powder) and polished rice in a ratio of 1:2. Cook rice into congee, when nearly done add chrysanthemum powder (amount one), then bring to a boil and serve twice daily warm. Contraindicated for spleen deficiency with loose stools.
Efficacy: For red swollen painful eyes, blurred vision, hypertension, dilation of coronary arteries and increased coronary blood flow.
8. Chrysanthemum, Cassia Seed and Prunella Decoction
Chrysanthemum 12 g, Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) 30 g, Prunella (Xia Ku Cao) 5 g. Put the three herbs in a pot, decoct, strain and take the decoction. One dose daily, use for 5–7 days per course.
Efficacy: Clears liver-fire and lowers blood pressure. Indicated for hypertension with headache and dizziness.
9. Chrysanthemum, Mulberry, Mint and Bamboo-Leaf Tea
Chrysanthemum 5 g, Mulberry Leaf 5 g, Mint 3 g, Bamboo Leaf (Dan Zhu Ye) 30 g, White Imperata Root (Bai Mao Gen) 30 g. Put all ingredients into a teapot, pour boiling water and steep for 10 minutes. Drink as a beverage as desired.
Efficacy: Acrid-cool to release exterior, clears and benefits the throat. Indicated for wind-heat common cold with fever, headache, spontaneous sweating, nasal congestion, sore throat and productive cough with thick phlegm.
10. Chrysanthemum Tea
Tea leaves 3 g, dried chrysanthemum 5–10 g, pour boiling water to steep and drink after meals.
Efficacy: Reduces fever and detoxifies, clears the liver and brightens the eyes, lowers lipids and has anti-aging effects. Drinking chrysanthemum with Longjing tea may also treat mild hypertension, conjunctivitis and dizziness from diminished vision.
11. Chrysanthemum and Cassia Seed Congee
Take Chrysanthemum and Cassia Seed (Jue Ming Zi) each 10 g, rice 100 g, rock sugar to taste. Roast Cassia Seed until fragrant, decoct together with chrysanthemum to obtain a strained juice, add rice and cook into thin congee, when cooked add rock sugar and simmer briefly. Take once daily.
Efficacy: Clears the liver, brightens the eyes and lowers blood pressure; can also treat red swollen painful eyes, photophobia with tearing, headache and dizziness, and constipation.
12. Two-Flower Tea
Honeysuckle (Jin Yin Hua) 50 g, Hang chrysanthemum 100 g, honey to taste. Remove impurities, wash and briefly pan-fry, cool and store in a clean jar. When needed take a small amount, place in a cup, pour boiling water and steep, add honey to taste. Take 2–3 times daily.
Efficacy: Clears heat and dispels wind, brightens the eyes and lowers blood pressure. Indicated for summer heat thirst, hypertension, coronary heart disease, palpitations and insomnia, sore throat and cough. Also suitable as a cooling summer beverage.
