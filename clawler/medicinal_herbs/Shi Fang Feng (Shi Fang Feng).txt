Cultivation and processing of Stone Ledebouriella (Shi Fang Feng)
Source
The root of the Apiaceae (Umbelliferae) plant Stone Ledebouriella (Shi Fang Feng). Roots are dug in autumn and winter, washed and sun-dried.
Morphology
Perennial herb, 40–120 cm tall. Root collar slightly thickened, with residual brownish leaf-sheath fibers; root long-conical, 0.5–1.2 cm in diameter, epidermis gray-brown. Stems erect, with longitudinal striations, lower part glabrous, upper part sometimes with fine soft hairs. Basal leaf petioles 8–20 cm long; leaf outline elliptic to triangular-ovate, 6–18 cm long, 5–15 cm wide, bipinnately and completely divided, ultimate segments lanceolate or ovate-lanceolate, cuneate at base, margins shallowly lobed or with 2–3 teeth, 0.8–1.2 cm long, both surfaces glabrous or with coarse hairs only at the base of the veins; cauline leaves similar to basal leaves but smaller, sessile, with only broad sheathing leaf sheaths. Compound umbels terminal and lateral; peduncles 3–15 cm long; rays 8–20, angled and nearly quadrangular; involucre 0–2, linear-lanceolate; involucel 6–10, linear; flowers white; calyx teeth long, acuminate; petals obcordate; stylopodium conical, styles deflexed. Mericarps ovoid-elliptic, 3–4 mm long, 2–3 mm wide, dorsal and median ribs linear and prominent, lateral ribs alate; one vitta in each furrow, two vittae on the commissure. Flowering July–September, fruiting September–October.
Habitat and distribution
Ecology: grows on hillside grasslands, under forests, forest margins and in mountain grassy places.
Distribution: distributed in Northeast China and Inner Mongolia, Hebei and Shandong.
Taste and nature
Taste bitter; pungent; nature slightly cold
Characteristics
Roots cylindrical or fusiform, sometimes branched. Surface gray-yellow or dark brown, with annular transverse wrinkles near the top of the root, below with longitudinal striations and transverse lenticels; top with residual stem base. Transverse section off-white, strongly fibrous, with concentric radiating rings. Aroma faintly fragrant. Best when dry, firm and aromatic.
Effects of Stone Ledebouriella (Shi Fang Feng)
Efficacy
The root of the Apiaceae (Umbelliferae) plant Stone Ledebouriella (Shi Fang Feng). Used for colds, cough, copious phlegm and asthma, headache and vertigo.
Channels entered
Enters the Lung and Liver meridians
Indications
Disperses wind and clears heat, descends qi and resolves phlegm. Indicated for colds, cough, phlegm and asthma, headache and vertigo.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoction, 3–9 g; or powdered.
Prescriptions using Stone Ledebouriella (Shi Fang Feng)
① Treats colds, cough and asthma: Stone Ledebouriella (Shi Fang Feng),
Bitter Apricot Kernel (Ku Xing Ren)
each three qian (approx. 9 g),
Perilla Seed (Zi Su Zi)
, Platycodon Root (Jie Geng)
each two qian, decocted in water and taken orally. (Hebei Chinese Materia Medica Manual)
② Treats cough in pregnant women: Stone Ledebouriella (Shi Fang Feng),
Chinese Angelica (Dang Gui)
each three qian, decocted in water and taken orally. (Hebei Chinese Materia Medica Manual)
Commentaries by various sources
1. Illustrated Classic of Materia Medica (Ben Cao Tu Jing): treats headache, vertigo and pain.
2. Shaanxi Chinese Herbal Medicine: descends qi and resolves phlegm, disperses wind-heat. Treats colds, cough, bronchitis, fullness and distention of the chest and hypochondrium, and wheezing.
