Cultivation and Processing of Common Scouring Rush (Mu Zei)
Source
This item is the dried aerial part of the Equisetaceae plant Common Scouring Rush (Mu Zei), Equisetum hiemale L. Harvest in summer and autumn, remove impurities, sun-dry or shade-dry.
Nature and Flavor
Sweet, bitter; neutral.
Preparation
Remove withered stems and remaining roots, spray with clean water, moisten slightly, cut into sections, dry.
Characteristics
The dried whole herb is long and tube-shaped, hollow with nodes, unbranched. Length 30–60 centimeters, diameter about 5 millimeters, each internode 3–6 centimeters long. Surface gray-green or yellow-green, with numerous longitudinal branches arranged straight, densely bearing fine spines, producing a rough feel to the touch. At the nodes there are tubular dark-brown scale leaves that are easily detached at the nodes. Texture brittle, easily broken; fracture surface hollow, inside with gray-white or light-green thin pith. Odor none; taste sweet, slightly bitter and astringent. The best quality has stems that are thick and long, green in color, firm in texture, and not easily shed at the nodes.
Effects of Common Scouring Rush (Mu Zei)
Efficacy
This item is the dried aerial part of the Equisetaceae plant Common Scouring Rush (Mu Zei).
Main Indications: cloudy films over the eyes, tearing in wind, intestinal wind with bleeding, bloody dysentery, malaria, sore throat, abscesses and swellings.
Channels
Enters
Lung channel, Liver channel.
Main Indications
Disperse wind and clear heat, release the muscle layer, dispel ocular films. Treats cloudy films over the eyes, tearing in wind, intestinal wind with bleeding, bloody dysentery, malaria, sore throat, and abscesses and swellings.
1. Jiahu Materia Medica (Jiahu Bencao): Indicated for eye diseases, expels ocular membranes. Also reduces accumulations and masses, benefits the liver and gallbladder, brightens the eyes, treats intestinal wind, stops dysentery and continuous uterine bleeding.
2. Compendium of Materia Medica (Bencao Gangmu): Releases the muscles, stops tearing, stops bleeding, expels wind-damp, relieves hernia pain, and treats prolapse of the large intestine.
3. Standard Materia Medica (Bencao Zheng): Induces sweating, releases the muscles. Treats typhoid and malaria. Expels wind-damp and disperses pathogenic fire.
4. Yuqiu’s Explanation of Medicines (Yu Qiu Yao Jie): Pacifies hard ulcerated swellings, expels wind and violent phlegm and saliva. Treats abscesses, scrofula, carbuncles, boils, tinea versicolor, scurf, and various uterine bleeding disorders, both red and white.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoct 3–9 g, or include in pills or powders; external use: grind to powder and sprinkle.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in those with qi and blood deficiency.
1. Commentary on the Materia Medica (Bencao Jingshu): Eye diseases caused by anger and summer heat injuring the blood with violent redness, swelling and pain are not suitable for this herb.
2. Collected Sayings on Materia Medica (Bencao Huiyan): Excessive use harms the liver; not suitable for prolonged use.
3. Origins of This Classic (Benjing Fengyuan): Excessive use causes eye swelling; not suitable for long-standing ocular films or blood deficiency.
Formulas Using Common Scouring Rush (Mu Zei)
1. For eye obstruction with blurred vision and excessive tearing: Scouring Rush Herb (nodes removed) one liang, grind to powder, mix with sheep liver and pound into pills; take two qian each time morning and evening after meals, taken with plain soup. (Fangmai Zhengzong)
2. Blurry vision with excessive tearing: Scouring Rush (nodes removed) and Atractylodes Rhizome (Cang Zhu) (soaked in rice-washing water) one liang each, grind together to powder. Each dose two qian, take with tea. Or mix with honey to make pills and swallow. (Shenghui Fang)
3. For wind-cold-damp seeking to induce sweating: Scouring Rush (nodes removed) one liang, Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang) and White Part of Green Onion (Cong Bai) five qian each. Decoction in water, drink hot to induce sweating. (Shenghui Fang)
4. For intestinal wind with bleeding: Scouring Rush (nodes removed, fried) one liang, Mu Man (Mu Mantou) (fried), Bitter Orange (Zhi Ke, prepared), Sophora Fruit (Huai Jiao, fried), Poria (Fu Ling), Schizonepeta (Jing Jie) five qian each. Grind to powder; each dose two qian, take with a strongly decocted jujube (date) broth. (Renzhai Zhizhi Fang — Mu Zei Powder)
5. For persistent bloody dysentery: Scouring Rush five qian, decoct in water and take warm. One dose daily. (Shenghui Fang)
6. For chronic rectal prolapse not cured for years: Scouring Rush, amount not specified, char by burning (calcine) until persistence is achieved, grind to powder, apply to the anus and press. (Sanyin Fang)
7. For continuous menstrual bleeding: Scouring Rush (fried) three qian, water one cup decocted down to seven-tenths, take warm. One dose daily. (Shenghui Fang)
8. For external traumatic bleeding, gastrointestinal bleeding, gynecological bleeding and other hemorrhages: Scouring Rush 50%, Phellodendron Bark (Huang Bai) 20%, Motherwort (Yi Mu Cao) 20%, Chinese Gall (Wu Bei Zi) 10%. Grind each to powder, pass through a 120-mesh sieve, mix evenly. External: sprinkle powder on the wound and press with gauze. Internal: 2 g each time, every 4–6 hours. (Inner Mongolia Compilation of New Medical Methods of Chinese Herbal Medicine)
9. For restless fetus or threatened miscarriage: Scouring Rush (nodes removed) and Sichuan Lovage (Chuan Xiong) equal parts, grind to powder. Each dose three qian, decoct in one cup of water with Honeysuckle (Jin Yin Hua) one qian and take. (Shengji Zong Lu)
10. For red painful throat: crush fresh Scouring Rush and squeeze out the juice, mix with honey and take. (Quanzhou Materia Medica)
11. For edematous beriberi, nephritis with skin disease, edema: Scouring Rush 4 qian 5 fen, Duckweed (Fu Ping) three qian, Adzuki Bean (Chi Xiao Dou) three liang, Red Dates six pieces. Decoction in water, take three times daily. (Modern Practical Chinese Medicine)
12. For stiff tongue with bleeding: decoct Scouring Rush in water and gargle. (Shenghui Fang)
13. Acute throat obstruction: char Scouring Rush over a cow-dung fire (calcine), each dose one qian, take with cold water; when bleeding occurs the condition is relieved.
14. Unstoppable hemorrhage: same prescription as above, take twice daily.
15. Bleeding from internal hemorrhoids: Scouring Rush and Bitter Orange (Zhi Ke) each two liang, Dry Ginger (Gan Jiang) one liang, Rhubarb (Da Huang) two qian and a half; stir-fry together in a pan until blackened and charred (retaining efficacy), grind fine. Each dose two qian, take with millet soup. Very effective.
16. Female uterine hemorrhage: Scouring Rush and Cyperus Rhizome (Xiang Fu Zi) each one liang, Poxiao (Poxiao, niter) half a liang, grind to powder. Each dose three qian; if the blood is dark, decoct in one bowl of wine; if the blood is bright red, decoct in one bowl of water, take with the dregs. Take twice daily. If there is pain below the navel, add Frankincense (Ru Xiang), Myrrh (Mo Yao), and Angelica Sinensis (Dang Gui) one qian each and decoct together. Avoid raw, cold, hard foods and pork, fish, wheat products, etc. This formula is called Lei’s Mu Zei Powder.
17. Small intestine hernia: finely scrape Scouring Rush, lightly stir-fry to powder. Take two qian with boiling soup; effective. Can also be taken with hot wine.
