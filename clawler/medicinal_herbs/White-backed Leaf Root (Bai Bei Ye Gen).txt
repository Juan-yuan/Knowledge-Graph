White-backed Leaf Root (Bai Bei Ye Gen)

Names
White Membrane Root (Bai mo gen), White Pu Root (Bai pu gen) (Lingnan Herbal Records), Wild Tong Root (Ye tong gen) (Minnan Folk Herbal Medicine).

Cultivation and Processing of Root of White-backed Leaf
Source
The root of the Euphorbiaceae plant White-backed Leaf Mallotus apelta (Lour.) Muell.-Arg.; harvested in summer and autumn, washed clean, used fresh, or sliced and sun-dried.
Morphology
White-backed Leaf is an erect shrub or small tree 1.5–3 m high; young branches, petioles and inflorescences are covered with white or pale yellow stellate pubescence; leaves alternate; petiole 1–8 cm long, densely covered with white stellate hairs; leaf broad-ovate, 4.5–23 cm long and 3.5–16 cm wide, apex gradually acuminate, base nearly truncate or shortly truncate or slightly cordate, with two glandular dots, margin entire or with three shallow lobes at the apex, occasionally sparse obtuse teeth, adaxial surface green, with stellate soft hairs or nearly glabrous, abaxial surface gray-white, densely covered with stellate pubescence with dense reddish-brown glandular dots; palmate veins three. Flowers unisexual and dioecious; male inflorescence is a simple or branched spike, terminal, 15–30 cm long, covered with yellow-brown tomentum; male flowers clustered; with short pedicels or nearly sessile; calyx 3–6-cleft, lobes ovate, unequal, densely hairy outside, with red gland dots inside. Stamens numerous, filaments free, anthers two-chambered; no petals; disc without glands. Female spike inflorescence unbranched, terminal or lateral, slightly shorter than male spikes, about 15 cm, cylindrical in fruit; female flowers solitary, sessile; calyx campanulate, 3–5-cleft, lobes ovate, 3–4 mm long, outer surface with stellate pubescence; no petals; ovary with soft spines densely covered with stellate hairs, 3–4 locules, style 3, short, fused at the base, covered with epidermal spines and dense stellate hairs. Fruiting spike cylindrical, over 2.5–15 cm long and 2–3 cm in diameter; capsules nearly globose, densely covered with plumose soft spines and gray-white or pale yellow stellate pubescence, soft spines 2–6 mm long; seeds nearly globose, black and shiny. Flowering April to July, fruiting August to November.
Habitat and Distribution
Ecological environment: grows in shrubbery beside mountain slopes and roads or at forest margins.
Resource distribution: distributed in Shaanxi, Jiangsu, Anhui, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Henan, Hunan, Guangxi, Guangdong, Hainan, Guizhou, Yunnan and other areas.
Properties and taste
Taste: slightly bitter; astringent; nature: neutral.
Effects of Root of White-backed Leaf
Efficacy
The root of the Euphorbiaceae plant White-backed Leaf. Mainly treats hepatitis, enteritis, turbid urinary discharge, leukorrhea, rectal prolapse, uterine prolapse, hepatosplenomegaly, and traumatic bruises and sprains.
Meridians
Spleen; Kidney; Bladder channels
Indications
Clears heat and dispels dampness, astringes, resolves stasis. Indicated for hepatitis, enteritis, turbid urinary discharge, leukorrhea, rectal prolapse, uterine prolapse, hepatosplenomegaly, and traumatic bruises and sprains.
Dosage and administration
Oral: decoction, 15–30 g.
Topical: appropriate amount, grind to powder and sprinkle; or soak in wine and apply; or decoct in water and wash.
Formulas Using Root of White-backed Leaf
1. For turbid urinary discharge: Root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen) five qian,
Poria with hostwood (Fu shen)
four qian,
Poria (Fu ling)
three qian. Decoction taken on an empty stomach. (Lingnan Herbal Records)
2. For uterine prolapse: Root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen) twelve liang,
rice vinegar (Mi cu)
two jin, simmer until reduced to half a jin, leave outdoors overnight exposed to the dew, take the next morning in one dose, take three consecutive doses, and rest in bed for one week; after recovery stew with yellow-headed turtle (Huang tou gui) and take for several days. (Lingnan Herbal Records)
3. For eye turbidity, red hot eyes, photophobia with tearing: fresh Wild Tong Root (Ye tong gen) one to two liang (washed and chopped),
pig liver (Zhu gan)
two to four liang. Stew with an appropriate amount of water and take. (Minnan Folk Herbal Medicine)
4. For shuang dan throat moth: Root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen),
honey (Feng tang)
soak until saturated, remove dregs, take the juice orally. (Lingnan Herbal Records)
5. For purulent otorrhea: Root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen) ground to powder, soak in an appropriate amount of wine to extract a concentrated liquid and drop into the ear, and also apply externally. (Lingnan Herbal Records)
6. For lumbar sprain: fresh Wild Tong Root (Ye tong gen) one liang, washed and chopped, half water and half wine with lean pork (Zhu chi rou) two to four liang, stew and take. (Minnan Folk Herbal Medicine)
7. For chronic hepatitis, splenic enlargement, enteritis and gastritis, rectal prolapse, uterine prolapse: dried root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen) five qian to one liang, decoct in water and take. (Guangzhou Troops "Commonly Used Chinese Herbal Medicine Manual")
8. For leukorrhea, turbid urinary discharge, hernia, postpartum wind paralysis: Root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen) five qian to one liang, decoct in water and take. (Guangxi Chinese Herbal Medicine)
9. For scrofula: dried root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen) two liang, appropriate amount of lean pork (Zhu shou rou), decoct in water and press. (Fujian Chinese Herbal Medicine)
10. For rabid dog bites: fresh root of White-backed Leaf (Bai bei ye gen) two to three liang, decoct in water and take. From the day of the bite, one dose daily for seven consecutive days, thereafter one dose every seven days; if symptoms develop, take several consecutive doses. (Jiangxi "Herbal Medicine Manual")
