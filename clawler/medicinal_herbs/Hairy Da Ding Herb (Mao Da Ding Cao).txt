Cultivation and Preparation of Mao Da Ding Cao (Mao Da Ding Cao)
Source
The whole herb of the Asteraceae plant Mao Da Ding Cao (Mao Da Ding Cao). Harvest in summer, wash clean, sun-dry or use fresh.
[Morphology]
Mao Da Ding Cao (Mao Da Ding Cao) is a perennial herb. Rhizomes short and stout, densely covered with white cottony hairs; numerous fibrous roots, dark brown. Leaves basal; with short petioles; leaf blades soft and thick, oblong or obovate, 5–10 cm long, 2.5–4.5 cm wide, apex blunt-rounded, base cuneate, margins entire, young leaves with soft hairs on the upper surface that fall off with age, underside densely covered with white cottony hairs. Flower stems erect, solitary, 10–40 cm high, covered with light brown cottony hairs; capitulum solitary at stem apex, about 3.5 cm in diameter; involucre in two series, linear-lanceolate, outer series slightly shorter, back densely covered with light brown cottony hairs; ligulate florets white, female, bilabiate, outer lip elongated with three lobes, inner series small with two deep lobes; tubular florets bisexual, corolla slightly bilabiate. Achenes linear-lanceolate, about 5 mm long, slightly flattened, with longitudinal ribs and fine hairs, beak very short at flowering and equal to the achene at maturity; pappus about 1 cm long, pale red, glossy. Flowering period May–June.
[Habitat and Distribution]
Ecological environment: grows on sunny slopes, grasslands and forest edges.
Resource distribution: distributed in Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Hunan, Guangdong, Guangxi, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan and Tibet.
Properties and Flavor
Bitter and pungent, neutral in nature.
1. "Tianbao Materia Medica": pungent, warm.
2. "Guangxi Record of Chinese Materia Medica": taste bitter, slightly sweet and pungent, neutral in nature, non-toxic.
Characteristics
Rhizomes clustered with many fibrous roots, roots may reach up to 11 cm, surface brownish; brittle texture, fracture yellow-white. Leaves clustered, often wrinkled; intact leaves when flattened are oblong or ovate, upper surface dark brown, underside brownish, covered with yellow-white downy hairs; brittle texture, some leaves contain a brown-yellow flower, peduncles hollow. Slight odor, astringent taste.
Prefer specimens with many leaves and little fragmentation.
Effects of Mao Da Ding Cao (Mao Da Ding Cao)
Efficacy
The whole herb of the Asteraceae plant Mao Da Ding Cao (Mao Da Ding Cao). Used for common cold with cough, epigastric distension and pain, diarrhea, dysentery, edema, turbid urinary discharge, sores and carbuncles, traumatic swelling and pain, and poisonous snake bites.
Meridians/Channels
1. "Sichuan Record of Chinese Materia Medica": enters the Liver and Lung meridians.
2. "Mindong Materia Medica": enters the Spleen, Kidney and Bladder meridians.
Indications
Clears heat and detoxifies, disseminates the Lung and stops cough, moves qi and activates blood. Indicated for common cold cough, epigastric distension and pain, diarrhea, dysentery, edema, turbid urinary discharge, sores and carbuncles, traumatic swelling and pain, and poisonous snake bites.
1. "Illustrated Examination of Plant Names and Realities": treats pediatric food accumulation (infant indigestion).
2. "Classification of Herbal Properties": disperses cold, treats cough and rheumatism.
3. "Tianbao Materia Medica": treats boils and ulcerations, and eruptive sores on the back.
4. "Compilation of Commonly Used Folk Herbs": promotes Lung qi, induces sweating, disperses cold and dispels wind, stops cough and calms wheezing, stops pertussis.
5. "Jiangxi Folk Herbs": treats unilateral headache and toothache; for children with high fever, irritability and thirst, and acute convulsions, decoct and take orally. Crush and apply to local sores and carbuncles.
6. "Sichuan Record of Chinese Materia Medica": treats leucorrhea due to yin deficiency, nocturnal emission and spermatorrhea, facial edema. Also applied externally for ulcerated bone with phlegm discharge.
7. "Mindong Materia Medica": expels dampness, moves qi, promotes urination, unblocks menses and breaks blood stasis. Treats edema, distention, hernia, prolapse, jaundiced obesity, amenorrhea, food accumulation, soreness of the waist and hips, and urinary retention.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoction, 6–15 g dried, 30–60 g fresh.
External use: appropriate amount, crush and apply as a poultice.
