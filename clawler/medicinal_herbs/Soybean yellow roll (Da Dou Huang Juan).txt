Cultivation and processing of dried black soybean sprout
Source
Obtained by sun-drying the seeds of Glycine max (L.) Merr., a species of the genus Glycine in the legume family Fabaceae, after they have germinated.
Properties and flavor
Sweet, neutral.
Processing
1. Dried black soybean sprout (Da Dou Huang Juan): Take black soybeans, wash clean, soak until the seed coat is slightly wrinkled, remove and place in a bamboo basket, cover with a damp cattail mat, sprinkle water 1–2 times daily to promote germination; when the sprouts reach 1 cm, take out and spread inside, first place where there is wind to air-dry to half dry (to prevent shedding of the seed coat), then sun-dry to finish.
2. To prepare Dried black soybean sprout: Put the dried black soybean sprout into a pot and add medicinal decoction made by boiling Lophatherum leaf (Dan Zhu Ye) and rush pith (Deng Xin Cao), cook together until the decoction is absorbed, then take out and sun-dry (for every 100 jin of dried black soybean sprout, use 20 liang of Lophatherum leaf (Dan Zhu Ye) and 10 liang of rush pith (Deng Xin Cao)).
Description
Seeds oblong-oval or kidney-shaped, slightly flattened, length 0.7–1.4 cm, width 5–8 mm; surface grayish yellow, dark brown or purple-brown, glossy, with transverse wrinkles, one side with an oblong hilum 2–3 mm long. Seed coat often cracked, broken or shed. Cotyledons yellow, thick and fleshy, radicle thin and long, extending out of the seed coat, curved, 0.5–1 cm long; brittle and easily broken. There are also a few ungerminated seeds with intact seed coats. Odorless, taste bland, with an oily sensation. Best when seeds are large, plump, wrinkled and with short sprouts.
Effects of dried black soybean sprout
Efficacy
Obtained by sun-drying the germinated seeds of Glycine max of the legume family.
Treats early-stage damp-warmth (damp-warm febrile disorders), unresolved damp-heat, scanty sweating, chest oppression, edema with distention, difficulty urinating, damp-bi painful obstruction, muscle cramps, and vexing pain of bones and joints.

Indications
Releases and disperses pathogenic factors from the exterior, and separates and drains damp-heat. Treats early-stage damp-warmth, unresolved damp-heat, scanty sweating, chest oppression, edema with distention, difficulty urinating, damp-bi painful obstruction, muscle cramps, and vexing pain of bones and joints.
① Ben Jing: treats damp-bi painful obstruction, muscle cramps, knee pain.
② Bie Lu: five viscera (– recorded as "five viscera deficiency"), accumulation of stomach qi, tonifies qi, stops toxin (one text reads "stops pain"). Removes black scurf, moistens and smooths the skin and hair.
③ Shi Liao Ben Cao: dispels women's retained lochia.
④ Gang Mu: eliminates accumulated heat in the stomach, reduces edema and abdominal fullness from water disorders.
⑤ Zhong Yao Zhi: releases the exterior and promotes urination to drain dampness. Treats internal accumulation of damp-heat, scanty sweating, difficulty urinating.
Usage and dosage
Internal use: decocted as a soup, 6–15 g; or pounded for juice; or used in powdered formulations.
Precautions and contraindications
Bencao Jing Jizhu: incompatible with the five ginsengs (Wu Shen) and Long Dan (Gentiana). Compatible with Qian Hu (Qian Hu), Wu Hui (Wu Hui), Apricot kernel (Xing Ren), Oyster shell (Mu Li). Counteracts Aconite (Wu Tou) toxicity.
Formulas using dried black soybean sprout
① For water disorders with generalized swelling, rapid breathing, and hesitant urination: Dried black soybean sprout (vinegar-mixed and stir-fried until dry, Da Dou Huang Juan), Rhubarb (Da Huang) (lightly baked to remove skin) each one liang. Pound and sift into a powder. Each dose two qian bi; at bedtime, take with a decoction of scallion white (Cong Bai) and tangerine peel (Ju Pi) and in the morning to promote large intestine discharge as needed. (Sheng Ji Zong Lu: Da Dou Powder)
② For wind in the head, damp-bi, muscle cramps and knee pain, accumulated heat in the stomach, and dry, difficult stools: Dried black soybean sprout (stir-fried) one sheng, ghee (Su) half liang. Make into powder; take one spoonful with warm water before meals, twice daily. (Pu Ji Fang: Huang Juan Powder)
③ For generalized painful obstruction with stagnation of the five viscera, accumulation in the stomach, tonifying qi and expelling toxins, moistening skin and hair, and tonifying kidney qi: Dried soybean suckers (Da Dou Nie) one jin. Stir-fry until fragrant and cooked, make into powder. Each dose half qian with warm wine, taken on an empty stomach; increase to one qian, three times daily. (Xuan Ming Lun Fang)
④ For infants with clenched mouth and trismus: use fresh bean sprouts from newborn stage, mash and grind, mix with breast milk and feed to the child, or press to extract the juice and give a small amount. (Sheng Hui Fang)
⑤ For elderly with phlegm-fire, frequent coughing, chest and hypochondriac fullness, aches in all joints, emaciation and weakness, poor appetite: Dried black soybean sprout one jin, sun-dry and stir-fry until dry, grind to fine powder. Take one qian each night with decoction of black dates. (Fang Mai Zheng Zong)
⑥ For yin epilepsy with chronic convulsions caused by excessive use of damp-heat medicines leading to heat signs and sudden convulsions; also for expelling intestinal parasites: Dried black soybean sprout (sun-dried), Herba Centipedae (Guan Zhong), Isatis root (Ban Lan Gen), Licorice, honey-fried (Gan Cao, Zhi) each one liang. Grind the above into a fine powder. Each dose half qian or one qian, boil in plain water and cool before taking. In severe cases use two qian of medicine, add a few drops of oil to slurry and boil. (Xiao Er Wei Sheng Zong Wei Lun Fang)
⑦ For dark hemorrhoids, dark dry face, and to moisten hair: Dried black soybean sprout one sheng, stir-fry until fragrant and grind to powder. Take one spoonful warm with wine on an empty stomach. (Pu Ji Fang)
Notes
(1) Used for damp-warmth and early-stage summer-damp, symptoms of fever, aversion to cold, heavy body, chest oppression, greasy tongue coating, etc. Dried black soybean sprout is a mild exterior-releasing agent; externally it can disperse and release exterior pathogens, and internally it can resolve and transform water-dampness. When damp-heat is accompanied by exterior symptoms it is often combined with Agastache (Huo Xiang) and Pei Lan (Pei Lan).
(2) Used for internal accumulation of damp-heat, fever with vexation and thirst, chest oppression and discomfort, heaviness and body aches. Dried black soybean sprout is good at opening and promoting discharge and is used for damp-warmth and summer-damp damp-heat disorders; it is often combined with Poria (Fu Ling), Talc (Hua Shi), and Baical skullcap (Huang Qin).
