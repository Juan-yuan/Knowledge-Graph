Cultivation and Processing of Self-heal Spike (Xiakucao)
Source
This product is the dried fruit-spikes of the Lamiaceae (mint family) plant Prunella vulgaris L. (Xiakucao). The fruit-spikes are harvested in summer when they turn brownish-red, impurities are removed, and they are sun-dried.
Properties and Taste
Bitter and pungent; cold in nature.
Description
The dried fruit-spike is long cylindrical or pagoda-shaped, 2.5–6.5 cm long, 1–1.5 cm in diameter, brown or light purplish-brown; the persistent calyces occur in several to many whorls arranged like overlapping tiles, each whorl having 5–6 short-stalked persistent calyces, with two opposite bracts below. The bracts are reniform, light yellow-brown, with distinct longitudinal veins, cuneate at the base, apex caudate, abaxial surface bearing white coarse hairs. The persistent calyx is labiate, the upper lip broad and slightly 3-lobed at the tip, the lower lip 2-lobed, lobes acute triangular, outer surface with coarse hairs. The corolla and stamens have fallen off. Inside the persistent calyx are four small nutlets, brown and shining. The material is light, brittle, slightly aromatic, and bland in taste. Those that are purplish-brown and have large spikes are preferred. Mainly produced in Jiangsu, Anhui, Zhejiang, Henan and other regions; other provinces also produce it.
Effects of Self-heal Spike (Xiakucao)
Actions
This product is the dried fruit-spikes of the Lamiaceae plant Prunella vulgaris (Xiakucao).
Treats scrofula, goiter, breast abscess, breast cancer, nocturnal eyeball pain, photophobia with lacrimation, headache and dizziness, facial paralysis (deviation of mouth and eye), muscle and bone pain, pulmonary tuberculosis, acute icteric infectious hepatitis, uterine bleeding, and abnormal vaginal discharge.
Channels Entered
Enters the Liver channel and the Gallbladder channel.
Indications
Clears the liver and disperses nodules. Treats scrofula, goiter, breast abscess, breast cancer, nocturnal eyeball pain, photophobia with lacrimation, headache and dizziness, facial paralysis, muscle and bone pain, pulmonary tuberculosis, acute icteric infectious hepatitis, uterine bleeding, and abnormal vaginal discharge.
(1) Clears the liver and brightens the eyes: used for liver-heat causing red, swollen, painful eyes, and for headache and dizziness due to liver-yang rising (such as hypertension); can be paired with Kuding tea (Kudingcha) and wild chrysanthemum (Yejuhua).
(2) Clears heat and disperses nodules: used for mastitis and parotitis; can be paired with Bupleurum (Chaihu), Red Peony Root (Chishao), and Thunberg Fritillary (Zhebeimu). For scrofula (e.g., lymph node tuberculosis) and goiter (e.g., simple thyroid enlargement), often combined with Oyster Shell (Muli) and Thunberg Fritillary (Zhebeimu).
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 6–15 g, large doses may be used up to 30 g; can be made into an ointment or included in pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, decocted for wash or pounded and applied as a poultice.
Precautions and Contraindications
Use with caution in those with spleen and stomach deficiency.
1. Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu: "Tugua is used as its assistant."
2. Depei Bencao: contraindicated in those with qi deficiency.
Prescriptions Using Self-heal Spike (Xiakucao)
1. For treating scrofula and fistulae, whether ulcerated or not, or long-standing discharging sores: Xiakucao 6 liang, water 2 zhong, decoct to seven tenths, remove dregs, take on an empty stomach. In severe deficiency make a concentrated paste decoction to take and apply to the lesion; frequent dosing is better. (Shesheng Zhongmiao Fang: Xiakucao Decoction)
2. For early-stage breast abscess
a. Xiakucao and Dandelion (Pugongying) equal parts. Decoction in wine and take, or make into pills. (Bencao Huiyan)
b. Pound Xiakucao in wine and take; use the dregs as a poultice. The breast will subside the same day and heal in three days. (Puji Fang)
3. For liver-deficiency causing eye pain, continuous cold tears, muscle and vessel pain, and photophobia or fear of sunlight
a. Xiakucao half liang, Cyperus rotundus rhizome (Xiangfuzi) 1 liang. Grind to powder. Take 1 qian per dose, take with waxed tea infusion as needed. (Jianyao Jizhong Fang: Bu Gan San)
b. Xiakucao 10 liang (cut fine, soaked in wine, steamed and sun-dried three times), Sweet Chrysanthemum (Ganjuhua), Cassia seeds (Juemingzi) each 2 liang, Buddleja flower (Mimenghua) 3 liang, Goji berry (Gouqi) 5 liang. Grind to powder, make into honey pills. Take 3 qian before meals morning and evening. (Bencao Huiyan)
4. For continuous uterine bleeding: Xiakucao powdered. Each dose one fangcun bi, taken with rice gruel. (Shenghui Fang)
5. For leukorrhea (red or white vaginal discharge): collect Xiakucao flowers at bloom, dry in shade and grind to powder. Take 2 qian before meals with rice gruel. (Bencao Gangmu)
6. For postpartum fainting and imminent collapse: squeeze juice from Xiakucao and take one cup. (Bencao Gangmu)
7. For facial paralysis (deviation of mouth and eye): Xiakucao 1 qian, Bile Arisaema (Dan Nanxing) 5 fen, Saposhnikovia root (Fangfeng) 1 qian, Uncaria hook (Gouteng) 1 qian. Decoction in water, add a drop of rice wine and take at bedtime while lying down. (Diannan Bencao)
8. For headache and dizziness:
a. Xiakucao and Rhizome of Aspidistra or Everlasting Green root (Wan Nianqing gen) each 15 g. Decoction in water, one dose daily. (Practical Internal Medicine of Traditional Chinese Medicine)
b. Fresh Xiakucao 2 liang, rock sugar 5 qian. Infuse in boiling water and simmer, take after meals. (Mindong Bencao)
9. For epilepsy and hypertension: fresh Xiakucao 3 liang, winter honey 1 liang. Infuse in boiling water and simmer. (Mindong Bencao)
10. For measles prevention: Xiakucao 15–60 g. Decoction in water, one dose daily for 3 consecutive days. (Xuzhou: Compilation of Single Prescriptions and New Medical Methods)
11. For pediatric bacillary dysentery: under 1 year old, Xiakucao 30 g, Half-branch Lotus (Banzhilian) 15 g; 2–6 years, Xiakucao and Banzhilian each 30 g; 6–12 years, each 45 g. Decoction in water and take. (National Exhibition of New Medical Methods for Chinese Herbal Medicine)
12. For acute tonsillitis and sore throat: fresh whole Xiakucao herb 2–3 liang. Decoction in water and take. (Compilation of Simple Effective Prescriptions of Herbal Medicine)
13. For contusions and bleeding wounds: crush Xiakucao and apply. (Simple Health Remedies)
14. For blows and cuts: chew Xiakucao in the mouth and apply the mashed herb to the wound.
15. For tinea versicolor and white spots: decoct a concentrated juice of Xiakucao and wash daily. (Bencao Gangmu citing Qian Kun Mi Yun)
16. For liver-deficiency nocturnal eyeball pain, severe night pain, chronic pain with blood injury: Xiakucao flowers 1 liang, Cyperus rotundus rhizome (Xiangfu) 2 liang (soak in child urine), Licorice (Gancao) 3 qian, Angelica sinensis (Danggui) 6 qian, White Peony Root (Baishao) 4 qian, Rehmannia (Shengdihuang) 1 liang, Astragalus (Huangqi) 2 liang. Each dose 5 qian, add a pinch of bud tea, decoct, remove dregs and take. (Zhang’s Medical Communication)
17. For hypertension
a. Xiakucao and Chrysanthemum (Juhua) each 10 g, Cassia seeds (Juemingzi) and Uncaria hook (Gouteng) each 15 g. Decoction in water, one dose daily. Take for one week, then add Cassia seeds 30 g daily, decoct and take in two divided doses, stop after two weeks. (Journal of Integrated Traditional and Western Medicine 1983 (3):176)
b. Xiakucao 30 g, Siegesbeckia (Xixian cao) 30 g, Motherwort (Yimucao) 30 g, Cassia seeds (Juemingzi) 35 g, Abalone shell (Shijueming) 30 g. Decoction and take. (Journal of Beijing College of Traditional Chinese Medicine 1989,12(6):41 — San Cao Er Ming Decoction)
18. For faintness, forgetfulness, and tendency to lie down: Xiakucao flower powder. Take one fangcun bi; effective. (Yaoxing Kao)
19. For pain from liver qi stagnation: Xiakucao 1 liang. Decoction and take. (Ji Ren Ji Yan Fang)
20. For thyroid adenoma: Xiakucao 30 g, crucian carp (one large fish or several small ones), remove scales and viscera, wash clean, add water and stew together with Xiakucao. Eat the fish and drink the soup. (Fujian Medicine and Pharmacy Journal 1980(2):55)
21. For pulmonary tuberculosis: Xiakucao 30 g, concentrate the decoction into a paste and dry in the sun, then add Artemisia annua (Qinghao) powder 3 g and Turtle Shell (Biejia) powder 1.5 g, mix well. This is a one-day dose (can also be made into pills), divided into three doses. (National Compilation of Chinese Herbal Medicine)
22. For yin carbuncle and unnamed swellings and toxins: fresh Xiakucao 5–7.5 g. Boil in water until soft, filter and discard dregs, concentrate to a paste. Internally take 2 tablespoons each time, three times daily; externally apply to sores 2–3 times daily. (Sichuan Traditional Chinese Medicine 1983 (6):44)
23. For bloody urine: Xiakucao burned to a char and powdered, take with rice gruel or water. (Zhengzhi Zhunsheng)
24. For menorrhagia: stir-fry Puhuang (Pollen) 9 g, Wulingzhi (Five Spirits) 9 g, Xiakucao 9 g. One dose daily, take in two boluses morning and evening. Continue for two menstrual cycles, do not stop during menses. (Chinese Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology 1986,21(4):215 — Regulating Menses Formula I)
25. For traumatic bleeding: Xiakucao 90 g, Oxalis (Cucao) 60 g, Schneeguehler herb (Xuejiancao) 30 g. Grind to fine powder and sprinkle on the wound, apply sterile dressing with pressure (1–2 minutes), then bandage. (National Compilation of Chinese Herbal Medicine)
26. For beriberi with frequent pain, swelling, and difficulty walking: Xiakucao (soaked in wine overnight, sun-dried, stir-fried) and Papaya (Mu Gua, vinegar-fried) equal parts. Grind to powder, take 3 qian each morning before food with plain water. (Bencao Huiyan citing Menming Ji)
Dietary Therapy
Stewed Pork with Xiakucao
Xiakucao 20 g, lean pork 50 g, pork sliced thin, simmer on low heat to make soup, take twice daily.
This medicinal dish clears liver heat, nourishes yin and supplements deficiency, and can treat deficiency-type hypertension.
