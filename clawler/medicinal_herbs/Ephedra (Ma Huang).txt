Cultivation and preparation of Ephedra (Máhuáng)
Source
Derived from the herbaceous stems of Ephedraceae plants: herbaceous Ephedra (Cǎo Máhuáng), Mùzéi Ephedra (Mùzéi Máhuáng) or medium Ephedra (Zhōng Máhuáng). Between August and October cut the green slender branches, or pull up with roots, remove soil and roots, place in a ventilated location to air-dry or dry until about 60 percent dry, then sun-dry. Store in a dry, ventilated place; protect from moisture and mildew.
Nature and Taste
Acrid, slightly bitter, warm.
Processing
1. Ephedra (Máhuáng): pick out impurities, remove all woody stems and residual roots, wash with water, slightly moisten, then cut into sections and dry to obtain.
2. Ephedra floss (Máhuáng róng): take clean Ephedra (Máhuáng) that has been processed and chopped, place in a grinding trough, and grind until the fibers loosen and form a floss-like texture.
3. Honey-treated Ephedra (Mì Máhuáng): take Ephedra sections, add cooked refined honey and a little boiling water, mix evenly, let steam slightly, place in a pot and stir-fry over low heat until not sticky to the touch, remove and cool. (Per 100 jin of Ephedra sections, use 10–15 jin of refined cooked honey)
Characteristics
1. Herbaceous Ephedra (Cǎo Máhuáng)
Also called: field Ephedra. Stems are slender, cylindrical and slightly compressed, with few branches, diameter about 1–2 mm, usually cut into small sections about 2–3 cm long. Surface pale green to yellow-green, with fine longitudinal ridges, slightly rough to the touch, nodes obvious, internodes 2.5–6 cm long. Nodes bear two membranous scale leaves (rarely three), about 3–4 mm long; upper part gray-white, acuminate, triangular, tips reflexed, bases brown-red, fused into a tubular sheath. Stem brittle and easily broken; fracture slightly fibrous, outer ring greenish-yellow, central pith red-brown. Slight aromatic odor, taste slightly bitter-astringent.
2. Mùzéi Ephedra (Mùzéi Máhuáng)
Stems are slender and cylindrical, highly branched, slightly finer than herbaceous Ephedra, diameter about 1–1.5 mm. Surface grass-green to yellow-green, with longitudinal ridges, not rough to the touch, internodes 1.5–3 cm long. Nodes bear two (rarely three) membranous scale leaves about 1–2 mm long; upper part short triangular, gray-white, tips mostly not reflexed, bases brown-red to dark brown, united into a tubular sheath. Otherwise similar to the above species.
3. Medium Ephedra (Zhōng Máhuáng)
Stems slender and cylindrical, diameter 1.5–3 mm, whole plant yellow-green, nodes bear three membranous scale leaves in whorls about 2–3 mm long, gray-white with acute tips, internodes 2–6 cm long, rough to the touch.
Among the three, the best specimens are those that are dry, have thick stems, pale green color, solid cores, and a bitter-astringent taste.
Actions of Ephedra (Máhuáng)
Efficacy
Derived from the herbaceous stems of Ephedraceae plants such as herbaceous Ephedra (Cǎo Máhuáng), Mùzéi Ephedra (Mùzéi Máhuáng) or medium Ephedra (Zhōng Máhuáng).
Main indications: treats exterior excess in cold damage (shanghan) presenting with fever, aversion to cold and absence of sweating, headache, nasal congestion, pain of bones and joints; cough and asthma; wind-water edema with difficult urination; stubborn wind-bi causing numbness of the skin; wind rashes and pruritus.
Channels (Meridians)
Enters
Lung channel, Bladder channel.
Indications
Induces sweating, calms wheezing, and promotes urination. Treats exterior excess of cold damage with fever, aversion to cold and absence of sweating, headache, nasal congestion, and pain of bones and joints; cough and asthma; wind-water edema with difficulty urinating; stubborn wind-bi, skin numbness, and itchy wind rashes.
1. Benjing (The Classic Materia Medica): treats windstroke, headache from cold damage, and warm malaria. Induces sweating to release the exterior and expel pathogenic heat, stops rebellious cough and breath, dispels cold and heat, and dissolves hard accumulations.
2. Bielu: treats urgent and sluggish pathogenic qi of the five viscera, flank pain due to wind, residual afflictions of the breast. Stops excessive salivation, opens the interstices, releases the muscles; drains out noxious qi, disperses red and black spots and toxins.
3. Yaoxing Lun: treats stubborn toxic wind-bi on the body, causing numbness of skin and flesh.
4. Rihuazi Materia Medica: unblocks the nine orifices, regulates the blood vessels, and resists mountainous mists and miasma.
5. Zhenzhu Nang: drains excess in the protective qi, dispels cold from the nutritive qi, and induces sweating of the Taiyang and Shaoyin.
6. Diannan Materia Medica: treats nasal orifices blocked and obstructed, inability to smell fragrances, and lung-cold cough.
7. Compendium of Materia Medica (Bencao Gangmu): disperses red swollen painful eyes, treats edema, wind-swelling, and postpartum blood stasis.
8. Scientific Folk Herbs: treats asthma, hay fever, pertussis, bronchitis, etc.
9. Modern Practical Chinese Medicine: effective for joint pain.
10. Identification Methods of Chinese Medicine Properties: treats abdominal pain, diarrhea, hernia, eye disorders and common colds.
Dosage and Administration
Internal use: decoction, 1.5–10 g; or incorporated into pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, grind to powder for nasal insufflation or apply powdered.
Raw use: strong diaphoretic power; use for inducing sweating and promoting urination.
Honey-fried use: weaker diaphoretic power; honey-frying also moistens the Lung, stops cough and calms wheezing, and is commonly used.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated in those constitutionally weak who have spontaneous sweating, night sweats, or chronic asthma.
1. Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu: Magnolia bark (Hòupò) is used as an assistant. Incompatible with Magnolia flower bud (Xìnyí) and Pyrrosia (Shíwéi).
2. Bielu: should not be taken in large amounts; it causes debility.
3. Shu Materia Medica: Baiwei (Bái Wēi) is used as an assistant.
4. Introduction to Medicine: contraindicated in common colds with sweating and in those with yin deficiency or digestive impairment.
5. Ben Cao Jing Shu: contraindicated in exterior deficiency with spontaneous sweating, yin deficiency with night sweating; in lung deficiency with heat and abundant phlegm causing nasal obstruction; in sores with severe heat not due to cold stagnation; in debilitated persons with wind-cold, qi-deficient asthma; in yin-deficiency with blazing fire causing dizziness and headache; in southern windstroke paralysis, and in people with chronic yang deficiency and loose interstices.

Dietary Therapy
Ephedra and Apricot Porridge
Ephedra 5 g, Apricot kernel 15 g, milled rice 100 g. Decoction of Ephedra with water, remove scum and dregs; peel and trim the apricot kernels (preferably sweet apricot kernels), place in the decoction and cook 6–7 minutes, then add the milled rice and cook into porridge, then consume.
Efficacy: Acrid-warm to release the exterior, transforms phlegm and stops cough.
