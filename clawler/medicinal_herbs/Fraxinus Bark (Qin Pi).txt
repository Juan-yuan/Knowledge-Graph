Cultivation and Processing of Qinpi
Source
This product is the dried branch bark or dried bark of Oleaceae plants Manchurian ash (Fraxinus rhynchophylla Hance), Chinese ash (Fraxinus chinensis Roxb.), Szabo's ash (Fraxinus szaboana Lingelsh.) or Stylosa ash (Fraxinus stylosa Lingelsh.). It is peeled in the spring and autumn seasons and sun-dried.
Taste and properties
Bitter, cold.
Processing
Pick out impurities, wash clean, moisten thoroughly, then cut into pieces or sections and sun-dry.
Characteristics
Branch bark: forms rolled tubular or grooved pieces, length 10–60 cm, thickness 1.5–3 mm. Outer surface gray-white, gray-brown to black-brown or mottled, flat or slightly rough, with gray-white round lenticels and fine oblique wrinkles, some with branch scars. Inner surface yellow-white or brown, smooth. Texture hard and brittle, fracture fibrous, yellow-white. Odorless, bitter taste.
Dry bark: long strip-like flakes, thickness 3–6 mm. Outer surface gray-brown, with cracked grooves and red-brown round or transversely elongated lenticels. Texture hard, fracture strongly fibrous.
Effects of Qinpi
Functions
This product is the dried branch bark or dried bark of Oleaceae plants Manchurian ash, Chinese ash, Szabo's ash or Stylosa ash.
Main indications: bacillary dysentery, enteritis, leukorrhea, chronic bronchitis, red swollen painful eyes, lacrimation in wind, psoriasis.
Channels entered
Enters
Liver channel, Gallbladder channel.
Indications
Clears heat and dries dampness, relieves wheezing and stops cough, brightens the eyes. Treats bacillary dysentery, enteritis, leukorrhea, chronic bronchitis, red swollen painful eyes, lacrimation in wind, psoriasis.
1. Benjing: indicated for wind-cold-damp painful obstruction (bi), washes away cold qi, removes heat, bluish opacities and white membranes in the eye.
2. Bie Lu: treats male sperm deficiency, female leukorrhea, childhood convulsions and feverishness; can be used to wash the eyes as a decoction.
3. Yaoxing Lun: brightens the eyes, removes chronic heat in the liver, two red swollen painful eyes with continuous tearing from wind; treats childhood fever: use as a bathing decoction.
4. Rihuazi Materia Medica: washes the liver, benefits the essence, brightens the eyes, calms childhood febrile convulsions, treats wind obstruction of the skin, reduces fever.
5. Zhang Yuansu: treats excessive uterine bleeding in women.
6. Tangye Materia Medica: indicated for severe dysenteric diarrhea due to heat, lower burner deficiency.
7. Jilin Chinese Materia Medica: treats intestinal wind with bloody stools.
8. Heilongjiang Commonly Used Chinese Materia Medica Manual: treats enteritis and diarrhea.
9. Shaanxi Chinese Materia Medica: treats stye (hordeolum).
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoction, 6–12 g; or included in pill formulations.
External use: decoction for washing.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated in those with spleen and stomach deficiency-cold.
1. Bencao Jing Jizhu: Euphorbia (Da Ji) acts as its envoy; incompatible with Evodia (Zhu Yu).
2. Yaoxing Lun: incompatible with Kuhu and Fangkui.
3. Benjing Fengyuan: contraindicated for those with stomach deficiency and poor appetite.
Formulas containing Qinpi
1. For hot dysenteric diarrhea with continuous discharge:
Pulsatilla (Baitouweng) two liang, Phellodendron bark (Huangbai) three liang, Coptis (Huanglian) three liang, Qinpi three liang. Take the four ingredients in seven sheng of water, boil down to two sheng, remove dregs, take warm one sheng. If not cured, take another sheng. (Treatise on Febrile Diseases (Shanghan Lun): Baitouweng Decoction)
2. For chronic bacterial dysentery: Qinpi 12 g, raw Sanguisorba (Diyu) 9 g, Toona root bark (Chungenpi) 9 g each. Decoction in water. (Hebei Chinese Materia Medica Manual)
3. For diarrhea: Qinpi three qian. Decoction with sugar, take in divided doses. (Heilongjiang Commonly Used Chinese Materia Medica Manual)
4. For red eyes and eye ulcers: Qinpi one large liang. Soak in one large sheng of clear water in a white porcelain bowl; in spring and summer soak for more than one meal time until a bluish color appears; then wrap the end of a chopstick with soft cloth, dip in the bluish liquid and apply to the affected eye while lying supine, first from the outer canthus filling inwards; slight pain not to be feared; repeat three to five times between meals; then lie on one side to let hot juices drain. Apply ten times or more daily; usually cured within two days. Avoid vinegar and radish. (Recent Effective Formulas)
5. For stye with constipation: Qinpi 9 g, Rhubarb (Dahuang) 6 g. Decoction in water. Contraindicated in pregnancy. (Hebei Chinese Materia Medica Manual)
6. For female leukorrhea red or white discharge and continuous uterine bleeding: Qinpi three liang, Moutan bark (Mudanhpi) two liang, Angelica (Danggui) one liang of the body; wash all in wine, fry and grind to powder, refine with honey into pills the size of sycamore seeds. Take five qian each morning with plain decoction. (Materia Medica Huiyan)
7. For pediatric convulsions with fever and intermittent steaming fevers: Qinpi and Poria (Fuling) each one qian, Licorice (Gancao) five fen, Juncus pith (Dengxincao) twenty strands. Decoction in water. (Essentials of Pediatrics)
8. For psoriasis: Qinpi 30–60 g, decoct and wash affected areas daily or every 2–3 days; each decoction can wash three times (use warm water). (National Compilation of Chinese Materia Medica)
9. For acute bacillary dysentery: Qinpi and Sophora flavescens root (Kushen) each 12 g, fried radish seed (Laifuzi) and Guangmuxiang (Aucklandia, Guangmuxiang) each 9 g. Grind to fine powder, mix with boiling water to form a paste, take 9–12 g each dose, 3–4 times daily. (Guoyi Forum 1986, (2):52)
10. For chronic protracted bloody diarrhea formula: Sage (Shuweicao), Rose root (Qiangwei gen), Qinpi. The three herbs in equal parts, chew orally, then soak and decoct in water, remove dregs, boil in a heavy copper pot into pills the size of sycamore seeds, take five or six pills three times daily, increase slightly as needed until cured. Can also take a half sheng of concentrated juice. (Prescriptions Worth a Thousand Gold)
11. For acute hepatitis: Qinpi 9 g, Capillaris herb (Yinchen) and Dandelion (Pugongying) each 30 g, Phellodendron bark (Huangbai) 9 g, Rhubarb (Dahuang) 9 g. Decoction in water. (Shanxi Chinese Materia Medica)
12. For toxic ulcers from the "field spider" (spider bite sores resembling scabies): Qinpi strongly decocted and taken orally; apply shell powder (Bengfen), talc (Huashi), and Chuan fritillary (Chuanbeimu) powder topically. (Zhi Zhi Fang)
