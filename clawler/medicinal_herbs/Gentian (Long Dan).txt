Cultivation and Processing of Chinese Gentian
Source
This product is the dried roots and rhizomes of Gentianaceae plants: Gentiana manshurica Kitag. (Tiao Ye Long Dan), Gentiana scabra Bunge (Long Dan), Gentiana triflora Pall. (San Hua Long Dan), or Gentiana rigescens Franch. (Dian Long Dan). The first three are commonly called Long Dan; the latter is commonly called Jian Long Dan. Harvested in spring and autumn, washed clean, and dried.
Properties and Flavor
Bitter, cold.
Processing
Remove impurities, wash clean, moisten thoroughly, cut into sections, and dry.
Description
(1) Chinese Gentian: rhizomes mostly prostrate, length 0.5–3 cm, diameter 3–8 mm, with multiple stem scars; below are more than 4–30 roots, often more than 20; roots slender cylindrical, slightly twisted, diameter 1–3 mm; surface gray‑white or yellow‑brown, upper part with distinct transverse striations, lower part with longitudinal wrinkles and fine root scars. Texture brittle, absorbs moisture and softens easily; fracture surface yellow‑brown, wood portion dotted yellow‑white, in rings, central pith obvious. Slight odor, extremely bitter taste.
(2) Gentiana manshurica (Tiao Ye Long Dan): rhizomes mostly erect, nodular or long nodular, length 0.5–1.5 cm, diameter 4–7 mm, below clustered with 2–16 roots, often fewer than 10. Roots about 15 cm long, diameter 2–4 mm; surface yellow‑brown or gray‑brown, with twisted longitudinal wrinkles, upper part with dense transverse striations and a few raised lateral root scars.
(3) Gentiana triflora (San Hua Long Dan): rhizomes mostly erect, length 1–5.5 cm, diameter 0.7–1.5 cm, below more than 4–30 roots, often more than 15, root diameter 1–6 mm; surface yellow‑white. Transverse striations are generally distinct throughout.
(4) Gentiana rigescens (Dian Long Dan): rhizomes nodular, with 1–10 or more old stems, below more than 4–30 roots; roots slender spindle‑shaped, slightly curved, diameter 1–4 mm; surface light brown or brown‑black; cross section shows white wood in the center.
Effects of Chinese Gentian
Actions and Indications
This product is the dried roots and rhizomes of Gentianaceae plants Tiao Ye Long Dan, Long Dan, San Hua Long Dan, or Jian Long Dan.
Mainly treats excessive heat in the liver channel, convulsions and mania, type B encephalitis, headache, red eyes, sore throat, jaundice, summer‑heat dysentery, abscesses and ulcers, swollen and painful scrotum, and damp itching of the genital area.
Channels Entered
Enters the Liver channel and the Gallbladder channel.
Indications
Drains Liver and Gallbladder excess fire, clears damp‑heat from the lower burner. Treats excess heat in the Liver channel, convulsions and mania, type B encephalitis, headache, red eyes, sore throat, jaundice, summer‑heat dysentery, abscesses and ulcers, swollen painful scrotum, and damp itching of the genital area.
1. Benjing: treats alternating cold and heat between the bones, fright and lameness from pathogenic qi, restores disconnected injuries, stabilizes the five zang, and kills gu poison.
2. Bie Lu: eliminates latent stomach heat, seasonal warm‑heat, heat‑induced diarrhea, expels small intestinal parasites, benefits Liver and Gallbladder qi, and stops fright and vigilance.
3. Yaoxing Lun: treats pediatric convulsions entering the heart, robust fever and bone heat, and abscesses; treats epidemic fevers with yellowing and mouth ulcers.
4. Rihuazi Bencao: treats rebellious behavior, pediatric marasmus (gan qi), delirium with febrile disease, sores and scabies, clarifies the eyes, and relieves vexation.
5. Bencao Tujing: ancient formulas frequently use it for treating jaundice.
6. Luchanyan Bencao: treats alcohol‑induced toxic bloody stools and intestinal wind bleeding.
7. Yixue Qiyuan: treats yellow eyes and red swelling of the eyes, eyeball distention, raised nodular congealed flesh that is unbearably painful. The "Zhu Zhi Mijue" says: treats lower‑body wind‑damp and damp‑heat, swelling and pain from below the navel to the feet, and cold‑damp beriberi.
8. Diannan Bencao: treats sore throat, and is used to wash ulcers and scabies toxins and swellings.
9. Yixue Rumen: treats sudden cardiac pain, heart pain due to parasitic attack, and limb pain.
10. Bencao Gangmu: treats sore throat, wind‑heat, and night sweating.
Usage and Dosage
Internal use: decoct and drink, 3–6 g; or include in pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, decoct for washing; or grind to powder and mix for topical application.
Precautions and Contraindications
Contraindicated in spleen and stomach deficiency with diarrhea and in those without damp‑heat or excess fire; do not take on an empty stomach.
Formulas Using Chinese Gentian
1. For typhoid fever with mania: Powder Grass Chinese Gentian (Cao Long Dan), add chicken egg white and white honey, dilute with cool water and take two qian. (Shanghan Yunyao)
2. For excess fire and damp‑heat of the Liver and Gallbladder channels with ribside pain, deafness, bitter taste in the mouth from gallbladder discharge, muscle weakness, seminal sweating, genital swelling and pain, white turbidity in urine and bloody urine: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao, wine‑fried), Baical Skullcap (Huang Qin, dry‑fried), Gardenia Fruit (Zhi Zi, wine‑fried), Alisma (Ze Xie), Akebia (Mu Tong), Plantain Seed (Che Qian Zi), Chinese Angelica (Dang Gui, wine‑washed), Raw Rehmannia (Sheng Di Huang, wine‑fried), Bupleurum (Chai Hu), Licorice (Gan Cao, raw); decoct in water and drink. (Jufang Long Dan Xie Gan Tang)
3. For night blindness (unable to see at night): one liang Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao) and one liang Coptis (Huang Lian). Grind both into fine powder; after eating, dip hot sheep liver in the powder and take. (Luchanyan Bencao)
4. For summer heat causing gritty eyes: fresh Chinese Gentian (crushed for juice) one he, Coptis (soaked for juice) one spoonful, mix and apply as drops. (Shi Yi De Xiao Fang)
5. For purulent discharge from the eye: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao) and Chinese Angelica (Dang Gui) in equal parts, powdered. Each dose two qian, take with warm water. (Fei Hong Ji)
6. For grain‑induced jaundice with dizziness after eating, anxiety and yellowing caused by overeating and stomach qi rising and attacking: Sophora Root (Ku Shen) three liang, Chinese Gentian (Long Dan) one he, form Ox Gall pills about the size of wuzi, take five pills with raw wheat juice three times daily. (Buque Zhouhou Fang)
7. For sore throat and swelling: a handful of Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao), crush for juice and gargle and swallow. (Bencao Huiyan)
8. For sudden unstoppable lower bleeding: a handful of Grass Chinese Gentian (Cao Long Dan), cut and boil five liters of water down to two and a half liters, divide into five doses; if not better, continue. (Yao Sengtan Collection of Tested Prescriptions)
9. For persistent night sweating after typhoid, or all night sweats in women and children: any amount of Chinese Gentian, dry roast, grind to fine powder; each dose one large qian, mix with three liang pig gallbladder juice and a little warm wine, take on an empty stomach before lying down. (Yang Family Treasury Formula: Long Dan Decoction)
10. For scrotal wind ulcers, itching, or rupture with discharge, also called embroidered‑ball wind: Bitter Chinese Gentian (Ku Long Dan Cao), frost‑treated peach leaves (Jing Shuang Tao Ye), Beehive/Honeycomb (Feng Fang), Veratrum (Li Lu), Psoralea (Bu Gu Zhi). Grind to a fine powder and mix with sesame oil for topical application. (Diannan Bencao)
11. For acute icteric infectious hepatitis: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan) and Artemisia (Yin Chen) 12 g each, Curcuma (Yu Jin) and Phellodendron (Huang Bai) 6 g each; decoct in water and drink. (Qingdao Chinese Herbal Manual)
12. For yin jaundice: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan) and Qin Jiao (Qin Jiao, roots washed of soil) one liang and a half each, Cimicifuga (Sheng Ma) one liang. Coarsely pulverize the first two herbs and sieve. Each dose five qian and a spoonful, soak in one and a half bowls of water overnight, decoct to eight tenths the next morning, add five he of yellow ox milk, decoct down to one bowl, strain. Take on an empty stomach in two warm doses, repeat once a day as needed to produce bowel movement. (Shengji Zonglu Long Dan Tang)
13. For sudden bloody urine with stem‑center pain: a handful of Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao), decoct and drink.
14. For scrotal itching that remains moist and does not dry, eventually making the scrotal skin dry and rough and increasing the itch: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao) two liang, Chinese Gall (Wu Bei Zi) five qian, Liu Ji Nu (Liu Ji Nu) one liang. Boil in one large jar of water to near boiling, strain out the dregs, add five fen camphor (ground), wait until the decoction cools enough to handle, and soak to wash. (Three to four formulas from Bencao Huiyan)
15. For hypertension: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao) 9 g, Prunella (Xia Ku Cao) 15 g. Decoction in water and drink. (Fujian Materia Medica)
16. For congestion of the pupil and violent red eye pain or pterygium formation: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao), Asarum (Xi Xin), Saposhnikovia (Fang Feng) each two liang. Decoction with a small piece of refined sugar and drink. (Zhengzhi Zunsheng)
17. For roundworm attacking the heart like stabbing pain with vomiting clear water: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan) one liang (remove head, grate). Two bowls of water boiled down to one bowl, strain. Fast overnight, take at dawn in one dose. (Shenghui Fang)
18. For yang‑toxin typhoid with poisonous qi in the viscera, wild speech, and inclination to run away: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan) one liang (remove the pith head), iron powder (Tie Luo) two liang. Pulverize finely and make into powder. Each dose one qian, mixed with whetstone water for administration at any time. (Puji Fang)
19. For pediatric persistent fever with convulsions: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan, remove pith head) and Dragon Tooth (Long Chi) each three fen, Calculus Bovis (Niu Huang) one fen finely powdered. Grind into fine powder, add Musk (She Xiang) two qian, form pills with refined honey the size of a grain of millet. Administer at any time with five pills following Jing Jie decoction. (Shenghui Fang)
20. For infantile malnutrition with fever: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao, remove pith), Coptis (Huang Lian, remove rootlets, lightly fried), Green Tangerine Peel (Qing Pi, remove white pith), Quisqualis Seed (Shi Jun Zi, peel removed and roasted). Grind equal parts into fine powder, mix with pig gallbladder juice into pills the size of radish seeds. Each dose twenty pills, adjust as needed, take at bedtime with hot water. (Jufang Long Dan Pills)
21. For sore throat and chronic throat constriction with difficulty swallowing congee: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan) one liang, Alum (Dan Fan, ground), Frankincense (Ru Xiang, ground) each one fen. Pound and grind the three to a uniform powder, bind with refined sugar into pills the size of peas. Each dose one pill, wrap in cotton, hold in mouth to dissolve and swallow saliva; if not improved, repeat. (Shengji Zonglu Long Dan Gao)
22. For scrofula under the neck whether new or old, if hot select and clean Long Dan; pound the single herb into a powder. Each dose one qian and a spoonful, take with wine or rice beverage after meals and before bed. On overcast days, continue taking. (Shengji Zonglu Qingliang San)
23. For infants who sweat all over during the night: Chinese Gentian (Long Dan Cao) in any amount, or add Saposhnikovia (Fang Feng), powdered and made into vinegar‑paste pills the size of green beans. Each dose five to seven pills, take with rice beverage. (Youke Le Cui Tongshen Wan)
24. To promote lactation postpartum when milk does not flow: Di Dan Cao (Di Dan Cao), Trichosanthes Root (Tian Hua Fen) (as Gua Lou Gen), and Lettuce Seed (Wo Ju Zi) in equal parts, powdered. Each dose two qian, mix with warm scallion and wine and take, three to four times daily. (Puji Fang)
