Cultivation and Processing of Zhi Zi
Source
This product is the dried mature fruit of Gardenia jasminoides Ellis, family Rubiaceae; its root can also be used medicinally. Fruits are harvested when ripe and red-yellow from September to November, stems and impurities removed, steamed until steam issues or briefly scalded in boiling water, removed and dried. Roots are dug in summer and autumn, washed and sun-dried.
Macroscopic morphology
Gardenia (Shan Zhi) is an evergreen shrub, 1–2 m tall. Young twigs green, initially pubescent then nearly glabrous. Leaves opposite, sometimes ternate or whorled with short petiole; two stipules on the inner side of the petiole; leaf blade leathery, ovate, broadly lanceolate or obovate, 6–14 cm long, 2–7 cm wide, apex acute or acuminate, base cuneate, entire margin, glossy above, only with short hairs clustered in axils under the veins; lateral veins pinnate. Flowers large, very fragrant, terminal or axillary with short pedicel; calyx green, 2–3 cm long, 5–7 lobes linear-lanceolate, usually slightly longer than the calyx tube; corolla campanulate-dish-shaped, white turning creamy yellow, fused at base into a tube with 6–7 lobes arranged in a rotate manner, lobes rounded at apex; stamens equal in number to corolla lobes, attached at the throat of the corolla, filaments very short, anthers linear, longitudinally dehiscent, two locules; pistil one, ovary inferior, one locule. Fruit deep yellow, obovoid or oblong-ellipsoid, 2–4 cm long, with 5–9 wing-like longitudinal ribs, apex with a linear persistent calyx. Seeds numerous, fresh yellow, flattened-ellipsoid. Flowering May to July, fruiting August to November.
Habitat and distribution
Ecology: grows in hilly mountains or shrublands on slopes.
Distribution: found in central-south and southwest China and in Jiangsu, Anhui, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Taiwan and other areas.
Taste and properties
Bitter, cold.
Processing
1. Raw Zhi Zi: sieve out ash and debris, pick out impurities, crush and sift; or trim both ends.
2. Shan Zhi Ren: take clean Zhi Zi, cut in half with scissors, peel off outer skin and remove kernels.
3. Shan Zhi Pi: the outer pericarp peeled from raw Zhi Zi.
4. Fried Zhi Zi: take crushed Zhi Zi, place in a pan and stir-fry over low heat until golden yellow, remove and cool.
5. Jiao Zhi Zi (charred Zhi Zi): take crushed Zhi Zi, place in a pan and stir-fry over high heat until charred, remove and cool.
6. Zhi Zi Charcoal: take crushed Zhi Zi, place in a pan and stir-fry over high heat until dark brown-black while retaining efficacy, remove and cool.
7. From Lei Gong Pao Zhi Lun: when using Zhi Zi, first peel it completely, take the required amount, soak in licorice (Gan Cao) water overnight, strain and dry, then pound and sieve into a powder like red-gold dust before use.
Characters (appearance and organoleptic)
Zhi Zi: dried fruit oblong or ellipsoid, 1–4.5 cm long, 0.6–2 cm thick. Surface deep red or red-yellow, with 5–8 longitudinal ribs. Apex with remaining calyx, other end slightly tapered with pedicle scar. Pericarp thin and brittle, inner surface red-yellow and glossy, with 2–3 raised false septa, many seeds inside bound into a mass. Seeds rounded-flattened, deep red or red-yellow, densely covered with small wart-like protrusions. When soaked in water the water is dyed bright yellow. Aroma faint, taste slightly sour. Best quality are small, intact, with full kernels and red color inside and out. Lower quality are large, pericarp brown-yellow, kernels shriveled, color red-yellow.
Jiao Zhi Zi (charred Zhi Zi): shape like Zhi Zi or irregular fragments, surface charred brown or black. Pericarp thin and brittle, inner surface brown, seed mass brown or brownish-black. Aroma faint, taste slightly sour and bitter.
Therapeutic actions of Zhi Zi
Functions
This product is the dried mature fruit of Gardenia (Zhi Zi) of the Rubiaceae family.
Treats vexation and insomnia from febrile disease, jaundice, strangury, diabetes (wasting-thirst), red eyes, sore throat, vomiting blood, nosebleed, bloody dysentery, hematuria, heat-toxic sores and ulcers, sprains and swelling pain.
Channels entered
Enters the Heart channel, Liver channel, Lung channel, Stomach channel.
Main indications
Clears heat, drains fire, cools the blood. Treats vexation and insomnia from febrile disease, jaundice, strangury, wasting-thirst, red eyes, sore throat, vomiting blood, nosebleed, bloody dysentery, hematuria, heat-toxic sores and ulcers, sprains and swelling pain.
1. Ben Jing: treats pathogenic qi of the five viscera, heat qi in the stomach, facial redness, alcohol blisters and bloody nose, vitiligo white lesions, red lesions, sores and ulcers.
2. Ben Cao Jing Ji Zhu: resolves zhi zhu toxin.
3. Bie Lu: treats eye heat and pain, intense heat of chest and large or small intestine, vexation in the heart, heat in the stomach.
4. Yao Xing Lun: kills scorpion and insect poison, eliminates heat-toxic wind, promotes urination for five types of strangury, treats central evil, unblocks urination, resolves five kinds of jaundice, brightens the eyes, treats intermittent febrile diseases by clearing heat and wasting-thirst and parched mouth.
5. Shi Liao Ben Cao: treats aphonia, purpura wind, jaundice and accumulated heat causing restlessness.
6. Yi Yuan Yi Xue Qi Yuan: treats heat invading the Heart channel, relieves vexation, removes upper-jiao deficiency heat, treats wind.
7. Yao Lei Fa Xiang: treats vexation and inability to sleep, mind in turmoil to near collapse, blood stasis with difficult urination.
8. Zhu Zhenheng: drains fire of the three jiaos, clears heat in the epigastrium and blood, treats heat syncope chest pain, resolves heat accumulation and moves stagnated qi.
8. Ben Cao Gang Mu: treats vomiting blood, nosebleed, bloody dysentery, lower body bleeding, blood strangury, trauma with blood stasis, recovery from cold damage with chronic fatigue, heat syncope headache, hernia, scalds and burns.
10. Ben Cao Bei Yao: raw Zhi Zi drains fire, charred Zhi Zi stops bleeding, ginger-juice fried treats vexation and vomiting, use kernel (ren) for internal heat, pericarp (pi) for exterior heat.
11. Guangzhou Military Commonly Used Chinese Herbs Manual: clears heat and detoxifies, cools blood and drains fire. Treats jaundice-type hepatitis, favism jaundice, high fever from cold, dysentery, nephritis with edema, nosebleed, mouth and tongue ulcers, mastitis, sores and swollen toxins.
Dosage and administration
Internal use: decoction, 5–10 g; or used in pills or powders.
External use: appropriate amount, grind into powder or mix and apply as a poultice.
Precautions and contraindications
Contraindicated in spleen-deficiency with loose stools.
1. Ben Cao Hui Yan: for vomiting blood or nosebleed not due to violent yang-fire, avoid use.
2. De Pei Ben Cao: contraindicated when the pathogen is at the exterior and deficiency fire rises.
Prescriptions using Zhi Zi
1. For cold-damage with sweating and vomiting; after purging if there is deficiency vexation and inability to sleep, oppression in the chest: Zhi Zi 14 pieces (split), fragrant fermented soybean (lightly fermented soybeans, Dan Dou Chi) 4 he (wrapped in cotton). Boil the two ingredients in four sheng of water until two and a half sheng remain, add the fermented soybean and boil to one and a half sheng, remove dregs, divide into two doses. Warm take one dose; if it induces vomiting, stop; take the second dose afterwards. (Shang Han Lun: Zhi Zi Chi Tang)
2. For convalescence from severe cold-damage with lingering fatigue: Bitter Orange (Zhi Shi) three pieces (roasted), Zhi Zi 14 pieces (split), fermented soybean one sheng (wrapped in cotton). Boil these three in seven sheng of clear liquid until four sheng remain; add Zhi Shi and Zhi Zi and boil to two sheng, then add fermented soybean and boil five or six times; remove dregs, warm and divide into two doses, cover to induce slight sweating. If there is retained food, take Da Huang about five or six pieces the size of checkers. (Shang Han Lun: Zhi Shi Zhi Zi Chi Tang)

Dietary therapy recipe
Zhi Zi Fresh Lotus Root and Imperata Root Congee
Zhi Zi 10 g (kernels ground into fine powder and reserved), fresh lotus root 60 g, Imperata root (Bai Mao Gen) 30 g, japonica rice 100 g. Wash and thinly slice the fresh lotus root; decoct Imperata root and strain out dregs. Put Imperata root juice, lotus slices and rice into a pot, add appropriate water and cook into congee. When cooked, add Zhi Zi kernel powder and simmer a little longer. Take one dose daily in two divided portions for 3–5 days as one course.
Function: clears heat, generates fluids, cools the blood and stops bleeding. Indicated for stomach heat with vomiting blood and related conditions.
