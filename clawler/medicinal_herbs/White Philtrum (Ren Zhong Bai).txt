Cultivation and Preparation of Ren Zhong Bai (Ren Zhong Bai)
Source
It is the solid substance naturally precipitated from the urine of healthy humans. Scrape the long-settled urine encrustation from the inner surfaces of old urine jars, chamber pots, etc., remove impurities, and sun-dry.
Taste and Properties
Taste: salty; nature: cold
Processing
Ren Zhong Bai (Rén Zhōng Bái): place in clear water and wash for 4–7 days, changing the water frequently; take out, scrape off impurities, sun by day and expose by night for 15 days, turn over once daily until odorless, and dry.
Calcined Ren Zhong Bai: take Ren Zhong Bai, place in a crucible, calcine over charcoal until red, remove and cool.
Water-levigated Ren Zhong Bai: grind Ren Zhong Bai to a fine powder, then levigate in water until no sound.
Appearance
This product appears as irregular plate-like pieces of uneven size, thickness about 3–5 mm. Surface is gray-white, uneven, often with spindle-shaped or tumor-like protuberances; the bottom is relatively flat. Texture is hard and brittle, breaks easily. The fracture shows distinct layers of varying thickness. Has a urine-like odor and tastes slightly salty. Prefer those that are dry, gray-white in color, hard in texture, and free of impurities.

Indications
Clears heat, reduces fire, and resolves blood stasis. Treats fever from overexertion, pulmonary consumption, epistaxis, hematemesis, throat obstruction, dental canker, and mouth and tongue sores.
1. Bie Lu: treats nasal bleeding and scalds and burns.
2. Tang Materia Medica: chiefly for tight lip sores.
3. Rihuazi Materia Medica: treats fever from overwork, pulmonary consumption, heat in the heart and diaphragm, profuse nasal bleeding and vomiting blood, emaciation and intense thirst.
4. Ben Cao Yanyi Buyi: can drain liver fire and disperse yin fire.
5. Ben Cao Meng Quan: stops expectoration of blood from pulmonary abscess.
6. Compendium of Materia Medica: reduces fire and disperses blood stasis; treats throat and oral ulcers, scrofula, bleeding from various orifices, and bleeding through the skin with sweating.
7. Ben Cao Zheng: burn and grind to a powder; greatly treats moist ulcerations and rotten sores, chancroid and malignant sores, promotes flesh regeneration, and is good at resolving toxic heat.
8. Yu Jiu Yao Jie: clears the heart and drains fire, cools the blood and stops epistaxis.
Dosage and Administration
Internal: grind to a powder, 3–6 g. External: appropriate amount, grind to powder for dusting, mixing, or topical application.
Cautions and Contraindications
Ben Cao Cong Xin: contraindicated in cases of yang deficiency without internal heat, poor digestion, or weak intestines.
Prescriptions using Ren Zhong Bai
1. For chronic continuous nasal bleeding: old cotton five cun (burnt to ash), Ren Zhong Bai one fen. Grind finely into a powder; each dose is mixed with freshly drawn water and taken, two qian. (Shenghui Fang)
2. For bleeding from the nose with sweating that does not stop for five to seven days: Ren Zhong Bai—amount not limited; scrape onto a new tile, dry over fire, grind and add a small amount of musk (She Xiang), take with wine. (Jingyan Fang)
3. For throat obstruction, throat abscess, throat ringworm, bilateral breast sores, and oral canker:

5. For mouth and tongue sores:
Ren Zhong Bai seven fen, Ku Fan three fen. Grind and mix evenly; wipe away saliva and apply several times. (Binhu Ji Jian Fang)
