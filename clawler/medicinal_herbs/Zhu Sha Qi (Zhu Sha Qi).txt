Cultivation and Processing of Zhu Sha Qi
Source
Derived from the Polygonaceae, the Polygonum species Mao Mai Liao (Polygonum cillinerve (Nakai) Ohwi [P. multiflorum Thunb. var. cillinerve (Nakai) Steward]); the tuberous root is used medicinally. It is dug in spring and autumn, the fibrous roots are removed, washed clean, sliced and sun-dried for later use. Alternatively it may be stir-fried with honey or fried with vinegar before use.
[Morphology]
Mao Mai Liao (Mao Mai Liao) is a perennial creeping herb; the rhizome is swollen into tuberous, woody masses. Stems are slender, hollow, green-purple, branching at the tips. Stipules are membranous, brown, nearly transparent. Leaves are alternate; petioles 0.5–5 cm long, grooved above and with sticky papillary protuberances or minute hairs beneath; leaf blades are oblong-elliptic, 6–11 cm long and 3–6 cm wide. Inflorescences are terminal or axillary panicles; flowers have distinct pedicels; perianth is white or pale purple, 5-cleft, the midvein of the outer segments has a wing; stamens 8, ovary three-angled, styles 3, stigma shield-shaped. Achenes are trigonal, black-purple, enclosed by the expanded membranous-winged perianth. Flowering period in summer.
[Habitat and Distribution]
Ecological environment: grows on slopes, roadsides, river flats or among loose stones.
Resource distribution: distributed in Northeast China, Northwest China and Hubei, Hunan, Sichuan, Guizhou and other areas.
Nature and flavor
Bitter, slightly astringent, cool. Slightly toxic.
Appearance
The tuberous root is irregularly nodular or somewhat cylindrical, 8–15 cm or longer, 3–7 cm in diameter, surface brownish-yellow. The root crown bears numerous stem bases forming nodules. Woody rings of deep to pale yellow, with scattered pale yellow wood bundles near the pith. Odor faint, taste bitter.
Effects of Zhu Sha Qi
Actions
Derived from the Polygonaceae, the Polygonum species Mao Mai Liao; the tuberous root is used medicinally. Used for tonsillitis, gastritis, enteritis, dysentery, urinary tract infection, hematemesis, epistaxis, bloody stool, functional uterine bleeding, irregular menstruation; externally for traumatic injuries and bleeding from wounds.
Meridians entered
Lung; Large Intestine; Liver meridians
Indications
Clears heat and detoxifies, relieves pain, stops bleeding, regulates menstruation. Used for tonsillitis, gastritis, enteritis, dysentery, urinary tract infection, hematemesis, epistaxis, bloody stool, functional uterine bleeding, irregular menstruation; externally for traumatic injury and bleeding from wounds.
Dosage and administration
Internal: 3–9 g, decocted in water or taken soaked in wine, or ground to powder and taken with liquid;
External: appropriate amount, powdered or formulated into a 20–30% ointment or a 1% solution.
Precautions and contraindications
Use with caution in pregnancy.
Formulas containing Zhu Sha Qi
1. For postpartum blood stasis, cold limbs, bluish lips and abdominal distention, coma: Hong Yao Zi (Hong Yao Zi) one liang, Tou Hong Hua (Tou Hong Hua) one qian. Two bowls of water, decoct to one bowl and take; promotes bowel and bladder passage. The blood is discharged downward by itself. (From "Lecturer Yu's Experience Formulas")
2. For acute gastric pain: Zhu Sha Qi (raw) two qian. Grind to powder and take with wine.
3. For acute bacillary dysentery: Zhu Sha Qi (raw, ground to powder) each dose seven fen. Take with warm boiled water, 3–4 times daily, may be taken for seven days consecutively.
4. For irregular menstruation, qi and blood accumulations and abdominal pain: Zhu Sha Qi (thoroughly boiled in vinegar) 3–4 qian. Decoction in water and take.
5. For metrorrhagia: Zhu Sha Qi (stir-fried with vinegar) 3–4 qian, decoct in water and take.
6. For vomiting blood, epistaxis, bloody stools: Zhu Sha Qi (Zhu Sha Qi), Imperata root (Bai Mao Gen), Gou Bai Pi (Gou Bai Pi), Lycium bark (Di Gu Pi) each 4 qian. Decoction in yellow wine and take. (Formulas 2 and below from "Shaanxi Chinese Materia Medica")
