Prescription of Guizhi Decoction Without Shaoyao (Guizhi Qu Shaoyao Tang)
Ingredients
Treatise on Cold Damage (Shanghan Lun).
Cinnamon Twig (Guizhi) 3 liang (peeled), Licorice (Gan Cao) 2 liang (honey-fried), Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang) 3 liang (sliced), Jujube (Da Zao) 12 pieces (broken).
Therapeutic actions
Promotes sweating, releases the muscles, dispels wind, removes yin and communicates yang.
Actions of Guizhi Decoction Without Shaoyao (Guizhi Qu Shaoyao Tang)
Indications
Taiyang disease; after purgation, in cases with a hurried pulse and chest fullness.
Administration
Use 7 sheng of water, boil down to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, take 1 sheng warm.
Commentaries of various authors
1. On Further Discussion (Shang Lun Pian): Use the acrid and sweet nature of Cinnamon Twig (Guizhi) to promptly disperse the Taiyang pathogenic factor; as for the intention of removing Shaoyao (Shao Yao), the two words "sour" and "contractive" are insufficient to fully express it, and because it may mistakenly purge one does not dare to use it, fearing it will again lead the yang pathogenic factor downward into the abdomen.
2. Collected Pearls on Cold Damage (Shanghan Guanzhu Ji): The pathogenic qi still resides in the yang division, therefore Cinnamon Twig (Guizhi), Licorice (Gan Cao), Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang), and Jujube (Da Zao) — sweet, acrid, warm medicines — draw it from the yang and expel it; those who remove Shaoyao (Shao Yao) fear that its sour and cold flavor would be sufficient to retain the pathogenic factor in the chest, and would also deprive Cinnamon Twig (Guizhi) of its nature.
