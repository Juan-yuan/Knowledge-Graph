Prescription of Kudzu Decoction (Ge Gen Tang)
Source
Treatise on Cold Damage (Shang Han Lun)
Ingredients
Kudzu root (Ge Gen) 12 g; Ephedra (Ma Huang) 9 g (nodes removed); Cinnamon twig (Gui Zhi) 6 g (peeled); Fresh ginger (Sheng Jiang) 9 g (sliced); Licorice (Gan Cao) 6 g (honey‑fried); Peony root (Shao Yao) 6 g; Jujube (Da Zao) 12 pieces (split)
Explanation
In the formula, kudzu root (Ge Gen) raises the body fluids and moistens the tendons and vessels, serving as the chief; ephedra (Ma Huang) and cinnamon twig (Gui Zhi) disperse wind‑cold and induce sweating to release the exterior, serving as deputies; peony root (Shao Yao) and licorice (Gan Cao) generate fluids and nourish the liquids, relax spasm and relieve pain as assistants; fresh ginger (Sheng Jiang) and jujube (Da Zao) harmonize the Spleen and Stomach and stimulate the Spleen and Stomach to generate qi as messengers. Together the herbs produce sweating to release the exterior, raise fluids and relax the tendons.
Actions of Kudzu Decoction (Ge Gen Tang)
Indications
Induces sweating and relieves toxicity, raises fluids and relaxes the tendons. Treats exterior excess due to wind‑cold, with aversion to cold and fever, headache, stiffness of the nape and back, body aches without sweating, slight abdominal pain, or diarrhea, or retching, or slight shortness of breath; tongue pale with white coating; pulse floating and tight. Currently used for common cold, influenza, measles, dysentery, and joint pain when the above symptoms are present.
Administration
Take the seven herbs above with 1 sheng (1 L) of water; first boil ephedra (Ma Huang) and kudzu root (Ge Gen) down to 800 ml, remove the scum, add the other herbs, then boil down to obtain 300 ml, remove the dregs; take 150 ml warm each time, cover to induce slight sweating.
