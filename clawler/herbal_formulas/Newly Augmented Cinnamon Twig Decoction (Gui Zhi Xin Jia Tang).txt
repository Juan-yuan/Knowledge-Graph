Newly Augmented Cinnamon Twig Decoction (Gui Zhi Xin Jia Tang)

Formula of Cinnamon Twig Newly Augmented Decoction (Gui Zhi Xin Jia Tang)
Composition
Cinnamon Twig (Gui Zhi) (peeled) 9 g Peony (Shao Yao) 12 g Licorice (Zhi Gan Cao) (roasted) 6 g Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang) (sliced) 12 g Jujube (Da Zao) (split) 12 pieces Ginseng (Ren Shen) 9 g
Actions of Cinnamon Twig Newly Augmented Decoction (Gui Zhi Xin Jia Tang)
Indications
Releases the muscles and expels wind, opens the nutritive (ying), replenishes body fluids (jin-ye) and harmonizes ying and wei. Used for, after sweating, body aches with a deep slow pulse. When the pulse is deep and slow there may be painful obstruction (bi), or contraction of the four limbs, or focal distention and obstruction below the heart.
Mnemonic
In the Newly Augmented Decoction increase peony and ginger, further add ginseng to strengthen the stomach. When sweat exits with body pain the exterior and interior are deficient; supporting the upright and expelling the pathogenic clearly manifests its efficacy.
Commentary
The diagnostic key for this pattern is a Gui Zhi Tang pattern with marked body pain, deficiency of stomach qi, and a deep slow pulse. This pattern is commonly seen in the later stage of acute Shang Han disease, or in various chronic diseases; when there are exterior signs together with symptoms of stomach qi deficiency, one may further examine to determine whether this formula pattern is present. If the exterior signs are unresolved while there are signs of interior deficiency, treatment must support the interior deficiency in order to dispel the external pathogen. If there are additionally signs such as reversal of cold (jue ni), diarrhea, or the like, this formula should not be used; one should follow the established principle of rescuing the interior first and then treating the exterior.
Usage
The above six ingredients: with 1 dou 2 sheng of water, boil down to obtain 3 sheng, remove the dregs, and take 1 sheng warm. This was called Gui Zhi Tang originally; now add Peony (Shao Yao), Fresh Ginger (Sheng Jiang), and Ginseng (Ren Shen).
