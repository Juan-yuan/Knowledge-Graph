Prescription of Ephedra Decoction (Ma Huang Tang)
Source
Treatise on Cold Damage (Shang Han Lun: Bian Taiyang Bing Mai Zheng Bing Zhi Zhong)
Classification
Exterior-releasing formula - acrid-warm release of the exterior
Ingredients
Ephedra (Ma Huang) 9 g, Cinnamon Twig (Gui Zhi) 6 g, Apricot Kernel (Xing Ren) 6 g, Honey-fried Licorice (Zhi Gan Cao) 3 g.
Efficacy of Ephedra Decoction (Ma Huang Tang)
Actions
Induces sweating to release the exterior, disseminates the Lung and relieves wheezing.
Indications
Exterior contraction of wind-cold: aversion to cold with fever, headache and body aches, absence of sweating with wheezing, tongue coating thin and white, pulse floating and tight. (This formula is commonly used for common cold, influenza, acute bronchitis, bronchial asthma, and other conditions characterized by exterior excess due to wind-cold.)
Key diagnostic points
Aversion to cold with fever, no sweating and wheezing, pulse floating and tight.
Administration
Take the four herbs with 9 sheng of water; first decoct the Ephedra, reduce by 2 sheng, skim off the upper scum, add the other herbs, boil down to 2.5 sheng, remove dregs, warm and take 8 he. Cover to obtain a slight, near-sweat; no need to sip gruel; the rest is to rest and recuperate as in the Gui Zhi method. (Modern usage: decoct in water and take warm, cover to induce slight sweating.)
Precautions
1. Generally used in winter;
2. Mostly used in young and middle-aged adults;
3. More commonly used in northern regions;
4. Ephedra Decoction (Ma Huang Tang) should not be used in patients with sore throat. A sore throat indicates exterior heat; treating a heat pattern with warm-hot herbs will inevitably worsen the condition, therefore it is contraindicated.
